## Current Requirements
The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has established recordkeeping requirements that mandate employers to report workplace injuries and illnesses comprehensively. These requirements aim to enhance transparency and improve workplace safety standards across various industries.

## Who Is Affected
Employers covered by the Occupational Safety and Health Act, particularly those with more than 10 employees, must comply with these regulations. However, certain low-risk industries may be exempt from these requirements. Employers in high-hazard industries are generally required to maintain detailed records of all work-related injuries and illnesses.

## What Employers Should Do
Employers should review their current recordkeeping practices to ensure compliance with OSHA's requirements. This may include updating their injury and illness logs, training staff on reporting procedures, and implementing processes to ensure accurate and timely reporting of incidents. Additionally, employers should consider consulting legal counsel to address any specific concerns regarding compliance.

## Key Deadlines
- **Ongoing**: Employers are required to maintain accurate records of workplace injuries and illnesses as they occur, with annual summaries due by February 1 of the following year.
