## Employment Challenges

### Remote Work Compliance
Professional services firms continue to navigate remote work arrangements. Ensure compliance with multi-state employment laws, particularly wage and hour regulations. Review employee classification under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) to avoid misclassification. The standard salary threshold remains $684/week or $35,568/year after the 2024 rule was vacated.

### Talent Retention
Retaining skilled professionals remains a challenge. Consider enhancing benefits packages to include flexible work options and professional development opportunities. Evaluate current compensation structures against industry benchmarks to remain competitive.

## Compliance Considerations

### EEO-1 Reporting
The EEO-1 Component 1 data collection for 2025 is due by July 31, 2026. Ensure your snapshot pay period is selected and data is accurately compiled. This is critical for compliance with EEOC regulations under 29 CFR Part 1602.

### Health Plan Reporting
Under the Affordable Care Act (ACA), employers with 50 or more full-time employees must file IRS Forms 1094-C and 1095-C by August 1, 2026. Verify that your data is correct and that forms are submitted on time to avoid penalties.

### California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA)
For firms operating in California, compliance with the CPRA is mandatory. Ensure data protection measures align with CPRA requirements, including consumer rights to access and delete personal information. Review your privacy policies and employee training programs accordingly.

## Practical Guidance

### Action Item: EEO-1 Compliance
Pull the EEO-1 snapshot pay period before May 31, 2026, to ensure accurate data collection. Verify that all demographic information is current and complete.

### Action Item: Health Plan Audit
Conduct a mid-year audit of your health plan offerings and ACA compliance status. Confirm that all employee data is up-to-date and ready for IRS filing by August 1, 2026.

### Action Item: Privacy Policy Review
For California-based operations, review and update your privacy policies to ensure CPRA compliance. Conduct staff training sessions to reinforce data protection protocols.

These steps will help ensure your professional services firm remains compliant and competitive in the current regulatory environment.
