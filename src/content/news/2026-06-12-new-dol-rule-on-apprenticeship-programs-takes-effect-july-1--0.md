## DOL's New Apprenticeship Rule

The Department of Labor (DOL) will implement a new rule on July 1, 2026, aimed at standardizing and streamlining apprenticeship programs across the United States. This rule, codified under 29 C.F.R. § 29.30, seeks to simplify the process for establishing and maintaining apprenticeship programs, making it easier for employers to develop their workforce through on-the-job training.

The rule introduces a unified set of standards for apprenticeship programs, focusing on improving consistency and quality. It mandates that all registered apprenticeship programs must include a minimum of 144 hours of related technical instruction per year and at least 2,000 hours of on-the-job training. These requirements are designed to ensure that apprentices receive comprehensive training and education.

## Employer Implications

Employers sponsoring apprenticeship programs must align their existing programs with the new standards by the effective date. This involves reviewing and potentially revising current training curricula to meet the minimum instructional and training hours. Additionally, employers must ensure that their programs are registered with the DOL to remain compliant.

The rule also emphasizes diversity and inclusion, requiring programs to implement affirmative action plans to promote equal opportunity. Employers must collect and report demographic data on participants to demonstrate compliance with these requirements. Failure to comply could result in penalties or loss of program registration.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Programs**: Audit existing apprenticeship programs to ensure they meet the new instructional and training hour requirements. Consider collaborating with educational institutions to enhance technical instruction offerings.

2. **Register Programs**: Ensure all apprenticeship programs are registered with the DOL by July 1, 2026, to avoid non-compliance.

3. **Implement Affirmative Action Plans**: Develop or update affirmative action plans to promote diversity within apprenticeship programs. Collect and report required demographic data to the DOL.

4. **Training for HR and Compliance Teams**: Educate HR and compliance teams about the new rule requirements to ensure smooth implementation and ongoing compliance.

By taking these steps, employers can leverage the benefits of apprenticeship programs while adhering to the new regulations. The streamlined standards aim to enhance workforce development, providing a skilled labor pool tailored to industry needs.
