## SEC's Climate Disclosure Rule

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) finalized its rule on climate-related disclosures for public companies on June 30, 2026. The rule requires companies to report detailed information about their greenhouse gas emissions and climate-related risks in their annual filings. This development is set to impact public companies significantly, with compliance required by fiscal year 2027.

## Key Requirements

Under the new rule, companies must disclose their direct (Scope 1) and indirect (Scope 2) greenhouse gas emissions. Additionally, if material, companies must report Scope 3 emissions, which include emissions from their supply chain and product use. The disclosures must be included in the companies' Form 10-K filings, as stipulated under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

The rule also mandates that companies describe their climate-related risks, including their impact on business strategy and financial planning. Companies must outline their governance processes for managing these risks, aligning with the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) framework.

## Implications for Employers

While the rule directly applies to public companies, private companies in their supply chains may also feel the ripple effects. Public companies might require their suppliers to provide emissions data to fulfill their own reporting obligations. This could lead to increased scrutiny and demands for transparency from suppliers, including small and mid-sized businesses.

## Action Items

Public companies should start preparing for compliance by conducting a comprehensive audit of their emissions data. Implementing robust tracking systems for Scope 1, 2, and potentially Scope 3 emissions is crucial. Companies should also review their risk management and governance frameworks to ensure they align with the new requirements.

For private companies supplying public firms, it is advisable to proactively assess and improve their emissions tracking capabilities. Establishing clear communication channels with public clients to understand their data needs will be essential.

## Conclusion

The SEC's climate disclosure rule marks a significant shift in corporate reporting obligations, emphasizing transparency and accountability in addressing climate-related risks. Public companies and their supply chains must act swiftly to adapt to these new requirements, ensuring compliance by the 2027 deadline.
