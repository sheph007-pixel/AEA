## DOL Finalizes Rule on Employee Meal and Rest Breaks

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on May 15, 2026, establishing federal standards for meal and rest breaks for non-exempt employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The rule, effective August 1, 2026, mandates that employers provide non-exempt employees with a 30-minute unpaid meal break for shifts exceeding six hours and a 10-minute paid rest break for every four hours worked.

The rule aims to standardize break practices across states, many of which have existing laws with varying requirements. Employers operating in states without specific meal and rest break laws must now comply with these federal standards. The DOL's rule is codified under 29 C.F.R. § 785, which outlines the conditions under which breaks must be provided and compensated.

### Compliance Requirements

Employers must ensure that meal breaks are uninterrupted and free from work duties. If an employee is required to perform any work during their meal break, the time must be compensated as paid work time. Rest breaks, although shorter, must also be duty-free to qualify as compensable time.

The rule also requires employers to maintain accurate records of breaks taken, similar to other timekeeping obligations under the FLSA. Employers should update their timekeeping systems and train supervisors to ensure compliance with the new requirements.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers should review existing meal and rest break policies to ensure they align with the new federal standards. Update employee handbooks and communicate changes to all staff.

2. **Train Supervisors and Managers**: Supervisors should be trained on the new requirements to ensure they do not interrupt employee breaks. Proper training can prevent inadvertent violations.

3. **Adjust Timekeeping Systems**: Implement or update timekeeping systems to accurately track break times. This may involve software updates or changes to manual timekeeping practices.

4. **Recordkeeping Compliance**: Ensure that all break times are documented in accordance with FLSA recordkeeping requirements. This includes both meal and rest breaks.

5. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the complexity of integrating federal and state requirements, consulting with legal counsel can help ensure full compliance and mitigate risks.

### Implications for State Laws

While the federal rule sets a baseline, employers must still comply with any state laws that provide greater protections or benefits to employees. For example, if a state law mandates longer breaks or additional rest periods, employers must adhere to the more favorable state provisions.

Employers should conduct a thorough review of applicable state laws in conjunction with the new federal standards to ensure comprehensive compliance. Failure to comply with either federal or state requirements can result in significant penalties and legal challenges.

The DOL's new rule on meal and rest breaks represents a significant shift in federal labor standards, requiring proactive adjustments from employers to meet the August 1, 2026, compliance deadline.
