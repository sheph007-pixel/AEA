## EEOC's New Guidance

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) issued new guidance on June 1, 2026, clarifying employers' obligations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 regarding religious accommodations. The guidance aims to assist employers in understanding how to handle requests for religious accommodations, including dress and grooming practices, religious observances, and other expressions of faith in the workplace.

The EEOC's guidance emphasizes that employers must provide reasonable accommodations for employees' sincerely held religious beliefs unless doing so would cause an undue hardship on the operation of the business. This aligns with the standard set forth in 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(j), which defines religion to include all aspects of religious observance and practice, as well as belief.

## Key Points for Employers

Employers should note that the EEOC's guidance provides examples of what constitutes a reasonable accommodation. This includes flexible scheduling, voluntary shift substitutions, job reassignments, and modifications to workplace policies or practices. The guidance also clarifies that the definition of "undue hardship" remains a minimal burden on the employer, which is less stringent than the "undue hardship" standard under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

Employers are advised to engage in an interactive process with employees requesting religious accommodations. This involves a dialogue to explore possible accommodations and assess their feasibility. The EEOC underscores the importance of documenting these discussions to ensure compliance and avoid potential disputes.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers should review and, if necessary, update their policies on religious accommodations to ensure compliance with the new EEOC guidance. This includes training HR personnel and managers on how to handle accommodation requests appropriately.

2. **Document Accommodation Requests**: Implement a system for documenting all religious accommodation requests and the interactive process followed. This can serve as evidence of compliance in the event of an EEOC investigation or litigation.

3. **Assess Undue Hardship Claims**: Carefully evaluate any claims of undue hardship to ensure they meet the EEOC's defined threshold. Employers should be prepared to justify any denial of accommodation requests based on undue hardship with concrete evidence.

4. **Engage in Interactive Process**: Establish a clear procedure for engaging in the interactive process with employees requesting accommodations. This should include steps for exploring alternative accommodations if the initial request poses an undue hardship.

The EEOC's guidance provides a framework for employers to navigate religious accommodation requests effectively. By taking proactive steps to align with these guidelines, employers can foster an inclusive workplace while minimizing legal risks.
