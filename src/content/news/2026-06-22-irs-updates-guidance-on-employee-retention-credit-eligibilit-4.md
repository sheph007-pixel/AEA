## IRS Clarifies Employee Retention Credit Eligibility

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidance on June 15, 2026, clarifying eligibility criteria for the Employee Retention Credit (ERC) under the CARES Act. This clarification is crucial for businesses that have been navigating the complexities of claiming this tax credit, which was designed to encourage employers to retain employees during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The ERC provides eligible employers with a refundable tax credit against certain employment taxes. The recent guidance addresses ambiguities related to the definition of a "significant decline in gross receipts" and the criteria for "full or partial suspension of operations" due to governmental orders. 

### Key Points from the Guidance

The IRS has specified that a "significant decline in gross receipts" is defined as a reduction of more than 50% in gross receipts compared to the same quarter in 2019. This definition applies to the period from March 13, 2020, to December 31, 2021. For businesses that started operations after 2019, the IRS provides alternative methods for calculating this decline.

Additionally, the IRS elaborated on what constitutes a "full or partial suspension of operations." This includes scenarios where an employer’s operations are fully or partially suspended due to governmental orders limiting commerce, travel, or group meetings due to COVID-19. The guidance emphasizes that mere economic hardship is insufficient; there must be a direct link to governmental orders.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Financial Records**: Employers should assess their quarterly gross receipts for the relevant periods to determine if they meet the "significant decline" threshold. This may involve consulting with financial advisors or accountants to ensure accuracy.

2. **Document Government Orders**: Businesses claiming the credit based on a suspension of operations should maintain thorough documentation of the governmental orders that impacted their operations. This includes dates, the specific nature of the restrictions, and how these restrictions affected business activities.

3. **Consult Tax Professionals**: Given the complexities of ERC eligibility and the potential for significant financial implications, employers are advised to consult with tax professionals or legal counsel. This ensures that claims are accurately filed and that businesses maximize their eligible credits.

### Implications for Small to Mid-Sized Businesses

For small and mid-sized businesses, the ERC can provide substantial financial relief. However, the updated IRS guidance underscores the importance of understanding and complying with the specific eligibility criteria. Failure to adhere to these guidelines can result in denied claims or potential penalties.

The IRS's clarification aims to streamline the process for businesses seeking to claim the ERC, reducing uncertainty and administrative burdens. Employers should act promptly to review their eligibility under the updated guidance to take full advantage of this credit.
