## IRS Clarifies Tax Treatment of Employee Relocation Expenses

The IRS issued new guidance on April 30, 2026, regarding the tax treatment of employee relocation expenses, affecting employers who provide relocation benefits. The guidance clarifies that certain relocation expenses, previously considered non-taxable, are now subject to federal income tax under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) of 2017 (26 U.S.C. § 132(g)). This change requires immediate adjustments to employer policies and payroll systems.

## Key Changes and Implications

Under the TCJA, the moving expense deduction was suspended for tax years 2018 through 2025, except for active-duty military members. The latest guidance from the IRS reaffirms that employer-paid relocation expenses, such as moving household goods and travel costs, are taxable wages. This clarification impacts payroll processing and requires employers to withhold federal income taxes on these benefits.

Employers must update their relocation policies to reflect these changes. Failure to do so could result in non-compliance with federal tax laws, leading to potential penalties and interest charges. Additionally, affected employees may experience unexpected tax liabilities, impacting their net compensation and overall satisfaction with relocation packages.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Relocation Policies:** Employers should immediately review their current relocation policies to ensure compliance with the IRS guidance. This includes updating any references to tax-free treatment of moving expenses.

2. **Adjust Payroll Systems:** Ensure that payroll systems are configured to treat relocation benefits as taxable income. This includes proper withholding of federal income taxes, Social Security, and Medicare taxes.

3. **Communicate with Employees:** Clearly communicate the changes to employees who are eligible for relocation benefits. Provide guidance on how these changes may affect their tax liabilities and net compensation.

4. **Consult Tax Professionals:** Engage with tax advisors or payroll specialists to ensure compliance with the updated IRS rules and to assist with any necessary adjustments to payroll processing systems.

5. **Monitor Further Developments:** Stay informed about any additional IRS announcements or changes to the TCJA that may impact relocation expenses or other fringe benefits.

## Conclusion

The IRS's clarification on the tax treatment of relocation expenses under the TCJA requires immediate attention from employers. By updating policies, adjusting payroll systems, and effectively communicating with employees, employers can ensure compliance and mitigate any negative impacts on their workforce.
