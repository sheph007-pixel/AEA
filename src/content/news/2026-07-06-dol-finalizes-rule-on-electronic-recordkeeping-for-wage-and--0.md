## New Electronic Recordkeeping Requirements

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on July 1, 2026, requiring employers to maintain electronic records for wage and hour compliance starting January 1, 2027. This rule, under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), aims to enhance accessibility and accuracy of employee wage records.

The rule requires employers to store records electronically, ensuring they are easily retrievable for DOL audits and employee access. This includes timekeeping records, payroll data, and any relevant documentation related to wage calculations or exemptions. The DOL emphasizes that electronic records must be maintained in a format that is secure, tamper-proof, and accessible to authorized personnel.

## Impact on Employers

Employers with 2-500 employees must assess their current recordkeeping systems to ensure compliance with the new electronic requirements. This rule impacts all employers covered by the FLSA, including those with employees classified under exemptions such as the executive, administrative, and professional categories (29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1)).

Noncompliance could result in penalties or increased scrutiny during DOL audits. Employers are advised to work with IT and HR departments to implement or upgrade electronic recordkeeping systems that meet the new standards.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Current Recordkeeping Systems**: Evaluate existing systems to determine if they can support electronic storage and retrieval of wage and hour records.

2. **Implement Secure Electronic Systems**: If necessary, invest in new software or services that provide secure and compliant electronic recordkeeping solutions.

3. **Train HR and Payroll Staff**: Ensure staff are trained on the new system and understand how to maintain and retrieve records as required by the DOL.

4. **Review Data Security Protocols**: Strengthen data protection measures to prevent unauthorized access and ensure records are tamper-proof.

5. **Prepare for DOL Audits**: Develop processes for quick retrieval of records to facilitate efficient DOL audits and minimize disruption.

As the effective date approaches, it is crucial for employers to prioritize these updates to avoid potential penalties and ensure smooth compliance with the new rule.
