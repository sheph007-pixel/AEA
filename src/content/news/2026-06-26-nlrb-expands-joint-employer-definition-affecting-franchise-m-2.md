## Broader Joint Employer Definition Adopted

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a new rule on June 15, 2026, expanding the criteria for determining joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This rule, effective immediately, significantly impacts businesses operating under franchise models and those using staffing agencies.

The rule redefines joint employers as entities that share or codetermine the essential terms and conditions of employment, such as hiring, firing, discipline, supervision, and direction. Previously, the NLRB required direct and immediate control over these aspects to establish joint employer status. The new rule allows for indirect control or reserved authority to suffice, aligning with the NLRB's Browning-Ferris decision precedent.

## Implications for Employers

Franchisors and companies using third-party labor providers must reassess their relationships and agreements in light of this broader definition. The rule's expansion means that more businesses could be held liable for unfair labor practices and collective bargaining obligations as joint employers.

Businesses should review contracts with franchisees and staffing agencies to identify any clauses that could imply control over employment terms. This includes evaluating policies that dictate work schedules, employee training, or operational procedures that might suggest shared control.

## Action Items for Compliance

1. **Audit Existing Contracts**: Examine current agreements with franchisees and staffing agencies for language that could establish joint employer status. Look for clauses indicating control over employment conditions.

2. **Revise Operational Policies**: Adjust policies that might imply indirect control over another entity's employees. Ensure that operational guidelines do not overstep into areas that could be interpreted as shared employment control.

3. **Legal Consultation**: Engage legal counsel to navigate the implications of the new rule and to ensure compliance with the expanded joint employer criteria.

4. **Training for Management**: Implement training programs for management to understand the implications of the new rule and how to maintain compliance without overstepping into joint employer territory.

The NLRB's decision marks a significant shift in labor relations, emphasizing the need for businesses to carefully manage their relationships with franchisees and third-party labor providers to mitigate potential liabilities.
