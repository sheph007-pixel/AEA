## IRS Updates ESPP Guidelines

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has released new guidelines for Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs), effective July 1, 2026, affecting how companies administer these plans. The changes are part of the IRS's ongoing efforts to ensure compliance with the Internal Revenue Code Section 423, which governs ESPPs.

## Key Changes to Note

The new guidelines clarify the tax treatment of ESPPs and introduce additional reporting requirements. Under Section 423, ESPPs allow employees to purchase company stock at a discount, typically up to 15%, without immediate tax consequences. The IRS now requires more detailed documentation of the purchase price and the discount offered to employees.

Furthermore, employers must ensure that the plan's offering period does not exceed 27 months, aligning with the statutory limit. The IRS has emphasized that any deviation from this period could disqualify the plan, resulting in adverse tax consequences for both the employer and employees.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Amend ESPP Documents**: Employers should immediately review their ESPP documents to ensure compliance with the new IRS guidelines. Amendments may be necessary to align with the clarified offering period and documentation requirements.

2. **Enhance Record-Keeping Practices**: Implement robust record-keeping systems to track employee stock purchases and discounts accurately. This will facilitate compliance with the new reporting requirements.

3. **Communicate with Employees**: Employers should communicate these changes to employees participating in ESPPs. Clear communication will help employees understand how the changes might impact their stock purchases and potential tax liabilities.

4. **Consult Tax Professionals**: Given the complexity of tax laws governing ESPPs, consulting with tax professionals or legal counsel is advisable to ensure full compliance and to address any specific concerns related to the new guidelines.

## Implications for Compliance

The updated IRS guidelines underscore the importance of maintaining compliant ESPPs. Non-compliance can lead to significant tax liabilities and penalties. Employers must act swiftly to align their plans with the new requirements to avoid these risks.

In summary, the IRS's new guidelines on ESPPs necessitate immediate attention from employers offering such plans. By taking proactive steps to review and amend plan documents, enhance record-keeping, and communicate changes to employees, employers can ensure compliance and continue to offer valuable stock purchase opportunities to their workforce.
