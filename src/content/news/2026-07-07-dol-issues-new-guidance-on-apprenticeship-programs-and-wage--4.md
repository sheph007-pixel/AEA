## DOL Clarifies Wage Compliance for Apprenticeship Programs

On June 30, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance regarding wage compliance for apprenticeship programs under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This guidance aims to ensure that employers properly compensate apprentices in accordance with federal wage laws, specifically addressing the minimum wage and overtime requirements under 29 U.S.C. § 206 and § 207.

The guidance clarifies that apprentices must be paid at least the federal minimum wage, currently set at $7.25 per hour, unless a higher state or local minimum wage applies. Additionally, apprentices are entitled to overtime pay at one and one-half times their regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek, unless they qualify for an exemption.

## Implications for Employers

Employers utilizing apprenticeship programs should review their current pay practices to ensure compliance with the FLSA. The DOL's guidance emphasizes that apprentices cannot be classified as independent contractors to avoid wage obligations. Misclassification could result in significant penalties, including back wages and damages.

Furthermore, the guidance stresses the importance of maintaining accurate records of hours worked by apprentices. Employers must ensure that all time, including training and instruction hours, is recorded and compensated appropriately. The DOL notes that failure to maintain proper records can complicate compliance and lead to disputes.

## Action Items for HR and Business Owners

1. **Audit Current Apprenticeship Pay Practices**: Employers should conduct a thorough audit of their apprenticeship programs to verify that all apprentices are receiving at least the applicable minimum wage and are compensated for overtime.

2. **Review Classification**: Ensure that apprentices are correctly classified as employees under the FLSA, not as independent contractors.

3. **Recordkeeping**: Implement robust time-tracking systems to accurately capture all hours worked by apprentices, including training and instruction.

4. **Training for Compliance**: Consider providing training for HR personnel and managers overseeing apprenticeship programs to ensure they understand and adhere to wage and hour laws.

5. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to review apprenticeship agreements and practices to ensure full compliance with DOL regulations.

This new guidance serves as a reminder of the DOL's focus on protecting workers' rights in apprenticeship programs. Employers who proactively align their practices with these guidelines will mitigate the risk of non-compliance and potential legal challenges.
