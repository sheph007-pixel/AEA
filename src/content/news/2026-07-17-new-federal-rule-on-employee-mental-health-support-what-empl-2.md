## New Federal Rule on Mental Health Support

The Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule on July 15, 2026, mandating that employers with more than 50 employees provide mental health support services starting January 1, 2027. This rule is part of the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA) and aims to ensure that mental health benefits are on par with physical health benefits.

## Key Provisions of the Rule

Under the new rule, employers must offer access to mental health professionals, such as counselors or therapists, either on-site or through covered telehealth services. The rule specifies that these services should be available at least once a month. Employers are also required to conduct annual mental health training for managers and HR personnel to recognize and address mental health issues promptly.

The rule further mandates that mental health benefits in group health plans should not have more restrictive financial requirements or treatment limitations than those applied to medical and surgical benefits. This aligns with the existing requirements under 29 U.S.C. § 1185a.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers need to review their current health benefits packages and ensure compliance with the new rule by the January 2027 deadline. This may involve negotiating with health plan providers to expand coverage options or contracting with third-party mental health service providers.

Additionally, employers should plan and schedule mental health training sessions for relevant staff before the end of 2026. Developing clear internal policies on mental health support and communicating these to employees will be crucial for compliance and effective implementation.

## Potential Challenges and Considerations

While the rule aims to improve workplace mental health, employers may face challenges, such as increased costs associated with expanding health benefits and organizing training sessions. It's important to consider these factors when budgeting for the upcoming year.

Employers should also be aware of potential privacy concerns related to mental health support. Ensuring that employee information is kept confidential and secure will be essential to maintaining trust and compliance with data protection laws.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new rule on mental health support underscores the growing importance of mental health in the workplace. By taking proactive steps to comply with the rule, employers can not only meet legal requirements but also foster a healthier, more supportive work environment.
