## NLRB Ruling on Joint Employer Status

On June 5, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a ruling that broadens the criteria for determining joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This decision, which revisits standards set in Browning-Ferris Industries, affects businesses that utilize staffing agencies or franchise models, potentially increasing their liability in labor disputes.

## What Changed?

The NLRB's decision expands the definition of a joint employer to include entities that exert indirect control over the terms and conditions of employment or have the potential to do so. Previously, direct and immediate control was required to establish joint employer status. This shift means that businesses could be held accountable for labor practices at workplaces they do not directly manage.

## Who Is Affected?

This ruling impacts small to medium-sized businesses (SMBs) that rely on third-party staffing agencies or operate under franchise agreements. Companies with fewer than 500 employees, who often use these models to manage costs and flexibility, must now consider the implications of being deemed a joint employer.

## Implications for Employers

Businesses classified as joint employers under the new standard may face increased obligations, including collective bargaining responsibilities and liability for unfair labor practices. This could lead to higher operational costs and necessitate changes in how businesses manage their workforce relationships.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Contracts:** Audit existing contracts with staffing agencies and franchise agreements to identify any language that might imply indirect control over employment terms.

2. **Evaluate HR Policies:** Assess HR policies and practices to ensure compliance with the new joint employer standard. Consider consulting with legal counsel to understand potential liabilities.

3. **Training and Communication:** Train managers and HR personnel on the implications of the NLRB's ruling to prevent inadvertent actions that could establish joint employer status.

4. **Strategic Adjustments:** Consider restructuring business relationships to minimize risks associated with being classified as a joint employer. This might involve renegotiating contracts to limit control over third-party employees.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's expanded definition of joint employer status marks a significant shift in labor relations, particularly for SMBs. By taking proactive steps to understand and mitigate the risks associated with this ruling, employers can better navigate the complexities of joint employment and safeguard their operations against potential liabilities.
