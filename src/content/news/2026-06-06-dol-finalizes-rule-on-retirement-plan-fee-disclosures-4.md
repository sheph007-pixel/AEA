## New DOL Rule on Retirement Plan Fee Disclosures

On June 1, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule requiring more transparent fee disclosures for retirement plans. This rule, effective January 1, 2027, aims to help employees better understand the costs associated with their 401(k) and other retirement plans. The rule amends the regulations under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA), specifically 29 C.F.R. § 2550.404a-5.

## Key Changes

The new rule mandates that plan administrators provide participants with a simplified fee disclosure statement. This statement must include a summary of all fees and expenses deducted from their accounts in a clear and concise format. The goal is to eliminate confusion and ensure participants can compare costs across different plans easily.

Under the amended regulations, disclosures must be presented in a standardized format, detailing administrative fees, individual service fees, and investment-related fees. Additionally, any changes to fee structures must be communicated to participants at least 30 days in advance.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should start preparing for the new disclosure requirements now to ensure compliance by the January 1, 2027, deadline. Key steps include:

1. **Review Current Disclosures**: Audit existing fee disclosures to identify areas needing adjustment to meet the new format requirements.
   
2. **Update Communication Materials**: Work with plan administrators to revise communication materials and ensure they adhere to the new standardized format.

3. **Train HR Staff**: Educate HR personnel on the new requirements to effectively assist employees with any questions about the changes.

4. **Coordinate with Plan Providers**: Engage with retirement plan providers early to ensure they are also prepared to comply with the new disclosure rules.

## Implications for Employers

This rule is part of a broader DOL initiative to enhance retirement plan transparency and empower employees to make informed decisions about their retirement savings. Employers who fail to comply with the new requirements risk penalties and potential legal challenges under ERISA.

By taking proactive steps to align with the new rule, employers can improve participant satisfaction and demonstrate a commitment to transparency and fiduciary responsibility.
