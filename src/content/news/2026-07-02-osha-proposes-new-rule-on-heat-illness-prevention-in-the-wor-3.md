## OSHA's Heat Illness Prevention Proposal

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) proposed a new rule on June 28, 2026, requiring employers to implement heat illness prevention plans in workplaces. This move comes as part of OSHA's efforts to address the rising number of heat-related illnesses and fatalities among workers, particularly in industries such as construction, agriculture, and manufacturing.

The proposed rule mandates that employers develop and implement a comprehensive heat illness prevention plan by January 1, 2027. Key components of the plan include monitoring temperature and humidity levels, providing adequate water and rest breaks, and implementing acclimatization procedures for new or returning workers. Employers must also train employees on recognizing heat illness symptoms and responding effectively.

## Impact on Employers

This proposed rule affects all employers with outdoor or indoor work environments where heat levels pose a risk to workers' health. Industries like construction, landscaping, agriculture, and manufacturing are particularly impacted due to their high exposure to heat.

Employers need to assess their current heat illness prevention measures and prepare to comply with the new requirements. This includes investing in training programs, updating workplace policies, and potentially modifying work schedules to mitigate heat exposure risks.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Develop a Heat Illness Prevention Plan**: Begin drafting a plan that includes monitoring environmental conditions, providing water and rest, and acclimatizing workers.
   
2. **Conduct Training**: Implement training sessions for employees to help them identify and respond to heat-related symptoms.

3. **Review Work Schedules**: Consider adjusting work hours to cooler times of the day or increasing the frequency of rest breaks during high-heat conditions.

4. **Monitor Conditions**: Invest in tools and systems to monitor workplace temperature and humidity levels effectively.

5. **Prepare for Compliance**: Ensure that all measures comply with OSHA's proposed guidelines by the January 1, 2027 deadline.

OSHA's proposal is currently open for public comment until September 30, 2026. Employers are encouraged to review the proposed rule and submit feedback. This rule aims to enhance worker safety and reduce the incidence of heat-related illnesses, aligning with OSHA's mission to ensure safe and healthful working conditions.
