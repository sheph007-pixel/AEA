## California's Pay Transparency Mandate

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed SB 1162 into law, mandating that employers include pay scales in all job postings starting January 1, 2026. This law applies to employers with 15 or more employees and aims to address pay disparities by promoting transparency in compensation practices.

The new law requires that job postings include a salary range that the employer reasonably expects to pay for the position. This move follows similar legislation in states like Colorado and New York and reflects a growing trend towards transparency in the hiring process. Employers failing to comply with this requirement may face penalties enforced by the California Labor Commissioner.

## Compliance Requirements

Employers need to prepare for the implementation of SB 1162 by auditing their current job postings and compensation practices. This involves determining appropriate salary ranges for each position and ensuring that these ranges are based on market data and internal equity considerations. Employers should also review their existing pay structures to identify and address any disparities that could lead to legal scrutiny.

Additionally, the law mandates that employers maintain records of job titles and wage history for each employee for the duration of their employment plus three years. This documentation will be critical in defending against any claims of pay discrimination or non-compliance with the new requirements.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Job Postings:** Review all current and future job postings to include salary ranges in compliance with SB 1162.
2. **Establish Salary Ranges:** Develop clear and justifiable salary ranges for each position based on market research and internal pay equity.
3. **Record Keeping:** Implement or update systems to maintain comprehensive records of job titles, salary ranges, and employee wage history.
4. **Training and Communication:** Educate HR personnel and hiring managers on the new requirements to ensure consistent and compliant communication with job candidates.

## Implications for HR and Legal Teams

HR and legal teams must collaborate to ensure that all aspects of the new law are understood and implemented effectively. This includes updating internal policies and procedures related to recruitment and compensation. Legal teams should also be prepared to handle any potential disputes or claims that arise from the implementation of pay transparency measures.

The enactment of SB 1162 underscores the importance of proactive compliance strategies in employment law. By taking the necessary steps now, employers can mitigate risks and foster a more equitable workplace environment.
