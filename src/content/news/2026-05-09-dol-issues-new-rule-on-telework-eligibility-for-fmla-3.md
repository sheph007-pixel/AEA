## DOL Clarifies Telework Eligibility Under FMLA

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has issued a new rule clarifying the eligibility criteria for teleworkers under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). Effective July 1, 2026, this rule aims to address ambiguity surrounding telework and FMLA eligibility, specifically focusing on the "worksite" definition for remote employees.

### Key Changes

The new rule specifies that for teleworkers, the "worksite" is the office to which they report and from which assignments are made, rather than the employee's home. This clarification impacts the FMLA's 50-employee threshold within a 75-mile radius, a crucial factor in determining FMLA eligibility.

### Implications for Employers

Employers with remote workers must reassess which employees qualify for FMLA leave. The rule requires employers to calculate the 50-employee threshold based on the physical office location, not the employee's home. This may result in some employees gaining or losing eligibility depending on their proximity to the main office.

### Action Items

1. **Review Employee Locations**: Employers should map out where their teleworkers report to ensure accurate FMLA eligibility assessments.

2. **Update Policies**: Amend FMLA policies to reflect the updated definition of "worksite" for remote employees.

3. **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees about the new rule and how it may affect their FMLA eligibility.

4. **Train HR Staff**: Ensure HR teams understand the new criteria and can accurately determine eligibility for teleworkers.

This new rule is part of the DOL's broader efforts to adapt labor laws to the evolving work environment, where telework has become more prevalent. Employers must act promptly to comply with these changes and avoid potential legal challenges.
