## FTC Updates Green Guides

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) released updated guidance on July 10, 2026, concerning environmental marketing claims, commonly known as the "Green Guides." These revisions aim to clarify how businesses can truthfully market their products as environmentally friendly. The update impacts companies that make claims about the sustainability, recyclability, and overall environmental impact of their products.

## Key Changes in the Green Guides

The updated Green Guides include more stringent requirements for substantiating claims about the environmental benefits of products. For instance, businesses must provide clear evidence to support claims that a product is "biodegradable" or "compostable." The FTC has also emphasized that claims of "carbon neutrality" must be backed by credible and comprehensive carbon offset programs.

Additionally, the guidance now explicitly addresses claims related to "recyclable" products. Businesses must ensure that recycling facilities for their products are available to a substantial majority of consumers or communities where the item is sold. This change aims to prevent misleading claims that could confuse consumers about the actual recyclability of products.

## Implications for Employers

Employers should review their current marketing strategies and product labels to ensure compliance with the updated Green Guides. Misleading environmental claims can result in significant penalties under the FTC Act, which prohibits deceptive advertising practices.

Businesses are advised to conduct an audit of all marketing materials that include environmental claims. This audit should assess whether claims are substantiated by scientific evidence and whether the language used is clear and unambiguous. Any claims that cannot be substantiated should be revised or removed.

## Action Items for Compliance

1. **Audit Marketing Claims**: Review all marketing materials and product labels for compliance with the updated Green Guides. Ensure that all environmental claims are backed by credible evidence.

2. **Train Marketing Teams**: Educate marketing and product development teams on the new requirements to prevent misleading claims and ensure accurate communication of product benefits.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to evaluate the risk of current claims and develop strategies for compliance. Legal experts can provide guidance on the nuances of the updated Green Guides.

4. **Monitor Developments**: Stay informed about any further updates or enforcement actions by the FTC related to environmental marketing claims.

The FTC's updated Green Guides reflect a growing emphasis on transparency and honesty in environmental marketing. By ensuring compliance, businesses can avoid penalties and maintain consumer trust in their brand's environmental commitments.
