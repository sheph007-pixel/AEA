## USCIS Updates Verification Rules

The U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) announced significant changes to the Form I-9 employment verification process, effective August 1, 2026. These changes impact how employers verify the identity and employment authorization of new hires. The revised rules aim to streamline the verification process while enhancing security measures.

## Key Changes to Form I-9

Under the new regulations, employers will be required to use an updated version of Form I-9. This form includes enhanced security features and revised instructions to improve accuracy in completion. Notably, the updated form allows for electronic verification, reducing the need for physical document handling.

Another critical change is the introduction of a mandatory re-verification process for employees with work authorization that is set to expire. Employers must now track expiration dates more diligently and ensure timely re-verification to avoid penalties.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers must prepare for these changes by updating their internal I-9 compliance procedures. Key action steps include:

1. **Adopt the New Form**: Ensure the updated Form I-9 is integrated into your onboarding process by August 1, 2026. Discard outdated versions to prevent compliance issues.

2. **Train HR Staff**: Provide training for HR personnel on the new form and verification procedures. Familiarize them with the electronic verification option and the re-verification requirements.

3. **Audit Current Records**: Conduct an audit of existing Form I-9 records to identify employees with expiring work authorization. Establish a system for timely re-verification to maintain compliance.

4. **Implement a Tracking System**: Develop a system to track work authorization expiration dates and schedule re-verification in advance. Consider using HR software that offers automated reminders.

## Compliance and Penalties

Failure to comply with the new Form I-9 requirements can result in significant penalties. Employers found using outdated forms or failing to complete re-verification may face fines ranging from $250 to $2,000 per violation, as stipulated under 8 U.S.C. § 1324a.

The USCIS emphasizes the importance of these changes in maintaining a secure and efficient employment verification process. Employers are encouraged to take proactive steps to ensure a smooth transition to the new requirements.
