## IRS Guidance on Fringe Benefits for Remote Employees

On June 1, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new guidance clarifying the tax treatment of fringe benefits provided to remote workers. This guidance is crucial as more companies adapt to hybrid and fully remote work models. The IRS's update addresses how employers should handle benefits like home office stipends, internet reimbursements, and wellness program incentives, ensuring compliance with current tax laws.

The IRS guidance emphasizes that certain benefits provided to remote employees may be excluded from taxable income under specific conditions. For example, reimbursements for home office expenses must meet the criteria set forth in IRC Section 132(d), which defines working condition fringe benefits. To qualify, these expenses must be both ordinary and necessary for the employee's role and substantiated with appropriate documentation.

## Key Points for Employers

Employers must ensure that reimbursements for internet and phone services are directly related to business use to qualify for tax exclusion. The IRS notes that personal use portions of these services are taxable and should be reported as such. This distinction is vital for maintaining compliance and avoiding potential tax liabilities.

Additionally, the IRS has clarified that wellness program incentives provided to remote employees, such as gym memberships or virtual fitness classes, can be excluded from taxable income if they meet the requirements of IRC Section 132(j)(4). These incentives must be part of a qualified wellness program, and employers should maintain records that demonstrate the program's compliance with IRS standards.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Reimbursement Policies**: Audit current policies on home office and internet reimbursements to ensure compliance with IRS guidelines. Ensure that all reimbursed expenses are substantiated and meet the criteria for tax exclusion.

2. **Update Employee Documentation**: Implement procedures for employees to document business use of reimbursed services. This documentation is critical for substantiating the tax-exempt status of these benefits.

3. **Evaluate Wellness Programs**: Assess whether current wellness programs meet the requirements for tax-exempt status under IRC Section 132(j)(4). Make necessary adjustments to program offerings and documentation practices.

4. **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees about any changes in the handling of fringe benefits and the importance of maintaining proper documentation for reimbursed expenses.

Employers should act promptly to align their fringe benefit policies with the latest IRS guidance to avoid potential tax issues and ensure that employees receive the full benefits intended.
