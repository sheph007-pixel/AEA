## New DOL Guidance on FMLA Eligibility for Remote Workers

On April 28, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance clarifying Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) eligibility for remote employees. This guidance impacts how employers calculate the 50-employee threshold within a 75-mile radius, a key criterion for FMLA eligibility under 29 U.S.C. § 2611(2)(B)(ii).

## Remote Work and the 75-Mile Radius

The DOL's guidance addresses a growing concern as remote work becomes more prevalent. Traditionally, FMLA eligibility requires employees to work at a location where the employer has at least 50 employees within a 75-mile radius. With remote work, determining this radius has become complex. The DOL now specifies that the 75-mile radius should be measured from the employee's "worksite," defined as the office to which they report or from which their assignments are made.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must reassess their FMLA policies to ensure compliance with this new guidance. This includes identifying the designated worksite for remote employees and calculating the 75-mile radius from these locations. Failure to comply could lead to legal challenges and penalties.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Remote Employee Worksites**: Determine and document the official worksite for each remote employee. This may involve reviewing employment contracts and job descriptions.

2. **Recalculate 75-Mile Radius**: Use the designated worksites to recalculate the 75-mile radius and determine if remote employees meet the FMLA eligibility requirements.

3. **Update FMLA Policies**: Amend FMLA policies and employee handbooks to reflect the new guidance. Clearly communicate any changes to employees.

4. **Train HR Staff**: Ensure HR teams are trained on the new guidance and understand how to apply it to remote work scenarios.

## Conclusion

The DOL's clarification on FMLA eligibility for remote workers underscores the need for employers to adapt their policies to the evolving work environment. By taking proactive steps to comply with the new guidance, employers can avoid potential legal pitfalls and ensure that eligible employees can access their FMLA rights.
