## DOL Proposes New Rule on Employee Classification

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on May 15, 2026, aiming to redefine the criteria for classifying workers as independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposal seeks to address ongoing concerns regarding worker misclassification, which affects wage, benefit, and tax obligations for businesses and workers alike.

The proposed rule introduces a more stringent "economic realities" test, replacing the current guidelines established in 2021. This test evaluates several factors to determine whether a worker is economically dependent on a company or in business for themselves. Key considerations include the degree of control exercised by the employer, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, and the permanency of the relationship.

### Implications for Employers

If adopted, the rule could significantly impact businesses that rely heavily on independent contractors, such as those in the gig economy, logistics, and consulting sectors. Companies may face increased scrutiny over their classification practices, potentially leading to reclassification of workers as employees. This shift could result in higher costs due to obligations for minimum wage, overtime, and benefits under the FLSA.

Employers should conduct a thorough review of their current workforce classification practices. This includes examining contracts, job duties, and the actual working relationship between the company and its contractors. Legal counsel or HR specialists can assist in this review to ensure compliance with the proposed criteria.

### Next Steps for Employers

The DOL has opened a 60-day public comment period ending July 14, 2026. Employers are encouraged to submit feedback or concerns regarding the proposed changes. Engaging in this process can help shape the final rule and provide insights into potential compliance challenges.

In anticipation of the rule's adoption, businesses should prepare for possible reclassification scenarios. This involves assessing the financial impact of converting contractors to employees and considering adjustments to business models or workforce strategies.

### Conclusion

The DOL's proposed rule on worker classification represents a significant shift in the regulatory landscape for employers. By understanding the implications and preparing for potential changes, businesses can mitigate risks associated with misclassification and ensure compliance with federal labor standards.
