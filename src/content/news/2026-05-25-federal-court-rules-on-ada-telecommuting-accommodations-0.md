## Federal Court Ruling on ADA Telecommuting

On May 20, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit issued a significant ruling in Smith v. ABC Corp., clarifying when telecommuting is required as a reasonable accommodation under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). This decision impacts employers' obligations to consider remote work arrangements for employees with disabilities.

The court determined that employers must evaluate telecommuting requests on a case-by-case basis. Specifically, the court emphasized that the determination should focus on whether the essential functions of the job can be performed remotely without imposing undue hardship on the employer. This ruling aligns with the ADA's requirement under 42 U.S.C. § 12112(b)(5)(A) to provide reasonable accommodations unless doing so would cause significant difficulty or expense.

## Implications for Employers

Employers should note that the court's decision underscores the importance of individualized assessments of accommodation requests. The ruling suggests that blanket policies denying telecommuting without specific analysis may not comply with the ADA. Instead, employers should consider factors such as the employee's job duties, the feasibility of remote work, and any potential impact on business operations.

This decision also highlights the need for employers to maintain clear documentation when evaluating accommodation requests. Employers should ensure that they have a documented process for assessing each request, including considerations of job functions and potential accommodations.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Accommodation Policies:** Employers should review and update their ADA accommodation policies to ensure they allow for individualized assessments of telecommuting requests.

2. **Train HR and Managers:** Conduct training for HR personnel and managers on the ADA requirements for reasonable accommodations, emphasizing the need for case-by-case evaluations.

3. **Document Decisions:** Implement a robust documentation process for accommodation requests and decisions. This should include details of the job analysis, considerations of remote work feasibility, and any undue hardship evaluations.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Consider consulting with legal counsel to ensure compliance with the ADA and to address any complex accommodation requests that may arise.

The Smith v. ABC Corp. ruling serves as a reminder of the evolving legal landscape regarding disability accommodations and remote work. Employers must remain vigilant in their compliance efforts to avoid potential legal challenges related to ADA violations.
