## IRS Clarifies Taxation on ESPPs

On June 12, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new guidance regarding the taxation of Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs), which will affect both employer reporting obligations and employee tax liabilities. The guidance, codified under IRS Notice 2026-45, clarifies the application of tax rules under 26 U.S.C. § 423, particularly concerning the disposition of stock acquired through ESPPs.

## Key Changes and Employer Responsibilities

The new IRS guidance specifies that employers must report the fair market value of ESPP shares at the time of purchase on employees' W-2 forms. This requirement applies to shares purchased under a qualified ESPP and sold within one year of purchase or two years from the grant date, whichever is longer. This clarification aligns with the IRS's broader efforts to ensure compliance with federal income tax regulations.

Employers should review their payroll systems to ensure accurate reporting of ESPP transactions. The IRS has indicated that failure to comply with these reporting requirements may result in penalties under 26 U.S.C. § 6721 for incorrect information returns.

## Employee Tax Implications

For employees, the guidance confirms that income from the sale of ESPP shares will be subject to ordinary income tax if the shares are sold in a disqualifying disposition, as outlined in the guidance. The amount subject to tax is the difference between the stock's fair market value at purchase and the purchase price. This income must be reported on the employee's tax return for the year in which the sale occurs.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should take immediate steps to comply with the new reporting requirements:

1. **Audit Current ESPP Reporting Practices**: Ensure that your payroll system is capable of tracking ESPP transactions and reporting them accurately on employee W-2 forms.
   
2. **Employee Communication**: Update ESPP plan documentation and communicate changes to employees, highlighting the tax implications of disqualifying dispositions.

3. **Training for Payroll Staff**: Conduct training sessions for payroll and HR staff to familiarize them with the new reporting requirements and ensure compliance.

4. **Consult Tax Professionals**: Consider consulting with tax professionals to verify that your company's ESPP practices align with the new IRS guidance.

The IRS has set an effective date of January 1, 2027, for these changes, providing employers with a limited window to adjust their systems and processes. By taking proactive steps now, employers can mitigate the risk of non-compliance and ensure that both they and their employees understand the tax implications of ESPPs under the new guidance.
