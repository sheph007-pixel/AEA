## EEOC Revises Religious Accommodation Guidance

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) updated its guidance on religious accommodations on July 1, 2026, following the Supreme Court's decision in Groff v. DeJoy, 600 U.S. ___ (2024). This revision clarifies employers' obligations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 regarding religious accommodations.

## Key Changes in EEOC Guidance

The Supreme Court's decision in Groff v. DeJoy redefined the standard for what constitutes an "undue hardship" for employers when denying religious accommodations. Previously, employers could deny accommodations if they imposed more than a de minimis cost. The new standard requires employers to demonstrate a significant difficulty or expense relative to the size and resources of the business.

The EEOC's updated guidance reflects this shift, emphasizing that employers must engage in a meaningful dialogue with employees requesting religious accommodations. Employers are now expected to consider various factors, such as the nature of the accommodation, its impact on business operations, and possible alternatives that could mitigate any hardship.

## Employer Action Items

1. **Review Accommodation Policies**: Employers should review and update their religious accommodation policies to align with the new EEOC guidance. This includes ensuring that policies clearly outline the process for requesting accommodations and the criteria for evaluating requests.

2. **Train HR and Management**: Conduct training sessions for HR personnel and managers to ensure they understand the new standards for religious accommodations. This training should cover the need for interactive discussions with employees and the evaluation of undue hardship based on the revised criteria.

3. **Document Accommodation Requests**: Implement a system for documenting all accommodation requests and the employer's response. This documentation will be crucial if an employer's decision is challenged and can demonstrate compliance with Title VII requirements.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the complexity of the new standards, employers should consult with legal counsel to ensure their policies and practices comply with the updated guidance and the Supreme Court's ruling.

## Implications for Employers

The EEOC's revised guidance underscores the importance of a careful and individualized assessment of religious accommodation requests. Employers must balance their operational needs with the obligation to accommodate employees' religious practices unless doing so would result in significant difficulty or expense.

Failure to comply with these updated requirements could lead to legal challenges and potential liability under Title VII. By proactively updating policies and training staff, employers can better navigate the complexities of religious accommodations and reduce the risk of litigation.
