## EEOC Clarifies Religious Accommodation Obligations

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance on May 15, 2026, detailing employer obligations to accommodate religious beliefs and practices under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000e). This guidance comes after increased inquiries about balancing religious accommodations with workplace policies.

## Key Aspects of the Guidance

The guidance emphasizes that employers must provide reasonable accommodations for employees' sincerely held religious beliefs unless doing so would cause undue hardship. The EEOC reiterates that "undue hardship" is anything more than a de minimis cost, a standard reaffirmed by the Supreme Court in the 2025 case of Groff v. DeJoy.

Employers are reminded that accommodations can include flexible scheduling, voluntary shift substitutions, and exceptions to dress codes. The EEOC also clarifies that while employers can require employees to provide information about their religious beliefs, they must avoid intrusive or overly burdensome inquiries.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their current policies and practices to ensure compliance with the EEOC's guidance. Consider the following steps:

1. **Policy Review**: Examine existing policies on religious accommodations and ensure they align with the EEOC's standards.
   
2. **Training**: Conduct training sessions for HR personnel and managers to handle accommodation requests appropriately and sensitively.
   
3. **Documentation**: Implement a clear process for documenting accommodation requests and the rationale for any denials based on undue hardship.
   
4. **Dialogue with Employees**: Encourage open communication with employees regarding their accommodation needs and explore feasible solutions collaboratively.

## Potential Challenges

While the guidance provides clarity, implementing accommodations can present challenges, particularly for small businesses with limited resources. Employers must balance the need to accommodate religious practices with operational constraints. The EEOC suggests that employers engage in a cooperative dialogue with employees to find mutually acceptable solutions.

## Conclusion

The EEOC's new guidance on religious accommodations underscores the importance of respecting employees' religious beliefs while maintaining workplace efficiency. Employers should take proactive steps to ensure they meet these obligations under Title VII. Failure to comply could result in legal challenges and potential liability. By fostering an environment that respects religious diversity, employers can enhance workplace harmony and compliance.
