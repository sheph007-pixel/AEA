## DOL Clarifies Remote Work Expense Reimbursements

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on July 1, 2026, clarifying employers' obligations to reimburse remote employees for business-related expenses. This rule, effective August 1, 2026, addresses ambiguities in the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regarding the reimbursement of expenses incurred by employees working from home.

The rule, codified under 29 C.F.R. § 531.35, specifies that employers must reimburse remote employees for necessary business expenses that reduce their earnings below the federal minimum wage of $7.25 per hour. This includes costs such as internet service, office supplies, and other necessary equipment used primarily for business purposes.

## Key Requirements for Employers

Employers must ensure that all remote work expenses that are necessary for performing job duties are reimbursed if these costs would drop an employee's earnings below the minimum wage threshold. The rule mandates clear communication between employers and employees about which expenses qualify for reimbursement.

Additionally, the DOL requires employers to implement a written policy that outlines the process for submitting and approving reimbursement requests. This policy should be distributed to all employees and updated regularly to reflect any changes in remote work arrangements or reimbursement procedures.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Expense Policies**: Employers should review their current expense reimbursement policies to ensure compliance with the new rule. If necessary, update these policies to include specific guidance on remote work expenses.

2. **Communicate with Employees**: Clearly communicate the updated reimbursement policy to all employees, especially those working remotely. Ensure that employees understand which expenses are eligible for reimbursement and the process for submitting claims.

3. **Audit Reimbursement Practices**: Conduct an audit of past reimbursement practices to identify any discrepancies or areas for improvement. This will help ensure compliance and avoid potential legal issues.

4. **Train HR and Payroll Staff**: Provide training to HR and payroll staff on the new rule and updated reimbursement procedures. This will help facilitate smooth processing of reimbursement requests and ensure consistent application of the policy.

## Implications for Employers

Non-compliance with the new rule could result in penalties and legal challenges. Employers should act promptly to align their policies and practices with the DOL's requirements. By doing so, they can avoid potential disputes and foster a supportive environment for remote employees.

This rule underscores the importance of fair compensation practices in a remote work setting. As remote work becomes more prevalent, clear guidelines on expense reimbursements will be crucial for maintaining compliance and employee satisfaction.
