## The Regulatory Landscape

The rapid adoption of AI tools in the workplace is attracting increased scrutiny from federal and state regulators. Agencies such as the EEOC and DOL, along with multiple state legislatures, have signaled interest in addressing potential discrimination and privacy concerns associated with AI-driven employment decisions. Employers should monitor regulatory developments in this area closely.

## Where AI May Create Risk

Employers using AI tools should be aware of potential compliance issues in several areas:

- **Hiring and screening**: AI tools that screen resumes or conduct initial assessments may inadvertently produce outcomes that disproportionately affect protected classes
- **Performance monitoring**: Employee surveillance and productivity tracking tools may raise privacy and labor law questions
- **Accommodation decisions**: AI systems may not properly account for disability accommodations or other protected situations
- **Pay decisions**: Algorithmic pay-setting tools could potentially perpetuate or create pay disparities

## What Employers Should Do

For businesses using or considering AI workplace tools:

- **Conduct a bias audit**: Review AI tools for potential disparate impact on protected groups before deployment
- **Maintain human oversight**: Keep humans involved in significant employment decisions rather than delegating entirely to AI
- **Document your process**: Record your evaluation of AI tools, including what steps you took to assess fairness and accuracy
- **Review vendor claims carefully**: Not all AI vendors have adequately tested their tools for bias
- **Stay current on regulations**: Multiple jurisdictions have enacted or proposed AI-specific employment laws, and employers should verify which requirements apply to them

## The Bottom Line

AI tools can improve efficiency, but they generally do not eliminate employer liability. When an AI tool produces a discriminatory outcome, the employer -- not the vendor -- is typically held responsible. Employers should consider treating AI as an assistant rather than a decision-maker and building compliance checks into their adoption process.
