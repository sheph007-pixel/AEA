## Proposed DOL Rule on Employee Scheduling

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on May 1, 2026, aimed at regulating employee scheduling practices across various industries. This rule, if enacted, will require employers to provide schedules at least 14 days in advance and compensate workers for last-minute changes. The proposal is currently open for public comment until July 1, 2026.

## Who Is Affected?

The proposed rule targets employers in retail, food service, and hospitality sectors, which frequently utilize variable scheduling. However, it may extend to other industries relying on just-in-time scheduling models. The DOL cites the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) as the foundation for this regulation, aiming to ensure fair labor practices and reduce unpredictable work hours for employees.

## Key Provisions

The rule mandates that affected employers provide work schedules at least two weeks in advance. If changes occur within this period, employers must pay a "predictability pay" equivalent to one hour's wages for each altered shift. Additionally, if an employee is sent home before completing half of their scheduled shift, the employer must compensate them for half of the scheduled hours.

## Implications for Employers

Businesses may face increased administrative burdens and labor costs due to the predictability pay requirements. The rule could necessitate adjustments in workforce management systems and processes to comply with the advance notice requirements.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Current Scheduling Practices:** Assess your current scheduling systems to determine how they align with the proposed requirements.
   
2. **Evaluate Workforce Management Software:** Consider upgrading or implementing software solutions that facilitate advanced scheduling and track compliance with the proposed rule.
   
3. **Budget for Potential Costs:** Prepare for potential increases in labor costs associated with predictability pay and administrative adjustments.

4. **Submit Comments:** Engage in the public comment process by submitting feedback to the DOL before the July 1 deadline. This is an opportunity to voice concerns or suggest modifications to the proposed rule.

## Conclusion

The DOL's proposed scheduling rule represents a significant shift in how employers must plan and communicate work hours. By taking proactive steps now, businesses can better prepare for potential changes and minimize disruptions to their operations. As the proposal progresses, staying informed and involved in the regulatory process will be crucial for employers navigating these potential new requirements.
