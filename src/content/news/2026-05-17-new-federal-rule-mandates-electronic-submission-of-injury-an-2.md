## OSHA's Electronic Submission Requirement

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has mandated that certain employers must electronically submit their Form 300A data by March 2, 2026. This rule, published on April 15, 2026, requires establishments with 250 or more employees, and those with 20 to 249 employees in specific high-risk industries, to comply with the electronic submission requirement. The regulation aims to improve workplace safety by enhancing data transparency and accessibility.

## Who Is Affected?

The rule impacts two main groups of employers:

1. **Large Establishments**: Employers with 250 or more employees, regardless of industry, must submit their Form 300A data electronically.
   
2. **High-Risk Industries**: Employers with 20 to 249 employees in designated high-risk industries, such as manufacturing, construction, and healthcare, are also required to comply.

These requirements are codified under 29 CFR § 1904.41, which outlines the specifics of the electronic submission process and the industries classified as high-risk.

## What Employers Need to Do

Employers should prepare to meet the March 2, 2026, deadline by taking the following actions:

- **Review Form 300A Data**: Ensure that all injury and illness records for the previous calendar year are accurate and complete.
  
- **Register with the ITA**: Employers must register with OSHA’s Injury Tracking Application (ITA) to submit their data electronically. This process involves creating a login, verifying establishment information, and uploading the necessary data files.

- **Audit Records**: Conduct an internal audit of Form 300A records to verify accuracy and compliance with OSHA recordkeeping standards.

- **Train HR and Safety Personnel**: Ensure that relevant staff are trained on the electronic submission process and understand the importance of accurate recordkeeping.

## Potential Penalties

Failure to comply with the electronic submission requirement could result in penalties under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. OSHA has emphasized that timely and accurate data submission is crucial for effective workplace safety monitoring and enforcement.

## Conclusion

Employers should act promptly to ensure compliance with OSHA's new electronic submission rule. By preparing their Form 300A data for electronic submission and training relevant personnel, employers can avoid penalties and contribute to a safer work environment. For detailed guidance, employers can refer to OSHA's official website or consult with legal counsel specializing in workplace safety regulations.
