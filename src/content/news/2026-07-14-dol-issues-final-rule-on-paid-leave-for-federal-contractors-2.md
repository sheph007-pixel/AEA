## New Paid Leave Mandate for Federal Contractors

The Department of Labor (DOL) has issued a final rule requiring federal contractors to provide paid leave to employees, effective January 1, 2026. This rule, published under the authority of Executive Order 13706, mandates that federal contractors offer up to seven days of paid sick leave annually. The regulation applies to all federal contracts entered into or renewed after the effective date.

## Key Provisions

The rule specifies that employees accrue one hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked, up to a maximum of 56 hours per year. Unused leave must carry over to the following year, although employers are not required to allow employees to accrue more than 56 hours at any given time. The rule applies to all employees working on or in connection with a covered federal contract, including part-time and temporary workers.

## Compliance Requirements

Federal contractors must update their leave policies to comply with the new requirements. This includes amending employee handbooks and informing employees of their rights under the new rule. Contractors must also keep accurate records of hours worked and leave taken for a period of three years.

## Enforcement and Penalties

The DOL's Wage and Hour Division will enforce the rule. Non-compliance could result in contract termination, suspension of payments, and disbarment from future federal contracts. Employers should ensure that their payroll systems can track leave accrual and usage to avoid penalties.

## Action Items for Employers

- **Review and Update Policies**: Amend leave policies to incorporate the new accrual and carryover provisions.
- **Train HR Staff**: Ensure HR personnel understand the new requirements and can communicate them effectively to employees.
- **Audit Payroll Systems**: Verify that payroll systems can accurately track and report leave accrual and usage.
- **Inform Employees**: Clearly communicate the new leave entitlements to all affected employees.

Federal contractors should act promptly to align their policies with the new DOL rule to avoid disruptions and potential penalties.
