## DOL Proposes New Joint Employer Standard

On July 7, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule to revise the joint employer standard under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposed rule aims to clarify when multiple businesses share liability for wage and hour violations. The revision could significantly impact businesses that use staffing agencies, subcontractors, or franchise models.

## What the Proposed Rule Entails

The proposed rule seeks to simplify the determination of joint employer status by focusing on the degree of control a business exerts over workers. Specifically, it examines factors such as hiring and firing authority, supervision and control over work schedules, determining pay rates, and maintaining employment records. This marks a shift from the previous standard that considered a broader range of factors, potentially reducing the number of businesses classified as joint employers.

## Implications for Employers

If enacted, the rule could decrease the liability risk for businesses that do not directly control the day-to-day activities of workers provided by another entity. This change is particularly relevant for franchises and companies utilizing temporary staffing solutions. However, businesses that exert significant control over these workers may still be deemed joint employers and thus liable for compliance with wage and hour laws.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their current contracts and operational practices with third-party labor providers to assess potential joint employer status. Key actions include:

1. **Evaluate Control**: Assess the level of control your business has over the workers' employment conditions.
2. **Contract Revisions**: Consider amending contracts to clarify the division of responsibilities and control between your business and third-party labor providers.
3. **Compliance Audit**: Conduct an internal audit to ensure compliance with FLSA requirements, particularly if your business might qualify as a joint employer under the new rule.

## Next Steps and Timeline

The DOL will accept public comments on the proposed rule until September 5, 2026. After the comment period, the DOL will review feedback and potentially revise the proposal before issuing a final rule. Employers should stay informed about developments and be prepared to adjust practices accordingly.

This proposed rule represents a significant shift in joint employer liability, offering potential relief for businesses with limited control over workers supplied by other entities. However, it also underscores the need for careful evaluation of business relationships to ensure compliance with evolving labor standards.
