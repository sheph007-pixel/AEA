## IRS Updates ESOP Valuation Guidance

On May 15, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidance on the valuation of Employee Stock Ownership Plans (ESOPs), a move that affects businesses sponsoring these plans. The updated guidelines, detailed in IRS Notice 2026-38, refine the methodologies for determining the fair market value of employer securities in ESOPs, which are crucial for compliance with Internal Revenue Code Section 401(a).

The revisions address the increased scrutiny from the IRS on valuation practices, which have been a point of contention in recent audits. The guidance emphasizes the importance of using qualified independent appraisers to assess the value of company stock in ESOPs, aligning with best practices to ensure accuracy and fairness in valuations.

## Key Changes and Implications

The new guidance specifies that ESOP valuations must consider both market and income approaches, with a particular focus on the risk associated with the business's operational environment. Additionally, the IRS underscores the necessity for appraisers to document their assumptions and methodologies comprehensively, providing a transparent trail for audits.

This update comes in response to concerns about overvaluation or undervaluation of company stock, which can lead to significant tax liabilities or penalties under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). The IRS's focus on rigorous valuation practices aims to protect both employees and plan sponsors from potential financial discrepancies.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers sponsoring ESOPs should immediately review their current valuation processes to ensure they align with the new IRS guidelines. This includes:

1. Engaging qualified independent appraisers who are familiar with the latest IRS requirements.
2. Documenting valuation methodologies thoroughly to facilitate transparency and compliance during IRS audits.
3. Considering a review of past valuations to identify and rectify any discrepancies that could lead to compliance issues.

Employers should also consult with legal and financial advisors to update their ESOP policies and ensure that all aspects of their plan administration meet the new standards. Failure to comply with the updated guidance could result in penalties and increased scrutiny from the IRS.

## Conclusion

This IRS update on ESOP valuations underscores the agency's commitment to ensuring fair and accurate stock valuations within employee ownership plans. By adhering to the new guidelines, employers can safeguard their ESOPs against potential legal and financial pitfalls, maintaining compliance and trust with their employees.
