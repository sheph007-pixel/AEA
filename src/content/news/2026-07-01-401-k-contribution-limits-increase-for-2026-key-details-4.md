## IRS Raises 401(k) Contribution Limits for 2026

The IRS announced an increase in the 401(k) contribution limits for 2026, effective January 1, 2026. The new limit allows employees to contribute up to $23,000 annually, up from the previous limit of $22,500. The catch-up contribution limit for employees aged 50 and over will also rise to $7,500, up from $7,000. These changes are detailed in IRS Notice 2026-55 and are part of the annual adjustment for cost-of-living increases.

## Impact on Employers and Action Items

Employers sponsoring 401(k) plans must update their plan documents and payroll systems to accommodate the new limits. It's crucial to communicate these changes to employees, ensuring they are aware of the increased savings opportunities. HR departments should revise any educational materials related to retirement planning to reflect the updated contribution limits.

Additionally, employers should review their plan's nondiscrimination testing to ensure compliance with the new limits. This testing ensures that benefits do not disproportionately favor highly compensated employees, as required under Internal Revenue Code § 401(a)(4).

## Strategic Considerations

With the increased limits, employers might consider enhancing their 401(k) matching contributions to remain competitive in attracting and retaining talent. As retirement benefits play a significant role in employee satisfaction, offering a robust 401(k) match can be a compelling component of a total compensation package.

Employers should also evaluate their automatic enrollment and escalation features. These features can help employees maximize their contributions, taking full advantage of the new limits. According to research, automatic enrollment significantly boosts participation rates, and automatic escalation can help employees gradually increase their savings rates over time.

## Conclusion

The increase in 401(k) contribution limits for 2026 presents an opportunity for employers to enhance their retirement benefits offerings. By updating plan documents, communicating changes to employees, and considering strategic enhancements to matching contributions, employers can ensure compliance and improve employee satisfaction.
