## Federal Court Decision on Arbitration

On May 28, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York upheld the Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021, confirming that employers cannot enforce mandatory arbitration for sexual harassment claims. This decision affects all employers who use arbitration agreements as part of their employment contracts.

The ruling stems from a challenge by a group of employers who argued that the Act violated the Federal Arbitration Act (FAA). However, the court found that the Act is a valid exercise of Congress's power to regulate arbitration agreements in cases involving sexual harassment and assault.

## Implications for Employers

Employers with arbitration agreements in place must review and revise these documents to ensure compliance with the Act. Any clause mandating arbitration for sexual harassment claims is now unenforceable. This ruling emphasizes the need for employers to provide clear pathways for employees to bring such claims to court if they choose.

### Action Items

1. **Review Arbitration Agreements:** Employers should conduct an audit of current arbitration agreements to identify and amend any clauses that mandate arbitration for sexual harassment claims.

2. **Update Employee Handbooks:** Ensure that employee handbooks reflect changes to the arbitration policy, clearly stating that sexual harassment claims can be pursued in court.

3. **Communicate Changes:** Inform employees of their rights under the new legal framework. This can be done through meetings, emails, or updated policy documents.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Engage with legal experts to navigate the implications of this ruling on your specific arbitration policies and overall dispute resolution strategies.

## Broader Impact on Arbitration Practices

This decision may signal a shift in how arbitration is used in employment contracts, particularly concerning sensitive issues like harassment and discrimination. Employers must stay informed about potential legislative changes and court decisions that could further affect arbitration practices.

By ensuring compliance with this ruling, employers not only adhere to legal standards but also demonstrate a commitment to addressing sexual harassment claims transparently and fairly.
