## New Requirements for Retirement Plans Under SECURE Act 2.0

The SECURE Act 2.0, signed into law on December 29, 2022, introduces significant changes to retirement plan administration, with many provisions taking effect on January 1, 2026. Employers must prepare to comply with these new requirements to avoid penalties and ensure smooth plan operations.

### Key Provisions Affecting Employers

One of the most impactful changes is the mandatory automatic enrollment for new 401(k) and 403(b) plans. Under Section 101 of the Act, employers must automatically enroll eligible employees at a minimum contribution rate of 3%, escalating annually by 1% until reaching at least 10%, but not more than 15%. This requirement applies to plans established after December 29, 2022, with exceptions for small businesses with ten or fewer employees, new businesses less than three years old, and church and government plans.

Additionally, Section 107 introduces a new "Saver's Match" replacing the existing nonrefundable tax credit for low- and moderate-income workers. Starting in 2026, the federal government will match 50% of contributions up to $2,000 per individual, deposited directly into the retirement account. Employers should update their plan documents and communicate these changes to employees to maximize participation.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers must review and amend their plan documents to incorporate these changes. Specifically, they should:

- Implement systems to facilitate automatic enrollment and escalation for eligible employees.
- Update payroll systems to handle the new Saver's Match and ensure accurate reporting.
- Communicate changes to employees, highlighting the benefits of increased participation and the new government match.

Employers should also consider consulting with plan administrators or legal advisors to ensure compliance with the SECURE Act 2.0 provisions and to address any specific concerns related to their workforce.

### Additional Considerations

The SECURE Act 2.0 also includes provisions aimed at increasing retirement savings and improving plan access. For instance, Section 113 allows part-time employees who work at least 500 hours per year for two consecutive years to participate in employer-sponsored retirement plans. Employers must track hours accurately to comply with this requirement.

Furthermore, the Act enhances the catch-up contribution limits for employees aged 60 to 63, allowing them to contribute an additional $10,000 annually, indexed for inflation. Employers should update their plan limits and ensure employees are aware of these opportunities to boost their retirement savings.

### Conclusion

The SECURE Act 2.0 represents a significant shift in retirement plan administration, with several provisions designed to increase participation and savings. Employers must take proactive steps to integrate these changes into their plans and systems by January 1, 2026. By doing so, they can enhance their benefits offerings and support their employees' financial well-being.
