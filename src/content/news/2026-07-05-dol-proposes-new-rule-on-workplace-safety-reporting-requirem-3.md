## New DOL Proposal on Safety Reporting

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 28, 2026, that aims to enhance workplace safety reporting requirements. The proposed changes would affect businesses with 10 or more employees, requiring them to submit detailed reports on workplace injuries and illnesses electronically. This proposal seeks to streamline data collection and improve transparency in workplace safety practices.

The proposed rule would amend the Occupational Safety and Health Administration's (OSHA) current reporting requirements under 29 CFR § 1904.41. Currently, only establishments with 250 or more employees are required to submit detailed reports electronically. The new rule would lower this threshold to include businesses with 10 or more employees, significantly expanding the scope of reporting.

## Key Changes and Requirements

Under the proposed rule, affected employers must submit detailed information on workplace injuries and illnesses annually. The data collected would include the number of incidents, the nature of the injuries, and any corrective actions taken. The DOL aims to use this data to identify industry trends and develop targeted safety initiatives.

The proposal also introduces a requirement for employers to submit reports within seven days of an incident. This is a shift from the current 30-day reporting period, intended to provide more timely data for analysis and intervention.

## Implications for Employers

If implemented, the new rule would require substantial changes in how businesses track and report safety incidents. Employers should prepare by reviewing their current reporting processes and ensuring they can meet the proposed seven-day reporting deadline. This might involve investing in new software or training staff to handle increased reporting demands.

Additionally, the expanded reporting requirements could lead to increased scrutiny from OSHA. Businesses may face more frequent inspections and audits based on the data submitted. Therefore, maintaining accurate records and implementing effective safety measures will be crucial to avoid potential penalties.

## Next Steps

The DOL has opened a 60-day public comment period ending August 27, 2026, during which stakeholders can submit feedback on the proposed rule. Employers are encouraged to review the proposal and provide input, especially regarding the feasibility of the new reporting requirements.

To prepare for potential changes, businesses should consider conducting an internal audit of their current safety reporting practices. This audit can help identify any gaps or areas for improvement to ensure compliance with the new requirements if they are enacted.

In summary, the DOL's proposed rule represents a significant shift in workplace safety reporting, with the potential to impact thousands of businesses. Employers should stay informed about developments and actively participate in the rule-making process to ensure their concerns are addressed.
