## New DOL Rule on Overtime Pay for Part-Time Workers

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a new rule on June 30, 2026, clarifying overtime pay eligibility for part-time workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule aims to address ambiguities surrounding overtime calculations for employees working less than 40 hours per week.

### Key Provisions

The new rule specifies that part-time employees are eligible for overtime pay if they work more than 40 hours in a single week, aligning with the FLSA's standard overtime requirement (29 U.S.C. § 207(a)(1)). Importantly, the rule clarifies that employers cannot average hours over multiple weeks to avoid paying overtime. For example, if a part-time employee works 30 hours one week and 50 hours the next, the employee is entitled to 10 hours of overtime for the second week.

Additionally, the rule defines "workweek" as a fixed and regularly recurring period of 168 hours, or seven consecutive 24-hour periods. Employers must establish a consistent workweek for each employee to accurately calculate overtime.

### Impact on Employers

This clarification impacts employers who rely on part-time labor, particularly in industries with fluctuating schedules such as retail and hospitality. Employers must ensure accurate tracking of hours worked each week to comply with the new rule. Failure to do so could result in penalties and back pay for unpaid overtime.

Employers should review their timekeeping systems and payroll processes to accommodate this change. It is advisable to audit current employee classifications and workweek definitions to ensure compliance. Employers may also need to update employee handbooks and training materials to reflect these changes.

### Action Items

1. **Audit Timekeeping Systems**: Ensure your systems accurately track weekly hours worked for all employees, including part-time staff.
   
2. **Review Workweek Definitions**: Confirm that each employee's workweek is clearly defined and consistently applied.

3. **Update Payroll Processes**: Adjust payroll systems to automatically calculate overtime for any hours worked over 40 in a single workweek.

4. **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees about the new rule and how it affects their pay. Update training materials and employee handbooks as necessary.

5. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Consider consulting with legal counsel to ensure your policies comply with the new DOL rule and to mitigate any potential legal risks.

By taking these steps, employers can ensure compliance with the new DOL rule and avoid potential liabilities. The rule underscores the importance of precise record-keeping and consistent application of overtime policies for all employees, regardless of their part-time status.
