## DOL Strengthens Child Labor Protections

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule enhancing child labor protections under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), effective August 1, 2026. This regulatory change aims to address increasing concerns about the safety and welfare of minors in the workplace. The rule modifies existing provisions, particularly focusing on hazardous occupations and permissible work hours for minors under 18.

## Key Changes to the FLSA

The updated rule introduces stricter guidelines on hazardous occupations prohibited for minors. It expands the list of banned activities for those aged 16 and 17, including certain tasks in construction and manufacturing deemed unsafe. The rule also tightens restrictions on the number of hours minors can work during school days and extends protections to cover new technological work environments, such as those involving complex machinery and digital platforms.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must review and adjust their current practices to comply with the new requirements. This includes conducting a thorough audit of positions currently held by minors and ensuring that none involve newly classified hazardous duties. Additionally, employers should update work schedules to align with the new hour restrictions, particularly for school days, to avoid potential violations.

## Compliance and Penalties

Non-compliance with the updated child labor provisions can result in significant penalties. The DOL has emphasized its commitment to enforcing these rules through increased inspections and penalties for violations. Employers found in breach may face fines up to $11,000 per violation, as per 29 U.S.C. § 216(e).

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Job Roles**: Review all job descriptions and duties assigned to minors to ensure compliance with the updated list of hazardous occupations.
2. **Update Work Schedules**: Adjust minors' work hours to comply with new restrictions on school days.
3. **Training and Communication**: Conduct training sessions for managers and HR personnel to understand the new requirements and ensure proper implementation.
4. **Policy Revisions**: Amend company policies and employee handbooks to reflect the new child labor regulations.

By taking these proactive steps, employers can ensure they remain compliant with the updated FLSA provisions and protect the welfare of minor employees.
