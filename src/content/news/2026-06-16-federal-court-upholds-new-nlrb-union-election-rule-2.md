## Federal Court Upholds New NLRB Union Election Rule

On June 10, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia upheld the National Labor Relations Board's (NLRB) revised rule on union election procedures, which had been challenged by several business groups. The rule, which took effect on April 14, 2026, modifies the timeline and procedures for union representation elections, significantly impacting how employers must respond to unionization efforts.

The new rule reinstates several provisions from the Obama-era "quickie election" rule that had been rolled back during the Trump administration. Key changes include shortening the time between the filing of a petition and the election date, limiting pre-election hearings, and requiring employers to provide more comprehensive employee contact information to unions.

### Key Provisions and Implications

Under the revised rule, the period between the filing of a petition and the election is reduced to as few as 10 days, compared to the previous average of 23 days. This compressed timeline means that employers have less time to communicate with employees about unionization and to prepare for the election.

Additionally, the rule limits the scope of pre-election hearings, which are now focused solely on determining whether a question concerning representation exists. This change reduces the ability of employers to litigate issues related to voter eligibility and the appropriate bargaining unit prior to the election.

Employers are also required to provide unions with a list of eligible voters within two business days after the direction of the election. This list must include not only names and home addresses but also personal email addresses and phone numbers if available, expanding the unions' ability to reach employees.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers must adapt quickly to these changes to effectively manage unionization efforts. Here are critical steps to take:

1. **Review and Update Policies:** Ensure that your communication policies and strategies are compliant with the new rule. Consider training management on effective, lawful communication regarding unionization.

2. **Prepare for Shorter Timelines:** Develop rapid response plans for handling union election petitions. This includes identifying key personnel who will manage the process and ensuring they are well-versed in the new requirements.

3. **Audit Employee Information:** Verify that employee contact information is accurate and complete, as it must be provided to unions promptly upon the filing of an election petition.

4. **Engage Legal Counsel:** Consult with labor attorneys to navigate the complexities of the new rule and to ensure compliance with NLRB regulations.

The court's decision to uphold the NLRB's rule underscores the shifting regulatory landscape surrounding labor relations. Employers must remain vigilant and proactive in adapting to these changes to mitigate potential risks associated with unionization efforts.
