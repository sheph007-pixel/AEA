## California Privacy Rights Act Enforcement Begins July 1, 2026

The California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) becomes enforceable on July 1, 2026, requiring employers to reassess their data privacy practices. The CPRA, which amends the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), introduces stricter regulations on how businesses handle personal information, including employee data.

## Key Requirements for Employers

Under the CPRA, employers must ensure compliance with several new obligations. The act mandates transparency in data collection, requiring businesses to inform employees about the categories of personal information collected and the purposes for which it is used. Employers must provide a detailed privacy policy and ensure it is easily accessible to employees.

The CPRA also introduces the concept of data minimization, limiting the collection of personal information to what is necessary for the disclosed purposes. Employers must review their data collection practices to ensure they do not exceed this requirement.

## Employee Rights and Employer Obligations

Employees gain expanded rights under the CPRA, including the right to access their personal information, request corrections, and opt-out of the sale of their data. Employers must establish processes to handle these requests efficiently and within specified timeframes.

The CPRA also imposes stricter requirements for data security. Employers are obligated to implement reasonable security measures to protect personal information from unauthorized access or disclosure. Failure to comply can result in significant penalties, including fines of up to $7,500 per intentional violation.

## Action Items for Employers

To comply with the CPRA, employers should:

1. **Update Privacy Policies**: Ensure that privacy policies reflect CPRA requirements and are accessible to employees.
   
2. **Review Data Practices**: Conduct an audit of data collection and processing activities to ensure compliance with data minimization principles.
   
3. **Enhance Security Measures**: Implement robust security protocols to protect employee data, reducing the risk of breaches.
   
4. **Establish Request Procedures**: Develop clear procedures for handling employee requests related to their personal information.

5. **Train HR and IT Staff**: Educate relevant staff on CPRA requirements and the importance of maintaining compliance.

## Conclusion

As the CPRA enforcement date approaches, employers should take immediate steps to align their data practices with the new requirements. By doing so, they can mitigate legal risks and demonstrate a commitment to protecting employee privacy.
