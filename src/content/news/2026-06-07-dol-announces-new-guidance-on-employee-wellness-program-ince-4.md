## DOL Guidance on Wellness Program Incentives

The Department of Labor (DOL) has issued new guidance regarding employee wellness program incentives, effective July 1, 2026. This guidance clarifies how employers can structure incentives without violating the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA).

The DOL's announcement aims to address confusion surrounding permissible incentives in wellness programs. The guidance specifies that incentives must not be so substantial as to be coercive, aligning with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission's (EEOC) prior interpretations. Under the ADA, employers must ensure that participation in wellness programs is voluntary, meaning that incentives should not pressure employees into disclosing protected health information.

## Key Provisions

The guidance outlines that incentives should not exceed 30% of the total cost of self-only coverage under the employer's group health plan. This limit is designed to prevent undue pressure on employees to participate. Furthermore, the guidance reaffirms that any health information collected must be kept confidential and separate from personnel files, as required under both the ADA and GINA.

Employers offering wellness programs must also provide reasonable accommodations to employees with disabilities who wish to participate but require modifications to do so. This includes making alternative standards available to those unable to meet standard health outcomes due to a medical condition.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Wellness Programs**: Employers should audit their current wellness programs to ensure compliance with the new incentive limits. This includes recalculating incentive values to align with the 30% cap of self-only coverage costs.

2. **Update Employee Communications**: Clearly communicate any changes in program structure or incentives to employees. Ensure that all program materials highlight the voluntary nature of participation and the confidentiality of health information.

3. **Ensure Accommodations**: Develop and document procedures for providing reasonable accommodations to employees with disabilities who wish to participate in wellness programs.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the complexities surrounding ADA and GINA compliance, employers may benefit from consulting with legal counsel to ensure their wellness programs meet all regulatory requirements.

By taking these steps, employers can continue to offer valuable wellness programs while minimizing legal risks. The DOL's new guidance provides a clearer framework for structuring incentives, ultimately benefiting both employers and employees by promoting health and wellness in a compliant manner.
