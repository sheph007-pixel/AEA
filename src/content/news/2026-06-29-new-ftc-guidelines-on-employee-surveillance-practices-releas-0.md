## FTC Issues New Guidelines on Employee Surveillance

On June 28, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) released new guidelines on employee surveillance practices, directly affecting how companies monitor their workforce. These guidelines are designed to ensure that employee monitoring is conducted in a manner that respects privacy rights while maintaining compliance with federal regulations.

The FTC's guidelines emphasize transparency and fairness in surveillance practices. Employers must now clearly inform employees about the types of monitoring being conducted, the data being collected, and the purposes for such collection. This move aligns with the FTC's broader efforts to protect consumer and employee privacy in an increasingly digital workplace.

## Key Aspects of the Guidelines

The guidelines require employers to conduct a privacy impact assessment before implementing new surveillance technologies. This assessment should evaluate the necessity and proportionality of the monitoring measures, considering less intrusive alternatives where possible.

Additionally, the guidelines stipulate that monitoring practices should be limited to work-related activities unless explicit consent from employees is obtained. Employers are encouraged to anonymize data wherever feasible to minimize privacy risks.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers should review their current surveillance policies to ensure they comply with the new guidelines. Consider updating employee handbooks and privacy notices to reflect these changes.

2. **Conduct Privacy Impact Assessments**: Before deploying new surveillance technologies, conduct thorough assessments to evaluate their impact on employee privacy. Document findings and any measures taken to mitigate privacy risks.

3. **Train HR and IT Staff**: Provide training for HR and IT personnel on the new guidelines to ensure they understand the requirements and can implement them effectively.

4. **Communicate with Employees**: Clearly communicate any changes in surveillance practices to employees. This includes the types of data collected, the purposes of collection, and how the data will be used.

## Legal Implications

Failure to comply with these guidelines could result in enforcement actions by the FTC, including fines and mandatory corrective measures. Employers should be proactive in aligning their practices with the guidelines to avoid potential legal challenges.

By implementing these guidelines, the FTC aims to balance the need for workplace monitoring with the protection of employee privacy rights. Employers are encouraged to act promptly to ensure compliance and maintain trust within their workforce.
