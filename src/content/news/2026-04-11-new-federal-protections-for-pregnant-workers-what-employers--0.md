## New Protections Under the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act

Effective June 27, 2023, the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) has introduced significant new protections for employees affected by pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions. This law mandates that employers with 15 or more employees provide reasonable accommodations to workers who require them due to pregnancy-related conditions. As a result, employers must review and possibly revise their workplace policies to ensure compliance with this federal legislation.

### Key Provisions of the PWFA

The PWFA requires employers to engage in an interactive process with employees who request accommodations due to pregnancy-related conditions. Accommodations can include but are not limited to:

- More frequent breaks
- Modified work schedules
- Temporary reassignment to a less strenuous position
- Access to certain equipment or facilities, such as ergonomic chairs

Employers are required to provide these accommodations unless doing so would cause undue hardship. This term, "undue hardship," is not explicitly defined in the law, meaning employers should consider the size of their business and the nature of the accommodations when assessing whether an undue hardship exists.

### Implications for Employers

1. **Policy Review and Update**: Employers need to review their current policies and procedures regarding pregnancy-related accommodations. This is crucial not only for compliance but also for fostering an inclusive workplace culture. 

2. **Training for Managers and HR**: It is essential to train supervisors and HR personnel on the requirements of the PWFA and the importance of facilitating accommodations. This includes understanding the interactive process and recognizing the signs of pregnancy-related conditions.

3. **Documentation**: Employers should maintain documentation of accommodation requests and the steps taken to address them. This documentation can be critical if disputes arise regarding compliance or perceived discrimination.

4. **Fostering Open Communication**: Creating an environment where employees feel comfortable discussing their needs is important. Employers should encourage open communication regarding accommodations and support.

5. **Monitor for Compliance**: Employers must stay informed about enforcement actions and guidance from agencies such as the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC), which is responsible for enforcing the PWFA. Regular audits of accommodation practices can help ensure compliance.

### Conclusion

The implementation of the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act represents a pivotal shift in how employers must approach pregnancy-related accommodations. With the law taking effect, it is crucial for employers to act swiftly to align their practices with these new requirements. By fostering an inclusive workplace and ensuring that all employees are supported, businesses can not only comply with the law but also enhance employee morale and retention.
