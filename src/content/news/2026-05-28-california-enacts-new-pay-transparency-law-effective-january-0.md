## California's New Pay Transparency Law

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed into law a new pay transparency requirement on May 15, 2026. This law mandates employers in California to include pay ranges in job postings starting January 1, 2027. The law applies to all employers with 15 or more employees, aligning with similar legislation in states like New York and Colorado.

## Key Requirements

Under the new law, codified as California Labor Code Section 432.3, employers must disclose the salary or hourly wage range for any job, promotion, or transfer opportunity advertised. The law aims to promote pay equity and reduce wage disparities based on gender and race.

Employers must also provide current employees with the pay scale for their position upon request. This requirement is intended to foster transparency and allow employees to make informed decisions about their career paths within the company.

## Compliance and Penalties

The California Labor Commissioner will enforce the law. Non-compliance may result in penalties ranging from $100 to $10,000 per violation, with higher penalties for repeated offenses. Employers should take immediate steps to ensure compliance by the effective date.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Job Postings**: Review and update all job postings to include salary ranges before January 1, 2027. Ensure that internal systems and processes can support this requirement for future postings.

2. **Update Employee Handbooks**: Include information about employees' rights to request pay scales and the company's compliance commitments.

3. **Train HR and Recruiting Teams**: Conduct training sessions to ensure that HR and recruiting teams understand the new requirements and can accurately communicate pay scales.

4. **Review Compensation Structures**: Analyze current compensation structures to ensure they align with market rates and internal equity. Adjustments may be necessary to comply with the new transparency requirements.

5. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to navigate any complexities and ensure full compliance with the new law.

## Broader Implications

California's move reflects a growing trend towards pay transparency in the United States. Employers operating in multiple states should monitor similar legislative developments and consider adopting a consistent approach to pay transparency across all locations. This proactive strategy can help mitigate legal risks and enhance employer brand reputation in a competitive labor market.
