## New Federal Rule on Remote Work Expense Reimbursements

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on July 15, 2026, mandating that employers reimburse certain expenses incurred by employees working remotely. Effective January 1, 2025, this rule requires employers to cover necessary and reasonable expenses directly related to remote work, aligning with the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) provisions.

### Key Provisions

The rule stipulates that employers must reimburse costs that are essential for employees to perform their duties from home. This includes internet service, office supplies, and equipment such as computers and printers. The rule does not cover discretionary expenses or those unrelated to job performance. The DOL clarifies that reimbursement must ensure employees' net earnings do not fall below the federal minimum wage.

### Compliance Requirements

Employers must establish and communicate a clear policy outlining which expenses qualify for reimbursement. The policy should detail the procedure for submitting expense claims and the documentation required. Employers should audit existing remote work policies to align with the new rule.

### Impact on Employers

This rule affects all employers with remote workers, regardless of company size. Non-compliance could lead to penalties under the FLSA. Employers should review their financial planning to accommodate these additional costs and consider consulting with legal counsel to ensure compliance.

### Action Items

1. **Audit Current Policies**: Review and update remote work policies to include the new reimbursement requirements.
   
2. **Develop Reimbursement Procedures**: Create a system for employees to submit expense claims, including necessary documentation.

3. **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees about the new reimbursement policy and procedures before the January 1, 2025, effective date.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Ensure all policy changes comply with the FLSA and other relevant laws.

Employers should act promptly to integrate these changes into their business operations to avoid potential legal issues.
