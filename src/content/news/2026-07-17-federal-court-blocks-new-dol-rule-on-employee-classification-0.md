## Federal Court Blocks New DOL Rule on Employee Classification

A federal district court in Texas issued an injunction on July 15, 2026, halting the Department of Labor's (DOL) new rule that sought to redefine the criteria for classifying workers as employees or independent contractors. The rule, which was set to take effect on August 1, 2026, aimed to broaden the definition of "employee" under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), potentially affecting millions of workers across various industries.

### Background

The DOL's rule intended to replace the Trump administration's 2021 rule, which emphasized economic dependence as the primary factor in worker classification. The new rule proposed a multi-factor test, considering elements such as the permanence of the working relationship and the degree of control exercised by the employer. The change aimed to extend employee protections, including minimum wage and overtime, to more workers.

### Court's Decision

The court's decision to block the rule came in response to a lawsuit filed by several business groups, including the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, which argued that the DOL exceeded its statutory authority. The plaintiffs contended that the rule would impose significant burdens on businesses, particularly small and medium-sized enterprises, by increasing labor costs and complicating compliance.

The court agreed with the plaintiffs, citing concerns about the DOL's rulemaking process and the potential economic impact on businesses. The injunction prevents the rule from taking effect while the case proceeds through the courts.

### Implications for Employers

Employers should continue to classify workers based on the existing 2021 rule until further notice. The court's decision provides temporary relief from the anticipated changes, but employers should remain vigilant as the legal battle continues.

### Action Items

1. **Review Current Classifications**: Ensure that current worker classifications align with the existing 2021 DOL rule. This includes evaluating the economic realities and the degree of control over workers.

2. **Monitor Legal Developments**: Stay updated on the progression of the lawsuit and any potential appeals or settlements that may alter the rule's status.

3. **Prepare for Possible Changes**: Consider the potential impact of the DOL's proposed rule should it eventually be implemented. This may involve assessing the financial implications and operational adjustments needed to comply.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal advisors to understand the nuances of worker classification under both the current and proposed rules. This will help mitigate risks and ensure compliance with FLSA requirements.

As the legal proceedings unfold, businesses must stay informed about the status of the DOL's rule and be prepared to adapt to any changes in worker classification standards.
