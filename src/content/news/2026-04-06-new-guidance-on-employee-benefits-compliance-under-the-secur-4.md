## Understanding the SECURE 2.0 Act's Impact on Employee Benefits

The SECURE 2.0 Act, enacted in December 2022, continues to shape the landscape of employee benefits and retirement plans, with recent guidance from the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) and the Department of Labor (DOL) clarifying key provisions that employers need to address. As HR professionals and business owners navigate these changes, it is crucial to understand the implications for compliance and employee engagement.

One of the most significant changes introduced by SECURE 2.0 is the increase in the age for required minimum distributions (RMDs) from retirement accounts. The new law raises the RMD age from 72 to 73 starting in 2023, and it will increase to 75 in 2033. Employers must ensure that their retirement plans are updated to reflect this change, allowing employees to defer withdrawals longer, potentially enhancing their retirement savings.

Furthermore, SECURE 2.0 expands access to retirement plans for part-time workers. Starting in 2024, employers will be required to allow long-term part-time employees—those who have worked at least 500 hours per year for three consecutive years—to participate in 401(k) plans. This change is designed to encourage savings among a broader workforce, particularly in industries where part-time employment is prevalent. Employers should review their eligibility criteria and consider how they will communicate these changes to affected employees.

Another critical aspect of SECURE 2.0 is the introduction of a new provision that allows employers to offer “catch-up contributions” for individuals aged 60 to 63. These contributions can be up to $10,000, significantly increasing the retirement savings potential for older employees. Employers need to assess how this might affect payroll systems and overall plan contributions.

In addition to these changes, the Act promotes the use of emergency savings accounts linked to retirement plans, allowing employees to withdraw a limited amount of funds without penalty for emergency expenses. Employers may consider implementing this feature to enhance their benefits offerings and support employee financial wellness.

Employers should also be aware of new reporting requirements imposed by SECURE 2.0, including updated disclosures related to plan fees and investment performance. Compliance with these requirements will be essential to avoid penalties and ensure transparency with employees regarding their retirement options.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Retirement Plan Documents**: Ensure that your retirement plans are updated to reflect the changes in RMD age and part-time employee eligibility.
   
2. **Communicate Changes**: Develop a communication strategy to inform employees about new benefits and provisions under SECURE 2.0, particularly the benefits of catch-up contributions and emergency savings accounts.

3. **Training and Resources**: Provide training for HR staff on the implications of SECURE 2.0 to ensure they can answer employee questions effectively.

4. **Consult with Legal or Compliance Experts**: Engage with legal counsel or compliance experts to ensure that your organization meets all new regulatory requirements and avoids potential penalties.

By staying informed about the SECURE 2.0 Act and its ongoing implications, employers can better position themselves to support their employees' financial futures while ensuring compliance with evolving regulations.
