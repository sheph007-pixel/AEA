## SEC Mandates Climate Disclosures

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) finalized a rule on July 12, 2026, requiring public companies to disclose climate-related risks in their financial statements. This rule, set to take effect on December 15, 2026, mandates detailed reporting on climate impact, which may indirectly affect private companies in supply chains or those considering going public.

## Key Requirements

Under the new rule, companies must include climate-related financial disclosures in their annual reports (Form 10-K). This includes information on climate-related risks that are reasonably likely to have a material impact on their business, operations, or financial condition. Additionally, companies must disclose greenhouse gas emissions data, both direct emissions (Scope 1) and indirect emissions from purchased energy (Scope 2).

## Implications for Employers

While the rule directly applies to public companies, private companies with aspirations of going public or those in the supply chain of public companies should prepare for similar scrutiny. This may involve implementing systems to track and report on climate-related data, which could also become a requirement from their publicly traded partners.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Assess Climate Impact**: Conduct a thorough review of your company's climate impact and risks. This includes evaluating operations, supply chains, and potential regulatory changes.
   
2. **Develop Reporting Systems**: Establish or enhance systems to accurately track and report greenhouse gas emissions. Consider third-party verification to ensure data accuracy.

3. **Engage Stakeholders**: Communicate with stakeholders, including investors and partners, about your company's climate-related initiatives and compliance efforts.

4. **Evaluate Supply Chains**: If your company is part of a supply chain for a public company, prepare for potential requests for climate-related data and disclosures.

## Preparing for Future Compliance

The SEC's rule signals a broader trend towards increased regulatory focus on environmental, social, and governance (ESG) factors. Employers should consider integrating ESG considerations into their long-term strategic planning. This proactive approach can mitigate risks and position companies favorably in the eyes of investors and regulatory bodies.

Employers not directly impacted by this rule should nonetheless monitor developments in ESG regulations, as similar requirements could expand to other sectors or become a standard expectation from investors and partners.

In conclusion, while the SEC's climate-related disclosure rule primarily targets public companies, its ripple effects will likely influence a wide range of businesses. By taking proactive steps now, employers can better position themselves for future regulatory landscapes and stakeholder expectations.
