## EEOC Clarifies Paid Leave Policies Under ADA

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance on June 1, 2026, clarifying how employers must handle paid leave policies under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). This guidance aims to ensure that paid leave policies do not discriminate against employees with disabilities.

The new guidance emphasizes that employers must provide reasonable accommodations in their paid leave policies. Specifically, the EEOC highlights that a rigid application of leave policies that results in the termination of an employee with a disability may violate the ADA. Employers are required to consider modifying their leave policies to accommodate the needs of employees with disabilities, provided such modifications do not cause undue hardship.

## Key Points for Employers

Under the ADA, employers must engage in an interactive process with employees requesting accommodations due to disabilities. The EEOC's latest guidance stresses that this process should extend to requests for leave as an accommodation. Employers should not automatically deny requests for leave that exceed the standard leave policy limits without considering whether additional leave can be granted as a reasonable accommodation.

The guidance also clarifies that employers cannot penalize employees for taking leave as a reasonable accommodation. This means that leave taken as an accommodation should not count against an employee in performance evaluations or attendance records.

## Action Items

Employers should review and, if necessary, revise their leave policies to ensure compliance with the EEOC's guidance. This includes training HR personnel and managers on the requirements of the ADA as they relate to leave policies. Employers should also document the interactive process undertaken with employees when considering leave requests as accommodations.

Additionally, companies should ensure that their policies clearly communicate the process for requesting leave as an accommodation, and that employees know their rights under the ADA. By proactively addressing these issues, employers can reduce the risk of discrimination claims and foster a more inclusive workplace.

The EEOC's guidance is a reminder of the importance of accommodating employees with disabilities, not only to comply with legal requirements but to support a diverse and capable workforce.
