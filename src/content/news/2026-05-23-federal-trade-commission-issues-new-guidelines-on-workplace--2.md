## FTC Releases Workplace Surveillance Guidelines

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) released new guidelines on May 18, 2026, addressing the permissible scope of workplace surveillance. These guidelines aim to clarify the boundaries for monitoring employee activities, ensuring compliance with privacy laws while balancing legitimate business interests.

The guidelines specify that employers must provide clear disclosures to employees about the nature and extent of surveillance. This includes informing employees about any monitoring of electronic communications, video surveillance, and location tracking. The guidelines emphasize transparency and require that disclosures be made before implementing any surveillance measures.

### Compliance with Privacy Laws

The FTC guidelines align with existing privacy laws such as the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) and state-specific regulations. Employers must ensure that their surveillance practices do not infringe on employees' reasonable expectation of privacy. The guidelines recommend obtaining explicit consent from employees, particularly for monitoring personal devices used for work purposes.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers should review and, if necessary, revise their surveillance policies to comply with the new FTC guidelines. Key steps include:

1. **Policy Review**: Audit current surveillance practices and ensure they are aligned with the new guidelines. Update privacy policies to reflect any changes in surveillance methods.
   
2. **Employee Notification**: Develop clear communication strategies to inform employees about surveillance practices. This includes revising employee handbooks and conducting informational sessions.

3. **Consent Procedures**: Implement procedures to obtain and document employee consent for surveillance, especially when involving personal devices or off-duty activities.

4. **Data Security Measures**: Ensure that data collected through surveillance is securely stored and access is restricted to authorized personnel only. This minimizes the risk of data breaches and unauthorized access.

### Balancing Monitoring and Privacy

The FTC's guidance underscores the importance of balancing workplace monitoring with employee privacy rights. Employers are encouraged to limit surveillance to areas where there is a legitimate business need and to avoid excessive or intrusive monitoring. Regular audits of surveillance practices can help ensure compliance and address any potential privacy concerns.

These new guidelines from the FTC serve as a critical reminder for employers to evaluate their surveillance practices and prioritize transparency and employee privacy. By adhering to these guidelines, employers can mitigate legal risks and foster a more transparent workplace environment.
