## EEOC Updates Harassment Guidance

On July 10, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on workplace harassment. This update revises the agency's position on harassment under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, emphasizing the need for employers to adopt proactive measures to prevent harassment in the workplace.

## Key Changes in the Guidance

The new guidance underscores the importance of a comprehensive anti-harassment policy that clearly defines unacceptable behavior, including but not limited to sexual harassment, racial discrimination, and bullying. It also highlights the necessity of effective complaint procedures and thorough training programs for employees and management.

The EEOC's guidance clarifies that employers are liable for harassment by supervisors if it results in a tangible employment action, such as termination or demotion. Employers can avoid liability if they can prove they exercised reasonable care to prevent and correct harassment and that the employee unreasonably failed to take advantage of these preventive or corrective opportunities.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review and update their anti-harassment policies to align with the EEOC's latest guidance. This includes revising definitions of harassment to encompass the broader interpretations outlined by the EEOC. Employers should also ensure that their complaint procedures are accessible and encourage reporting without fear of retaliation.

Training programs should be revisited to incorporate the new guidance, focusing on creating an inclusive workplace culture that actively discourages harassment. Employers should document all training sessions and maintain records of attendance to demonstrate compliance.

## Compliance Deadline

While the EEOC's guidance does not impose a specific compliance deadline, employers are advised to update their policies and training programs by December 31, 2026, to mitigate potential liability and foster a safe work environment.

## Conclusion

The EEOC's updated guidance on workplace harassment necessitates immediate attention from employers. By revising policies, enhancing training programs, and fostering an environment that encourages reporting, employers can reduce the risk of harassment claims and ensure compliance with federal laws.
