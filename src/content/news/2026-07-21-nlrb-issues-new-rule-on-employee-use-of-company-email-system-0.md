## NLRB Rule on Email Use

On July 15, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a new rule that limits employers' ability to restrict employees' use of company email systems for union-related communications. This rule, which amends previous guidance under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), Section 7, emphasizes employees' rights to engage in protected concerted activities.

The rule overturns the 2019 decision in *Caesars Entertainment*, which allowed employers to prohibit the use of company email for non-work purposes. Now, restrictions on email use must be justified by a business necessity, such as maintaining productivity or preventing harassment.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must reassess their email use policies to ensure compliance with the new rule. Policies that broadly prohibit non-work-related email use without a clear business justification could be deemed unlawful. Employers should document the specific business reasons for any restrictions on email use, focusing on operational needs or legal obligations.

## Action Items

1. **Review and Revise Policies**: Employers should audit their current electronic communication policies. Amend any blanket prohibitions on non-work-related email use to align with the new NLRB rule.

2. **Document Justifications**: Clearly document business justifications for any restrictions on email use. This documentation should detail how the restrictions support business operations or legal compliance.

3. **Train Management**: Ensure that managers and HR personnel are trained on the new rule and understand the limits of permissible restrictions on email use.

4. **Monitor Compliance**: Regularly review and update policies and practices to remain compliant with NLRB standards and to address any changes in business operations that might affect email use policies.

By taking these steps, employers can mitigate risks of unfair labor practice charges while respecting employees' rights under the NLRA.
