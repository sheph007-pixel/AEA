## Understanding State-Level Retirement Savings Mandates

In recent years, a growing number of states have implemented mandated retirement savings programs aimed at increasing retirement savings among private sector employees. As of October 2023, approximately 17 states may have enacted legislation requiring employers to either offer a retirement plan or enroll their employees in state-sponsored retirement savings programs. This trend reflects a broader effort to address the retirement savings crisis impacting millions of Americans.

### What Employers Need to Know

These mandates vary significantly by state, but they typically require employers to either establish a qualified retirement plan, such as a 401(k), or participate in a state-run program that provides a Roth IRA for employees who do not have access to an employer-sponsored plan. States such as California and Illinois are known to be leading this initiative. Failure to comply with these regulations can result in penalties, making it crucial for employers to stay informed about the specific requirements in their state.

Employers with fewer than five employees may be exempt in some states, but those with more employees generally must comply. For instance, in California, employers with one or more employees must comply. This includes offering a retirement plan or enrolling in the state program, which typically allows employees to contribute a percentage of their wages through payroll deductions, with automatic enrollment features to simplify participation.

### Legal Considerations and Compliance

Employers should closely monitor the regulations in their respective states, as compliance deadlines may differ. For example, California’s mandate required compliance by employers with five or more employees by June 30, 2022, while other states may have different timelines. It is essential for HR professionals and business owners to consult with legal counsel or a compliance specialist to understand the nuances of these laws and ensure their organization meets all necessary obligations.

In addition to compliance, employers should consider the potential impact on employee recruitment and retention. Offering a retirement savings option can enhance the attractiveness of a benefits package, particularly in a competitive labor market where candidates increasingly prioritize financial wellness benefits.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Assess Current Benefits**: Review existing retirement benefit offerings to determine if they meet state requirements or identify gaps that need to be addressed.

2. **Consult Legal Experts**: Engage with legal counsel or a benefits consultant to navigate the complexities of state mandates and ensure compliance with all applicable laws.

3. **Educate Employees**: Communicate changes in retirement benefits to employees, emphasizing the importance of saving for retirement and how they can participate in the new programs.

4. **Stay Informed**: Regularly monitor updates from state agencies and industry associations to remain aware of any changes in legislation or compliance requirements.

By proactively addressing these state-level retirement savings mandates, employers can not only fulfill legal obligations but also enhance their employee value proposition, fostering a more financially secure workforce.
