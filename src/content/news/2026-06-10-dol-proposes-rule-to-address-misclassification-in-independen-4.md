## DOL's Proposed Rule on Worker Classification

On June 8, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule to redefine the criteria for classifying workers as independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposal seeks to address the ongoing issue of worker misclassification, which affects wage and hour protections for millions of workers.

The proposed rule introduces a revised "economic reality" test, which evaluates whether a worker is economically dependent on the employer for work or is in business for themselves. The test considers factors such as the nature and degree of control over the work, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, and the amount of skill required for the work.

## Implications for Employers

Employers with workers currently classified as independent contractors should reassess these classifications in light of the proposed criteria. Misclassification can lead to significant liabilities, including back wages, taxes, and penalties. The DOL estimates that the new rule could affect various industries, particularly those with a high prevalence of gig and freelance work.

The DOL's proposal aligns with President Biden's administration's broader efforts to strengthen worker protections and ensure fair compensation. The public comment period for the proposed rule is open until August 7, 2026. Employers are encouraged to submit feedback during this period.

## Action Items for Employers

To prepare for potential changes, employers should:

1. **Review Current Classifications**: Conduct an audit of current independent contractor agreements to ensure compliance with the proposed "economic reality" test.
   
2. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with employment law experts to understand the implications of the proposed rule on your workforce.

3. **Prepare for Reclassification**: Consider the potential need to reclassify workers and the associated costs, including overtime, benefits, and payroll taxes.

4. **Submit Comments**: Participate in the rulemaking process by submitting comments to the DOL before the deadline, providing insights into how the changes could impact your business.

The DOL's initiative reflects a significant shift in federal labor policy, emphasizing the protection of worker rights and fair compensation. Employers should act promptly to mitigate risks associated with misclassification and ensure compliance with evolving regulations.
