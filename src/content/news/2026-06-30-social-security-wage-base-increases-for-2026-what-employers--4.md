## Social Security Wage Base Increase

The Social Security Administration (SSA) announced on October 12, 2025, that the Social Security wage base will increase to $168,600 for 2026. This change affects the amount of earnings subject to the Social Security portion of the Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) tax. Employers must adjust their payroll systems to accommodate this new wage base starting January 1, 2026.

## Impact on Payroll Taxes

The increase from the 2025 wage base of $165,000 means that employees earning above this threshold will see more of their income subject to the 6.2% Social Security tax rate. Employers are responsible for matching this contribution, resulting in a maximum Social Security tax of $10,453.20 per employee for both the employer and employee in 2026.

## Medicare Tax Unchanged

It's important to note that the Medicare tax rate remains unchanged at 1.45% on all earnings, with no wage base limit. Additionally, the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax continues to apply to wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers and $250,000 for married couples filing jointly, solely impacting the employee portion.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should update payroll systems by December 31, 2025, to reflect the new Social Security wage base. Training payroll staff on these changes is crucial to ensure accurate withholding. Employers should also communicate these updates to employees, as it may affect their take-home pay.

For accurate compliance, verify that third-party payroll providers are also aware of and prepared for these changes. Reviewing and adjusting budget forecasts for 2026 to account for increased payroll tax expenses may be necessary.

## Planning for Future Adjustments

The SSA typically announces wage base adjustments in October, giving employers lead time to prepare. Monitoring these announcements annually and planning for incremental increases can help mitigate unexpected financial impacts.

Employers should also consider how these changes might affect employee compensation strategies, particularly for those near the wage base limit. Reviewing benefits packages and potentially adjusting salary structures may be prudent to maintain competitive compensation offerings.

By staying informed and proactive, employers can ensure seamless compliance with payroll tax requirements and maintain employee satisfaction.
