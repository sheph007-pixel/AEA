## IRS Updates HSA Contribution Limits for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidelines on May 15, 2026, regarding Health Savings Accounts (HSAs), including new contribution limits and eligibility criteria for the tax year 2026. These changes are critical for employers who offer HSAs as part of their employee benefits package.

For 2026, the annual contribution limit for individuals with self-only coverage under a high-deductible health plan (HDHP) will increase to $4,050, up from $3,850 in 2025. For family coverage, the limit rises to $8,100, compared to $7,750 in the previous year. Additionally, the catch-up contribution for individuals aged 55 and older remains unchanged at $1,000.

## HDHP Minimum Deductibles and Out-of-Pocket Maximums

The IRS also adjusted the minimum deductible and maximum out-of-pocket expenses for HDHPs, which are required for HSA eligibility. For 2026, the minimum deductible for self-only coverage will be $1,500, and for family coverage, it will be $3,000. The maximum out-of-pocket expenses will be $7,500 for self-only coverage and $15,000 for family coverage.

These adjustments reflect the IRS's annual review to account for inflation and ensure that HSAs remain a viable option for managing healthcare costs.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should update their benefits materials and payroll systems to reflect these new limits and criteria before the open enrollment period for the 2026 plan year. It's also advisable to communicate these changes to employees, highlighting how they can maximize their HSA contributions and potential tax savings.

Additionally, employers sponsoring HDHPs should verify that their plans meet the updated deductible and out-of-pocket maximum requirements to maintain HSA eligibility for their employees.

## Implications for Employers

Offering HSAs can be a valuable component of a competitive benefits package, providing tax advantages for both employers and employees. The updated contribution limits and eligibility criteria may require adjustments in employer contributions or matching strategies to align with these new thresholds.

Employers should also consider providing educational resources or workshops to help employees understand the benefits of HSAs and how to effectively manage their healthcare expenses.

For further details, employers can refer to IRS Notice 2026-32, which outlines the specific changes and provides additional guidance on HSA administration for the upcoming year.
