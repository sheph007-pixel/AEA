## Joint Employer Definition Expanded by NLRB

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) finalized a rule on May 30, 2026, expanding the criteria for determining joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This rule, effective September 1, 2026, broadens the scope of liability for businesses that share control over workers with other entities. Specifically, the rule impacts businesses that use staffing agencies, franchise models, or subcontractors, requiring them to reassess their labor practices and contractual agreements.

## What Changed?

The rule redefines a joint employer as any entity that possesses or exercises substantial direct or indirect control over essential terms and conditions of employment. This includes decisions related to hiring, firing, discipline, supervision, and direction. Previously, the focus was primarily on direct and immediate control. This shift means that businesses could be held jointly liable for labor law violations committed by their partners or contractors.

## Impact on Small Businesses

Small businesses employing contractors or engaging with staffing agencies may now find themselves classified as joint employers. This classification subjects them to collective bargaining obligations and potential liability for unfair labor practices. The expansion could lead to increased operational costs and necessitate changes in how these businesses manage their workforce relationships.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Contracts**: Employers should scrutinize existing contracts with third-party labor providers to identify clauses that may imply indirect control over employment terms.

2. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Legal advice is crucial to understand the implications of the new rule and to develop strategies to mitigate potential liabilities.

3. **Reassess Labor Practices**: Evaluate current labor practices to ensure compliance with the broadened joint employer criteria. This might involve restructuring relationships with staffing agencies or franchisees.

4. **Training for Management**: Implement training programs for managers and HR personnel to understand the new rule and its impact on day-to-day operations.

## Looking Ahead

The broadened joint employer definition is likely to face legal challenges, but businesses must prepare for its implementation. The rule underscores the importance of understanding the nuances of labor relationships and maintaining compliant practices. As the September 1 implementation date approaches, staying proactive in assessing and adjusting business operations will be key to navigating this regulatory change.
