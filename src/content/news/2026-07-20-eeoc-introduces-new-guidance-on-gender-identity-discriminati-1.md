## EEOC Clarifies Gender Identity Discrimination Rules

On July 15, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) issued new guidance clarifying the protections against gender identity discrimination under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. This move follows several high-profile cases and aims to provide clearer directives for employers regarding compliance with federal anti-discrimination laws.

### Key Provisions of the Guidance

The EEOC's guidance explicitly states that discrimination based on gender identity, including transgender status, constitutes sex discrimination under Title VII. This aligns with the Supreme Court's ruling in Bostock v. Clayton County (2020), which affirmed that Title VII's prohibition on sex discrimination includes discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity.

The guidance emphasizes that employers must treat employees consistent with their gender identity. This includes access to restrooms and locker rooms, the use of names and pronouns, and dress codes. Employers are also advised that refusal to hire, promote, or provide equal employment opportunities based on gender identity is unlawful.

### Implications for Employers

Employers with 15 or more employees, which fall under Title VII's jurisdiction, must ensure their policies and practices comply with these clarified standards. This involves reviewing and potentially revising current policies on workplace conduct, dress codes, and facilities access to ensure they are inclusive of gender identity considerations.

### Action Items for Compliance

1. **Policy Review and Update**: Audit employee handbooks and non-discrimination policies to explicitly include gender identity and expression as protected categories.

2. **Training Programs**: Implement or update diversity and inclusion training programs to include modules on gender identity discrimination, ensuring that all employees understand their rights and responsibilities.

3. **Facilities and Dress Codes**: Ensure that facilities such as restrooms and locker rooms are accessible according to employees' gender identity. Review dress codes to remove any gender-specific requirements that could lead to discrimination.

4. **Complaint Procedures**: Establish clear procedures for employees to report discrimination or harassment based on gender identity. Ensure that these complaints are handled promptly and in accordance with EEOC guidelines.

### Moving Forward

The EEOC's guidance underscores the importance of fostering an inclusive workplace environment that respects and affirms all employees' gender identities. Employers should act promptly to align their practices with these federal standards, not only to avoid potential legal challenges but also to cultivate a workplace culture of equality and respect.
