## New Rule on Retirement Plan Fee Disclosures

The Department of Labor (DOL) has finalized a rule requiring employers to provide detailed fee disclosures for retirement plans, effective January 1, 2025. This rule aims to enhance transparency for participants in employer-sponsored retirement plans, such as 401(k) and 403(b) plans, by ensuring they receive clear information about the fees and expenses associated with their investments.

Under this rule, employers must disclose all fees, including administrative, investment, and individual service fees, in a manner that is easy for plan participants to understand. The rule is part of the DOL's ongoing efforts to protect retirement savings and ensure that plan participants are fully informed about the costs that can impact their retirement savings.

## Implementation Details

The new disclosure requirements are outlined in 29 C.F.R. § 2550.404a-5. Employers must provide an annual disclosure statement to all plan participants, detailing the specific fees deducted from their accounts. Additionally, any changes to fee structures must be communicated within 60 days of the change.

The DOL emphasizes that these disclosures must be presented in a straightforward format, avoiding technical jargon. The goal is to empower employees to make informed decisions about their retirement investments by clearly understanding the costs involved.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should start by reviewing their current retirement plan fee structures and disclosure practices. Here are the key steps to ensure compliance by the January 2025 deadline:

1. **Audit Current Fee Disclosures**: Conduct a thorough review of existing fee disclosure documents to identify any gaps or areas needing improvement.
   
2. **Update Disclosure Formats**: Work with plan administrators to redesign fee disclosures, ensuring they meet the new clarity and transparency standards set by the DOL.

3. **Communicate with Employees**: Plan a communication strategy to educate employees about the new disclosures and what they mean for their retirement savings.

4. **Training for HR and Benefits Staff**: Ensure that HR and benefits personnel are trained on the new requirements and can effectively communicate these changes to employees.

5. **Monitor Compliance**: Establish ongoing processes to regularly review and update disclosures as needed, ensuring continuous compliance with the DOL rule.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new rule on retirement plan fee disclosures is a significant step towards enhancing transparency and protecting employee retirement savings. By preparing now, employers can ensure they meet the January 2025 compliance deadline and provide their employees with the information they need to make informed retirement planning decisions.
