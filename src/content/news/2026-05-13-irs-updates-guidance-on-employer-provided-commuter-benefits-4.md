## IRS Clarifies Commuter Benefits Rules

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has issued updated guidance on the tax treatment of employer-provided commuter benefits, effective immediately. This clarification impacts how employers administer benefits related to transportation and parking expenses under 26 U.S.C. § 132(f).

The updated guidance addresses ambiguities in the tax code regarding the pre-tax treatment of commuter benefits, which include transit passes, vanpooling, and parking. The IRS confirms that the monthly limit for tax-exempt commuter benefits remains $300 for 2026, as previously adjusted for inflation.

## Key Changes and Implications

One significant clarification is the treatment of unused benefits. The IRS specifies that any unused commuter benefits must be forfeited at the end of each month, aligning with the "use-it-or-lose-it" rule. Employers cannot carry over unused amounts to subsequent months or cash them out to employees. This rule aims to prevent abuse of the benefit system and ensures compliance with the statutory limits.

Additionally, the guidance elaborates on the substantiation requirements for tax-exempt treatment. Employers must maintain adequate records to substantiate the provision of these benefits. Acceptable documentation includes receipts, vouchers, or electronic records from transit providers, ensuring that the benefits are used for their intended purpose.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should review their current commuter benefits policies to ensure compliance with the updated IRS guidance. Specifically, they should:

- **Audit Current Benefits Programs**: Verify that monthly limits are enforced and that unused benefits are not carried over or cashed out.
- **Update Record-Keeping Practices**: Ensure systems are in place to collect and retain the necessary documentation for substantiation.
- **Communicate Changes to Employees**: Inform employees about the "use-it-or-lose-it" policy to avoid confusion and potential dissatisfaction.

Employers who fail to comply with these guidelines risk losing the tax-exempt status of their commuter benefits, potentially increasing their tax liabilities and administrative burdens.

## Conclusion

The IRS's updated guidance on commuter benefits provides essential clarity for employers managing these programs. By aligning their policies with the new rules, employers can maintain compliance and continue to offer valuable benefits to their employees without incurring additional tax burdens.
