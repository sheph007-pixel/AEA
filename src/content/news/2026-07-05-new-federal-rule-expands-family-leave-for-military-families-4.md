## Expanded FMLA Protections for Military Families

The Department of Labor (DOL) has finalized a rule expanding Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) protections for military families, effective January 1, 2026. This amendment to the FMLA, codified at 29 U.S.C. § 2612(a)(1)(E), extends leave entitlements to cover additional circumstances related to military service.

## Key Changes

The new rule broadens the definition of "qualifying exigency" leave, allowing eligible employees to take up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for issues related to a family member's military deployment. This includes attending military events, arranging for alternative childcare, and addressing financial and legal arrangements. The rule also expands coverage to include veterans discharged within the past five years, rather than the previous two-year limit.

Additionally, the rule introduces a new category of leave for "rest and recuperation," permitting employees to take up to 15 days of leave during a military family member's short-term, temporary rest and recuperation leave from deployment.

## Implications for Employers

Employers with 50 or more employees must update their FMLA policies and employee handbooks to reflect these changes. Organizations should ensure that their HR teams are trained on the expanded definitions and new leave categories. This includes adjusting processes for leave requests and documentation requirements to comply with the updated regulations.

## Action Items

1. **Policy Updates:** Amend FMLA policies and employee handbooks to include the new leave entitlements for military families.
   
2. **Training:** Conduct training sessions for HR personnel to familiarize them with the expanded leave provisions and ensure they can effectively manage requests.
   
3. **Communication:** Inform employees about the updated FMLA provisions, particularly those who may benefit from the expanded military family leave options.

## Conclusion

The DOL's expansion of FMLA protections reflects a significant shift in recognizing the diverse needs of military families. Employers must promptly align their policies and practices with the new rule to ensure compliance and support their employees effectively.
