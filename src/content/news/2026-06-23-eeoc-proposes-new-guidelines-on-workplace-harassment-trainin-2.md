## EEOC Proposes New Guidelines on Workplace Harassment Training

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) proposed new guidelines on June 20, 2026, aimed at enhancing workplace harassment training. This initiative seeks to update the standards for harassment prevention programs under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. The proposed guidelines are open for public comment until August 31, 2026, and could significantly impact the way employers approach harassment training.

### Key Changes in the Guidelines

The proposed guidelines emphasize interactive training methods over traditional lecture-style sessions. The EEOC suggests that interactive training, which includes role-playing and real-life scenarios, is more effective in preventing harassment. Employers are encouraged to incorporate these methods to comply with the new standards.

Additionally, the guidelines recommend annual training for all employees, including management, to reinforce anti-harassment policies. They also stress the importance of tailoring training sessions to reflect the specific workplace environment and industry, ensuring relevance and engagement.

### Implications for Employers

Employers should prepare to reassess their current harassment training programs. The proposed guidelines suggest a shift from compliance-focused training to a more dynamic and engaging approach. Companies may need to invest in new training materials and possibly hire external trainers specializing in interactive methods.

Moreover, the guidelines highlight the need for documentation. Employers should maintain records of training sessions, including attendance and content covered, to demonstrate compliance in the event of an EEOC investigation.

### Action Items for HR Professionals

1. **Review Current Training Programs**: Evaluate existing harassment training to identify areas needing improvement or updates to align with the proposed guidelines.

2. **Plan for Interactive Methods**: Consider integrating interactive elements into training sessions. This might involve workshops, group discussions, or virtual reality simulations.

3. **Schedule Regular Training**: Ensure that all employees, including executives, participate in annual harassment training sessions.

4. **Document Everything**: Keep detailed records of all training activities, including dates, participant lists, and training materials used.

5. **Submit Comments**: Consider submitting feedback on the proposed guidelines to the EEOC by the August 31 deadline. This is an opportunity to influence the final version of the guidelines.

As the EEOC moves forward with these proposed changes, employers must be proactive in adapting their harassment training programs. By doing so, they not only ensure compliance but also foster a safer and more respectful workplace environment.
