## IRS Clarifies Tax Treatment of Educational Assistance

On July 10, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released new guidance regarding the tax treatment of employer-provided educational assistance programs under Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 127. This guidance aims to clarify how employers can offer educational benefits to employees without those benefits being subject to federal income tax.

Section 127 allows employers to provide up to $5,250 per year in educational assistance to employees, which can be excluded from the employees' gross income. The new guidance addresses ambiguities in the application of this exclusion, particularly concerning the types of educational expenses that qualify and the documentation required to substantiate these benefits.

## Key Points for Employers

The IRS guidance specifies that educational assistance can cover tuition, fees, books, supplies, and equipment, provided they are directly related to the employee's course of study. Notably, the guidance excludes tools or supplies that employees can keep after completing the course, as well as meals, lodging, and transportation.

Employers must ensure that their educational assistance programs are documented in a formal written plan. This plan should detail the eligibility requirements, the types of educational expenses covered, and the maximum benefit amounts. Importantly, the plan must not favor highly compensated employees as defined by IRC Section 414(q).

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Plans:** Employers should review their current educational assistance plans to ensure compliance with the new IRS guidance. Amendments may be necessary to align with the clarified definitions of qualifying expenses.

2. **Document Compliance:** Employers must maintain adequate records that demonstrate compliance with Section 127 requirements. This includes keeping copies of the educational assistance plan, employee applications, and proof of expenses covered.

3. **Communicate Changes:** Employers should communicate any changes in the educational assistance program to employees, highlighting any new requirements or limitations.

4. **Consult Tax Professionals:** Given the complexity of tax regulations, consulting with tax professionals can help ensure that the educational assistance programs meet all IRS requirements and avoid unintended tax liabilities.

By adhering to the new guidance, employers can continue to offer valuable educational benefits to their workforce while ensuring compliance with federal tax laws. This not only aids in employee development but also enhances recruitment and retention efforts in a competitive job market.
