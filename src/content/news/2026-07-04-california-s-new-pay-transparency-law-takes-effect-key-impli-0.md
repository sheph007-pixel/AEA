## California's Pay Transparency Law Now in Effect

California's new pay transparency law, Senate Bill 1162, took effect on January 1, 2026. The law requires employers with 15 or more employees to disclose salary ranges in all job postings. This significant change aims to promote pay equity and transparency across the state.

The California Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) enforces this law, which mandates that employers include a pay scale for each job advertised. Failure to comply can result in penalties, including fines up to $10,000 per violation. The law applies to both internal and external job postings, impacting how businesses attract and retain talent.

## What Employers Need to Know

Employers must now include specific salary ranges in job advertisements. This requirement is not limited to base pay; it also includes bonuses and other compensation forms if applicable. The law covers all job advertisements, including those posted online, in print, or through third-party recruiters.

Additionally, employers must provide the pay scale for a position to current employees upon request. This provision ensures that existing employees have access to the same information as prospective hires, fostering transparency within the organization.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Job Postings**: Employers should immediately audit their job postings to ensure compliance. This includes revising existing advertisements and setting up processes for new postings to include the required pay information.

2. **Establish a Pay Scale Database**: Create a centralized database of pay scales for all positions. This will facilitate compliance and streamline responses to employee inquiries about pay ranges.

3. **Train HR and Recruiting Teams**: Conduct training sessions for HR and recruiting teams to ensure they understand the new requirements and can accurately convey pay information to candidates and employees.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the potential for significant penalties, employers should consult with legal counsel to ensure their pay transparency practices align with SB 1162.

5. **Monitor Compliance**: Regularly review job postings and internal processes to ensure ongoing compliance. Consider implementing periodic audits to identify and correct any discrepancies.

## Broader Implications

California's law reflects a growing trend toward pay transparency across the United States. Other states, including New York and Colorado, have enacted similar laws, and federal legislation may follow. Employers nationwide should stay informed about these developments, as they could impact multi-state operations.

By complying with SB 1162, California employers not only avoid legal repercussions but also position themselves as leaders in promoting pay equity. This can enhance employer branding and attract talent in a competitive job market.
