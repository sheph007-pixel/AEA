## New OSHA Rule on Chemical Exposure

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) announced a new rule on July 10, 2026, setting revised permissible exposure limits (PELs) for several hazardous chemicals in the workplace. This rule, effective January 1, 2027, is part of OSHA's ongoing efforts to enhance worker safety by updating outdated exposure limits.

The rule revises PELs for substances including benzene, formaldehyde, and silica, among others. These changes are based on recent scientific research and recommendations from the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH). The updates aim to reduce the risk of occupational illnesses, such as respiratory diseases and cancer, associated with long-term exposure to these chemicals.

## Who is Affected?

Employers in industries such as manufacturing, construction, and chemical processing, where these substances are commonly used, will be directly impacted. Companies with processes involving these chemicals must assess their current exposure levels and implement necessary control measures to comply with the new limits.

## Key Changes

- **Benzene**: The new PEL is set at 0.5 parts per million (ppm), down from the previous 1 ppm. This change reflects the carcinogenic nature of benzene and aims to provide greater protection for workers.
- **Formaldehyde**: The revised limit is 0.3 ppm, reduced from 0.75 ppm. This adjustment addresses the risks of respiratory and skin irritation, as well as the potential for cancer.
- **Silica**: The PEL for respirable crystalline silica is now 25 micrograms per cubic meter (µg/m³), lowered from 50 µg/m³. This aims to prevent silicosis, a serious lung disease.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers must conduct a thorough review of their current workplace practices and exposure levels. Key steps include:

1. **Exposure Assessment**: Conduct air monitoring to determine current exposure levels of the affected chemicals.
2. **Engineering Controls**: Implement or upgrade ventilation systems and other engineering controls to minimize exposure.
3. **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**: Ensure appropriate PPE is available and used correctly by employees.
4. **Training**: Update training programs to educate workers about the risks associated with these chemicals and the importance of using protective measures.
5. **Recordkeeping**: Maintain accurate records of exposure levels and control measures as required by OSHA standards.

## Compliance Timeline

Employers must achieve compliance by January 1, 2027. OSHA has indicated that it will provide resources and guidance to assist businesses in meeting the new requirements. Non-compliance could result in significant penalties, including fines and increased scrutiny from OSHA inspectors.

By proactively addressing these changes, employers can protect their workforce from hazardous exposures and avoid potential legal and financial repercussions.
