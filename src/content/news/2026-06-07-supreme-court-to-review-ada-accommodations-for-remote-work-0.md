## Supreme Court to Review ADA Accommodations for Remote Work

The Supreme Court has agreed to hear a case that could redefine employers' obligations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) concerning remote work accommodations. The case, Smith v. TechCorp, will be heard in the fall of 2026 and could have significant implications for how businesses handle requests for remote work as a reasonable accommodation for employees with disabilities.

### Background

The ADA (42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq.) mandates that employers provide reasonable accommodations to qualified individuals with disabilities unless doing so would cause undue hardship. The rise of remote work during the COVID-19 pandemic has led to increased requests for remote work as an accommodation, raising questions about what constitutes a reasonable accommodation in this context.

In Smith v. TechCorp, the plaintiff, an employee with a chronic medical condition, requested to work from home as an accommodation. TechCorp denied the request, citing the need for in-person collaboration as essential to the employee's role. The lower courts were divided on whether remote work was a reasonable accommodation in this instance, leading to the Supreme Court's involvement.

### Implications for Employers

The Supreme Court's decision could provide much-needed clarity on the extent to which employers must accommodate remote work requests under the ADA. Employers should prepare for potential changes in compliance requirements by reviewing their current accommodation policies and practices.

### Action Items

1. **Review Accommodation Policies:** Employers should review their ADA accommodation policies to ensure they are flexible enough to incorporate potential changes resulting from the Supreme Court's decision.

2. **Document Accommodation Requests:** Maintain thorough documentation of all accommodation requests and the interactive process used to address them. This documentation can be crucial if the reasonableness of an accommodation is challenged.

3. **Evaluate Essential Job Functions:** Reassess job descriptions to ensure that essential functions are clearly defined, particularly those that may require in-person presence.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Employers should consult with legal counsel to understand the potential implications of the Supreme Court's decision and to prepare for any necessary policy adjustments.

The outcome of Smith v. TechCorp will likely impact ADA compliance strategies nationwide. Employers should stay informed about the case's developments and be ready to adapt their policies accordingly.
