## NLRB Decision Alters Joint-Employer Criteria

On June 30, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a decision refining the criteria for determining joint-employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). The decision in Browning-Ferris Industries of California, Inc. alters the framework for assessing when two or more businesses are considered joint employers of the same workers, impacting liability for labor practices and collective bargaining obligations.

Under the revised standard, the NLRB will consider both direct and indirect control over essential terms and conditions of employment, such as hiring, firing, discipline, supervision, and direction. This marks a departure from the previous standard, which focused primarily on direct and immediate control.

## Implications for Employers

The decision has significant implications for businesses using staffing agencies, subcontractors, or franchise models. Companies could now be held jointly liable for labor violations and required to engage in collective bargaining if they exert indirect control over workers.

Employers should carefully evaluate their agreements and practices with third-party labor providers. This includes reviewing contracts to identify areas where indirect control might be interpreted as joint employment. Legal counsel should be consulted to assess potential risks and liabilities.

## Action Items

1. **Audit Contracts**: Review contracts with staffing agencies and subcontractors to identify clauses that might imply indirect control over workers. Consider revising terms to limit exposure.

2. **Training**: Train managers and supervisors on the implications of the joint-employer standard. Ensure they understand the boundaries of permissible control over third-party workers.

3. **Legal Consultation**: Engage with legal counsel to evaluate current business practices and contracts. Determine potential liabilities and develop strategies to mitigate risks.

4. **Policy Review**: Reassess internal policies and procedures related to third-party labor to ensure compliance with the new joint-employer criteria.

By taking proactive steps, businesses can better navigate the complexities introduced by the NLRB's decision and minimize potential liabilities associated with joint-employer status.
