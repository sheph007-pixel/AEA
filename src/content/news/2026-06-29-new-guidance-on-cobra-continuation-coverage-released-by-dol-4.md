## DOL Clarifies COBRA Coverage for Remote Workers

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued updated guidance on June 28, 2026, concerning COBRA continuation coverage, specifically addressing the complexities surrounding remote and hybrid work arrangements. This clarification aims to assist employers in understanding their obligations under the Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (COBRA) when dealing with employees who work remotely.

### Key Updates

The guidance, detailed in the DOL's Field Assistance Bulletin No. 2026-2, emphasizes that remote employees are entitled to the same COBRA continuation coverage rights as their on-site counterparts. This includes access to group health plans after employment termination or a reduction in hours that affects eligibility.

Under 29 U.S.C. § 1161, COBRA mandates that employers with 20 or more employees offer continued health insurance coverage to eligible employees and their families after qualifying events. The new guidance clarifies that the determination of employer size and the applicability of COBRA should include remote workers, irrespective of their physical location.

### Implications for Employers

Employers must ensure compliance with the updated COBRA guidelines by evaluating their current workforce, including remote employees, to accurately determine their obligations. This may involve revisiting how employee counts are calculated, particularly for businesses operating with a significant number of remote workers across different states.

Additionally, the DOL's bulletin suggests that employers should review their health plan documents and COBRA notices to ensure they reflect the inclusion of remote employees. This may require amendments to existing policies to explicitly state coverage terms for those not physically present at the employer's primary location.

### Action Items

1. **Assess Workforce Size:** Recalculate your employee count to include remote workers in determining COBRA applicability.
   
2. **Review Plan Documents:** Ensure health plan documents and COBRA notices accurately represent coverage for remote employees.

3. **Update HR Policies:** Amend HR policies to clarify COBRA rights and coverage for remote and hybrid employees.

4. **Train HR Staff:** Conduct training sessions for HR personnel to ensure they understand the updated guidance and can effectively communicate COBRA rights to employees.

These steps are crucial for maintaining compliance and avoiding potential penalties associated with COBRA violations. Employers should consult with legal counsel or benefits advisors to ensure their policies align with the latest DOL guidance.

### Conclusion

The DOL's recent clarification on COBRA coverage for remote workers underscores the evolving nature of workplace benefits administration. By taking proactive measures to incorporate these updates, employers can better navigate the complexities of remote work arrangements and uphold their legal responsibilities under COBRA.
