## New Federal Guidelines on Mental Health Parity in Employee Benefits

On May 15, 2026, the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) released updated guidance to ensure compliance with the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act of 2008 (MHPAEA). This guidance affects employer-sponsored health plans, requiring them to provide mental health and substance use disorder benefits on par with medical and surgical benefits.

### Key Changes

The new guidance clarifies the application of parity requirements, emphasizing non-quantitative treatment limitations (NQTLs). Employers must ensure that their health plans do not impose more restrictive NQTLs on mental health and substance use disorder benefits than on medical/surgical benefits. This includes rules on prior authorization, step therapy protocols, and network adequacy.

### Compliance Requirements

Employers sponsoring health plans must conduct and document a comparative analysis of NQTLs. This analysis should demonstrate that mental health and substance use disorder benefits are comparable to those for medical and surgical benefits. The HHS will require plans to submit these analyses upon request to verify compliance.

The guidance also specifies that plans must disclose criteria for medical necessity determinations and the processes for developing and applying NQTLs. This transparency is intended to facilitate compliance and improve access to mental health services.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Health Plan Policies**: Employers should immediately review their health plan policies to identify any NQTLs that may be more restrictive for mental health and substance use disorder benefits.

2. **Conduct Comparative Analysis**: Prepare a detailed comparative analysis of NQTLs as required by the new guidance. Ensure that documentation is thorough and ready for HHS review if requested.

3. **Update Disclosures**: Ensure that the plan's criteria for medical necessity determinations and NQTL development processes are clearly documented and disclosed to plan participants.

4. **Coordinate with Insurers and Plan Administrators**: Work closely with insurers and third-party administrators to ensure that plan designs comply with MHPAEA requirements and that necessary changes are implemented swiftly.

### Potential Penalties

Failure to comply with the MHPAEA can result in significant penalties. Employers could face enforcement actions by the Department of Labor and the Internal Revenue Service, including excise taxes and other penalties.

### Conclusion

Employers need to act swiftly to ensure compliance with the new HHS guidance on mental health parity. By reviewing health plan policies and conducting required analyses, employers can avoid potential penalties and support employee access to necessary mental health services.
