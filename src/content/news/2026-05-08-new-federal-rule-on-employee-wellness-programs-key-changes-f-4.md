## EEOC Issues New Rule on Wellness Programs

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new regulations on May 1, 2026, affecting employer-sponsored wellness programs. The rules, under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA), will take effect on January 1, 2027. Employers must review their wellness initiatives to ensure compliance with these changes.

## Key Changes Under the New Rule

The EEOC's new rule clarifies the extent to which employers can incentivize participation in wellness programs. It restricts incentives to no more than 30% of the total cost of self-only coverage under the employer's health plan. This limit applies to both participatory and health-contingent wellness programs.

Additionally, the rule mandates that all wellness programs must be voluntary. Employers cannot penalize employees for choosing not to participate, nor can they require participation as a condition for health plan enrollment. The regulations also stipulate that any health information collected must be kept confidential and cannot be used for employment decisions.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must audit their existing wellness programs to ensure they meet the new incentive limits and maintain the voluntary nature required by the EEOC. This may involve recalculating incentives offered to employees and adjusting program structures to avoid any potential penalties.

HR professionals should also review how employee health information is handled and ensure that confidentiality protocols are robust. Training sessions for staff managing these programs may be necessary to prevent inadvertent breaches of employee privacy.

## Preparing for Compliance

To prepare for the January 1, 2027, compliance deadline, employers should:

1. **Audit Existing Programs**: Evaluate current wellness programs against the new EEOC regulations. Ensure that incentives do not exceed the 30% cap and that participation remains voluntary.

2. **Update Policies**: Amend employee handbooks and wellness program materials to reflect the new rules. Clearly communicate any changes to employees to avoid confusion.

3. **Enhance Confidentiality Measures**: Strengthen data protection measures to safeguard employee health information. This includes training personnel on proper data handling and updating IT security protocols.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal experts to interpret the new regulations and ensure all aspects of the wellness program comply with federal laws.

By taking these steps, employers can align their wellness programs with the EEOC's latest regulations and continue to offer valuable health initiatives to their workforce without risking non-compliance penalties.
