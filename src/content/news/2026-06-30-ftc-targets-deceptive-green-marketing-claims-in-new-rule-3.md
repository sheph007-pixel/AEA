## FTC Green Guides Update

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) issued new guidance on June 15, 2026, targeting deceptive environmental marketing claims. The updated "Green Guides" aim to prevent misleading advertising practices that falsely imply environmental benefits. The revised guidelines require businesses to substantiate their environmental claims with clear and specific evidence.

## Key Changes

The FTC's revised Green Guides emphasize transparency in claims such as "eco-friendly," "sustainable," and "recyclable." Under the new rules, businesses must provide scientific evidence supporting these claims. For example, a product labeled as "recyclable" must be recyclable in a substantial majority of communities where it is sold (16 C.F.R. Part 260).

The update also introduces stricter standards for carbon offset claims. Companies must disclose if offsets will not be realized within two years and provide details on the offset project’s location and method.

## Implications for Employers

Businesses using environmental claims in marketing must reassess their advertising strategies to ensure compliance. Misleading green claims can lead to FTC enforcement actions, including fines and corrective advertising orders.

## Action Items

1. **Audit Marketing Materials**: Review all marketing and advertising materials for compliance with the new Green Guides. Ensure that all environmental claims are backed by credible evidence.

2. **Train Marketing Teams**: Conduct training sessions for marketing and advertising teams on the updated guidelines to prevent future violations.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to review claims and ensure they meet the FTC's substantiation requirements.

4. **Update Documentation**: Maintain clear records of evidence supporting any environmental claims to demonstrate compliance if challenged.

The updated Green Guides reflect the FTC's commitment to protecting consumers from deceptive environmental claims. Businesses should take immediate steps to align their marketing practices with the new guidelines to avoid potential penalties.
