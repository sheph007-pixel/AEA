## IRS Updates HSA Contribution Limits and Eligible Expenses

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has released new guidance on Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) for the 2026 tax year, effective immediately. This update includes revised contribution limits and clarifications on eligible expenses, impacting employers who offer HSAs as part of their benefits packages.

### Contribution Limits Increased

For 2026, the IRS has increased the annual contribution limits for HSAs. Under IRC § 223(b)(2), the new limit for individuals with self-only coverage under a high-deductible health plan (HDHP) is $4,050, up from $3,850 in 2025. For family coverage, the limit rises to $8,100, compared to $7,750 previously. Employers should update their payroll systems to reflect these new contribution limits, ensuring compliance and avoiding potential tax penalties for employees.

### Clarification on Eligible Expenses

The IRS has also provided further clarification on what constitutes eligible medical expenses under IRC § 213(d). Notably, the guidance confirms that expenses for over-the-counter medications and menstrual care products remain eligible for reimbursement from HSAs, FSAs, and HRAs without a prescription. This aligns with the provisions introduced under the CARES Act and provides continuity for employees managing their healthcare costs.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers should take immediate steps to communicate these changes to their employees. Updating employee handbooks and benefits communication materials is crucial to ensure that employees are aware of the new limits and eligible expenses. Additionally, HR departments should verify that their benefits administration systems are configured to accommodate the updated contribution limits.

### Considerations for Plan Design

Organizations may also want to review their HDHP offerings to ensure they remain competitive and attractive to employees. With the increased contribution limits, employees have greater flexibility in managing their healthcare expenses, which could influence plan selection during open enrollment periods.

The IRS's updated guidance on HSAs underscores the importance of keeping abreast of regulatory changes in benefits administration. By acting promptly, employers can ensure compliance and continue to offer valuable, tax-advantaged savings options to their workforce.
