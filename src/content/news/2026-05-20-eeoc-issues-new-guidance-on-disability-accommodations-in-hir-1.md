## EEOC Clarifies ADA Compliance for Hiring

On May 15, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance on accommodating disabilities during the hiring process under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). This guidance aims to ensure that employers do not inadvertently discriminate against applicants with disabilities and provides clear instructions on how to comply with ADA requirements.

## Key Points of the Guidance

The EEOC's guidance emphasizes that employers must provide reasonable accommodations to applicants with disabilities unless doing so would cause undue hardship. It clarifies that accommodations may include modifications to the application process, such as providing materials in accessible formats or allowing alternative methods for completing assessments.

Employers are reminded that they cannot ask disability-related questions or require medical examinations before making a conditional job offer. The guidance also highlights the importance of engaging in an interactive process with applicants to determine appropriate accommodations.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their hiring policies and procedures to ensure compliance with the new EEOC guidance. Specifically, they should:

1. **Audit Application Processes:** Examine current application methods to identify any barriers for applicants with disabilities. Ensure materials are available in accessible formats.

2. **Train Hiring Managers:** Conduct training sessions for HR staff and hiring managers on ADA requirements and the importance of the interactive process.

3. **Update Job Descriptions:** Clearly define essential job functions in job descriptions to facilitate discussions about potential accommodations.

4. **Review Pre-Offer Practices:** Confirm that no disability-related inquiries or medical exams occur before a job offer is made.

5. **Document Accommodation Efforts:** Maintain records of all accommodation requests and the steps taken to address them.

## Legal Framework

The ADA, codified at 42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq., prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities in all areas of public life, including jobs. The EEOC's role is to enforce these laws and provide guidance to help employers comply.

## Conclusion

By following the EEOC's latest guidance, employers can better accommodate applicants with disabilities and avoid potential legal pitfalls. This proactive approach not only ensures compliance but also fosters a more inclusive workplace environment.
