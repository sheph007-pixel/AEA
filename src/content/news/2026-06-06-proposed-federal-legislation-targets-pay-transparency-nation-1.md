## Federal Pay Transparency Bill Introduced

On May 30, 2026, the U.S. House of Representatives introduced the "Fair Pay Act," a bill that would require all employers nationwide to include salary ranges in job postings. This legislation, if enacted, would standardize pay transparency practices across the country, which are currently governed by a patchwork of state and local laws.

The bill aims to address wage disparities and promote equitable pay practices by ensuring prospective employees have access to salary information before applying. This federal move follows the lead of states like California and New York, which have implemented similar requirements in recent years.

## Impact on Employers

Should the Fair Pay Act pass, all employers with 15 or more employees will need to disclose a "good faith" salary range for each job posting. The bill defines "good faith" as the salary range the employer genuinely expects to pay for the position. Failure to comply could result in penalties enforced by the Department of Labor (DOL), which would oversee the implementation and enforcement of the law.

Employers will need to review their current job posting practices to ensure compliance. This could involve auditing current salary structures and developing clear guidelines for setting and communicating pay ranges.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Current Job Postings:** Review existing job advertisements to identify any that lack salary information. Prepare to update these postings if the bill passes.

2. **Evaluate Compensation Structures:** Assess current pay scales to ensure they align with the "good faith" requirement. Consider conducting a compensation analysis to identify any existing pay disparities.

3. **Develop Compliance Processes:** Establish internal processes for setting and approving salary ranges in job postings. This may involve training HR personnel on the new requirements.

4. **Monitor Legislative Developments:** Stay updated on the progress of the Fair Pay Act through Congress. Be prepared to implement changes quickly if the bill becomes law.

## Broader Implications

The introduction of the Fair Pay Act reflects a growing trend towards increased pay transparency and equity in the workplace. For employers, this could mean heightened scrutiny of pay practices and a push towards more standardized compensation policies.

While the bill is still in the early stages of the legislative process, its passage could lead to significant changes in how companies advertise jobs and manage compensation. Employers should begin preparations now to ensure a smooth transition should the bill become law.
