## NLRB Expands Joint Employer Criteria

On May 30, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a decision that broadens the criteria for determining joint employer status. This ruling revises the standard set by the NLRB in 2020, potentially increasing liability and bargaining obligations for many businesses.

Under the new ruling, companies can be considered joint employers if they have indirect control over the essential terms and conditions of employment or possess the authority to exercise such control. This represents a shift from the previous standard, which required direct and immediate control over workers' essential terms and conditions.

## Impact on Employers

The expanded definition means that more businesses, particularly those using staffing agencies or subcontractors, could be classified as joint employers. This classification can lead to increased responsibilities, including collective bargaining obligations and liability for unfair labor practices committed by the other employer.

Specifically, businesses that engage in temporary staffing arrangements, franchise operations, or outsourcing may need to reassess their labor practices. The decision could affect contractual relationships and necessitate adjustments in how companies structure their employment agreements.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should promptly review their current contracts and relationships with third-party labor providers. Key steps include:

1. **Audit Contracts**: Examine existing contracts with staffing agencies and subcontractors for language that could imply indirect control over employment terms.
   
2. **Review Policies**: Ensure that policies and practices do not inadvertently assert control over another entity's employees.

3. **Legal Consultation**: Engage with legal counsel to understand the implications of this ruling on your business operations and to explore potential adjustments to mitigate risks.

4. **Training**: Train managers and HR personnel on the implications of the new joint employer standard to prevent actions that could trigger joint employer liability.

The NLRB's decision underscores the importance of carefully managing relationships with third-party labor providers. Employers must act swiftly to understand and adapt to these changes to avoid unintended liabilities and ensure compliance with labor laws.
