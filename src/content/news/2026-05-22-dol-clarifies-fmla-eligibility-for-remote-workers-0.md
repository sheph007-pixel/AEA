## FMLA Eligibility Clarification for Remote Workers

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) issued a field assistance bulletin on May 15, 2026, clarifying how employers should determine Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) eligibility for remote workers. This guidance addresses confusion surrounding the 12-month employment requirement and the 1,250 hours of service threshold for employees working from home.

### Key Points of the Guidance

According to the FMLA (29 U.S.C. § 2611), employees must have worked for the employer for at least 12 months and completed 1,250 hours of service in the past year to qualify for leave. The new DOL bulletin specifies that hours worked remotely count towards this 1,250-hour threshold, provided they are compensable under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).

Furthermore, the guidance clarifies that the "worksite" for remote employees is the office to which they report or from which their assignments are made. This determination is crucial for assessing whether the employer meets the FMLA's 50-employee threshold within a 75-mile radius.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their current FMLA policies to ensure compliance with the new guidance. Specifically, they should:

1. **Audit Remote Work Hours**: Verify that remote employees' hours are accurately tracked and recorded to meet the 1,250-hour requirement.
   
2. **Identify Employee Worksites**: Clearly define the worksite for each remote employee to determine FMLA applicability based on the 50-employee threshold.

3. **Update Employee Handbooks**: Reflect these clarifications in employee handbooks and communicate changes to all staff, ensuring they understand how FMLA eligibility is determined in a remote work context.

4. **Train HR Staff**: Provide training for HR personnel to accurately assess FMLA eligibility for remote workers, including understanding the implications of the worksite definition.

### Implications for Employers

This bulletin is part of the DOL's ongoing effort to adapt employment laws to the realities of remote work. By clarifying these criteria, the DOL aims to prevent disputes and ensure consistent application of FMLA rights across different work environments. Employers must act promptly to align their practices with this new guidance to avoid potential compliance issues.

Failure to accurately determine FMLA eligibility could result in legal challenges and penalties. Therefore, proactive adjustments and clear communication with employees are essential steps for maintaining compliance and supporting employee rights under the FMLA.
