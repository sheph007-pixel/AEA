## SEC Cybersecurity Disclosure Rule Takes Effect

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has implemented a new rule effective July 1, 2026, requiring public companies to disclose material cybersecurity incidents within four business days. This rule, codified under Regulation S-K Item 106, aims to enhance transparency and protect investors by ensuring timely disclosure of cybersecurity risks and incidents.

The rule mandates that public companies must report any cybersecurity incident deemed material, meaning it could affect an investor's decision-making. This includes unauthorized access, data breaches, and significant service interruptions. The disclosure must be made in the company's Form 8-K, a report that keeps shareholders informed of major events.

## Implications for Employers

The new rule emphasizes the importance of robust cybersecurity measures and incident response plans. Employers must promptly assess incidents to determine materiality and ensure timely disclosure. This requires close collaboration between IT, legal, and compliance teams to evaluate the impact of any cybersecurity event.

Failure to comply with the disclosure requirements can result in SEC enforcement actions, including fines and other penalties. Public companies should review and possibly update their cybersecurity policies and incident response protocols to align with the new requirements.

## Action Items for Companies

1. **Review Incident Response Plans**: Ensure your company has a comprehensive incident response plan that includes criteria for determining the materiality of cybersecurity incidents.

2. **Train Key Personnel**: Conduct training sessions for IT, legal, and compliance teams to familiarize them with the new disclosure requirements and the process for evaluating incidents.

3. **Enhance Cybersecurity Measures**: Strengthen cybersecurity defenses to prevent incidents from occurring. This includes regular security audits, employee training, and implementing advanced security technologies.

4. **Establish a Disclosure Committee**: Form a committee responsible for assessing and deciding on the disclosure of cybersecurity incidents. This committee should include members from IT, legal, compliance, and executive management.

5. **Update SEC Filings**: Ensure that your company's SEC filings reflect the new disclosure requirements. This may involve revising risk factor disclosures related to cybersecurity risks.

By adhering to these steps, companies can better manage cybersecurity risks and ensure compliance with the SEC's new disclosure rule. As cybersecurity threats continue to evolve, maintaining transparency and protecting investor interests remain paramount.
