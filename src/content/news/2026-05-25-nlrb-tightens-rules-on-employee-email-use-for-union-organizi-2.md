## NLRB Decision Restricts Email Use

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a new ruling on May 20, 2026, significantly restricting employees' rights to use company email systems for union organizing activities. This decision reverses a 2014 precedent set in the Purple Communications case, which allowed employees at companies with email systems to use those systems for union communications during nonworking time.

The case, known as XYZ Corp., 375 NLRB No. 1 (2026), reaffirms employers' rights to maintain control over their email systems, provided they do not discriminate against union-related communications. The ruling emphasizes that employees do not have a statutory right to use employer email for Section 7 activities under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) unless email is the only reasonable means for communication.

## Implications for Employers

Employers should review and, if necessary, update their electronic communication policies to align with this ruling. Ensure that policies are neutral and do not single out union activities for different treatment compared to other non-business communications. This ruling provides more leeway for employers to enforce restrictions on email use, but policies must be applied consistently to avoid claims of discrimination.

Employers should also consider conducting training sessions to educate management and HR teams on the implications of this decision, ensuring compliance with the new standards. Additionally, it's advisable to document any policy changes and communications to employees regarding these updates to mitigate potential legal risks.

## Action Items

1. **Review Email Policies:** Audit current email and electronic communication policies to ensure they comply with the new NLRB ruling. Confirm that restrictions are applied uniformly and do not target union activities specifically.
   
2. **Policy Updates:** If changes are necessary, update policies to reflect the company's stance on non-business use of email systems, ensuring no discrimination against union-related communications.

3. **Training and Communication:** Organize training for HR and management to familiarize them with the ruling's implications. Clearly communicate any policy changes to employees, emphasizing the importance of non-discriminatory application.

4. **Documentation:** Keep thorough records of policy updates and communications with employees to protect against potential legal challenges.

This NLRB decision marks a pivotal shift in the landscape of employee rights related to communication and union organizing, reinforcing employer control over company resources while mandating non-discriminatory application of communication policies.
