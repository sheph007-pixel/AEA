## IRS Issues Guidance on Remote Work Reimbursements

On May 30, 2026, the IRS released guidance clarifying the tax treatment of reimbursements for remote work expenses. This guidance is crucial for employers managing the financial aspects of remote work arrangements. It addresses the conditions under which reimbursements for home office expenses can be considered non-taxable.

The IRS guidance specifies that reimbursements for business expenses are non-taxable if they meet the requirements of the accountable plan under 26 U.S.C. § 62(c). To qualify, expenses must have a business connection, employees must substantiate the expenses, and employees must return any excess reimbursement within a reasonable period.

## Key Points for Employers

Employers need to ensure that their reimbursement policies align with the IRS's accountable plan requirements. Failure to comply could result in reimbursements being classified as taxable income, subject to payroll taxes.

The guidance highlights common remote work expenses, such as internet service, office supplies, and equipment. Employers should evaluate which expenses are necessary for employees to perform their duties effectively from home.

## Action Items

1. **Review and Amend Reimbursement Policies**: Employers should audit their current reimbursement policies to ensure compliance with the IRS's accountable plan requirements. Consider amending policies to explicitly state the types of expenses covered and the documentation required.

2. **Employee Communication**: Clearly communicate any policy changes to employees. Provide guidance on how to substantiate expenses and the process for submitting reimbursement requests.

3. **Recordkeeping**: Implement robust recordkeeping practices to substantiate business expenses. Maintain documentation of all reimbursements and ensure employees submit receipts and other proof of expenses.

4. **Training for HR and Payroll**: Conduct training sessions for HR and payroll staff to familiarize them with the new guidance and ensure accurate processing of reimbursements.

## Looking Ahead

As remote work continues to evolve, staying informed of tax implications is critical. Employers should monitor any further IRS announcements or changes in tax law that could impact remote work arrangements. Adhering to the IRS guidance will help avoid potential tax liabilities and ensure compliance with federal tax regulations.
