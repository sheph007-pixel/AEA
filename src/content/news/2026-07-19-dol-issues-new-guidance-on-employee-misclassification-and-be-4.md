## New DOL Guidance on Misclassification

The Department of Labor (DOL) released guidance on July 15, 2026, addressing the implications of employee misclassification on benefits eligibility under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). This guidance aims to clarify how misclassifying workers as independent contractors can affect their access to benefits such as health insurance, retirement plans, and other employee benefits.

## Implications for Employers

Misclassification can lead to significant legal and financial consequences for employers. Workers classified as independent contractors are not eligible for benefits under ERISA-covered plans. The DOL guidance emphasizes that employers must ensure proper classification to avoid penalties and potential litigation.

The guidance highlights the importance of using the "economic realities" test to determine employee status. This test considers factors such as the degree of control the employer has over the worker and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should conduct an immediate audit of their workforce classification practices. This includes reviewing contracts and job descriptions to ensure alignment with the economic realities test. Employers should also:

- Verify that all workers classified as independent contractors genuinely meet the criteria.
- Reassess benefit eligibility for any workers reclassified as employees.
- Update internal policies and training programs to reflect the new guidance.

## Legal and Financial Risks

Failure to comply with the DOL's guidance can result in back payments for benefits, taxes, and penalties. Employers may also face lawsuits from misclassified workers seeking benefits and protections owed to employees.

The DOL's guidance serves as a reminder of the importance of accurate worker classification. Employers should work closely with legal counsel to navigate these complexities and ensure compliance.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new guidance on misclassification underscores the critical link between proper worker classification and benefits eligibility. Employers must take proactive steps to review and correct their classification practices to mitigate risks and ensure compliance with federal laws.
