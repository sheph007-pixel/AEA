## DOL's New Remote Work Safety Rule

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a new rule on July 20, 2026, requiring employers to implement safety standards for remote work environments by January 1, 2027. This rule, under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSHA), aims to address the growing number of employees working remotely and ensure their safety and health are adequately protected.

## Key Provisions

The rule mandates that employers conduct risk assessments of remote work sites, which can be done virtually, to identify potential hazards. Employers must then develop a safety plan that addresses identified risks, such as ergonomic issues, electrical safety, and emergency procedures.

Employers with 15 or more employees must provide training on safe remote work practices and document compliance with the new rule. Failure to comply could result in penalties under OSHA's general duty clause (29 U.S.C. § 654).

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Conduct Virtual Risk Assessments:** Employers should begin assessing remote workspaces to identify potential hazards. This can be done through virtual tours or employee self-assessments guided by employer-provided checklists.

2. **Develop a Safety Plan:** Create a comprehensive plan addressing identified risks. This plan should include ergonomic guidelines, emergency response protocols, and electrical safety measures.

3. **Implement Training Programs:** Develop and deliver training programs focused on remote work safety. Ensure employees understand how to maintain a safe work environment and are aware of emergency procedures.

4. **Document Compliance:** Keep detailed records of risk assessments, safety plans, and training sessions. Documentation will be crucial in demonstrating compliance during potential OSHA inspections.

## Implications for Small Businesses

For businesses with fewer than 15 employees, the rule is less prescriptive but still requires a basic safety plan and encourages voluntary compliance with the risk assessment and training components. Small businesses should consider leveraging resources from industry associations or OSHA's consultation services to meet these requirements effectively.

## Preparing for the January 2027 Deadline

With the compliance deadline just months away, employers need to act swiftly to ensure their remote work policies align with the new DOL requirements. Engaging with safety consultants and utilizing OSHA resources can help streamline the process. Employers should also communicate with their remote workforce to gather feedback and ensure the safety measures are practical and effective.

This new rule reflects the DOL's commitment to adapting workplace safety standards to the changing work environment. As remote work becomes a permanent fixture in many industries, adhering to these guidelines will not only ensure compliance but also promote a safer and healthier workforce.
