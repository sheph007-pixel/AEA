## DOL Revises Tip Credit Regulations

The Department of Labor (DOL) has announced an update to the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) tip credit regulations, effective July 1, 2026. The new rule, published on May 15, 2026, modifies how employers can apply the tip credit toward meeting the federal minimum wage requirements for tipped employees. This change affects restaurants, bars, and other businesses employing tipped staff across the United States.

## Key Changes in the Regulation

Under the FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 203(m)), employers can pay tipped employees a lower direct cash wage, provided tips make up the difference to meet the federal minimum wage. The new rule clarifies and limits the use of the tip credit to time spent on tasks directly related to tipped duties.

The regulation introduces a "20% rule," stipulating that employers cannot apply the tip credit for time exceeding 20% of a workweek spent on tasks not generating tips. Previously, employers faced ambiguity regarding non-tipped duties, often leading to disputes and litigation.

Additionally, the DOL has specified that tasks supporting tipped work, such as cleaning tables or setting up dining areas, can be included under the tip credit, provided they do not exceed the 20% threshold of the workweek.

## Employer Action Items

Employers with tipped staff must adjust their payroll practices by July 1, 2026, to comply with the new rule. This involves:

1. **Reviewing Job Descriptions**: Clearly define tasks that qualify as tipped duties and those considered supporting or unrelated tasks.
   
2. **Tracking Work Hours**: Implement detailed time-tracking systems to ensure compliance with the 20% rule. This may involve modifying existing timekeeping software or adopting new solutions.

3. **Training Managers**: Educate managers and supervisors on the new requirements to prevent unintentional violations and ensure accurate task allocation.

4. **Updating Employee Handbooks**: Amend handbooks and policies to reflect the updated tip credit regulations and communicate these changes to employees.

## Implications for Employers

Failure to comply with the updated regulations could result in wage claims and penalties. Employers should conduct audits of their current practices and consult legal counsel to address potential compliance issues. The DOL's Wage and Hour Division will likely increase enforcement efforts following the rule's implementation, making proactive compliance essential.

In summary, the DOL's revision of the FLSA tip credit regulations demands immediate attention and action from employers in the hospitality and service industries. By preparing now, businesses can avoid costly legal challenges and ensure fair treatment of their tipped employees.
