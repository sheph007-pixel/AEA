## FTC Revises Green Guides

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) revised its Green Guides on June 15, 2026, impacting how businesses can market their products as environmentally friendly. The Green Guides, first issued in 1992 and last updated in 2012, provide guidance on how to avoid making misleading environmental claims under Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45). The recent updates aim to address the surge in eco-friendly marketing and ensure claims are substantiated and clear to consumers.

## Key Changes in the Guidelines

The updated Green Guides introduce stricter standards for terms like "recyclable," "compostable," and "carbon neutral." For instance, the term "recyclable" can only be used if recycling facilities are available to at least 75% of consumers or communities where the product is sold. Similarly, "compostable" claims must be backed by evidence that the entire product or package will break down into usable compost in a safe and timely manner, consistent with composting programs available to consumers.

The guidelines also address newer claims such as "carbon neutral." Businesses must now provide clear evidence of how carbon neutrality is achieved, including details of any carbon offset programs used. The FTC emphasizes that vague claims like "eco-friendly" or "green" are likely to be misleading without specific qualifying information.

## Implications for Employers and HR Professionals

Employers, particularly those in marketing and product development, must review their current marketing materials and claims to ensure compliance with the updated Green Guides. This is crucial to avoid potential FTC enforcement actions, which can include fines and corrective advertising orders.

HR professionals should collaborate with marketing and legal teams to provide training on the new guidelines. This training should cover how to substantiate environmental claims and the documentation required to support such claims. Additionally, any third-party certifications used in marketing materials should be verified for accuracy and relevance.

## Action Items for Businesses

1. **Audit Marketing Claims**: Conduct a comprehensive audit of all current marketing materials to identify any claims that may be impacted by the new guidelines.

2. **Substantiate Claims**: Gather and organize evidence to support any environmental claims, ensuring that documentation is readily accessible in the event of an FTC inquiry.

3. **Revise Marketing Strategies**: Update marketing strategies and materials to align with the new guidelines, including removing or modifying any claims that cannot be substantiated.

4. **Training and Compliance**: Implement training programs for marketing and product development teams to understand and apply the new guidelines effectively.

5. **Monitor Developments**: Stay informed about any further updates or clarifications from the FTC regarding environmental marketing claims.

By taking these steps, businesses can mitigate the risk of FTC scrutiny and maintain consumer trust in their environmental marketing efforts.
