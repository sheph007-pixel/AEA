## FTC Updates Green Guides for Environmental Marketing Claims

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) released updated guidelines on June 1, 2026, for businesses making environmental claims about their products. These revised "Green Guides" aim to combat misleading marketing practices and ensure that eco-friendly claims are clear and substantiated.

The FTC's Green Guides, first introduced in 1992, provide marketers with guidance on how to avoid making deceptive claims regarding the environmental attributes of their products. The latest update reflects growing consumer interest in sustainability and addresses new marketing trends that have emerged since the last revision in 2012.

### Key Changes and Implications

The updated Green Guides introduce stricter requirements for claims about recyclability, compostability, and carbon offsets. For instance, products labeled as "recyclable" must be collected and processed in a substantial majority of communities. Claims about "carbon neutrality" now require clear evidence of offsetting emissions within a reasonable time frame.

Additionally, the FTC has addressed emerging terms such as "biodegradable" and "renewable energy." Marketers must ensure that such claims are supported by scientific evidence and disclose any significant limitations.

### Employer Action Items

1. **Review Marketing Materials**: Companies should audit their current marketing materials to ensure compliance with the new guidelines. This includes reevaluating claims related to recyclability, biodegradability, and carbon neutrality.

2. **Substantiate Claims**: Businesses must gather and maintain documentation to substantiate any environmental claims. This includes scientific studies, certifications, and evidence of actual environmental benefits.

3. **Train Marketing Teams**: Educate marketing and sales teams on the updated guidelines to prevent the use of misleading or unsupported claims in promotional materials.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Consider seeking legal advice to navigate complex claims and ensure compliance with the FTC's standards.

Failure to comply with the updated Green Guides can result in enforcement actions by the FTC, including fines and corrective advertising requirements. As consumers become more environmentally conscious, transparent and accurate marketing will be crucial in maintaining trust and avoiding legal pitfalls.

By aligning marketing practices with the FTC's updated guidelines, businesses can effectively communicate their commitment to sustainability while minimizing the risk of regulatory scrutiny.
