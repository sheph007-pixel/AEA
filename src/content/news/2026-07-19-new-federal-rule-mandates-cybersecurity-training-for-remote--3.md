## New Cybersecurity Training Requirement

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has issued a new rule requiring employers to implement mandatory cybersecurity training for remote workers by January 1, 2027. This regulation, under the Cybersecurity Enhancement Act of 2024 (15 U.S.C. § 7401), aims to bolster defenses against rising cyber threats targeting remote work environments.

## Key Details

The DHS rule mandates that all businesses with remote employees provide annual cybersecurity training. The training must cover phishing awareness, secure password practices, and identifying suspicious network activities. This rule applies to any business with more than 10% of its workforce operating remotely, regardless of the company's size.

## Compliance Steps

Employers should begin by assessing their current cybersecurity training programs to ensure they meet the new requirements. If existing programs fall short, companies must develop or procure training modules that cover the specified topics. Additionally, employers must keep detailed records of training completion for each remote worker as part of compliance verification.

## Action Items

- **Audit Existing Training**: Review current cybersecurity training programs to identify gaps relative to DHS requirements.
- **Develop New Training Modules**: Create or update training materials to include phishing, password security, and network monitoring.
- **Implement a Tracking System**: Establish a system to document employee participation and completion of the required training.
- **Schedule Annual Training**: Plan for regular updates and annual training sessions to maintain compliance.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the new DHS rule could result in fines up to $50,000 per infraction. Businesses should prioritize compliance to avoid these penalties and reduce the risk of cyber incidents.

## Conclusion

With the deadline approaching, businesses must act swiftly to align their cybersecurity training with federal mandates. By doing so, they not only comply with the law but also enhance their overall security posture, safeguarding both company and employee data in the increasingly digital working environment.
