## OSHA's New Rule on Workplace Violence Prevention

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has issued a new rule requiring healthcare and social service employers to implement workplace violence prevention programs by January 1, 2027. This rule, published on June 15, 2026, aims to address the increasing incidents of workplace violence in these sectors, which have higher rates of such incidents compared to other industries.

Under the new rule, covered employers must develop and implement a comprehensive workplace violence prevention plan. The plan should include procedures for identifying and assessing workplace violence hazards, implementing measures to prevent and control these hazards, and providing training to employees on recognizing and responding to workplace violence.

## Key Requirements and Employer Actions

According to the rule, employers must conduct an annual assessment of their workplace to identify potential violence risks. This assessment should consider factors such as the layout of the workplace, staffing levels, and the presence of security personnel. Employers are required to document these assessments and any measures taken to mitigate identified risks.

Training is another critical component of the rule. Employers must ensure that all employees receive initial and annual training on the workplace violence prevention plan. This training should cover techniques to de-escalate potentially violent situations, how to report incidents, and the employer's procedures for responding to workplace violence.

Employers are also required to establish a system for employees to report workplace violence incidents promptly. This system should ensure that employees can report incidents without fear of retaliation. Additionally, employers must keep records of all reported incidents and the actions taken in response.

## Compliance and Penalties

Healthcare and social service employers should begin preparing to comply with the new OSHA rule immediately. Failure to comply by the January 2027 deadline could result in significant penalties. OSHA has the authority to issue fines for non-compliance, and these fines can be substantial, especially for repeated violations.

To ensure compliance, employers should review their current workplace violence policies and procedures. If necessary, they should revise these policies to meet the new OSHA requirements. Employers may also consider consulting with legal or safety experts to ensure their plans are comprehensive and effective.

In summary, the new OSHA rule on workplace violence prevention represents a significant shift in regulatory expectations for healthcare and social service employers. By taking proactive steps to develop and implement a compliant workplace violence prevention plan, employers can not only avoid penalties but also create a safer work environment for their employees.
