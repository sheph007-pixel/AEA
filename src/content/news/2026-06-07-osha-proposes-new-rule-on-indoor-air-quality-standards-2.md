## OSHA Proposes Indoor Air Quality Standards

On June 5, 2026, the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) published a proposed rule to establish mandatory indoor air quality standards in workplaces. This proposal aims to address concerns about air quality and its impact on employee health and productivity. The rule, if enacted, would set specific ventilation and air quality requirements for employers across various industries.

## Key Provisions of the Proposed Rule

The proposed regulation would require employers to maintain indoor air quality at levels that minimize airborne contaminants. Key provisions include:

- **Ventilation Requirements**: Employers must ensure that ventilation systems meet specified air exchange rates to reduce pollutant levels.
- **Air Quality Monitoring**: Regular monitoring and documentation of air quality, including measuring levels of carbon dioxide, volatile organic compounds, and particulate matter.
- **Corrective Actions**: Employers must take timely corrective actions if air quality falls below the prescribed standards.
- **Employee Training**: Mandated training for employees on recognizing symptoms of poor air quality and reporting procedures.

## Impact on Employers

If finalized, this rule would impact employers in sectors such as manufacturing, healthcare, and office environments. Employers would need to invest in upgrading HVAC systems, conduct regular air quality assessments, and implement training programs.

## Compliance Timeline and Action Items

OSHA has opened a 60-day public comment period ending on August 4, 2026. Employers should review the proposed rule and consider submitting feedback. If the rule is enacted, compliance will be mandatory within one year of the final rule's publication.

Action items for employers include:

1. **Evaluate Current Systems**: Assess existing ventilation systems and identify necessary upgrades.
2. **Plan for Monitoring**: Develop a strategy for regular air quality monitoring and documentation.
3. **Budget for Compliance**: Allocate resources for potential system upgrades and employee training programs.
4. **Engage with OSHA**: Participate in the public comment process to voice concerns or support for specific provisions.

By proactively addressing these areas, employers can better prepare for the potential changes and ensure a healthier workplace environment.
