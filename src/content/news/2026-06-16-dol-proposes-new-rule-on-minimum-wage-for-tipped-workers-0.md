## DOL Proposes New Rule on Minimum Wage for Tipped Workers

On June 14, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule that could significantly impact the minimum wage for tipped workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The proposed rule aims to modify the tip credit provisions, which allow employers to pay tipped employees a lower direct cash wage if their tips make up the difference to the federal minimum wage.

### Key Changes Proposed

The DOL's proposal seeks to tighten the conditions under which employers can apply the tip credit. Specifically, the rule would require employers to ensure that tipped employees spend at least 80% of their workweek performing duties that directly generate tips. This marks a shift from the previous guidance that allowed for more flexibility in the tasks that could be counted towards the tip credit.

Additionally, the rule proposes that any non-tipped duties must be incidental to tipped duties and limited to no more than 20% of the employee's total hours worked in a workweek. This change is designed to prevent employers from relying too heavily on tipped employees for non-tipped work while still benefiting from the tip credit.

### Implications for Employers

Employers in the hospitality and service industries, where tipping is common, will need to closely monitor the tasks assigned to tipped employees. The proposed rule necessitates clear documentation of the time employees spend on tipped versus non-tipped duties to ensure compliance.

Failure to adhere to these requirements could result in employers being required to pay the full federal minimum wage, currently set at $7.25 per hour, for all hours worked by tipped employees, regardless of the tips received.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Job Descriptions:** Ensure that job descriptions for tipped positions clearly delineate tipped duties from non-tipped duties.

2. **Implement Time-Tracking Systems:** Consider adopting or updating time-tracking systems to accurately record the time spent on tipped and non-tipped duties.

3. **Train Managers and Supervisors:** Educate those in charge of scheduling and assigning tasks to understand and comply with the proposed rule's requirements.

4. **Prepare for Documentation:** Develop a system for maintaining records that demonstrate compliance with the 80/20 rule regarding tipped and non-tipped duties.

### Next Steps

The proposed rule is open for public comment until August 15, 2026. Employers are encouraged to review the proposal and submit any comments or concerns to the DOL before the deadline. The feedback received during this period will influence the final rule, which could take effect later this year or in early 2027.

Staying informed about these developments is crucial for businesses relying on tipped workers. Taking proactive steps now will help ensure compliance and minimize the risk of costly wage disputes in the future.
