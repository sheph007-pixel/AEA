## OSHA Mandates Enhanced Injury Reporting

On June 15, 2026, the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) finalized a new rule requiring employers to electronically report detailed information on workplace injuries and illnesses. This rule, effective January 1, 2027, affects establishments with 100 or more employees in certain high-risk industries.

## Key Requirements

Under the new rule, covered employers must submit Form 300, Log of Work-Related Injuries and Illnesses, and Form 301, Injury and Illness Incident Report, electronically to OSHA. Previously, only Form 300A, Summary of Work-Related Injuries and Illnesses, was required. This change aims to improve data accuracy and enhance OSHA's ability to identify workplace hazards.

## Affected Industries

The rule targets specific high-risk industries such as manufacturing, construction, and healthcare. These sectors have historically reported higher rates of workplace injuries and illnesses. Employers in these industries should prepare for increased scrutiny and potential inspections based on the data submitted.

## Employer Action Items

1. **Audit Current Records**: Employers should review their current injury and illness records for accuracy. Ensure that all incidents are properly logged and categorized according to OSHA standards.

2. **Update Reporting Systems**: Implement or upgrade electronic reporting systems to ensure timely and accurate submission of Forms 300 and 301 starting January 1, 2027.

3. **Train HR and Safety Personnel**: Conduct training sessions for HR and safety teams on the new reporting requirements to ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties.

4. **Review Injury Prevention Programs**: Evaluate and enhance existing workplace safety programs to reduce the incidence of reportable injuries and illnesses.

## Compliance and Penalties

Failure to comply with the new reporting requirements could result in penalties under 29 U.S.C. § 666. OSHA emphasizes the importance of accurate and timely reporting to improve workplace safety and reduce injury rates. Employers should prioritize compliance to avoid costly fines and reputational damage.

## Conclusion

This new OSHA rule represents a significant shift in workplace injury reporting standards. Employers in affected industries must take proactive steps to ensure compliance by the January 1, 2027 deadline. Enhanced electronic reporting will allow OSHA to better identify and address workplace hazards, ultimately improving safety for all employees.
