## DOL Clarifies Employee Classification for Remote Work

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on June 10, 2026, clarifying criteria for classifying employees in remote work arrangements under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule aims to address ambiguities in determining employee versus independent contractor status in the context of remote work, which has surged post-pandemic.

The rule emphasizes the "economic realities" test to assess whether a worker is economically dependent on the employer or is in business for themselves. This test considers factors such as the nature and degree of control over the work, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, and the permanence of the working relationship. The rule is codified in 29 C.F.R. Part 795.

## Key Implications for Employers

Employers must re-evaluate their classification of remote workers in light of these clarified criteria. Misclassification can lead to significant liabilities, including back wages and penalties under the FLSA. The rule does not alter the existing salary threshold for exempt employees, which remains at $684 per week (29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1)).

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Remote Work Agreements**: Examine existing contracts and job descriptions for remote workers to ensure alignment with the new rule. Adjust language to reflect the criteria outlined in the "economic realities" test.

2. **Conduct Classification Audits**: Perform audits of current remote worker classifications. Identify any positions that may require reclassification from independent contractor to employee status.

3. **Training for HR and Management**: Train HR personnel and managers on the new rule's criteria to ensure consistent application across the organization. Understanding the nuances of the "economic realities" test is crucial for compliance.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to review classification decisions and mitigate potential risks. Legal advisors can provide guidance on implementing changes and handling disputes.

## Looking Ahead

The DOL's clarification on remote work classification is a timely response to the evolving nature of work arrangements. Employers should act swiftly to align their practices with the new rule to avoid costly misclassification errors. As remote work continues to be a significant component of the workforce landscape, staying compliant will be essential for minimizing legal exposure and fostering a stable working environment.
