## Department of Labor Introduces Overtime Rule for Agricultural Workers

The Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule on May 5, 2026, mandating overtime pay for certain agricultural workers. This rule, effective July 1, 2026, aims to extend overtime protections under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) to this traditionally exempt sector.

## Key Provisions and Applicability

The rule applies to agricultural workers employed by large-scale farms with annual gross sales exceeding $1 million. These workers will now receive overtime pay for hours worked beyond 48 per week. The DOL estimates that this change will affect approximately 150,000 workers nationwide.

Previously, agricultural workers were generally exempt from overtime provisions under 29 U.S.C. § 213(b)(12). The new rule modifies this exemption, aligning with broader labor rights trends and increasing protections for workers in physically demanding jobs.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers in the agricultural sector must prepare for compliance by reviewing their payroll systems and employment contracts. Farms meeting the $1 million sales threshold should:

- **Audit Work Hours**: Track and document weekly hours to ensure compliance with the new overtime threshold.
- **Adjust Payroll Systems**: Update systems to calculate overtime pay for eligible workers starting July 1.
- **Revise Employment Contracts**: Amend contracts to reflect changes in overtime eligibility and compensation.
- **Train HR and Payroll Staff**: Ensure staff understand the new requirements and can effectively implement changes.

## Potential Challenges and Considerations

While the rule aims to improve worker conditions, employers may face challenges such as increased labor costs and administrative burdens. Some may need to reconsider staffing models or explore mechanization to offset additional expenses.

Employers should also monitor for potential state-level legislation that could impose stricter requirements or offer additional exemptions. States like California and Washington have historically enacted more stringent labor laws that could impact compliance strategies.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new overtime rule for agricultural workers marks a significant shift in employment law, reflecting growing attention to labor rights in agriculture. Employers must act promptly to ensure compliance by the July 1 deadline, balancing operational needs with legal obligations. This development underscores the importance of staying informed about regulatory changes affecting workforce management.
