## Enhanced Fee Disclosure Requirements

On July 1, 2026, the Department of Labor's (DOL) new rule on retirement plan fee disclosures will take effect, impacting employers offering retirement plans such as 401(k)s. The rule, issued under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA), requires plan administrators to provide more detailed information about fees and expenses to participants. The aim is to enhance transparency and empower employees to make informed decisions about their retirement savings.

## Key Provisions

The rule mandates that plan administrators disclose all direct and indirect compensation received by service providers in connection with plan services. This includes a breakdown of administrative fees, investment fees, and individual service fees. The disclosures must be presented in a format that is clear and understandable, ensuring that participants can easily compare costs associated with different investment options.

In addition, the rule requires that these disclosures be distributed annually and upon any significant change in plan fees. Failure to comply with these requirements could result in penalties under ERISA.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers must review and update their current fee disclosure processes to align with the new DOL requirements. This includes coordinating with plan administrators and service providers to ensure all necessary information is accurately captured and communicated to participants.

Employers should also consider conducting a comprehensive audit of their retirement plan offerings to evaluate fee structures and ensure competitiveness. This could involve benchmarking against industry standards and exploring opportunities to negotiate lower fees with service providers.

## Potential Impact

The enhanced fee disclosure requirements are expected to increase scrutiny on retirement plan costs, potentially leading to greater competition among service providers. Employers may see an increase in inquiries from employees seeking clarification on fees and investment options, necessitating additional resources for participant education and support.

Ultimately, the rule aims to foster a more transparent retirement planning environment, benefiting both employers and employees by promoting informed decision-making and potentially reducing overall plan costs.

Employers are encouraged to consult with legal and financial advisors to ensure full compliance with the new rule and to address any specific concerns related to their retirement plan offerings.
