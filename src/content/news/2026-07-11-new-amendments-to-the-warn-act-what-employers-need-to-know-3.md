## WARN Act Amendments Effective September 1, 2026

The Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act has been amended, with changes taking effect on September 1, 2026. These amendments alter the notification requirements for employers planning mass layoffs or plant closures. The changes aim to provide greater protection for workers while imposing new compliance obligations on businesses.

## Key Changes to the Act

The amendments to the WARN Act, codified under 29 U.S.C. § 2101 et seq., include a reduction in the threshold for what constitutes a mass layoff. Previously, a mass layoff was defined as affecting 50 or more employees. The new threshold is 25 employees, significantly expanding the scope of the Act. Additionally, the notification period has been extended from 60 days to 75 days.

Another major change involves the definition of an "employment loss." Under the amended Act, any reduction in work hours by more than 50% for six months or longer is now considered an employment loss, triggering WARN Act requirements.

## Implications for Employers

Employers with 100 or more full-time employees must now provide a 75-day advance notice to affected workers and local government officials if they plan to lay off 25 or more employees. This change requires employers to reassess their layoff strategies and communication plans to ensure compliance with the new rules.

The amendments also increase penalties for non-compliance. Employers failing to provide adequate notice could face fines of up to $750 per day of violation, a significant increase from the previous $500 per day. This necessitates a review of current layoff notification procedures to avoid costly penalties.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Layoff Policies**: Employers should immediately review and update their layoff policies and procedures to ensure they align with the new requirements. This includes revising notification timelines and thresholds.

2. **Train HR Staff**: HR teams must be trained on the new requirements to effectively manage compliance and communication with affected employees.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the increased penalties, consulting with legal counsel to ensure full compliance with the amended WARN Act is advisable.

4. **Update Communication Plans**: Employers should update their internal and external communication plans to reflect the new 75-day notice period and expanded definitions of employment loss.

The amendments to the WARN Act reflect a significant shift in federal labor policy, emphasizing greater worker protection. Employers must act promptly to adapt to these changes and avoid potential penalties.
