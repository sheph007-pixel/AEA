## DOL Clarifies Wellness Program Incentives

On July 15, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance concerning employer-sponsored wellness programs and the use of incentives. This guidance aims to clarify compliance requirements under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA). The new guidance is crucial for employers who offer wellness programs as part of their benefits package.

## Key Provisions

The guidance reaffirms that wellness programs must be "voluntary" to comply with ADA and GINA. This means employers cannot mandate participation or penalize employees who choose not to participate. Additionally, incentives must not be so substantial as to be considered coercive. The DOL suggests that incentives should not exceed 30% of the total cost of self-only coverage under the employer's health plan, aligning with previous interpretations by the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC).

## Compliance Requirements

Employers must ensure that wellness programs do not require employees to undergo medical examinations or provide genetic information as a condition of participation. If a program does collect health information, it must be reasonably designed to promote health or prevent disease, and not be overly burdensome or a subterfuge for violating ADA or GINA.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their wellness programs to ensure compliance with the new DOL guidance. Specific steps include:

1. **Evaluate Incentives**: Confirm that incentives offered do not exceed 30% of the cost of self-only coverage to avoid being deemed coercive.
   
2. **Program Design Review**: Ensure that wellness programs are designed to genuinely promote health and are not overly burdensome.

3. **Privacy Protections**: Implement robust privacy protections for any health information collected, ensuring it is not used for discriminatory purposes.

4. **Voluntary Participation**: Clearly communicate to employees that participation in wellness programs is voluntary and that non-participation will not result in penalties.

## Conclusion

With this new guidance, the DOL seeks to balance the promotion of employee health through wellness programs with the protection of employee rights under ADA and GINA. Employers should take immediate steps to align their wellness programs with these guidelines to avoid potential legal challenges.
