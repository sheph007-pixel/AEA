## NLRB Revises Joint Employer Standard

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a final rule on July 16, 2026, revising the standard for determining joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This rule significantly impacts businesses that rely on staffing agencies, franchises, or subcontractors by broadening the circumstances under which they can be deemed joint employers.

Under the new rule, a company can be considered a joint employer if it has indirect control over essential employment terms, such as hiring, firing, discipline, supervision, and direction. This marks a departure from the previous standard, which required direct and immediate control.

### Key Details

The rule reinstates the broader joint employer standard that was in place prior to 2020. The NLRB emphasizes that even minimal indirect control or reserved authority to control essential terms of employment can establish joint employer status. This includes situations where a company has contractual rights to dictate terms but does not exercise those rights.

### Impact on Employers

Employers in industries heavily reliant on third-party labor sources, such as construction, hospitality, and logistics, need to reassess their contracts and operational practices. The broadened definition increases the likelihood of shared liability for unfair labor practices and collective bargaining obligations.

### Action Items

1. **Review Contracts**: Examine existing contracts with staffing agencies, franchisees, and subcontractors to identify clauses that may imply indirect control over employment terms.
   
2. **Adjust Practices**: Modify practices that could be construed as exercising control over another entity’s employees, such as setting work schedules or performance standards.

3. **Legal Consultation**: Consider consulting with labor law experts to evaluate potential liabilities and ensure compliance with the revised standard.

4. **Training**: Implement training for managers and HR personnel on the implications of the new rule and how to mitigate risks of being classified as a joint employer.

### Conclusion

The NLRB's revised joint employer rule demands immediate attention from employers to avoid unexpected liabilities. By proactively reviewing and adjusting business relationships and practices, companies can better navigate the complexities introduced by this regulatory change.
