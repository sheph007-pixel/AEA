## Federal Agencies Update Mental Health Parity Compliance Guidelines

On June 5, 2026, the Departments of Labor, Health and Human Services, and the Treasury jointly released updated guidance on the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA). This guidance aims to ensure that employer-sponsored health plans provide mental health and substance use disorder benefits on par with medical and surgical benefits. The MHPAEA prohibits plans from imposing less favorable benefit limitations on mental health services compared to other medical services.

### Key Changes and Requirements

The updated guidance clarifies several aspects of compliance, emphasizing non-quantitative treatment limitations (NQTLs). Employers must ensure that any restrictions on mental health benefits, such as prior authorization requirements or network adequacy, are comparable to those applied to medical benefits.

Additionally, the guidance introduces a new requirement for plans to perform and document comparative analyses of NQTLs. Employers must be able to demonstrate that the processes and strategies used to apply NQTLs to mental health benefits are comparable to, and no more stringent than, those applied to medical benefits.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Health Plans**: Employers should immediately review their health plan documents to ensure compliance with the new guidance. Pay particular attention to NQTLs and ensure that mental health benefits are not being limited more stringently than medical benefits.

2. **Conduct Comparative Analyses**: Prepare and document a comparative analysis of NQTLs as required by the updated guidance. This analysis should be readily available for federal agency review upon request.

3. **Consult Legal and Benefits Advisors**: Engage with legal counsel or benefits advisors to ensure that all aspects of the health plan comply with the MHPAEA as clarified by the new guidance.

4. **Communicate with Employees**: Inform employees about any changes to their mental health benefits and ensure they understand the parity requirements.

### Implications for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the MHPAEA can result in significant penalties. The Departments of Labor, Health and Human Services, and the Treasury have increased their enforcement efforts, and non-compliant plans may face audits and fines.

Employers should act promptly to align their health plans with the updated guidelines. By ensuring compliance, employers not only meet legal obligations but also support the mental well-being of their workforce, which can lead to increased productivity and employee satisfaction.

Stay tuned for further updates as federal agencies continue to refine and enforce mental health parity requirements.
