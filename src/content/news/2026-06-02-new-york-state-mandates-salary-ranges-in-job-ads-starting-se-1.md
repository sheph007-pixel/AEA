## New York Salary Transparency Law

New York's new salary transparency law, signed by Governor Kathy Hochul, takes effect September 17, 2026. This law mandates that employers with four or more employees include salary ranges in all job advertisements. The legislation, part of the New York Labor Law § 194-b, aims to promote pay equity and reduce gender and racial wage gaps.

## Key Requirements

Employers must list a minimum and maximum salary or hourly wage for each job, promotion, or transfer opportunity advertised. The range should reflect the employer's good faith belief of the compensation at the time of posting. This requirement applies to positions that can or will be performed, at least in part, in New York State.

## Impact on Employers

The law affects both in-state and out-of-state employers hiring for roles that can be performed remotely in New York. Failure to comply may result in penalties, including fines and potential civil action. The New York State Department of Labor (NYSDOL) is tasked with enforcing the regulation and may issue fines up to $1,000 for first-time violations, escalating to $3,000 for subsequent offenses.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Job Postings**: Review current and upcoming job postings to ensure compliance with the new salary range requirements.
   
2. **Update Internal Policies**: Develop or revise policies to standardize how salary ranges are determined and communicated.

3. **Train HR Staff**: Educate human resources and recruitment teams on the new requirements to ensure consistent application across all job postings.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Consider consulting with legal professionals to navigate any complexities related to multi-state postings and compliance.

## Broader Implications

This law aligns with a growing trend of salary transparency initiatives across the United States. Similar measures have been implemented in states like California and Colorado, reflecting a broader movement towards transparency in compensation practices. Employers nationwide should anticipate potential federal action or additional state legislation in the future, as transparency continues to be a focal point in employment law.

## Conclusion

Employers must act promptly to comply with New York's salary transparency law by the September deadline. This change not only ensures legal compliance but also positions companies as fair and equitable employers in a competitive job market.
