## The Proposal

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) that would formally expand the definition of "serious health condition" under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) to explicitly include a broader range of mental health conditions. While mental health conditions have long been eligible for FMLA leave when they meet the existing definition, the proposed rule would remove ambiguity and lower the documentation burden for employees seeking leave for conditions such as anxiety disorders, major depression, PTSD, and substance use disorders.

The comment period is open through June 30, 2026, and a final rule is expected later this year.

## Key Provisions

- **Explicit mental health coverage**: The proposal would add specific language confirming that mental health conditions qualify as serious health conditions without requiring inpatient care as a precondition
- **Streamlined certification**: Employers would be limited to requesting one medical certification per leave request for mental health conditions, rather than requiring periodic recertification during the leave period
- **Telehealth documentation**: Healthcare providers would be permitted to issue FMLA certifications based on telehealth appointments, reflecting current healthcare delivery practices
- **Intermittent leave clarity**: The proposal provides clearer guidance on intermittent leave for episodic mental health conditions, addressing a frequent source of employer-employee disputes

## What This Means for Employers

If finalized, the rule would affect employers with 50 or more employees. Key impacts include:

1. **Higher leave utilization**: Employers may see an increase in FMLA leave requests related to mental health, particularly for intermittent leave
2. **Reduced ability to challenge certifications**: The streamlined certification process would limit the information employers can request, potentially making it harder to identify misuse
3. **Policy updates required**: Employee handbooks and leave policies will need to be revised to reflect the new definitions and procedures
4. **Manager training**: Front-line managers will need updated training on how to handle mental health leave requests without inadvertently violating employee rights

## Recommended Actions

- **Review current FMLA policies** and identify areas that may need updating if the rule is finalized
- **Train HR staff and managers** on the proposed changes, emphasizing the importance of consistent and compliant handling of mental health leave requests
- **Evaluate your EAP and wellness programs** to ensure employees have access to mental health resources, which may help reduce extended leave durations
- **Consider submitting comments** during the public comment period if the proposed rule would significantly impact your operations
- **Consult legal counsel** to assess how the proposed changes interact with state-level leave laws, which may already provide broader protections
