## NLRB Revises Union Election Procedures

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a final rule on May 12, 2026, revising procedures for union representation elections. The rule, effective July 31, 2026, shortens the timeline for union elections, impacting how quickly employers must respond to union petitions.

The new rule modifies several key aspects of the election process under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). Notably, it reduces the time between the filing of a petition and the election date from a median of 23 days to as few as 14 days. This change requires employers to act swiftly in organizing their responses and communications.

## Key Changes and Implications

Under the revised procedures, employers must post a Notice of Petition for Election within two business days of receiving the petition, a reduction from the previous requirement of three days. Additionally, the pre-election hearing will now occur eight days after the petition filing, compared to the previous 13 days. Employers must be prepared to present all legal arguments and evidence at this hearing, as delays or continuances will be less likely to be granted.

The rule also alters the timeline for submitting the voter eligibility list, commonly known as the Excelsior list. Employers must now provide this list within five business days after the direction of election, down from seven days. The list must include names, job classifications, work locations, shifts, and contact information of eligible voters.

## Action Items for Employers

To comply with the new NLRB rule, employers should take several proactive steps:

1. **Review Union Avoidance Strategies**: Ensure that management is trained on lawful communication practices regarding unionization efforts. This includes understanding what constitutes unfair labor practices under the NLRA.

2. **Prepare for Accelerated Timelines**: Develop a rapid response plan for handling union petitions. This includes designating a response team and legal counsel who can act quickly when a petition is filed.

3. **Update Employee Records**: Maintain accurate and up-to-date employee records to quickly compile the Excelsior list within the shortened timeframe.

4. **Communicate with Employees**: Consider conducting informational meetings to discuss the implications of unionization and company policies. Ensure these communications comply with NLRA regulations to avoid allegations of coercion or intimidation.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's revised rule on union election procedures significantly accelerates the election process, requiring employers to be more agile and prepared. By understanding the changes and implementing strategic action plans, employers can effectively navigate the new landscape of union elections. Compliance with these new requirements is crucial to avoid legal challenges and ensure smooth operations during union organizing activities.
