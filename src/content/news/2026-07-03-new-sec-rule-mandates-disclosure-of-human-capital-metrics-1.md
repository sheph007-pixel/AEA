## SEC Mandates Human Capital Disclosures

On June 30, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) enacted a new rule requiring public companies to disclose specific human capital metrics in their annual reports. This development, under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, aims to enhance transparency regarding workforce management and corporate governance.

## Key Requirements

Under the new rule, companies must report on metrics such as workforce diversity, turnover rates, and employee training programs. The SEC's decision follows increasing investor demand for more detailed insights into how companies manage their human capital, which is considered a critical asset.

The rule does not prescribe a one-size-fits-all approach; instead, it allows companies to determine which metrics are most relevant to their business model and industry. However, it emphasizes the need for consistency and comparability in reporting to facilitate investor analysis.

## Implications for Employers

HR departments in public companies must now integrate these reporting requirements into their standard practices. This may involve collaborating with finance and legal teams to ensure accurate and comprehensive disclosures. Additionally, companies should prepare for potential scrutiny from investors and regulators regarding the reported data.

## Action Items for HR Professionals

1. **Review Current Metrics**: Assess existing data collection processes related to human capital metrics. Identify any gaps in current reporting and determine how to address them.

2. **Enhance Data Systems**: Invest in or upgrade HR information systems to ensure they can capture and report the necessary data accurately and efficiently.

3. **Collaborate Across Departments**: Work closely with finance and legal teams to align on reporting requirements and ensure compliance with SEC standards.

4. **Prepare for Investor Inquiries**: Develop a strategy for communicating human capital metrics to investors, focusing on transparency and the business impact of workforce management strategies.

This rule represents a significant shift in how companies report on their workforce, emphasizing the growing importance of human capital in corporate governance. As companies adapt to these new requirements, HR professionals will play a crucial role in ensuring compliance and leveraging these disclosures to enhance corporate reputation and investor relations.
