## EEOC's New Guidelines on AI Bias

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) issued new guidance on June 10, 2026, addressing artificial intelligence (AI) bias in recruitment processes. This guidance aims to prevent discriminatory practices in hiring and comes into effect on July 1, 2026. It impacts all employers using AI tools for recruitment, assessment, and selection processes.

## What the Guidance Entails

The EEOC's guidance clarifies how Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 applies to AI-driven recruitment. It emphasizes that employers must ensure their AI tools do not disproportionately screen out candidates based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin. This aligns with the EEOC's ongoing efforts to address emerging technologies in the workplace.

Employers are required to audit their AI systems to identify and mitigate potential biases. The guidance suggests regular reviews of AI algorithms and data sets to ensure compliance with anti-discrimination laws.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers using AI in their hiring processes should take immediate steps to comply with the new guidance. Key actions include:

1. **Conduct Audits**: Review and audit AI tools and algorithms used in recruitment for potential biases. This should be done regularly to ensure ongoing compliance.

2. **Training and Education**: Provide training for HR staff and decision-makers involved in recruitment to understand AI tools and their implications under Title VII.

3. **Vendor Accountability**: If using third-party AI solutions, ensure vendors comply with EEOC guidelines. Request documentation of bias testing and mitigation measures.

4. **Documentation**: Maintain thorough records of AI audits, adjustments made to algorithms, and training provided to staff. This documentation will be crucial in demonstrating compliance if questioned by the EEOC.

## Implications for Employers

Failure to comply with the new EEOC guidance could result in investigations and potential penalties for discriminatory practices. Employers should proactively address these requirements to avoid legal repercussions and promote fair hiring practices.

As AI continues to integrate into recruitment, staying abreast of regulatory changes is essential. The EEOC's guidance marks a significant step in ensuring AI technologies are used responsibly and equitably in the workplace.
