## DOL Tightens Mental Health Parity Compliance

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on June 30, 2026, mandating stricter compliance with the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA). This rule affects employers offering group health plans and requires them to ensure that mental health and substance use disorder benefits are not more restrictive than medical and surgical benefits. The new regulations, codified in 29 C.F.R. § 2590.712, aim to eliminate discrepancies in treatment limitations and financial requirements between mental health and physical health benefits.

## Key Changes and Requirements

The rule introduces more detailed reporting requirements for employers. Group health plans must now provide comparative analyses of the design and application of non-quantitative treatment limitations (NQTLs). This includes a thorough evaluation of factors, evidentiary standards, and processes used to apply these limitations. Employers are required to submit these analyses to the DOL upon request, ensuring transparency and accountability.

Additionally, the rule clarifies the criteria for determining "substantially all" and "predominant" as they relate to financial requirements and treatment limitations. This aims to prevent plans from imposing stricter limitations on mental health benefits compared to medical benefits.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should immediately review their group health plans to ensure compliance with the new rule. This includes conducting a comprehensive audit of mental health and substance use disorder benefits against the new standards. Employers must document their analyses and be prepared to provide this documentation to the DOL if requested.

HR professionals should collaborate with benefits administrators to update plan documents and ensure that all NQTLs are compliant. Training sessions for HR staff and benefits managers on the new requirements may also be necessary to facilitate smooth implementation.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the new rule could result in significant penalties. The DOL has emphasized its commitment to enforcing the MHPAEA, and non-compliant employers may face corrective actions, including fines and required plan amendments. Ensuring compliance not only avoids penalties but also supports the well-being of employees by providing equitable access to mental health care.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new rule on mental health parity compliance underscores the importance of equal treatment for mental health and medical benefits. Employers must take immediate steps to align their health plans with the new standards to avoid penalties and promote a healthier workplace. By adhering to these regulations, employers can ensure they are providing fair and equitable health benefits to their employees.
