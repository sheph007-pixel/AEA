## EEOC Issues New Guidance

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on May 20, 2026, regarding religious accommodations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. This update clarifies employers' obligations to accommodate employees' religious practices and beliefs, emphasizing the need for reasonable adjustments unless they cause undue hardship on the business.

## Key Details of the Guidance

The EEOC's guidance reaffirms that employers must accommodate employees' religious practices unless doing so imposes more than a de minimis cost or burden. This clarification aligns with the Supreme Court's interpretation in Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison, which defines "undue hardship" as anything more than minimal cost. The guidance provides examples of accommodations, such as flexible scheduling, voluntary shift substitutions, and modifications to workplace policies.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must evaluate requests for religious accommodations on a case-by-case basis. The EEOC emphasizes that blanket refusals are not acceptable. Instead, employers should engage in an interactive process with employees to identify potential accommodations. Failure to adequately consider religious accommodation requests can lead to claims of discrimination under Title VII.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Policies**: Audit existing policies to ensure they allow for religious accommodations. Amend policies that might inadvertently restrict religious practices.

2. **Train HR Staff**: Conduct training sessions for HR personnel and managers to recognize and handle accommodation requests appropriately.

3. **Document Processes**: Maintain thorough documentation of accommodation requests and the interactive process. This can serve as evidence of compliance in case of disputes.

4. **Evaluate Costs**: Assess the potential costs of accommodations to determine if they truly pose an undue hardship.

The EEOC's updated guidance serves as a reminder of the importance of fostering an inclusive workplace that respects religious diversity. Employers should take proactive steps to ensure compliance and avoid potential legal challenges.
