## Revised Tip Credit Regulations Announced

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has announced a new rule affecting the utilization of the tip credit under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), set to take effect on July 1, 2026. This change will impact employers in the hospitality and service sectors, where tipping is a common practice. The revised rule addresses the allocation of tip credits when employees perform both tipped and non-tipped duties, providing clarity on the 80/20 rule and introducing a new 30-minute threshold for non-tip-generating tasks.

## Key Changes and Implications

Under the FLSA, employers can pay tipped employees a lower direct wage and count tips toward meeting the federal minimum wage requirement, a practice known as taking a "tip credit." The new rule modifies the current interpretation of the 80/20 rule, which limits the amount of time tipped employees can spend on non-tip-producing duties while still allowing employers to claim the tip credit.

### 80/20 Rule Clarification

Previously, the 80/20 rule stipulated that tipped employees could spend no more than 20% of their workweek on non-tip-generating duties without losing the tip credit. The DOL's new rule maintains this percentage but adds clarity by specifying that non-tipped duties must be directly related to tipped work. Additionally, the rule introduces a 30-minute cap on continuous non-tipped duties, meaning that if an employee spends more than 30 consecutive minutes on non-tipped tasks, the employer cannot take the tip credit for that time.

### Employer Action Items

- **Review Job Duties**: Employers should audit the job duties of tipped employees to ensure compliance with the new rule. This includes distinguishing between tipped and non-tipped tasks and ensuring that non-tipped duties do not exceed 20% of the workweek or 30 continuous minutes.
- **Training and Communication**: Train managers and supervisors on the new requirements to avoid inadvertent violations. Clear communication with employees about their duties and time allocation is essential.
- **Payroll Adjustments**: Adjust payroll systems to accurately track and document the time spent on tipped versus non-tipped duties. This will be crucial in case of DOL audits or employee disputes.

## Legal and Financial Impact

Non-compliance with the revised tip credit rule could result in significant financial liabilities for employers, including back pay for employees and potential penalties. Employers should consult with legal counsel to ensure that their practices align with the new requirements and to mitigate risks associated with misclassification or improper wage calculations.

This regulatory update underscores the importance of meticulous record-keeping and proactive management of employee duties. As the July 2026 implementation date approaches, businesses in the affected sectors should act promptly to align their operations with the new DOL standards, ensuring both compliance and the continued benefit of the tip credit.
