## NLRB Seeks to Expedite Union Elections

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) proposed a rule on June 1, 2026, intended to streamline the process for union elections. This initiative, titled "Expedited Election Procedures," aims to reduce the time from petition to election, potentially affecting the way employers handle unionization efforts.

The rule seeks to amend existing procedures by shortening the timeline for pre-election hearings and limiting the scope of disputes that can delay elections. Under the proposed changes, employers would have less time to prepare for union elections, as the period between the filing of a petition and the election date could be reduced significantly.

## Key Changes Proposed

The proposed rule introduces several key changes:

1. **Pre-Election Hearings:** The timeline for pre-election hearings could be reduced to seven days from the current 14 days. This change is intended to expedite the process and limit delays caused by procedural challenges.

2. **Filing of Statements of Position:** Employers would be required to file their statements of position within five days of receiving the petition, a reduction from the current seven-day requirement.

3. **Post-Election Procedures:** The proposal also aims to streamline post-election procedures by limiting the grounds on which employers can challenge election results.

These changes are designed to facilitate quicker resolutions and minimize the potential for procedural delays that have historically extended the election process.

## Implications for Employers

If enacted, the proposed rule would require employers to adjust their strategies for handling union petitions. Companies may need to:

- **Enhance Preparedness:** With shorter timelines, employers should ensure they have robust procedures in place to address unionization efforts swiftly and effectively.

- **Legal Consultation:** Engaging with legal counsel early in the process can help navigate the expedited timelines and ensure compliance with the new requirements.

- **Employee Communication:** Employers should prioritize clear and consistent communication with employees regarding their rights and the implications of unionization.

## Next Steps

The NLRB is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until July 15, 2026. Employers and other stakeholders are encouraged to submit feedback during this period. After reviewing the comments, the NLRB will decide whether to implement the rule as proposed or make modifications.

Employers should stay alert to further developments and be prepared for potential changes that could impact their operations and strategies regarding unionization efforts.
