## FLSA Salary Basis Rule Revised for 2026

The Department of Labor (DOL) announced revisions to the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) salary basis rule, effective January 1, 2026. The changes affect the classification of exempt employees under 29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1). Employers must ensure compliance to avoid misclassification penalties.

### Key Changes

The revised rule increases the minimum salary threshold for exempt employees to $55,000 annually. This change marks a significant increase from the previous threshold of $35,568 per year. Employers must review and adjust the salaries of employees classified as exempt to meet the new requirement.

Additionally, the rule introduces a mechanism for automatic updates to the salary threshold every three years, based on wage growth data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. This aims to maintain the relevance of the salary basis test in line with economic changes.

### Employer Action Items

1. **Audit Current Employee Classifications**: Employers should conduct an immediate audit of employee classifications to ensure compliance with the new salary threshold. Employees earning below $55,000 annually must be reclassified as non-exempt or receive salary adjustments.

2. **Budget for Salary Increases**: Businesses need to budget for potential salary increases for employees who must be reclassified or receive raises to maintain their exempt status.

3. **Update Payroll Systems**: Ensure payroll systems are updated to reflect the new salary thresholds and automatic adjustment mechanism. This will help in maintaining compliance during the transition and beyond.

4. **Communicate Changes to Employees**: Clearly communicate any changes in classification or salary adjustments to affected employees. Transparency will help mitigate potential dissatisfaction or confusion.

### Legal Implications

Non-compliance with the revised FLSA salary basis rule can result in significant legal and financial repercussions. Misclassified employees may be entitled to back pay for overtime hours worked, along with potential penalties and legal fees.

Employers should consult with legal counsel or HR professionals to navigate these changes effectively. Ensuring proper classification and payroll adjustments will protect businesses from costly litigation and maintain compliance with federal labor laws.

The DOL's revision reflects an ongoing effort to align wage regulations with current economic conditions, emphasizing the importance of fair compensation practices. Employers must act promptly to adapt to these changes and safeguard their operations against potential liabilities.
