## SEC's New Climate Disclosure Rules

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) announced on May 15, 2026, that public companies must comply with new climate-related disclosure requirements by December 31, 2026. These rules, designed to increase transparency on environmental impact, will affect how companies report climate risks and greenhouse gas emissions in their filings.

The SEC's final rule, codified under 17 C.F.R. § 210.14-02, mandates that companies include detailed climate-related information in their annual reports. This includes the financial impact of climate-related risks, governance processes overseeing these risks, and Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions. Scope 3 emissions disclosures are required if material or if the company has set a target that includes them.

## Implications for Employers

For HR directors and in-house counsel, these rules mean immediate action is needed to ensure compliance. Companies must integrate climate risk assessments into their existing risk management frameworks. This may involve collaborating with environmental consultants to accurately measure emissions and assess potential impacts on financial performance.

Public companies should begin auditing their current reporting practices to identify gaps in compliance with the new SEC requirements. Establishing a cross-functional task force involving legal, environmental, and financial departments may streamline this process.

## Action Items

1. **Audit Current Disclosures**: Review current climate-related disclosures to ensure they meet the new SEC standards. Identify areas needing enhancement or additional data collection.

2. **Develop Emissions Reporting Protocols**: Implement systems to accurately track and report Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions. Consider the materiality of Scope 3 emissions for your business operations.

3. **Update Governance Frameworks**: Ensure that climate-related risks are integrated into your company’s risk management and governance processes. This includes board oversight and executive accountability for climate-related issues.

4. **Engage External Experts**: Consider hiring environmental consultants or legal advisors specializing in SEC compliance to assist in the transition and ensure all disclosures are accurate and comprehensive.

5. **Employee Training**: Educate employees involved in reporting and compliance on the new requirements. Regular training sessions can help maintain compliance and improve data accuracy.

The SEC's climate disclosure rules represent a significant shift in how public companies must account for environmental risks. By taking prompt action, companies can not only comply with regulatory requirements but also enhance their transparency and accountability to investors and the public.
