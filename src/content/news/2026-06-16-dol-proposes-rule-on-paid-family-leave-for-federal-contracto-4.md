## DOL Proposes Rule on Paid Family Leave for Federal Contractors

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has proposed a new rule that would require federal contractors to provide paid family leave to their employees. Announced on June 15, 2026, this proposed rule aims to align with the Biden administration's ongoing efforts to expand family leave benefits across the federal workforce. If enacted, the rule would affect approximately 4 million workers employed by federal contractors.

Under the proposed rule, federal contractors would be required to offer a minimum of 12 weeks of paid family leave to employees for reasons including the birth or adoption of a child, caring for a seriously ill family member, or addressing personal health issues. This aligns with the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) but extends paid benefits, which are not currently mandated under FMLA.

### Key Details

The proposed rule, under the authority of Executive Order 14045, would amend Title 29 of the Code of Federal Regulations. According to the DOL, the rule is designed to ensure that employees working on federal contracts have access to paid leave, thereby promoting workforce stability and employee well-being.

The proposal includes specific provisions for calculating leave pay, which would be based on the employee's average weekly earnings. The rule also stipulates that contractors must maintain health benefits during the leave period, similar to FMLA requirements.

### Action Items for Employers

Federal contractors should begin reviewing their current leave policies to assess compliance with the proposed rule. Key steps include:

1. **Policy Review**: Examine existing leave policies to identify gaps in compliance with the proposed 12-week paid leave requirement.
   
2. **Budget Assessment**: Analyze financial implications, including the potential impact on payroll and benefits costs.
   
3. **Employee Communication**: Prepare to update employee handbooks and communicate potential changes to staff.

4. **Feedback Submission**: The DOL has opened a 60-day public comment period, ending on August 14, 2026. Employers can submit feedback through the Federal eRulemaking Portal.

### Implications for Small Contractors

While larger federal contractors may already offer similar benefits, small contractors might face significant financial and administrative challenges. The DOL has indicated that it will consider these challenges in the final rule, potentially offering phased implementation or exemptions for smaller entities.

As the rule progresses, federal contractors should stay informed about updates and prepare for potential implementation. The final rule could be published as early as November 2026, with an effective date likely set for mid-2027.

This proposed rule represents a significant shift in benefits policy for federal contractors, reflecting broader trends toward expanding paid family leave across the U.S. workforce. Employers should take proactive measures to ensure readiness for compliance and to support their employees effectively.
