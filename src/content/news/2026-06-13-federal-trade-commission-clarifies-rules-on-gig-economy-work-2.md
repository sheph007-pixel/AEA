## FTC Clarifies Gig Worker Classification Rules

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) issued new guidance on June 10, 2026, clarifying the classification of gig economy workers under Section 5 of the FTC Act. This guidance aims to address ongoing confusion about whether gig workers should be classified as independent contractors or employees, impacting companies relying heavily on gig labor.

The FTC's clarification comes amid increasing scrutiny of gig economy practices and aligns with recent trends in labor rights advocacy. The guidance delineates specific criteria to determine worker classification, focusing on the degree of control a company exerts over a worker, the permanency of the relationship, and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss. These factors are intended to prevent misclassification and ensure workers receive appropriate protections and benefits.

## Implications for Employers

Employers in the gig economy must now closely evaluate their worker classification practices to ensure compliance with the new FTC guidelines. Misclassification can lead to significant penalties, including back pay for misclassified workers and fines for non-compliance.

The FTC's updated guidance emphasizes the importance of accurately assessing the level of independence afforded to gig workers. Companies must ensure that their contractual agreements and operational practices align with the new criteria to avoid potential legal challenges.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Contracts**: Employers should review existing contracts with gig workers to ensure they reflect the degree of independence required under the new guidelines.

2. **Audit Classification Practices**: Conduct an internal audit of current worker classifications to identify potential misclassifications. This may involve consulting with legal counsel to navigate complex classification issues.

3. **Training for Management**: Implement training programs for managers and HR personnel to understand the new classification criteria and apply them consistently across the organization.

4. **Adjust Operational Practices**: Modify operational practices to increase worker independence where necessary. This might involve changes to scheduling flexibility, the ability for workers to accept or decline assignments, and the provision of tools and resources.

By adhering to the FTC's new guidance, companies can mitigate the risk of legal action and foster a more compliant and ethical approach to gig worker management. The FTC's emphasis on clear classification criteria underscores the agency's commitment to ensuring fair treatment for gig economy workers while providing clarity for employers navigating these complex issues.
