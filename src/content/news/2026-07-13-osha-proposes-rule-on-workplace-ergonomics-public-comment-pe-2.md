## OSHA Proposes New Ergonomics Rule

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) proposed a new rule on July 10, 2026, targeting workplace ergonomics to reduce musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs). This initiative, if enacted, will affect employers across various industries by requiring them to implement ergonomics programs tailored to their specific workplace hazards.

## Details of the Proposed Rule

The proposed rule mandates that employers identify ergonomic hazards and implement controls to mitigate these risks. This includes conducting regular assessments, providing training to employees, and maintaining records of any MSDs. The rule aims to address the high incidence of MSDs, which are among the most common work-related injuries.

The proposal outlines specific requirements for industries with higher risks, such as manufacturing, healthcare, and warehousing. These sectors will need to adopt more stringent measures, including ergonomic evaluations of workstations and tasks, to ensure compliance with the new standards.

## Public Comment Period Open

OSHA has opened a 60-day public comment period, ending September 8, 2026. Employers, HR professionals, and other stakeholders are encouraged to submit feedback on the proposed rule. Comments can be submitted electronically via the Federal eRulemaking Portal or by mail.

## What Employers Need to Do

Employers should review the proposed rule and assess how their current practices align with the new requirements. It is advisable to start identifying potential ergonomic hazards within their workplaces and consider initial steps toward compliance, such as ergonomic assessments and employee training programs.

Additionally, participating in the public comment process can provide valuable input to OSHA and potentially influence the final rule. Employers can highlight any challenges or suggest modifications to the proposed requirements to better fit their operational realities.

## Potential Impact and Next Steps

If finalized, this rule could significantly impact how companies approach workplace safety, particularly in ergonomics. Employers might face increased compliance costs, but the potential reduction in MSD-related injuries could lead to long-term savings in workers' compensation and improved employee productivity.

After the comment period closes, OSHA will review the feedback and may revise the proposal before issuing a final rule. Employers should stay informed on developments and prepare for potential changes to their ergonomics policies and practices.

By proactively engaging with the proposed rule, employers can better position themselves to comply with new standards and enhance workplace safety.
