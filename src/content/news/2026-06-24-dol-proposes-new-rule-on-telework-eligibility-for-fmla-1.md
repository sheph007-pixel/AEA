## DOL Proposes New Rule on Telework Eligibility for FMLA

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 20, 2026, to clarify telework eligibility under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). The rule aims to address ambiguities in FMLA eligibility for remote employees, a growing concern as telework becomes more prevalent. The proposal seeks to ensure that remote workers have equal access to FMLA benefits as their on-site counterparts.

Under the current FMLA regulations (29 U.S.C. § 2611), employees are eligible for leave if they have worked for their employer for at least 12 months, have clocked at least 1,250 hours over the past 12 months, and work at a location where the company employs 50 or more employees within 75 miles. The proposed rule clarifies that for teleworkers, the "worksite" is the office to which they report, not their home.

### Implications for Employers

This clarification is significant for employers with a substantial remote workforce. It ensures that employees working from home are not unfairly excluded from FMLA coverage due to their physical distance from a central office. The rule also highlights the necessity for employers to maintain accurate records of remote employees' work hours and reporting structures.

Employers should review their current telework policies and ensure they align with the proposed FMLA eligibility criteria. This includes verifying that remote employees' work locations are correctly designated in employment records. Additionally, HR departments should prepare to update employee handbooks and training materials to reflect these changes once the rule is finalized.

### Action Items

- **Audit Employee Records**: Confirm that remote employees' official worksites are accurately recorded as their reporting office, not their home address.
- **Policy Update**: Amend telework policies to reflect the proposed FMLA eligibility criteria for remote employees.
- **Training**: Schedule training sessions for HR personnel to ensure understanding and compliance with the new rule once it is enacted.

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until August 19, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the comment process to voice any concerns or support for the proposed changes. The final rule is expected to be issued by early 2027, providing ample time for businesses to adjust their policies and practices accordingly.
