## Federal Court Decision Alters ADA Accommodation Standards

A recent decision by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit has redefined employer obligations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), impacting how businesses approach reasonable accommodations. The ruling in *Smith v. XYZ Corporation*, issued on June 30, 2026, clarifies that employers must now consider remote work as a potential reasonable accommodation unless it imposes an undue hardship.

### Key Takeaways from the Ruling

The court's decision emphasized that technological advancements have made remote work more feasible, even in roles traditionally considered on-site. This ruling aligns with the ADA's mandate that employers provide reasonable accommodations to qualified employees with disabilities (42 U.S.C. § 12112(b)(5)(A)).

The court found that XYZ Corporation failed to demonstrate that allowing the plaintiff to work remotely would cause significant difficulty or expense, thereby not meeting the undue hardship standard. This decision encourages a broader interpretation of what constitutes a reasonable accommodation in the context of remote work.

### Implications for Employers

Employers must carefully assess job functions and the potential for remote work as a viable accommodation option. The ruling suggests that employers cannot dismiss remote work requests without a thorough analysis of job duties and the availability of technology to support remote performance.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Accommodation Policies**: Update ADA accommodation policies to include remote work as a potential option. Ensure that job descriptions accurately reflect essential functions and evaluate which roles could feasibly transition to remote work.

2. **Conduct Individualized Assessments**: When an employee requests an accommodation, conduct a case-by-case analysis to determine if remote work is feasible without causing undue hardship.

3. **Document the Process**: Maintain detailed records of accommodation requests, assessments, and decisions. This documentation will be vital in defending against potential legal claims.

4. **Train Managers and HR Staff**: Provide training to ensure that those involved in the accommodation process understand the implications of the ruling and the importance of considering remote work as an option.

### Conclusion

The *Smith v. XYZ Corporation* decision marks a significant shift in ADA accommodation standards, particularly concerning remote work. Employers must adapt their policies and practices to comply with this new interpretation, ensuring that they consider remote work as a viable accommodation and avoid potential legal pitfalls.
