## NLRB Expands Joint Employer Standard

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a final rule on July 20, 2026, expanding the criteria for determining joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This change could significantly impact small to mid-sized businesses that rely on staffing agencies or subcontractors.

The rule reverts to a broader standard, similar to the one used prior to 2020, where indirect control over terms and conditions of employment can establish joint employer status. This includes decisions related to hiring, firing, discipline, supervision, and direction. Businesses that share or co-determine essential terms of employment with another entity may now be considered joint employers.

## What This Means for Employers

The expanded joint employer standard means that businesses could be jointly liable for unfair labor practices and collective bargaining obligations if they are deemed joint employers with staffing agencies or subcontractors. This change affects industries like hospitality, construction, and retail, where temporary staffing is common.

Employers need to assess their current contracts and relationships with third-party labor providers. If your business exerts indirect control or has reserved the right to control essential employment terms, you could be classified as a joint employer.

## Action Items for Businesses

1. **Review Contracts:** Examine contracts with staffing agencies and subcontractors to identify any clauses that could imply control over employment terms. Amend contracts to clarify roles and responsibilities.

2. **Evaluate Employment Policies:** Ensure that your company’s policies do not inadvertently exert control over third-party employees. This includes policies on scheduling, discipline, and work conditions.

3. **Training for Managers:** Educate managers and supervisors on the implications of the joint employer rule to prevent actions that could establish joint employer status.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Work with legal experts to understand the potential liabilities and to develop strategies to mitigate risks associated with the new standard.

## Looking Ahead

The NLRB's decision reflects a shift towards greater accountability for businesses that use flexible staffing models. While this rule aims to protect workers' rights, it also imposes new compliance challenges for businesses. By taking proactive steps, employers can better navigate the complexities of joint employer status and maintain compliance with the NLRA.
