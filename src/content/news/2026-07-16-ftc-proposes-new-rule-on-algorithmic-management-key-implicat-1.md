## FTC Proposes Rule on Algorithmic Management

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) proposed a new rule on July 10, 2026, addressing algorithmic management in workplaces. This proposal aims to regulate how employers use algorithms and artificial intelligence (AI) for monitoring and managing employees. The rule, if enacted, will apply to companies with 15 or more employees and is expected to take effect in mid-2027.

The FTC's proposal focuses on transparency and fairness in algorithmic decision-making processes. It requires employers to disclose the use of AI systems in workplace monitoring and management. This includes informing employees about the types of data collected, how it is used, and the potential impact on employment decisions such as promotions, terminations, and performance evaluations.

## Key Requirements for Employers

Under the proposed rule, employers must provide clear and accessible information to employees about the AI systems in use. This includes:

1. **Disclosure**: Employers must inform employees when algorithmic management tools are used, detailing what data is collected and how it influences employment decisions.
   
2. **Data Review**: Employees must have the right to review the data collected about them and request corrections if inaccuracies are found.

3. **Impact Assessments**: Employers must conduct regular impact assessments of their AI systems to ensure they do not result in discriminatory practices or biases. These assessments should be documented and made available to the FTC upon request.

4. **Third-Party Audits**: Companies using third-party AI tools must ensure these providers comply with the FTC's standards. This involves obtaining certifications from vendors that their systems meet the required transparency and fairness criteria.

## Implications for HR Professionals

HR professionals need to prepare for these changes by reviewing current AI and algorithmic management practices. Key action items include:

- **Audit Current Systems**: Evaluate existing AI tools used for employee monitoring and management. Ensure they meet the proposed transparency and fairness standards.
  
- **Develop Disclosure Protocols**: Create clear communication strategies to inform employees about AI usage in the workplace. This includes drafting clear and concise notices that comply with the proposed rule.

- **Train HR Teams**: Conduct training sessions for HR teams to understand the implications of the rule and how to implement necessary changes effectively.

- **Engage with Vendors**: Work closely with AI vendors to ensure their systems comply with the FTC's proposed standards. Consider renegotiating contracts to include compliance guarantees.

The FTC's proposed rule marks a significant shift in how algorithmic management is regulated in the workplace. Employers should begin preparations to ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties. The public comment period for the proposed rule is open until September 15, 2026, allowing stakeholders to provide feedback on the potential impacts and challenges of implementation.
