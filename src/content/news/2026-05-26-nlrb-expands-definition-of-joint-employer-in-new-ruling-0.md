## NLRB's Revised Joint Employer Standard

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a ruling on May 20, 2026, expanding the criteria for determining joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). The decision in Browning-Ferris Industries of California, Inc., revisited the joint employer standard, potentially affecting businesses that use staffing agencies, subcontractors, or franchise models.

## Broader Criteria for Joint Employer Status

The NLRB's ruling redefines the joint employer standard to include entities that have indirect control over essential employment terms or have reserved the right to exert such control. This marks a shift from the previous requirement for direct and immediate control. The decision underscores the importance of contractual language and actual practice in determining joint employer relationships.

## Implications for Employers

This ruling could significantly impact companies relying on third-party contractors or staffing agencies. Businesses may face increased liability for labor violations and collective bargaining obligations if deemed joint employers. The decision affects industries with complex supply chains or franchising models, where control over working conditions is shared or ambiguous.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Contracts**: Employers should scrutinize agreements with staffing agencies, subcontractors, and franchisees to assess potential joint employer risks. Focus on language that might imply control over employment terms such as hiring, discipline, or scheduling.

2. **Assess Control Practices**: Evaluate actual practices to ensure they align with contractual terms. Avoid exerting control over employment decisions that could trigger joint employer status.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal advisors to understand the implications of the NLRB's ruling on your business operations and to develop strategies for mitigating potential liabilities.

4. **Training and Compliance**: Train HR teams and managers on the implications of the expanded joint employer criteria to ensure compliance and minimize risks.

This NLRB ruling demands careful attention to how businesses structure and manage relationships with third-party labor providers. Proactive measures can help mitigate risks associated with the expanded joint employer definition.
