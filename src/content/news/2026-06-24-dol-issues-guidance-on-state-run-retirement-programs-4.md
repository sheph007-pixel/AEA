## DOL Clarifies Employer Obligations in State-Run Retirement Plans

On June 20, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) released new guidance detailing employer responsibilities under state-run retirement savings programs. This guidance affects businesses operating in states with mandated retirement savings plans, such as California, Illinois, and Oregon.

State-run retirement programs generally require employers without existing retirement plans to facilitate employee participation in state-managed Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs). The DOL's new guidance provides clarity on compliance expectations, particularly concerning employer registration, employee enrollment, and contribution processes.

### Key Points of the Guidance

The guidance emphasizes that employers must register with their state's retirement program by the deadlines specified by each state. Failure to comply can result in penalties. For example, California imposes penalties of up to $500 per eligible employee for non-compliance under its CalSavers program.

Employers are required to facilitate payroll deductions for employee contributions to these IRAs. However, the DOL clarified that employers are not fiduciaries of the state-run plans and are not responsible for managing the funds or making investment decisions.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Verify State Requirements:** Employers should confirm whether their state mandates participation in a retirement savings program and understand the specific obligations. This includes deadlines for registration and employee enrollment.

2. **Register Timely:** Businesses must ensure they register with the appropriate state program by the specified deadlines to avoid penalties.

3. **Set Up Payroll Deductions:** Employers need to establish systems to facilitate automatic payroll deductions for employee contributions to the state-run IRAs.

4. **Inform Employees:** Employers should communicate with employees about the program, explaining how contributions will be deducted and managed.

### Implications for Small Businesses

Small businesses, particularly those without existing retirement plans, must pay close attention to these state requirements. The DOL's guidance aims to reduce confusion and streamline processes, but the onus remains on employers to comply with state mandates. Non-compliance can lead to financial penalties and potential legal challenges.

This new guidance marks a significant step in clarifying the role of employers in supporting state-run retirement initiatives. By understanding and fulfilling these obligations, employers can help ensure that their employees have access to retirement savings options, while minimizing legal and financial risks for their businesses.
