## DOL Mandates Telehealth Parity in Group Health Plans

The Department of Labor (DOL) has issued a final rule requiring parity between telehealth and in-person health services for employer-sponsored group health plans. This rule, published on July 10, 2026, will take effect on January 1, 2027. It mandates that telehealth services be provided on the same terms as in-person services, ensuring equal access and cost-sharing for employees.

The rule, part of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA) amendments, aims to address disparities in healthcare access that have been highlighted during the COVID-19 pandemic. It requires that group health plans cover telehealth services without imposing additional cost-sharing, prior authorization, or other limitations that do not apply to in-person visits.

## Key Provisions of the Rule

Under the new rule, employers must ensure that their health plans:

- Offer telehealth services with the same cost-sharing requirements as in-person visits.
- Do not impose additional restrictions or prior authorizations on telehealth services.
- Provide the same coverage limits and benefits for telehealth as for traditional in-person care.

These changes apply to all ERISA-covered group health plans, impacting a wide range of employers and their health benefit offerings.

## Employer Action Items

Employers need to take several steps to comply with the new rule by the January 1, 2027 deadline:

1. **Review Current Health Plans**: Audit existing group health plans to identify any discrepancies between telehealth and in-person service coverage.
   
2. **Amend Plan Documents**: Update plan documents to reflect the new parity requirements, ensuring no additional barriers exist for telehealth services.

3. **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees about the changes to their health benefits, emphasizing the availability and equality of telehealth services.

4. **Coordinate with Insurers**: Work with insurance carriers to ensure that plan offerings comply with the new DOL rule and that necessary adjustments are made promptly.

## Implications for Employers

This rule represents a significant shift in how employers must structure their health benefits. By eliminating disparities in telehealth coverage, the DOL aims to enhance healthcare accessibility and affordability for employees. Employers who fail to comply may face penalties under ERISA, making it crucial to act swiftly in aligning their health plans with the new requirements.

As telehealth continues to expand as a viable healthcare option, this rule ensures that employees can access necessary services without facing additional financial or administrative burdens. Employers should leverage this opportunity to enhance their benefits offerings, potentially improving employee satisfaction and retention.
