## EEOC Updates ADA Guidance

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on June 30, 2026, concerning disability accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). This guidance clarifies employer responsibilities and aims to enhance workplace inclusivity for individuals with disabilities.

## Key Changes and Implications

The updated guidance emphasizes the necessity for employers to engage in a timely, interactive process with employees requesting accommodations. It also clarifies that employers must consider telework as a reasonable accommodation, especially for roles where physical presence is not essential.

Additionally, the EEOC has provided more detailed examples of reasonable accommodations, including modified work schedules, provision of assistive technology, and job restructuring. Employers are reminded that undue hardship remains the primary limitation on the obligation to provide accommodations.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Accommodation Policies**: Employers should promptly review and update their accommodation policies to align with the new guidance. This includes ensuring that managers and HR personnel are trained on the interactive process and the types of accommodations that may be required.

2. **Assess Telework Options**: Given the emphasis on telework as a potential accommodation, employers should evaluate which positions can feasibly accommodate remote work arrangements and prepare to discuss these options with employees.

3. **Enhance Documentation Practices**: Employers should ensure thorough documentation of the accommodation process, including requests, discussions, and decisions, to demonstrate compliance with the ADA.

4. **Train Managers and HR Staff**: Conduct training sessions to educate managers and HR staff on the updated guidance and the importance of a proactive approach to accommodation requests.

## Compliance and Best Practices

Employers should remain vigilant in their ADA compliance efforts, as failure to adhere to these updated guidelines could result in increased scrutiny and potential legal challenges. By actively engaging in the accommodation process and maintaining open communication with employees, employers can foster a more inclusive workplace environment.

The EEOC's updated guidance serves as a critical reminder of the ongoing obligations under the ADA and the importance of accommodating employees with disabilities. Employers should act swiftly to integrate these changes into their workplace practices to ensure compliance and support a diverse workforce.
