## NLRB Revises Guidelines on Employee Handbook Policies

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued new guidelines on June 15, 2026, revising its approach to evaluating employee handbook policies. These changes directly affect how employers draft and enforce rules related to employee conduct, workplace behavior, and disciplinary procedures.

### Key Changes in the Guidelines

Under the revised guidelines, the NLRB will apply a more stringent standard when determining whether handbook policies could infringe on employees' rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). Specifically, the Board will scrutinize policies that might be interpreted as restricting employees' rights to engage in concerted activities, as protected under Section 7 of the NLRA.

The NLRB's new approach emphasizes the potential for an employee to reasonably construe a rule as limiting their NLRA rights. This shifts the focus from the employer's intent to the policy's potential impact on employee perceptions.

### Impact on Employers

Employers must now reassess their handbook policies to ensure compliance with the updated guidelines. Policies that are overly broad or ambiguous about employee conduct, communication, or workplace behavior may need revision. This includes rules on social media use, confidentiality, and workplace decorum, which could be perceived as limiting employees' rights to discuss wages, hours, and other terms of employment.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Revise Policies**: Conduct a thorough audit of your current employee handbook. Pay particular attention to policies that could be interpreted as limiting concerted activities under Section 7 of the NLRA.

2. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal professionals specializing in labor law to ensure that handbook revisions comply with the new NLRB guidelines and do not inadvertently violate the NLRA.

3. **Train HR and Management**: Educate HR personnel and management teams about the new guidelines and their implications for enforcing workplace policies. Ensure that they understand how to apply these policies without infringing on employee rights.

4. **Communicate Changes to Employees**: Clearly communicate any changes to handbook policies to your workforce. Transparency in how these policies are applied will help mitigate misunderstandings and potential grievances.

### Conclusion

The NLRB's revised guidelines on employee handbook policies represent a significant shift in how employer rules are evaluated concerning employee rights. By proactively reviewing and updating handbook policies, employers can better align with these changes, reducing the risk of legal challenges and fostering a compliant workplace environment.
