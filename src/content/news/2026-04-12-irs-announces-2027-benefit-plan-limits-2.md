## Early Projections

The IRS has released preliminary cost-of-living adjustment data that provides an early look at likely changes to benefit plan contribution limits for the 2027 plan year. While final numbers will be published later this year, the projections give employers a head start on benefits planning and open enrollment preparation.

Based on current inflation data, the following increases are expected:

## Projected 2027 Limits

### Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)
- **Self-only coverage**: Expected to increase to $4,400 (from $4,300 in 2026)
- **Family coverage**: Expected to increase to $8,750 (from $8,550 in 2026)
- **Catch-up contribution (age 55+)**: Expected to remain at $1,000

### Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs)
- **Health FSA**: Expected to increase to $3,350 (from $3,300 in 2026)
- **Dependent care FSA**: Expected to remain at $5,000 (this limit is set by statute and does not adjust for inflation)

### Retirement Plans
- **401(k) employee elective deferrals**: Expected to increase to $24,000 (from $23,500 in 2026)
- **Catch-up contribution (age 50+)**: Expected to remain at $7,500
- **Enhanced catch-up (ages 60-63)**: Expected to increase to $11,750 under SECURE 2.0 Act provisions

### High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP) Thresholds
- **Minimum deductible (self-only)**: Expected to increase to $1,700
- **Minimum deductible (family)**: Expected to increase to $3,400
- **Out-of-pocket maximum (self-only)**: Expected to increase to $8,500
- **Out-of-pocket maximum (family)**: Expected to increase to $17,000

## What Employers Should Do Now

- **Begin open enrollment planning**: Use the projected limits to start modeling plan design changes and employee communications for the upcoming enrollment period
- **Review plan documents**: Ensure plan documents allow for automatic adoption of new IRS limits, or prepare amendments as needed
- **Update payroll systems**: Coordinate with payroll providers to ensure systems can accommodate new contribution limits when finalized
- **Communicate early**: Consider providing employees with advance notice of expected limit increases so they can plan their benefits elections
- **Monitor the SECURE 2.0 enhanced catch-up**: The expanded catch-up provision for employees ages 60-63 is still relatively new — ensure your plan administrator is equipped to handle it correctly
