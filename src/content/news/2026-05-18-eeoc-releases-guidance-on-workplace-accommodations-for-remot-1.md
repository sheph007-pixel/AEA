## EEOC Guidance on Remote Work Accommodations

On May 15, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) issued new guidance clarifying employer obligations for accommodating remote workers under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). This guidance addresses the increasing prevalence of remote work and the need for employers to understand their responsibilities in providing reasonable accommodations to employees working from home.

### Key Points from the Guidance

The EEOC's guidance emphasizes that the ADA's requirement for reasonable accommodations applies equally to employees working remotely as it does to those working on-site. Employers must engage in an interactive process to determine appropriate accommodations for remote workers with disabilities. The guidance highlights several examples of accommodations, such as providing assistive technology, modifying communication methods, and adjusting work schedules.

The EEOC also clarifies that employers may need to consider the remote work environment when evaluating accommodation requests. For instance, if an employee requires ergonomic equipment to perform their job duties effectively from home, the employer may be obligated to provide such equipment or reimburse the employee for its purchase.

### Employer Action Items

Employers should review the EEOC's guidance and assess their current remote work policies to ensure compliance with the ADA. Key actions include:

1. **Audit Accommodation Procedures**: Evaluate current procedures for handling accommodation requests from remote workers. Ensure that the interactive process is clearly outlined and accessible to all employees.

2. **Update Policies**: Amend remote work policies to explicitly address the provision of reasonable accommodations. Include examples of potential accommodations and a clear process for requesting them.

3. **Train Managers**: Conduct training sessions for managers and HR staff on the new guidance. Emphasize the importance of the interactive process and the need to consider the unique aspects of remote work environments.

4. **Document Accommodation Efforts**: Maintain thorough documentation of all accommodation requests and the steps taken to address them. This documentation can be crucial in the event of a dispute or EEOC investigation.

### Implications for Employers

The EEOC's guidance underscores the necessity for employers to proactively address accommodation needs in a remote work context. With remote work becoming a standard practice, understanding and implementing these requirements is essential to avoid potential legal challenges and to support an inclusive workplace.

Employers should remain vigilant in monitoring any further developments or additional guidance from the EEOC regarding remote work accommodations. By staying informed and adapting policies accordingly, employers can better navigate the complexities of accommodating remote workers under the ADA.
