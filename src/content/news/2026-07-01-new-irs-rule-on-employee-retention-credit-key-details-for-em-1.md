## IRS Updates Employee Retention Credit Guidance

On June 15, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidance on the Employee Retention Credit (ERC), affecting eligibility criteria and claim procedures for employers. The ERC, originally established under the CARES Act, provides a refundable tax credit to businesses that retain employees during economic downturns. The latest IRS guidance clarifies several aspects of the credit, particularly for businesses that have experienced partial shutdowns or significant revenue declines.

## Key Changes in Eligibility and Claims

The updated guidance specifies that businesses experiencing a 20% or greater decline in gross receipts compared to the same quarter in 2019 remain eligible for the ERC. However, the IRS has introduced new documentation requirements. Employers must now provide detailed records of quarterly gross receipts and demonstrate the impact of COVID-19 related restrictions on their operations.

Additionally, the IRS has streamlined the claim process for the ERC. Employers can now apply the credit against payroll taxes directly through Form 941-X, Adjusted Employer's Quarterly Federal Tax Return or Claim for Refund. This change aims to expedite the refund process and reduce administrative burdens.

## Impact on Small and Medium-Sized Businesses

The updated ERC guidance is particularly relevant for small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) that struggled during the pandemic. The clarified eligibility criteria and streamlined claim process could provide much-needed financial relief. However, SMBs must ensure they maintain accurate and comprehensive records to support their claims, as the IRS has indicated a focus on compliance and fraud prevention.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Eligibility**: Assess whether your business meets the updated eligibility criteria, particularly focusing on revenue declines and operational disruptions.

2. **Prepare Documentation**: Compile necessary documentation, including quarterly gross receipts and records of any government-mandated shutdowns or restrictions impacting your business.

3. **Utilize Form 941-X**: If eligible, file for the ERC using Form 941-X to adjust previously filed payroll tax returns. Ensure all information is accurate to avoid potential IRS audits.

4. **Consult with Tax Professionals**: Consider engaging with tax advisors or legal counsel to navigate the updated ERC guidance effectively and ensure compliance.

The IRS's updated guidance on the Employee Retention Credit provides an opportunity for eligible employers to alleviate some financial pressures. By understanding the new rules and preparing the necessary documentation, businesses can maximize the benefits of this credit.
