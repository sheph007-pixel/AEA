## IRS Updates Documentation Requirements for ERC Claims

The IRS has issued new documentation requirements for the Employee Retention Credit (ERC) effective July 1, 2026. This update, detailed in IRS Notice 2026-45, aims to enhance compliance and reduce fraudulent claims. The ERC, initially introduced under the CARES Act, provides eligible employers with a refundable tax credit for retaining employees during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Under the new requirements, businesses must maintain detailed records proving eligibility and the calculation of the credit. This includes payroll records, documentation of the decline in gross receipts, and any government orders that impacted business operations. Employers must retain these records for at least four years after the date the tax becomes due or is paid, whichever is later.

## Key Changes and Implications

The IRS now mandates that employers submit a comprehensive summary report with their ERC claim. This report must outline the methodology used to calculate the credit, including a breakdown of qualified wages and any applicable health plan expenses. Additionally, businesses must identify the specific periods for which they are claiming the credit and provide supporting documentation for each period.

For those claiming the credit based on a significant decline in gross receipts, the IRS requires a comparative analysis of quarterly gross receipts for 2019 and the relevant quarters in 2020 or 2021. Employers must also document any government orders that resulted in a full or partial suspension of operations, including the dates and nature of the restrictions.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should immediately review their current ERC documentation practices to ensure compliance with the new IRS requirements. This involves auditing existing records for completeness and accuracy, particularly for claims involving government orders or significant declines in gross receipts.

Employers should also consider implementing a robust record-keeping system to manage and store the necessary documentation. This system should facilitate easy retrieval of records in the event of an IRS audit.

Furthermore, businesses that have already claimed the ERC should reassess their documentation to ensure it meets the new standards. If deficiencies are identified, employers should take corrective action promptly to mitigate potential penalties.

Finally, it is advisable for employers to consult with tax professionals to navigate these changes effectively and ensure that their ERC claims are fully compliant with the updated IRS guidelines.
