## IRS Clarifies HSA Contribution Rules for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidance on July 12, 2026, regarding Health Savings Accounts (HSAs). The guidance clarifies contribution limits and eligibility requirements for 2026, impacting employers who offer high-deductible health plans (HDHPs) with HSAs.

Under the new guidelines, the annual contribution limit for individuals with self-only HDHP coverage will increase to $4,150, while the limit for those with family coverage will rise to $8,300. These adjustments reflect cost-of-living increases and are crucial for employers managing benefits packages.

Additionally, the IRS confirmed that the minimum deductible for an HDHP remains at $1,600 for self-only coverage and $3,200 for family coverage. The maximum out-of-pocket expenses for HDHPs will also increase to $8,050 for self-only coverage and $16,100 for family coverage.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should review their current HSA offerings to ensure compliance with the updated limits. This includes updating payroll systems to accommodate the new contribution caps and communicating changes to employees. Employers may also need to adjust their benefits communications to highlight these changes during open enrollment periods.

Furthermore, employers should ensure that their HDHPs meet the minimum deductible and maximum out-of-pocket requirements to maintain HSA eligibility. Failure to comply with these standards could result in employees losing their ability to contribute to HSAs, which could impact overall benefits satisfaction.

## Strategic Considerations

Offering HSAs as part of a benefits package can be a strategic advantage for employers, as they provide a tax-advantaged way for employees to save for medical expenses. Employers should consider how these changes might affect employee contributions and overall participation rates in HSAs.

Employers might also evaluate the potential for increased employer contributions to HSAs as a means to enhance their benefits offerings and attract top talent. With healthcare costs continuing to rise, HSAs remain a valuable tool for employees to manage expenses effectively.

By staying informed of these IRS updates, employers can better position themselves to offer competitive benefits packages while ensuring compliance with federal regulations.
