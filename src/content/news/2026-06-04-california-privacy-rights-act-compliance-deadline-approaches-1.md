## Compliance Deadline for CPRA Employee Data Provisions

California employers must comply with the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) provisions related to employee data by January 1, 2026. The CPRA, which amends the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), extends privacy rights to employees, requiring businesses to implement new data protection measures.

### Key Requirements

The CPRA mandates that employers disclose the types of personal information collected from employees and the purposes for which it is used. Employers must also provide employees with the right to access, correct, and delete their personal information. This aligns with sections 1798.100 to 1798.199 of the California Civil Code, which outline consumer rights under the CCPA and CPRA.

### Impact on Employers

Businesses with annual gross revenues over $25 million, those that buy, sell, or share the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households, or those deriving 50% or more of annual revenues from selling consumer personal information are subject to the CPRA. Employers must ensure that their data handling practices comply with these requirements to avoid potential penalties.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Data Inventory**: Conduct a comprehensive audit of employee data to identify what personal information is collected, stored, and shared.
   
2. **Privacy Notices**: Update privacy notices to reflect CPRA requirements, clearly communicating data collection practices to employees.

3. **Data Access Procedures**: Implement procedures to facilitate employee requests for data access, correction, and deletion.

4. **Training**: Educate HR staff and management on CPRA compliance obligations and data handling best practices.

5. **Vendor Management**: Review contracts with third-party vendors to ensure they comply with CPRA requirements related to employee data.

### Enforcement and Penalties

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) is responsible for enforcing the CPRA. Non-compliance can result in fines of up to $7,500 per intentional violation. Given the significant penalties, employers should prioritize compliance efforts to minimize risk.

### Looking Ahead

As the January 1, 2026, compliance deadline approaches, employers should finalize preparations to meet CPRA requirements. This includes updating data handling policies, ensuring transparency with employees, and reinforcing privacy practices across the organization. Failure to comply could not only result in financial penalties but also damage to reputation and employee trust.
