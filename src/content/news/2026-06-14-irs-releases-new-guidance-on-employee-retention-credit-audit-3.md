## IRS Guidance on ERC Audits

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new guidance on June 12, 2026, regarding audits of the Employee Retention Credit (ERC) claims made by businesses for 2020 and 2021. This update affects employers who claimed the ERC under the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act and subsequent amendments. The ERC was designed to encourage businesses to keep employees on payroll during the COVID-19 pandemic by offering a refundable tax credit.

## Key Points of the Guidance

The IRS's new guidance outlines the audit process and documentation requirements for businesses that claimed the ERC. The agency will focus on verifying eligibility criteria, including the significant decline in gross receipts and the suspension of operations due to government orders. Employers must provide supporting documentation, such as payroll records and financial statements, to substantiate their claims.

### What Employers Need to Know

1. **Eligibility Verification**: Employers must demonstrate a significant decline in gross receipts or a full or partial suspension of operations due to governmental orders. The IRS will scrutinize these claims closely.

2. **Documentation Requirements**: Businesses should ensure they have comprehensive records, including payroll data, quarterly tax filings, and any communications related to government orders affecting operations.

3. **Audit Timeline**: The IRS has indicated that audits for ERC claims will begin in the third quarter of 2026. Employers should prepare for potential audits by reviewing and organizing relevant records now.

## Action Items for Employers

- **Review ERC Claims**: Employers should revisit their ERC claims to ensure compliance with IRS requirements and verify that all documentation is complete and accurate.

- **Organize Records**: Gather and organize all relevant documentation, including payroll records, financial statements, and any government communications about operational suspensions.

- **Consult Tax Advisors**: Engage with tax professionals to review claims and documentation, ensuring readiness for potential IRS audits.

The IRS's focus on ERC audit compliance underscores the importance of meticulous record-keeping and adherence to eligibility criteria. Employers should act promptly to mitigate potential audit risks and ensure smooth interactions with the IRS.
