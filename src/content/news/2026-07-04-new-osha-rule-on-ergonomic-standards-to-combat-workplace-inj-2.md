## New Ergonomic Standards Announced

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has announced new ergonomic standards aimed at reducing musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) in the workplace. These standards, published on June 28, 2026, will take effect on January 1, 2027, and are expected to impact a wide range of industries, particularly those with high rates of repetitive motion injuries.

### Key Provisions of the Rule

The new standards, detailed in 29 C.F.R. § 1910.900, require employers to implement specific ergonomic practices designed to minimize the risk of MSDs. Key provisions include:

- **Ergonomic Assessments:** Employers must conduct ergonomic assessments of workstations and tasks to identify potential hazards.
- **Training Programs:** Mandatory training for employees and supervisors on recognizing ergonomic risks and implementing safe practices.
- **Recordkeeping:** Employers must maintain detailed records of ergonomic assessments, training sessions, and any reported MSDs.

### Impact on Employers

The rule primarily targets industries such as manufacturing, warehousing, and healthcare, where repetitive tasks and manual handling are common. However, all employers are encouraged to review their current practices to ensure compliance.

Employers with 50 or more employees will have additional obligations, including the development of a formal ergonomic program that includes employee input and regular evaluation of ergonomic practices.

### Action Items for Employers

To prepare for the upcoming changes, employers should take the following steps:

1. **Conduct Ergonomic Assessments:** Begin evaluating workstations and tasks for ergonomic risks. Engage occupational health professionals if necessary.
   
2. **Develop Training Programs:** Create or update training materials to educate employees about ergonomic risks and safe practices.
   
3. **Review Recordkeeping Procedures:** Ensure that systems are in place to document ergonomic assessments, training, and any reported injuries related to MSDs.

4. **Engage Employees:** Involve employees in the development of ergonomic solutions to improve compliance and effectiveness.

### Compliance and Enforcement

OSHA will begin enforcement of the new standards starting January 1, 2027. Non-compliance may result in fines and increased scrutiny from OSHA inspectors. Employers are advised to complete all necessary preparations by the end of 2026 to avoid penalties.

### Conclusion

With the introduction of these new ergonomic standards, OSHA aims to significantly reduce the incidence of work-related MSDs. Employers should act promptly to align their practices with these new requirements, ensuring a safer and more productive work environment for their employees.
