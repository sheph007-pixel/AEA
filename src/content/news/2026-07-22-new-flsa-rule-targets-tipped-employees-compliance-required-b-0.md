## New Rule on Tipped Employees

The Department of Labor (DOL) has finalized a new rule affecting the wage calculations for tipped employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), set to take effect on December 1, 2026. This rule modifies the tip credit provisions, which allow employers to pay tipped employees a lower direct wage as long as their tips make up the difference to the federal minimum wage.

## Key Changes

The new rule requires employers to ensure that tipped employees spend at least 80% of their workweek performing tip-generating duties to qualify for the tip credit. This change aims to prevent employers from using tipped employees for non-tip-generating tasks without proper compensation. The rule also introduces a new tracking requirement, mandating employers to document the time employees spend on tipped versus non-tipped duties.

## Impact on Employers

Employers in the hospitality, restaurant, and service industries, where tipping is common, will need to reassess their wage calculation and record-keeping practices. The rule applies to all businesses utilizing the tip credit provision under the FLSA, specifically section 29 U.S.C. § 203(m).

## Compliance Steps

1. **Audit Current Practices:** Employers should immediately audit their current practices to ensure compliance with the new 80/20 rule. Review job descriptions and work schedules to align with the rule's requirements.

2. **Implement Tracking Systems:** Develop or update systems to accurately track the time employees spend on tipped and non-tipped duties. This may involve investing in new technology or retraining staff to document work activities effectively.

3. **Review Wage Calculations:** Ensure that tipped employees' wages, when combined with tips, meet or exceed the federal minimum wage. Adjust wage rates as necessary to comply with the new rule.

4. **Training and Communication:** Train managers and supervisors on the new requirements to ensure proper implementation. Communicate changes clearly to employees to avoid confusion and potential disputes.

## Legal Considerations

Non-compliance with the new rule could result in legal challenges and financial penalties. Employers should consult with legal counsel to understand the full implications and ensure that all aspects of the new rule are integrated into their operations by the December 2026 deadline.

The DOL's update emphasizes the importance of fair compensation practices and aims to protect tipped workers from wage discrepancies. Employers must act promptly to align their policies with the new requirements to avoid legal repercussions and maintain compliance.
