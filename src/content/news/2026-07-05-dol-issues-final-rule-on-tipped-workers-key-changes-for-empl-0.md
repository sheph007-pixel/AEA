## New Rule on Tipped Workers

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on July 1, 2026, addressing tipped workers' wages and tip pooling arrangements. This rule, effective December 1, 2026, revises the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regulations, specifically impacting employers in the hospitality and service industries.

The rule clarifies when employers can take a tip credit against minimum wage obligations under the FLSA. Employers can only claim a tip credit if tipped employees spend at least 80% of their time on tip-generating activities. This clarifies the "80/20 rule," which has been a point of contention and confusion in recent years.

## Tip Pooling and Minimum Wage

The new rule also revises tip pooling regulations. Employers who do not take a tip credit can now include non-tipped staff, such as cooks and dishwashers, in tip pools. This change aims to promote fairness and equity in workplaces where tip pooling is common.

However, employers who take a tip credit cannot include non-tipped employees in mandatory tip pools. This maintains the distinction between tipped and non-tipped workers' compensation.

## Compliance and Action Items

Employers must audit their current wage and hour practices to ensure compliance by the December 1 deadline. Key action items include:

1. **Review Job Duties**: Ensure tipped employees spend at least 80% of their time on tip-generating tasks to qualify for the tip credit.
   
2. **Audit Tip Pooling Practices**: Adjust tip pooling arrangements to comply with the new rule, ensuring non-tipped employees are only included if no tip credit is taken.

3. **Update Employee Handbooks**: Revise policies and procedures to reflect the new regulations, particularly in sections related to compensation and tip distribution.

4. **Training**: Conduct training sessions for managers and payroll staff to understand and implement the new requirements effectively.

## Legal Framework

The rule amends 29 C.F.R. §§ 531.50-531.59, which govern tipped employees under the FLSA. It aligns with recent court decisions and previous guidance, providing clarity on the longstanding 80/20 rule.

Employers should consult with legal counsel or HR professionals to navigate these changes and ensure adherence to the updated FLSA guidelines. Failure to comply can result in penalties and back wage liabilities.

With the hospitality and service sectors heavily reliant on tipped workers, understanding and implementing these changes is crucial for maintaining legal compliance and workforce satisfaction.
