## Federal Contractors Must Implement Paid Sick Leave by September 30, 2026

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has finalized a rule requiring federal contractors to provide paid sick leave to their employees, effective September 30, 2026. This rule applies to all new contracts and existing contracts renewed or extended on or after this date. The rule, issued under Executive Order 13706, mandates that contractors offer at least seven days of paid sick leave annually.

### Key Provisions and Compliance Requirements

Under the rule, employees must accrue at least one hour of paid sick leave for every 30 hours worked, up to a minimum of 56 hours per year. This leave can be used for personal illness, to care for a family member, or for absences related to domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking. Employers must allow unused sick leave to carry over annually, although they are not required to pay out unused leave upon termination.

Federal contractors need to update their leave policies to comply with these requirements. This includes revising employee handbooks and contracts to reflect the new accrual and usage guidelines. Contractors should also implement systems to accurately track employees' sick leave accruals and usage.

### Impact on Employers

This rule impacts all businesses holding federal contracts, including those in construction, manufacturing, and service sectors. Employers must ensure compliance to avoid penalties, which can include contract termination or suspension of future contract eligibility. Non-compliance may also result in back pay and reinstatement for affected employees.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Review and Update Policies:** Employers must audit and amend their existing leave policies to align with the new requirements. This may involve consulting with legal counsel to ensure all contractual obligations are met.
  
- **Implement Tracking Systems:** Establish or enhance tracking systems to monitor sick leave accrual and usage accurately. This will help in maintaining compliance and addressing any employee grievances promptly.

- **Employee Communication:** Clearly communicate the changes to all employees. Training sessions or informational meetings can be helpful to ensure that employees understand their rights and the procedures for requesting leave.

- **Monitor Contractual Changes:** Keep an eye on contract renewals or extensions, as the rule applies to any contracts modified after the effective date.

### Conclusion

The DOL's new rule on paid sick leave for federal contractors represents a significant shift in employee benefits for those working under federal contracts. By September 30, 2026, affected employers must ensure their policies and practices comply with the new standards to avoid penalties and maintain contract eligibility. Proactive measures, including policy updates and employee education, will be essential to a smooth transition.
