## FTC Guidelines on Non-Disparagement Clauses

On July 20, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) issued new guidelines regarding the use of non-disparagement clauses in employment contracts. These guidelines aim to prevent clauses that could unfairly limit employees' rights to speak about their workplace experiences, potentially affecting employers nationwide.

Non-disparagement clauses are often included in employment contracts to prevent employees from making negative statements about their employers. However, the FTC's recent guidelines highlight concerns that such clauses can infringe on employees' rights, particularly under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) and other federal laws protecting free speech and whistleblowing activities.

## Key Points of the Guidelines

The FTC's guidelines emphasize that non-disparagement clauses must not:
- Inhibit employees from discussing wages, hours, or other terms and conditions of employment.
- Restrict employees from reporting violations of law to government agencies.
- Prevent employees from participating in investigations or legal proceedings.

The guidelines further clarify that overly broad non-disparagement clauses could be considered unfair or deceptive practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act, potentially leading to enforcement actions against employers.

## Implications for Employers

Employers should review their existing employment contracts and severance agreements to ensure compliance with the new guidelines. Specifically, contracts should be scrutinized for language that might be interpreted as restricting employees' rights to communicate about workplace issues or report legal violations.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Existing Contracts**: Employers should conduct a thorough audit of all employment contracts and severance agreements to identify and amend any non-disparagement clauses that could be seen as overly restrictive under the new guidelines.

2. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage legal counsel to review contract language and ensure compliance with both the FTC guidelines and applicable labor laws, including the NLRA.

3. **Train HR Personnel**: Provide training for HR teams on the implications of the new guidelines and the importance of drafting compliant non-disparagement clauses.

4. **Update Policies**: Revise company policies and employee handbooks to reflect any changes made to contract language and ensure that employees are aware of their rights.

The FTC's guidelines underscore the importance of balancing employers' interests in protecting their reputation with employees' rights to free speech and legal protections. By taking proactive steps to align with these guidelines, employers can mitigate potential legal risks and maintain fair employment practices.
