## Pay Scale Disclosure Mandate

On May 15, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule requiring employers to disclose the pay scale for positions in job offers. This rule, effective January 1, 2027, applies to all employers with 15 or more employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The move aims to promote pay transparency and address wage disparities.

## Compliance Requirements

Employers must include the salary range or hourly wage range for the position in all job offers. This applies to both new hires and internal promotions. The rule mandates that the disclosed pay scale must reflect what the employer reasonably expects to pay for the position. Failure to comply could result in penalties under the FLSA.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Job Offer Templates**: Ensure all job offer letters include a clear pay scale. This includes offers for full-time, part-time, and temporary positions.
   
2. **Evaluate Current Pay Structures**: Conduct a thorough review of current pay structures to ensure they align with market standards. This will help in setting accurate pay scales.

3. **Training for HR and Hiring Managers**: Provide training to HR and hiring managers on the new requirements to ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties.

4. **Audit Current Job Postings**: Review and update current job postings to include pay scales, if applicable, to align with the spirit of the new rule.

## Implications for Employers

The new rule is part of a broader effort to enhance pay transparency and reduce wage gaps. Employers should anticipate increased scrutiny of their pay practices and be prepared for potential employee inquiries regarding pay scales. Transparent communication about pay can improve employee trust and reduce turnover.

Employers have until the end of 2026 to comply with the new requirements. Early preparation and proactive adjustments to pay practices will be crucial in meeting the deadline and avoiding penalties.
