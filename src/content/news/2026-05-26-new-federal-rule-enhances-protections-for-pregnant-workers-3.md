## Pregnant Workers Fairness Act Takes Effect June 27, 2026

The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) will come into force on June 27, 2026, requiring employers to provide reasonable accommodations for employees affected by pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions. This federal mandate applies to employers with 15 or more employees, aligning with the threshold set by Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Under the PWFA, employers must engage in an interactive process with the employee to determine suitable accommodations unless such accommodations would impose an undue hardship on the operation of the business. This process mirrors the requirements found in the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), ensuring that pregnant workers are afforded similar protections.

## Key Provisions of the PWFA

The PWFA explicitly prohibits employers from denying employment opportunities based on the need to make reasonable accommodations for a qualified employee. It also bars employers from requiring an employee to take leave if another reasonable accommodation can be provided. This provision is crucial for businesses to understand, as it emphasizes accommodating employees in their roles rather than defaulting to leave.

The Act also mandates that job applicants be considered for positions without discrimination based on pregnancy-related needs. This means that during the hiring process, employers must consider how they can accommodate a candidate's pregnancy-related requirements rather than excluding them from consideration.

## Employer Action Items

Employers must update their policies and procedures to comply with the PWFA by the effective date. This includes revising employee handbooks to reflect the new accommodations process and training HR personnel and managers on the requirements and implementation of these accommodations.

Additionally, employers should review their job descriptions to identify essential functions and potential accommodations. This proactive step can streamline the interactive process when requests are made.

## Potential Impact and Compliance

The PWFA aims to create a more inclusive workplace for pregnant employees, reducing the risk of discrimination and promoting retention. Employers must carefully document all accommodation requests and the resulting actions to ensure compliance and protect against potential litigation.

Non-compliance with the PWFA can result in penalties similar to those under Title VII, including compensatory and punitive damages. Therefore, businesses should consult with legal counsel to ensure their policies are aligned with the new federal requirements.

By taking these steps, employers can not only comply with the PWFA but also foster a supportive environment for pregnant employees, enhancing overall workplace morale and productivity.
