## DOL's Proposed FMLA Rule

The Department of Labor (DOL) has proposed a new rule to simplify the process for requesting and managing leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). Announced on May 15, 2026, the proposed changes aim to reduce administrative burdens for employers and improve accessibility for employees. The proposal is open for public comment until July 14, 2026.

## Key Changes

The proposed rule introduces a standardized digital form for FMLA leave requests. This form is designed to be user-friendly and compatible with existing HR software systems. The goal is to streamline the submission and approval process, reducing paperwork and potential delays. Additionally, the rule proposes clearer guidelines on what constitutes sufficient medical certification, aiming to minimize disputes over documentation.

## Impact on Employers

Employers with 50 or more employees, who are already subject to FMLA requirements, should prepare for potential changes in their leave management systems. The introduction of a digital form could necessitate updates to HR software and training for HR personnel. Employers should review their current FMLA processes to identify areas for improvement in anticipation of the final rule.

## Action Items

1. **Review Current FMLA Procedures**: Assess your current FMLA leave management system and identify potential areas for integration with the proposed digital form.
   
2. **Engage with HR Software Providers**: Contact your HR software vendors to discuss potential updates or integrations needed to support the new digital form.

3. **Prepare for Training**: Plan for training sessions to familiarize HR staff with the new digital form and updated certification guidelines once the rule is finalized.

4. **Submit Comments**: Consider submitting feedback on the proposed rule to the DOL by the July 14, 2026 deadline. Engaging in the comment process allows employers to voice concerns or support for specific aspects of the proposal.

The proposed rule reflects the DOL's ongoing efforts to modernize FMLA processes, balancing the needs of both employers and employees. By preparing now, employers can ensure a smooth transition if the rule is enacted.
