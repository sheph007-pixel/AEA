## DOL Finalizes Rule on Minimum Wage for Tipped Employees

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on May 20, 2026, affecting how employers calculate the minimum wage for tipped employees. The rule, effective July 1, 2026, amends the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regulations, specifically 29 C.F.R. § 531.56(e), to clarify when employers can take a tip credit.

Under the new rule, employers can only claim a tip credit when tipped employees spend at least 80% of their time on tip-generating activities. This change requires employers to differentiate between time spent on direct tip-producing work and related non-tipped duties. The rule aims to ensure tipped workers receive the full minimum wage for non-tip-producing tasks exceeding 20% of their workweek.

### Implications for Employers

Employers in the hospitality and service industries must reassess their payroll practices. The new rule requires careful tracking of employee duties to ensure compliance. Employers should implement systems to document the time employees spend on various tasks, distinguishing between tip-eligible and non-tip-eligible work.

### Action Items

1. **Audit Current Practices**: Review current payroll and time-tracking systems to ensure they align with the new requirements. Ensure systems can accurately track and report time spent on different duties.
   
2. **Train Managers and Staff**: Educate managers and tipped employees about the changes. Ensure they understand which activities qualify for tip credit and how to log their time accurately.

3. **Adjust Schedules and Duties**: If necessary, adjust employee schedules and duties to comply with the 80/20 rule. Consider redistributing tasks to ensure tip-producing work remains the primary function.

4. **Review and Amend Policies**: Update employee handbooks and wage policies to reflect the new rule. Clearly outline expectations for task tracking and compliance.

Failure to comply with the new DOL rule could result in costly back pay liabilities and penalties. Employers should act promptly to ensure their practices align with the updated regulations by the July 1, 2026, deadline.
