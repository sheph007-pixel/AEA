## New FMLA Rule for Remote Workers

On June 1, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) issued revised regulations under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) to clarify eligibility requirements for remote workers. These changes address the growing number of employees working remotely and aim to resolve ambiguities in determining FMLA eligibility under the current framework.

The key update concerns the 50/75 rule, which requires that an employee must work at a location where the employer has 50 or more employees within 75 miles to qualify for FMLA leave. Under the new regulations, remote workers are considered to be based at the office to which they report or from which they receive assignments, rather than their home location. This adjustment ensures that remote employees are not unfairly excluded from FMLA protections due to their physical distance from a traditional office setting.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must reassess how they determine FMLA eligibility for their remote workforce. The DOL's clarification means that the geographic distribution of remote employees will now be tied to their reporting office, potentially altering the count of employees within the 75-mile radius. This could result in more remote workers becoming eligible for FMLA leave.

HR professionals should update their FMLA policies to reflect this change and communicate the new eligibility criteria to remote employees. Additionally, HR systems should be configured to track remote employees' reporting office locations accurately to ensure compliance with the revised rule.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Amend FMLA policies to incorporate the new definition of a remote worker's worksite. Ensure that all HR personnel are trained on the updated regulations.

2. **Communicate Changes**: Inform remote employees about the revised FMLA eligibility criteria and how it may affect their leave rights.

3. **Audit Employee Records**: Verify and update records to reflect the correct reporting office for each remote employee. Ensure that your HR software accurately tracks these details.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Consider seeking legal advice to ensure full compliance with the revised regulations and to address any potential legal implications for your organization.

The DOL's revised regulations represent a significant shift in how FMLA eligibility is determined for remote workers. By taking proactive steps to align policies and practices with the new rule, employers can better support their remote workforce and maintain compliance with federal leave requirements.
