## DOL Proposes Enhanced Lactation Accommodation Requirements

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on May 22, 2026, aiming to strengthen workplace lactation accommodations under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule seeks to expand the requirements for employers to provide adequate break time and private spaces for nursing employees, aligning with the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA) which took effect in June 2023.

### Key Provisions of the Proposed Rule

The proposed rule mandates that employers with 15 or more employees must provide a private, non-bathroom space for lactation needs. This space must be shielded from view and free from intrusion by coworkers and the public, ensuring privacy and comfort for nursing employees. The rule also specifies that lactation breaks should be considered paid time unless they coincide with standard break periods.

### Implications for Employers

Employers should prepare for potential changes by reviewing current lactation accommodation policies. The proposed rule emphasizes the need for clear communication with employees regarding available facilities and procedures for requesting accommodations. Additionally, businesses should assess their physical workspaces to ensure compliance with the enhanced privacy requirements.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Evaluate Current Facilities**: Conduct an audit of existing lactation spaces to ensure they meet the proposed privacy and accessibility standards.
   
2. **Policy Review**: Update employee handbooks and policies to reflect the potential changes, including procedures for requesting lactation accommodations.

3. **Training**: Provide training for HR personnel and managers on the new requirements to facilitate smooth implementation and compliance.

4. **Feedback Mechanism**: Establish a system for nursing employees to provide feedback on accommodation effectiveness and address any issues promptly.

### Next Steps

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until July 31, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the comment process to express concerns or support for the proposed changes. The final rule, if adopted, would likely take effect in early 2027, providing employers time to adjust their policies and facilities accordingly.

The proposed rule reflects a growing recognition of the importance of supporting nursing employees in the workplace. By enhancing lactation accommodations, the DOL aims to promote a more inclusive and supportive work environment for all employees.
