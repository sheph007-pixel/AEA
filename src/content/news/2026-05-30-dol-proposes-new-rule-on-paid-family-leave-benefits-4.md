## DOL Proposes Expansion of Paid Family Leave Benefits

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on May 29, 2026, aimed at expanding paid family leave benefits under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). This proposal seeks to increase the accessibility and duration of paid leave for employees, impacting businesses with 50 or more employees. The proposed changes could take effect as early as January 1, 2027, pending public comment and finalization.

Under the current FMLA provisions, eligible employees are entitled to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for specific family and medical reasons. The proposed rule intends to introduce a paid component, mandating that employers provide a minimum of six weeks of paid leave. The DOL suggests funding this through a combination of employer contributions and federal subsidies, though specifics on the funding mechanism are still under discussion.

## Key Changes and Implications

The proposed rule would amend Section 2612 of the FMLA (29 U.S.C. § 2612) to incorporate paid leave, aligning with similar state-level initiatives. Currently, states like California and New York offer paid family leave, and the federal proposal aims to standardize these benefits nationwide.

Employers should prepare for potential adjustments to their leave policies. If the rule is finalized, businesses will need to update their employee handbooks and payroll systems to accommodate the new paid leave requirements. Additionally, HR departments will need to train staff on administering the new benefits and ensure compliance with both federal and state regulations.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Current Leave Policies**: Evaluate existing family leave policies to identify necessary changes in anticipation of the new rule.
   
2. **Budget for Potential Costs**: Begin financial planning to cover potential costs associated with providing paid leave, including payroll adjustments and administrative expenses.

3. **Engage in Public Comment**: Participate in the public comment period to express concerns or support for the proposed changes. The DOL will accept comments until July 29, 2026.

4. **Monitor Developments**: Stay updated on the rule's progress and prepare to implement changes if it becomes finalized.

The DOL's proposal marks a significant shift in the landscape of employee benefits and compensation, aiming to provide greater support for workers balancing family and work responsibilities. Employers should act promptly to assess the impact of these potential changes and ensure readiness for compliance.
