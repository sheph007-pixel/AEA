## DOL Clarifies Tip Pooling Regulations

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on May 15, 2026, clarifying regulations around tip pooling in the hospitality and service industries. This rule, effective July 1, 2026, aims to provide clearer guidelines on how tips can be pooled and distributed among employees, directly impacting employers with tipped workers.

The rule addresses section 3(m) of the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), which permits tip pooling among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips. However, it prohibits managers and supervisors from participating in these pools. The new regulation specifically defines who qualifies as a "manager" or "supervisor," using the duties test from 29 C.F.R. § 541.100(a)(2)-(4) to determine eligibility.

## Key Changes and Implications

Under the new rule, employers can include back-of-house employees, such as cooks and dishwashers, in tip pools if no tip credit is taken. This change allows for a more equitable distribution of tips among staff, potentially increasing earnings for traditionally non-tipped positions. The regulation also reinforces that employers cannot retain any portion of employees' tips, regardless of whether they take a tip credit.

Employers must ensure that any tip pooling arrangement complies with these guidelines to avoid violations. The DOL emphasizes that any deductions or allocations must be clearly communicated to employees and documented accurately to withstand scrutiny during potential audits.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their current tip pooling practices and make necessary adjustments by July 1, 2026. Key actions include:

1. **Audit Current Tip Pooling Arrangements:** Ensure compliance with the new definitions of managers and supervisors. Adjust pools to exclude these roles if currently included.

2. **Update Employee Handbooks:** Clearly outline the revised tip pooling processes and distribute updated handbooks to affected employees.

3. **Train Management and Staff:** Conduct training sessions to educate employees about the new rules and their rights under the FLSA.

4. **Document and Communicate Changes:** Maintain thorough records of tip distributions and communicate any changes transparently to employees.

Failure to comply with these regulations can result in penalties, including back pay for misallocated tips. Employers in the hospitality and service sectors should act promptly to align their practices with the new DOL rule, ensuring fair compensation for all employees involved in tip pooling.
