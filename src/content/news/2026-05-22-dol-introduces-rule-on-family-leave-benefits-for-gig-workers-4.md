## DOL Rule Extends Family Leave Benefits to Gig Workers

The Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule on May 20, 2026, that extends certain Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) benefits to gig workers. This rule aims to address the growing number of workers in the gig economy who previously lacked access to traditional family leave benefits. The rule, effective January 1, 2027, mandates that gig platforms must provide eligible workers with unpaid, job-protected leave similar to that offered under FMLA.

Under the new rule, gig workers who have worked for a platform for at least 12 months and have logged 1,250 hours in the past year will qualify for up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for specified family and medical reasons. This includes the birth of a child, adoption, or serious health conditions affecting the worker or a family member. The rule applies to gig platforms with 50 or more workers.

## Implications for Employers

Gig platforms, such as ride-sharing and delivery services, must now adjust their policies to comply with the new DOL rule. Employers should begin by identifying eligible workers and updating their leave policies to accommodate these changes. Additionally, platforms will need to train HR personnel and managers on the new requirements to ensure compliance.

Failure to comply with the rule could result in penalties and legal challenges. Employers must keep accurate records of worker hours and leave requests to demonstrate compliance if audited by the DOL.

## Action Items for HR Professionals

1. **Audit Worker Hours**: Identify gig workers who meet the 1,250-hour requirement over the past 12 months.
   
2. **Update Leave Policies**: Amend existing leave policies to include eligible gig workers, ensuring they have access to the same FMLA benefits as traditional employees.

3. **Training and Communication**: Train HR staff and managers on the new rule and communicate changes to gig workers clearly and promptly.

4. **Record Keeping**: Implement systems to track leave requests and hours worked by gig workers to ensure compliance and facilitate reporting if required by the DOL.

The DOL's new rule reflects the evolving nature of the workforce and the need to provide equitable benefits to all workers, including those in non-traditional employment arrangements. By January 1, 2027, gig platforms must be prepared to offer these benefits, ensuring that gig workers receive the protections afforded under the FMLA.
