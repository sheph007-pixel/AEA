## EEOC Clarifies Title VII Protections

On June 28, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance clarifying that discrimination based on gender identity is prohibited under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. This guidance builds on the 2020 Supreme Court decision in Bostock v. Clayton County, which held that discrimination based on sexual orientation or gender identity is a form of sex discrimination under Title VII.

## Implications for Employers

This guidance means that employers must ensure their workplace policies and practices are free from discrimination against transgender and gender non-conforming employees. The EEOC's clarification emphasizes that any adverse employment actions, harassment, or failure to accommodate related to an employee's gender identity could constitute a violation of federal law.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should take immediate steps to audit their current policies and training programs to ensure compliance with this guidance. This includes updating non-discrimination policies to explicitly include gender identity and providing training to managers and employees on recognizing and preventing gender identity discrimination.

Additionally, employers should review their dress codes and bathroom policies to ensure they do not discriminate against transgender employees. The EEOC's guidance specifically highlights that employees must be allowed to use facilities that correspond with their gender identity.

## Legal Context and Compliance

Under Title VII (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2), it is unlawful for employers to discriminate against employees or applicants based on sex, which includes gender identity as interpreted by the EEOC and upheld by the Supreme Court. Employers with 15 or more employees are subject to these requirements.

Failure to comply with these protections could result in EEOC investigations and potential lawsuits. Employers found in violation may face penalties, including damages and required corrective actions.

## Conclusion

The EEOC's latest guidance underscores the importance of fostering an inclusive and compliant workplace environment. Employers should act promptly to align their policies with federal requirements, ensuring that all employees, regardless of gender identity, are treated with dignity and respect.
