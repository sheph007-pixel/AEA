## DOL's New Rule on Tipped Employee Overtime

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule on May 30, 2026, clarifying the calculation of overtime pay for tipped employees. This rule, effective July 1, 2026, aims to address confusion regarding how tips are factored into overtime calculations under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).

Under the FLSA, tipped employees are those who customarily and regularly receive more than $30 per month in tips. The new rule specifies that when calculating overtime, employers must use the full minimum wage of $7.25 per hour, rather than the lower cash wage of $2.13 per hour that employers may pay tipped workers, as permitted by the tip credit provision.

## Key Changes

The rule provides that for overtime hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek, the regular rate of pay must include all earnings, including tips, up to the minimum wage. Employers must then pay 1.5 times this regular rate for overtime hours. This clarification ensures that tipped employees receive the full benefit of overtime protections.

Previously, some employers calculated overtime based on the cash wage, leading to potential underpayment. The DOL's clarification aims to eliminate this practice and ensure compliance with federal wage laws.

## Employer Action Items

Employers with tipped employees should immediately review their payroll practices to ensure compliance with the new rule by July 1, 2026. Specific actions include:

- **Audit Current Payroll Systems**: Verify that your payroll system is set up to calculate overtime based on the full minimum wage, not the cash wage.
- **Train HR and Payroll Staff**: Ensure that those responsible for payroll understand the new requirements and are prepared to implement them.
- **Update Employee Handbooks**: Reflect the changes in overtime calculation methods in your employee handbooks and communicate these changes to affected employees.
- **Consult Legal Counsel**: If in doubt, consult with legal counsel to ensure full compliance and avoid potential penalties.

## Implications for Employers

Failure to adhere to these updated overtime calculation rules could result in significant penalties, including back wages and fines. Employers should take immediate steps to align their practices with the new rule to avoid legal challenges and ensure fair compensation for their employees.

The DOL's clarification underscores the importance of accurately calculating overtime pay for tipped employees, reinforcing their rights under the FLSA. Employers must act swiftly to comply with these changes and safeguard against potential liabilities.
