## New HSA Guidelines for 2026

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) released new guidance on May 8, 2026, regarding Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) that impacts contribution limits and eligibility criteria for employers offering high-deductible health plans (HDHPs). This guidance aims to clarify the adjustments for inflation and eligibility requirements under the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) § 223.

The DOL's announcement specifies that the maximum annual contribution for HSAs in 2026 will increase to $4,150 for individuals and $8,300 for families. This is a modest increase from the 2025 limits of $4,050 for individuals and $8,100 for families. The catch-up contribution for individuals aged 55 and older remains unchanged at $1,000.

## Employer Action Items

Employers offering HDHPs should update their benefits materials to reflect the new HSA contribution limits. HR departments must ensure that payroll systems are adjusted to accommodate the increased limits for employee HSA contributions starting January 1, 2026.

Additionally, employers should verify that their HDHPs meet the minimum deductible requirements, which remain at $1,600 for self-only coverage and $3,200 for family coverage. The maximum out-of-pocket expenses for HDHPs also remain unchanged at $8,050 for self-only coverage and $16,100 for family coverage.

## Eligibility Clarifications

The DOL's guidance also addresses eligibility criteria for HSAs. Employees must be enrolled in an HDHP and cannot be covered by any other non-HDHP health plan. Furthermore, they cannot be claimed as dependents on another person's tax return or enrolled in Medicare.

Employers should communicate these eligibility requirements clearly to employees during open enrollment periods and ensure that benefits documentation accurately reflects these criteria.

## Impact on Employers

The updated guidance provides an opportunity for employers to review their benefits offerings and ensure compliance with the latest regulations. By proactively adjusting to the new limits and eligibility criteria, employers can enhance employee satisfaction and reduce the risk of non-compliance penalties.

Employers may also consider offering educational sessions or workshops to help employees understand the benefits of HSAs and how to maximize their contributions and tax advantages.

In conclusion, the DOL's new guidance on HSAs for 2026 requires immediate attention from employers to update benefits documentation and payroll systems. Ensuring compliance with the updated contribution limits and eligibility criteria will help maintain a competitive benefits package and support employee financial wellness.
