## New Guidance for ESOP Fiduciaries

On June 15, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance clarifying fiduciary responsibilities for Employee Stock Ownership Plans (ESOPs). The guidance, detailed in Field Assistance Bulletin No. 2026-02, addresses key fiduciary duties under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA) for plan sponsors and trustees of ESOPs. This update is significant for businesses utilizing ESOPs as part of their employee benefits strategy.

## Key Points of the Guidance

The DOL's bulletin emphasizes the importance of prudence and loyalty in the management of ESOPs. Fiduciaries must ensure that all plan actions are made solely in the interest of plan participants and beneficiaries. The guidance highlights the need for thorough valuation processes when purchasing employer securities, which must reflect fair market value as required by ERISA Section 408(e).

The bulletin also underscores the necessity of having qualified independent appraisers conduct valuations. Fiduciaries are advised to rigorously assess the qualifications and methodologies of appraisers to avoid conflicts of interest and ensure accurate valuations.

## Implications for Employers

Employers sponsoring ESOPs should review the new guidance to ensure compliance with fiduciary obligations. The bulletin's emphasis on valuation processes suggests that employers may need to revisit existing appraisal practices. Engaging with qualified independent appraisers who adhere to professional standards is now more critical than ever.

Additionally, the DOL's guidance may necessitate updates to fiduciary training programs. Employers should ensure that all individuals involved in ESOP management are well-versed in the updated requirements to mitigate potential legal risks.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Valuation Processes**: Confirm that current appraisal practices meet the DOL's standards for independence and accuracy. Consider conducting an audit of recent valuations to identify any discrepancies.

2. **Engage Qualified Appraisers**: Verify the credentials and methodologies of appraisers used for ESOP valuations. Establish criteria for selecting appraisers based on the new guidance.

3. **Update Training Programs**: Implement training sessions for fiduciaries to cover the new guidance and reinforce the importance of prudence and loyalty in ESOP management.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with ERISA attorneys to review the new guidance and assess its impact on your ESOP. Legal counsel can provide insights into necessary policy adjustments and compliance strategies.

By taking these steps, employers can better align their ESOP practices with the DOL's expectations, thereby safeguarding against potential fiduciary breaches. The DOL's guidance serves as a timely reminder of the complexities involved in managing ESOPs and the critical nature of fiduciary duties under ERISA.
