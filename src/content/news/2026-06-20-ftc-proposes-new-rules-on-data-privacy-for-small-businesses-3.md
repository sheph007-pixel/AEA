## FTC Proposes New Data Privacy Rules

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has proposed new rules aimed at strengthening data privacy protections for small businesses. Announced on June 15, 2026, these rules are designed to address growing concerns over data breaches and consumer privacy. The proposal, if enacted, will require small businesses to implement more stringent data protection measures and enhance transparency in data handling practices.

## Key Provisions of the Proposal

The proposed rules introduce several key requirements for small businesses. Firstly, businesses will need to conduct regular data privacy audits to ensure compliance with the new standards. These audits must be documented and made available to the FTC upon request. Additionally, businesses will be required to provide clear and concise privacy notices to consumers, detailing how personal data is collected, used, and shared.

The proposal also mandates the appointment of a designated data protection officer (DPO) within each organization, regardless of size. This officer will be responsible for overseeing data protection strategies and ensuring adherence to the new regulations.

## Impact on Small Businesses

For small businesses, the proposed rules represent a significant shift in data management practices. Compliance will likely require additional resources and potentially new hires to meet the DPO requirement. However, the FTC believes that these measures are necessary to protect consumer data and enhance trust in digital transactions.

Businesses that fail to comply with the new rules could face substantial penalties. The FTC has indicated that fines could reach up to $50,000 per violation, underscoring the importance of adhering to the proposed standards.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should begin preparing for potential changes by reviewing current data privacy practices and identifying areas for improvement. Consider the following steps:

1. **Conduct a Data Privacy Audit**: Evaluate current data handling practices and identify any gaps in compliance.
   
2. **Update Privacy Notices**: Ensure that privacy notices are clear, comprehensive, and easy for consumers to understand.

3. **Designate a Data Protection Officer**: Appoint a qualified individual to oversee data protection efforts and ensure compliance with the new rules.

4. **Train Employees**: Implement training programs to educate employees about data privacy best practices and regulatory requirements.

The FTC has opened the proposal for public comment until August 31, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the comment process to express any concerns or suggestions regarding the proposed rules.

## Looking Ahead

While the proposed rules are not yet finalized, small businesses should take proactive steps to align with the anticipated changes. By preparing now, businesses can mitigate risks and ensure a smoother transition should the rules come into effect. The FTC's initiative reflects a broader trend towards increased data privacy regulation, highlighting the need for businesses to prioritize consumer data protection.
