## DOL Clarifies Training Time Compensation

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a new rule on May 25, 2026, clarifying when employers must compensate employees for training time. This rule, effective July 15, 2026, aims to address ambiguities in the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regarding compensable training activities.

Under the new rule, employers must pay for training time if the training is directly related to an employee's job and is conducted during regular working hours. This aligns with the FLSA's requirement that employees be compensated for all hours worked (29 U.S.C. § 203(g)).

## Key Details of the Rule

The rule specifies that training is compensable if it meets the following criteria: 

1. The training is required by the employer or by law.
2. The training is directly related to the employee's current job.
3. The employee is not performing productive work during the training.

Training outside regular working hours is generally not compensable unless the employer requires attendance or the training is necessary for the employee to perform their current job duties.

## Implications for Employers

Employers should review their training programs to ensure compliance with the new rule. This includes auditing training schedules and content to determine if they meet the new compensable criteria. Employers should also update payroll systems to accurately capture compensable training hours.

Additionally, employers should communicate these changes to employees to manage expectations and avoid potential disputes over unpaid training time.

## Action Items for Employers

- **Audit Training Programs**: Evaluate all training activities to determine if they are compensable under the new rule.
- **Update Payroll Systems**: Ensure that payroll systems are configured to track and compensate for training time accurately.
- **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees of the new compensation policy for training time to ensure clarity and compliance.

The DOL's clarification on training time compensation provides a clear framework for employers to follow, reducing the risk of wage and hour disputes. By taking proactive steps to align with the new rule, employers can ensure compliance and maintain positive employee relations.
