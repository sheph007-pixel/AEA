## New Proposal by DOL

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 28, 2026, to redefine the criteria for classifying workers as independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This move comes as part of an ongoing effort to provide clarity and reduce misclassification, which affects wage and hour protections.

## Key Changes

The proposed rule introduces a multi-factor test that focuses on the economic reality of the working relationship. Key factors include the nature and degree of control over the work, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, the amount of skill required for the work, the permanence of the relationship, and whether the work is part of an integrated unit of production.

## Implications for Employers

If implemented, this rule could significantly impact industries relying heavily on independent contractors, such as gig economy companies, construction, and transportation. Employers may need to re-evaluate their workforce classifications to ensure compliance and avoid potential liabilities, including back wages and penalties.

## Action Items

Employers should begin assessing their current workforce classifications against the proposed criteria. It would be prudent to consult with legal counsel to determine if any adjustments are necessary to align with the new standards. Additionally, consider the potential need for reclassifying certain workers as employees, which would entail providing them with benefits and protections under the FLSA.

## Next Steps

The proposed rule is open for public comment until August 31, 2026. Employers and other stakeholders are encouraged to submit feedback to help shape the final rule. The DOL will review comments before issuing a final rule, expected later this year.

Stay proactive in reviewing your classification practices and prepare for possible changes to maintain compliance with federal labor standards.
