## DOL Changes Overtime Pay for Fluctuating Workweek Method

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on May 20, 2026, altering the calculation of overtime pay under the fluctuating workweek method. This change, effective July 1, 2026, impacts employers using this method to calculate overtime for non-exempt employees with variable work hours.

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), the fluctuating workweek method allows employers to pay a fixed salary for fluctuating hours, with overtime compensation set at half the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a week. The new rule modifies how bonuses and premium payments are factored into the regular rate calculation.

### Key Changes

Previously, the regular rate for overtime was calculated by dividing the employee's weekly salary by the total hours worked in a week. Under the new rule, employers must include non-discretionary bonuses, commissions, and other incentive payments in the regular rate calculation. This means the regular rate will be higher, resulting in increased overtime pay obligations.

The DOL's amendment to 29 C.F.R. § 778.114 clarifies that all payments made to an employee, except those specifically excluded by the FLSA, must be included in the regular rate. This change aims to ensure fair compensation for employees working variable hours and receiving additional forms of compensation.

### Employer Action Items

1. **Review Compensation Structures**: Employers using the fluctuating workweek method should immediately review their compensation structures to ensure compliance. This includes identifying all forms of compensation that must be included in the regular rate calculation.

2. **Adjust Payroll Systems**: Update payroll systems to incorporate bonuses and incentive payments into the regular rate for overtime calculations. Ensure these changes are implemented by the July 1, 2026, effective date.

3. **Communicate with Employees**: Inform affected employees about the changes to their overtime pay calculations. Clear communication can help manage expectations and reduce potential disputes.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the complexity of the new rule, consulting with legal counsel or an HR expert can help ensure compliance and avoid costly penalties.

### Implications for Employers

The DOL's revision of the fluctuating workweek method underscores the importance of accurate overtime calculations. Employers must now account for additional compensation types, potentially increasing payroll costs. Non-compliance could lead to legal challenges and financial liabilities.

This regulatory change reflects the DOL's ongoing efforts to enhance worker protections and ensure fair compensation practices. Employers must act promptly to align their practices with the new requirements and avoid potential penalties.
