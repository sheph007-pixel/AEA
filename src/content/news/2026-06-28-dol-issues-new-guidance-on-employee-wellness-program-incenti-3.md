## New Guidance on Wellness Program Incentives

On June 27, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) released updated guidance on employee wellness program incentives, clarifying compliance requirements under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). This guidance impacts all employers offering wellness programs that provide incentives or rewards to employees.

The new guidance addresses the application of ERISA to wellness programs that offer financial incentives, such as premium discounts or cash rewards, contingent upon participation or achievement of health outcomes. This clarification is crucial for employers to ensure their wellness programs do not inadvertently fall afoul of ERISA regulations.

## Key Compliance Points

The DOL's guidance specifies that wellness programs must be voluntary and not require employees to meet specific health outcomes to receive incentives. Programs that condition rewards on achieving health outcomes must provide a reasonable alternative standard or waiver for employees who cannot meet the original standard due to medical conditions.

Employers must also ensure that wellness programs are reasonably designed to promote health or prevent disease. This means the programs should not be overly burdensome or a subterfuge for discrimination based on health factors.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Wellness Program Structure**: Employers should review their current wellness programs to ensure compliance with the new DOL guidance. This includes verifying that any incentives offered do not require employees to meet specific health outcomes without providing alternatives.

2. **Update Documentation**: Employers must update program documentation to clearly outline the availability of reasonable alternative standards or waivers for employees unable to meet health-based criteria.

3. **Communicate Changes**: Clearly communicate any changes in the wellness program to employees, emphasizing the voluntary nature of participation and the availability of alternative standards.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the complexity of ERISA compliance, employers should consult with legal counsel to ensure their wellness programs align with the latest DOL guidance and avoid potential legal pitfalls.

This updated guidance provides a framework for employers to design wellness programs that comply with ERISA while promoting employee health and engagement. Employers should act promptly to align their programs with these requirements to maintain compliance and avoid potential penalties.
