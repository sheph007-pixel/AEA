## Federal Cybersecurity Requirements Expand for Contractors

The Department of Defense (DoD) announced new cybersecurity requirements for federal contractors, effective January 1, 2027. The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) 2.0 will now apply to all contractors handling controlled unclassified information (CUI). This expansion aims to bolster national security by ensuring that contractors implement adequate cybersecurity measures.

### What Employers Need to Know

CMMC 2.0 introduces a tiered model with three levels of cybersecurity maturity. Level 1 requires basic cyber hygiene, Level 2 mandates the implementation of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) SP 800-171 practices, and Level 3 aligns with a subset of NIST SP 800-172 enhanced security requirements. All contractors dealing with CUI must at least achieve Level 2 certification.

The revised framework simplifies the original CMMC model but maintains rigorous standards to protect sensitive information. Certification must be obtained from a third-party assessment organization (C3PAO) for Levels 2 and 3, while Level 1 allows for self-assessment.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Assess Current Compliance**: Contractors should immediately evaluate their current cybersecurity practices against the CMMC 2.0 requirements. This assessment will help identify gaps and areas that need improvement.

2. **Engage a C3PAO**: Engage with a certified third-party assessment organization to schedule a compliance audit if aiming for Level 2 or Level 3 certification. Early engagement can help avoid delays as the deadline approaches.

3. **Implement Necessary Controls**: For those handling CUI, ensure that all 110 security controls outlined in NIST SP 800-171 are in place. This includes access controls, audit logging, and incident response plans.

4. **Develop a Training Program**: Establish a cybersecurity training program for employees to ensure they understand and adhere to the necessary security protocols.

### Implications for Federal Contractors

Failure to comply with CMMC 2.0 could result in the loss of federal contracts, as certification will be a prerequisite for bidding on new contracts involving CUI. This mandate underscores the importance of cybersecurity in protecting national interests and maintaining the integrity of the federal supply chain.

The DoD plans to incorporate CMMC 2.0 into new contract requirements starting in 2027, giving contractors a limited window to achieve compliance. As cybersecurity threats continue to evolve, the federal government is taking decisive steps to mitigate risks associated with contractor networks.

For more information, contractors can refer to the official [DoD CMMC website](https://www.acq.osd.mil/cmmc/) for detailed guidance and updates on the certification process.
