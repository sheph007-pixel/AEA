## New Guidance on FMLA and Mental Health

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance on May 15, 2026, clarifying the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) provisions related to mental health conditions. This guidance aims to help employers understand when employees are eligible for FMLA leave due to mental health issues, emphasizing conditions that may qualify as serious health conditions under the Act.

The FMLA, codified at 29 U.S.C. § 2601 et seq., grants eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave in a 12-month period for specified family and medical reasons, including serious health conditions that make the employee unable to perform their job functions. The new DOL guidance underscores that mental health conditions like depression, anxiety disorders, and PTSD can constitute serious health conditions if they involve inpatient care or continuing treatment by a healthcare provider.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should review their FMLA policies to ensure they align with the new guidance. Specifically, HR departments should:

1. **Update Training**: Ensure that HR staff and managers are trained to recognize legitimate FMLA requests related to mental health conditions.
   
2. **Review Documentation**: Evaluate the adequacy of documentation requirements for FMLA leave requests. The DOL emphasizes that employers may require certification from a healthcare provider but must not demand more information than the FMLA allows.

3. **Communicate Clearly**: Inform employees about their rights under FMLA, especially regarding mental health conditions. Clear communication can prevent misunderstandings and potential legal disputes.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to review FMLA policies and procedures to ensure compliance with the latest DOL guidance and mitigate risks of litigation.

## Broader Implications

This guidance comes as part of a broader effort to address mental health in the workplace, recognizing the increasing prevalence and impact of mental health issues on employees. The clarification aims to reduce stigma and ensure that employees with mental health conditions receive the same protections as those with physical health conditions.

Employers should be proactive in addressing mental health in their workplace policies, not only to comply with FMLA but also to support employee well-being and productivity. The DOL's emphasis on mental health highlights the need for employers to consider these conditions seriously and handle related FMLA requests with care and sensitivity.

By aligning policies with the latest guidance, employers can foster a more inclusive and supportive work environment, reducing the risk of costly disputes and enhancing overall employee satisfaction and retention.
