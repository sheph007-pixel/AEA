## Proposed Rule on Salary Basis Test

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed changes to the salary basis test for overtime exemptions under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) on June 8, 2026. The proposed rule would increase the minimum salary threshold for exempt employees to $1,059 per week, or $55,068 annually, from the current threshold of $684 per week ($35,568 annually), as specified in 29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1). This change could significantly affect the overtime eligibility of millions of workers across various industries.

The salary basis test is a critical component for determining whether employees qualify as exempt from overtime pay. Under the FLSA, employees must meet certain criteria related to their job duties and salary level to be classified as exempt. The proposed increase aims to adjust for inflation and ensure that the salary threshold reflects current economic conditions.

## Impact on Employers

If implemented, employers will need to re-evaluate their workforce to determine which employees would be affected by the increased salary threshold. Businesses with a significant number of employees currently classified as exempt under the existing threshold may face increased payroll costs if they choose to maintain exempt status by raising salaries. Alternatively, employers may need to reclassify employees as non-exempt, making them eligible for overtime pay.

The proposed rule also includes a provision for automatic updates to the salary threshold every four years, based on wage growth data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. This automatic update mechanism aims to ensure that the salary threshold remains relevant and reduces the need for frequent rulemaking.

## Next Steps for Employers

Employers should begin assessing their current employee classifications and salary structures to prepare for potential changes. Key actions include:

1. **Conducting a Salary Audit:** Identify employees who are currently classified as exempt but earn below the proposed new threshold. Evaluate the financial impact of potential salary adjustments or reclassification to non-exempt status.

2. **Reviewing Job Descriptions and Duties:** Ensure that job duties align with the exempt classification criteria. This may involve updating job descriptions and evaluating whether certain roles should remain exempt.

3. **Budget Planning:** Consider the financial implications of the proposed rule on payroll budgets. Employers may need to allocate additional funds to accommodate salary increases or overtime payments.

4. **Employee Communication:** Develop a communication plan to inform affected employees of any changes to their classification or compensation structure, ensuring transparency and compliance with labor laws.

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until August 7, 2026. Employers and HR professionals are encouraged to submit feedback and stay informed about the progress of this regulatory change. If finalized, the new salary basis test could take effect as early as January 2027, giving businesses a limited timeframe to implement necessary adjustments.
