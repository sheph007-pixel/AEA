## Supreme Court Decision on ADA and Remote Work

The Supreme Court issued a ruling on July 10, 2026, in the case of Smith v. TechCorp, significantly impacting how employers must handle remote work accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). The decision clarifies that employers are required to consider remote work as a reasonable accommodation unless it imposes an undue hardship on the business.

## Key Points of the Ruling

The court's decision mandates that employers must engage in an interactive process with employees requesting remote work due to a disability. This ruling stems from the ADA's requirement to provide reasonable accommodations to qualified employees with disabilities, as outlined in 42 U.S.C. § 12112(b)(5)(A). The court emphasized that technological advancements have made remote work a viable option, thus shifting the burden onto employers to demonstrate why remote work would be unfeasible.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must now reassess their policies regarding remote work accommodations. The ruling suggests that blanket policies against remote work may not hold up under legal scrutiny if challenged. Employers should prepare to justify their decisions with concrete evidence if they deny a remote work request due to undue hardship. Factors such as the nature of the job, cost implications, and impact on business operations will be critical in these assessments.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Accommodation Policies**: Employers should immediately review and, if necessary, update their ADA accommodation policies to ensure they include considerations for remote work.

2. **Document the Interactive Process**: Maintain detailed records of all interactive processes with employees requesting accommodations. This documentation will be crucial in demonstrating compliance with the ADA requirements.

3. **Assess Technological Capabilities**: Evaluate current technological infrastructure to support remote work effectively. This includes ensuring secure access to company systems and data for remote employees.

4. **Train HR Personnel**: Conduct training sessions for HR and management teams on the new requirements and best practices for handling accommodation requests under the ADA.

5. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the potential for increased litigation, consulting with legal counsel to understand the implications of this ruling and to develop a robust compliance strategy is advisable.

## Conclusion

The Supreme Court's decision in Smith v. TechCorp marks a pivotal shift in how remote work accommodations are to be handled under the ADA. Employers must act promptly to align their policies and practices with this new legal landscape to mitigate risks and ensure compliance.
