## New Rule on Tipped Employee Wage Calculation

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule clarifying the calculation of wages for tipped employees, with the rule set to take effect on August 1, 2026. This rule provides specific guidance on how employers should calculate wages when tipped employees perform both tipped and non-tipped duties, a practice commonly referred to as the dual jobs regulation.

The rule amends the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regulations, specifically 29 C.F.R. § 531.56(e), which addresses when an employer can take a tip credit for time spent on non-tipped duties. Under the new rule, employers can only apply the tip credit for time spent on tasks that directly support tipped duties if these tasks do not exceed 20% of the employee's workweek or 30 continuous minutes.

## Implications for Employers

Employers in the hospitality and service industries, where tipping is prevalent, need to reassess their payroll practices to ensure compliance. The rule requires detailed tracking of employee duties and time spent on each task, necessitating more precise record-keeping and potentially new time-tracking systems.

Failure to comply with these requirements could lead to wage and hour disputes, as employees may claim back pay for improperly calculated wages. Employers should review their current practices and train managers to differentiate between tipped and non-tipped duties accurately.

## Action Items

1. **Audit Current Practices**: Review current wage calculation methods for tipped employees to ensure they align with the new rule. Pay special attention to how time is tracked for non-tipped duties.

2. **Update Time-Tracking Systems**: Implement or upgrade time-tracking systems to accurately record time spent on tipped versus non-tipped tasks. This may involve integrating new software solutions or updating existing systems to capture detailed data.

3. **Train Managers and Staff**: Conduct training sessions for managers and employees to understand the distinctions between tipped and non-tipped duties and the importance of accurate time reporting.

4. **Monitor Compliance**: Establish regular audits to ensure ongoing compliance with the new regulations. This could involve periodic reviews of time records and payroll calculations.

By addressing these action items, employers can mitigate the risk of non-compliance and potential legal challenges. The DOL's clarification aims to protect employees' wages while providing clear guidelines for employers, ensuring fair compensation practices in the service industry.
