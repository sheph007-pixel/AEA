## DOL Clarifies Independent Contractor Status

On May 10, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance clarifying the criteria for determining independent contractor status under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This update aims to provide clearer distinctions between employees and independent contractors, impacting businesses across various sectors.

The guidance re-emphasizes the economic realities test, focusing on six core factors to assess whether a worker is economically dependent on the employer or is in business for themselves. These factors include the nature and degree of control over work, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, the amount of skill required, the permanence of the working relationship, the extent of integration into the employer's business, and the degree of independent business organization and operation.

## Impact on Employers

This clarification is crucial for employers who engage independent contractors, as misclassification can lead to significant legal and financial penalties. The DOL's emphasis on the economic realities test highlights the importance of evaluating the totality of circumstances rather than relying on a single factor.

Employers must reassess their current workforce to ensure compliance with the clarified guidelines. Misclassification risks include back pay for overtime and minimum wage violations, as well as penalties under 29 U.S.C. § 216(b).

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Worker Classifications**: Conduct a thorough audit of all independent contractor relationships. Examine contracts and work arrangements against the six factors highlighted by the DOL.

2. **Update Contracts**: Ensure that contracts with independent contractors explicitly outline the nature of the working relationship, emphasizing independence and lack of control by the employer.

3. **Train HR and Management**: Provide training to HR personnel and management on the updated guidance to prevent future misclassifications.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal advisors to review current practices and make necessary adjustments to align with the DOL's guidance.

5. **Document Compliance Efforts**: Maintain detailed records of classification decisions and the rationale behind them to defend against potential audits or legal challenges.

The DOL's new guidance underscores the importance of proper classification to avoid costly litigation and penalties. Employers should act promptly to align their practices with these clarified standards.
