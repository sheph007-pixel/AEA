## IRS Issues New Guidance on QSEHRA Limits for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidance on June 15, 2026, regarding the contribution limits and compliance requirements for Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangements (QSEHRAs) for the upcoming year. This guidance impacts small businesses with fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees that do not offer group health plans.

Under the new guidelines, the maximum annual QSEHRA contribution limit for 2026 is set at $5,850 for individual coverage and $11,800 for family coverage. These limits reflect an adjustment for inflation, consistent with Section 9831(d) of the Internal Revenue Code. Employers utilizing QSEHRAs must adhere to these caps to maintain compliance and avoid penalties.

## Key Compliance Requirements

The IRS guidance also emphasizes the importance of meeting specific notice requirements. Employers must provide eligible employees with a written notice at least 90 days before the start of the plan year or upon the employee's eligibility date. This notice should include the amount of the employee's permitted benefit under the QSEHRA for the year, information on how the QSEHRA might affect premium tax credits, and a statement instructing employees to inform any health insurance marketplaces about the QSEHRA benefit when applying for advance premium tax credits.

Failure to provide this notice can result in a penalty of $50 per employee, capped at $2,500 annually, under IRC Section 6652(o).

## Action Items for Employers

Employers offering QSEHRAs should take immediate steps to ensure compliance with the updated contribution limits and notice requirements. This includes:

- **Reviewing Contribution Limits**: Adjust any planned contributions for 2026 to align with the new IRS limits.
- **Updating Employee Notices**: Prepare and distribute the required QSEHRA notices to all eligible employees in advance of the 90-day deadline.
- **Consulting with Tax Advisors**: Engage with tax professionals to confirm that all aspects of the QSEHRA plan comply with IRS regulations and to strategize for any potential impacts on employee tax credits.

Small employers that effectively manage these requirements can continue to offer valuable health benefits while maintaining compliance with federal tax laws.
