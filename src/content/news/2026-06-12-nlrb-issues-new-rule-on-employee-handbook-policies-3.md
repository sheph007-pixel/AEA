## NLRB's New Rule on Employee Handbook Policies

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a new rule on June 10, 2026, affecting employee handbook policies. This rule, effective July 31, 2026, revises how the NLRB evaluates whether specific handbook provisions violate employees' rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA).

The rule reinstates the standard set in Lutheran Heritage Village-Livonia, 343 NLRB 646 (2004), which was overturned in 2017. Under this standard, a policy is deemed unlawful if employees could reasonably interpret it to restrict their rights to engage in protected concerted activities. This includes rights such as discussing wages or working conditions with coworkers.

### Key Changes

The NLRB's new rule targets policies that could be interpreted as infringing on employees' NLRA rights, even if the employer did not intend to restrict these rights. This includes policies related to confidentiality, social media, and workplace conduct.

Employers must now scrutinize their handbook policies to ensure they do not inadvertently infringe on employees' rights to organize and discuss work conditions. Policies that are overly broad or ambiguous could be challenged.

### Employer Action Items

1. **Review and Revise Policies:** Employers should review their employee handbooks and amend any policies that could be interpreted as limiting employees' rights under the NLRA. Pay particular attention to confidentiality clauses and social media policies.

2. **Legal Consultation:** Consider consulting with legal counsel to ensure compliance with the new NLRB rule. This can help prevent potential legal challenges and ensure that policies are clearly defined and lawful.

3. **Training for Management:** Train HR personnel and managers on the implications of the new rule. Ensure they understand which employee activities are protected under the NLRA and how to apply policies consistently.

4. **Document Changes:** Keep records of any changes made to employee handbooks and the rationale behind these changes. This documentation can be crucial if policies are challenged.

### Implications for Small and Mid-Sized Businesses

For businesses with 2-500 employees, the new rule means increased scrutiny of handbook policies. Small to mid-sized businesses may lack the legal resources of larger corporations, making it crucial to proactively address potential compliance issues.

The NLRB's decision underscores the importance of clear and precise language in employee handbooks. By aligning policies with the new rule, employers can reduce the risk of legal challenges and maintain a compliant workplace environment.

In conclusion, the NLRB's new rule on employee handbook policies requires immediate attention from employers. By taking proactive steps to review and revise policies, businesses can ensure compliance and safeguard against potential legal issues.
