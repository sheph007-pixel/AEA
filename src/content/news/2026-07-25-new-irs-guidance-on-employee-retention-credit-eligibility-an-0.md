## IRS Clarifies Employee Retention Credit Eligibility

On July 20, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new guidance regarding the Employee Retention Credit (ERC), aiming to clarify eligibility criteria and compliance requirements for employers. The ERC, established under the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act, provides a refundable tax credit to businesses that retain employees during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The updated guidance specifically addresses ambiguities surrounding the "gross receipts" test and the definition of "full-time employees" under the credit. According to the IRS, businesses must compare their quarterly gross receipts to the same quarter in 2019 to determine eligibility. A decline of more than 20% qualifies employers for the credit under 26 U.S.C. § 3134.

## Key Compliance Points

The IRS guidance emphasizes that only wages paid to employees who are not providing services due to COVID-19-related restrictions or a significant decline in gross receipts are eligible for the credit. Employers must ensure that these wages do not overlap with wages used to claim other credits, such as the Work Opportunity Tax Credit or the Paid Family and Medical Leave Credit.

Furthermore, the IRS clarified that employers can only include wages paid to full-time employees, as defined under the Affordable Care Act (30 hours per week or 130 hours per month), in their ERC calculations. This clarification aims to prevent the miscalculation of credits based on wages paid to part-time or seasonal workers.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should immediately review their payroll records and gross receipts to ensure compliance with the updated IRS guidance. It is crucial to conduct a thorough audit of wages paid to full-time employees during the applicable quarters and cross-reference these with other claimed credits to avoid duplication.

Additionally, businesses must maintain detailed documentation to substantiate their eligibility and the amount of credit claimed. This includes records of gross receipts, employee hours, and any government orders that restricted operations due to the pandemic.

Employers should also consider consulting with tax professionals or legal advisors to navigate the complexities of the ERC and ensure full compliance with IRS requirements. Failure to adhere to these guidelines could result in disallowed credits and potential penalties.

The IRS's recent guidance aims to streamline the ERC process and provide clarity for employers seeking to benefit from this significant tax relief measure. By taking proactive steps to align with these directives, businesses can maximize their credit claims while minimizing the risk of non-compliance.
