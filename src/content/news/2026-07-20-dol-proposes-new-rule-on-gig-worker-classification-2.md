## DOL Proposes New Rule on Gig Worker Classification

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on July 19, 2026, aimed at redefining the classification of gig workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposal seeks to clarify whether gig workers should be classified as independent contractors or employees, impacting businesses across various sectors that rely on gig economy labor.

### Key Details of the Proposal

The proposed rule introduces a new test for determining worker classification, focusing on the economic reality of the worker's relationship with the business. The DOL suggests a multi-factor analysis that includes the nature and degree of control over the work, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, the permanence of the relationship, and the degree of skill required for the work.

This proposal marks a shift from the Trump administration's rule, which was more lenient towards classifying workers as independent contractors. The Biden administration's approach aims to provide clearer guidelines and potentially expand the number of workers eligible for employee benefits and protections.

### Impact on Employers

If implemented, this rule could significantly impact companies utilizing gig workers, particularly in industries such as ride-sharing, delivery services, and freelance platforms. Businesses may face increased labor costs if gig workers are reclassified as employees, as they would then be entitled to minimum wage, overtime pay, and other benefits under the FLSA.

Employers should assess their current workforce classifications and consider potential adjustments to their business models. Legal counsel may be necessary to navigate the complexities of the new classification test and to ensure compliance with the updated regulations.

### Next Steps for Employers

The DOL has opened a 60-day public comment period, allowing stakeholders to provide feedback on the proposed rule. Employers are encouraged to submit comments by September 17, 2026, to voice their concerns or support for the rule.

In the meantime, businesses should:

1. **Review Current Worker Classifications**: Conduct an audit of current gig workers to determine potential impacts of reclassification.
   
2. **Consult Legal Experts**: Seek advice from labor law specialists to understand the implications of the new rule and prepare for possible changes.

3. **Prepare for Financial Implications**: Consider the financial impact of potential reclassification, including increased costs for wages and benefits.

4. **Engage in the Comment Process**: Participate in the public comment period to influence the final rule's outcome.

By proactively addressing these areas, employers can better prepare for the potential changes in gig worker classification and mitigate any adverse effects on their operations.
