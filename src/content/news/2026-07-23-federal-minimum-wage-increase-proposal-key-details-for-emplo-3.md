## Federal Minimum Wage Increase Proposal

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a rule on July 20, 2026, to increase the federal minimum wage to $15 per hour by 2027. This proposal, if enacted, would significantly impact employers across the United States, particularly those currently paying less than the proposed rate. The current federal minimum wage of $7.25 per hour has been in place since 2009.

### Proposed Changes

The DOL's proposal outlines a phased approach to reach the $15 per hour mark by 2027. The increase would occur in increments, starting with a rise to $10 per hour in 2026, followed by annual raises until the $15 target is achieved. This change aims to address wage stagnation and improve living standards for low-income workers.

### Impact on Employers

Employers with operations in states that already mandate higher minimum wages may see minimal impact. However, businesses in states adhering to the federal minimum wage will need to plan for increased labor costs. Industries heavily reliant on minimum wage workers, such as retail and hospitality, could face significant financial adjustments.

### Compliance and Planning

Employers should begin reviewing their payroll budgets and labor cost structures to accommodate these potential changes. Consider conducting a wage analysis to assess the impact on your business. It's crucial to plan for the gradual wage increases and adjust financial forecasts accordingly.

### Legal Considerations

Employers must ensure compliance with both federal and state wage laws. States with higher minimum wages will not be affected by the federal increase, but businesses operating across state lines need to remain vigilant about varying requirements. Additionally, employers should review the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) to ensure adherence to all wage and hour regulations.

### Next Steps

The DOL has opened a public comment period for the proposed rule, allowing stakeholders to provide feedback until September 15, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in this process to express concerns or support. Final rulemaking is expected by early 2027.

In the meantime, employers should prepare for potential implementation by reviewing employee compensation structures and considering adjustments to hiring and staffing strategies. This proactive approach will help mitigate the financial impact of the proposed wage increase and ensure smooth compliance with new regulations.
