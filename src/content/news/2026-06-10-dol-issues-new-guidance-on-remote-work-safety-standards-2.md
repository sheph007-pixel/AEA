## DOL Clarifies Remote Work Safety Obligations

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance on June 1, 2026, detailing employer responsibilities for ensuring safety standards in remote work environments. This guidance, effective July 15, 2026, aims to address the growing prevalence of remote work and the associated safety concerns that employers must manage under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSHA).

## Key Provisions

The DOL's guidance specifies that employers are responsible for identifying and mitigating hazards in remote workspaces. This includes ergonomic assessments to prevent musculoskeletal disorders and ensuring that remote work setups comply with OSHA standards. Employers must provide remote employees with resources and training to maintain a safe work environment, even when working from home.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should conduct virtual safety assessments for remote workers to identify potential hazards. This can be done through video calls or detailed self-assessment checklists provided to employees. Employers must also update their safety policies to include remote work provisions and ensure that all employees are trained on these new standards.

Additionally, employers should document all safety assessments and training to demonstrate compliance with OSHA requirements. This documentation will be crucial if an employee files a complaint or if an OSHA inspection occurs.

## Legal Implications

Failure to comply with the new guidance could result in penalties under OSHA. Employers should be aware that remote work safety is now a significant compliance area and must be treated with the same seriousness as on-site safety. The DOL's guidance does not introduce new laws but clarifies existing obligations under 29 U.S.C. § 654(a), which mandates that employers provide a workplace free from recognized hazards.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new guidance on remote work safety standards is a crucial development for employers managing remote teams. By taking proactive steps to assess and mitigate risks in remote work environments, employers can ensure compliance with OSHA standards and protect their workforce. Employers should act promptly to integrate these safety measures into their remote work policies before the July 15, 2026, effective date.
