## New DOL Proposal Could Redefine Gig Worker Status

On May 28, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule that seeks to redefine the classification of gig workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposal could significantly impact the gig economy, affecting companies that rely heavily on independent contractors, such as rideshare and delivery services.

### Key Details of the Proposal

The proposed rule introduces a stricter test for determining whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor. The DOL's proposal emphasizes the "economic reality" test, focusing on whether a worker is economically dependent on the employer for work. This includes evaluating factors such as the degree of control the employer has over the worker and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss based on managerial skill.

### Implications for Employers

If enacted, this rule could lead to a substantial reclassification of gig workers as employees, which would require companies to provide minimum wage, overtime pay, and other benefits mandated by the FLSA. This shift could increase labor costs for businesses that have traditionally relied on flexible, non-employee labor models.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Review Current Worker Classifications:** Employers should audit their workforce to ensure that current classifications align with the proposed criteria. This includes evaluating contracts and the actual working relationship with gig workers.
  
- **Prepare for Potential Cost Increases:** Businesses should assess the financial impact of potential reclassification, including the costs associated with employee benefits and compliance with wage and hour laws.

- **Engage in the Comment Period:** The DOL has opened a 60-day public comment period ending July 27, 2026. Employers should consider submitting comments to express concerns or support for the proposed rule, as stakeholder feedback could influence the final regulation.

### Next Steps

The DOL's proposal is part of a broader effort to address labor rights in the evolving gig economy. Employers should stay abreast of developments in this area, as changes to worker classification rules could have far-reaching implications for workforce management and business operations.

This proposed rule marks a significant potential shift in labor policy, and its final outcome will be closely watched by businesses, workers, and policymakers alike.
