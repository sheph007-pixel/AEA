## EEOC Updates Religious Accommodation Guidelines

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on June 15, 2026, detailing employers' obligations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 regarding religious accommodations. This update aims to provide clarity following recent court decisions and to help employers better understand their responsibilities in accommodating employees' religious practices.

Title VII prohibits employment discrimination based on religion and requires employers to reasonably accommodate employees' religious beliefs and practices unless doing so would cause undue hardship on the business. The EEOC's new guidance offers detailed examples and scenarios to illustrate what constitutes a reasonable accommodation and what might be considered an undue hardship.

## Key Changes and Clarifications

The updated guidance emphasizes the importance of engaging in an interactive process with employees requesting religious accommodations. It highlights that employers should consider various accommodation options, such as flexible scheduling, voluntary shift substitutions, and modifications to workplace policies, to accommodate religious practices.

One significant clarification in the guidance is the definition of "undue hardship." The EEOC now aligns its interpretation with the Supreme Court's decision in *Groff v. DeJoy*, which established that an accommodation causes undue hardship if it results in substantial increased costs or operational burdens on the employer. This is a shift from the previous standard, which allowed for a broader interpretation of what constituted an undue hardship.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should review their current policies and practices related to religious accommodations to ensure compliance with the updated EEOC guidance. This includes:

- **Training HR Staff and Managers:** Ensure that those involved in handling accommodation requests are familiar with the new guidance and understand the importance of the interactive process.
- **Policy Review and Updates:** Examine current accommodation policies to ensure they reflect the latest definitions and standards of undue hardship.
- **Documentation:** Maintain thorough documentation of all accommodation requests and the steps taken to address them, including the interactive process and any accommodations offered or denied.

## Conclusion

The EEOC's updated guidance on religious accommodations provides a clearer framework for employers to follow, emphasizing the need for a collaborative approach in handling accommodation requests. By aligning policies with the new guidance, employers can better navigate religious accommodation requests and minimize the risk of legal challenges. As always, consulting with legal counsel when addressing complex accommodation issues is advisable to ensure compliance and protect against potential liabilities.
