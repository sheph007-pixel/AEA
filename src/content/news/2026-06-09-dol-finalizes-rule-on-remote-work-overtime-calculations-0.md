## DOL Clarifies Overtime Calculations for Remote Workers

On June 8, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule clarifying how overtime pay should be calculated for remote workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule, effective August 1, 2026, addresses ambiguities related to tracking hours and calculating overtime for employees who work from home or other remote locations.

### Key Provisions

The new rule specifies that employers must ensure accurate tracking of hours for remote employees, similar to on-site workers. Electronic timekeeping systems are recommended to capture actual hours worked, including any overtime. Employers are reminded that the FLSA's overtime threshold of $684 per week (29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1)) remains applicable, and all hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek must be compensated at one and a half times the employee's regular rate.

### Employer Responsibilities

Employers must implement reliable systems to track remote work hours. This includes maintaining records of start and stop times, as well as any breaks taken during the workday. Employers should also provide clear written policies regarding overtime work, emphasizing that unauthorized overtime must still be compensated if the work is performed.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Ensure that remote work policies clearly outline expectations for time tracking and overtime approval. Update employee handbooks to reflect these changes.

2. **Implement Timekeeping Systems**: Invest in or upgrade electronic timekeeping systems to accurately capture remote work hours. Consider software that integrates with existing HR systems for seamless reporting.

3. **Train Managers and Employees**: Conduct training sessions for both managers and remote employees to ensure understanding of the new regulations and the importance of accurate timekeeping.

4. **Audit Current Practices**: Perform an internal audit of current timekeeping and overtime calculation practices to identify any gaps or areas of non-compliance.

### Potential Implications

The rule aims to prevent wage theft and ensure that remote workers receive fair compensation for all hours worked. Non-compliance could result in costly penalties and back pay obligations. Employers should take proactive steps to align their practices with the new requirements to avoid legal challenges.

By addressing these issues now, employers can mitigate the risk of disputes and ensure compliance with federal labor standards. The DOL's clarification provides an opportunity for businesses to refine their remote work policies and protect themselves from potential liabilities.
