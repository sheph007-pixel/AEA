## NYC AI Hiring Law Takes Effect

New York City's Local Law 144, which regulates the use of automated employment decision tools (AEDTs) in hiring and promotions, took effect on July 5, 2026. This law impacts employers using AI-driven tools to screen candidates or make employment decisions.

Under the new law, employers must conduct bias audits on their AEDTs annually. The audit must assess whether the tool has a disparate impact on individuals based on race, ethnicity, or sex. Employers are required to make the results of these audits publicly available on their websites.

## Compliance Requirements

Employers must also provide candidates with a written notice at least ten business days before using an AEDT. This notice should inform candidates that an automated tool will be used in the decision-making process and provide details about the type of data collected.

Failure to comply with these requirements can result in civil penalties. The New York City Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) enforces the law and may impose fines ranging from $500 to $1,500 per violation.

## Action Items for Employers

- **Conduct Bias Audits:** Ensure that any AEDT used in hiring or promotions undergoes an annual bias audit. Document the findings and make them accessible on your company's website.

- **Update Candidate Notices:** Revise your candidate communication processes to include the required notice about AEDT usage at least ten business days before the tool is applied.

- **Review Vendor Agreements:** If using third-party vendors for AI-driven hiring tools, confirm that they comply with Local Law 144 and can provide necessary audit documentation.

- **Train HR Teams:** Educate HR personnel on the new requirements to ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties.

## Broader Implications

While New York City is currently at the forefront of regulating AI in employment decisions, other jurisdictions may follow suit. Employers nationwide should monitor developments and prepare for similar regulations in other states or cities. 

This law underscores the growing scrutiny of AI tools in the workplace and the importance of transparency and fairness in their application. Employers should proactively address potential biases in their hiring processes to align with evolving legal standards.
