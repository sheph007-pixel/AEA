## IRS Updates Telecommuting Expense Deduction Rules

On May 15, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new regulations regarding the tax treatment of telecommuting expenses, effective for the 2026 tax year. This update affects how employers reimburse employees for home office expenses and the subsequent tax implications for both parties. The changes are codified in 26 U.S.C. § 280A, which governs deductions for business use of a home.

## Key Changes and Implications

Under the new rule, the IRS has clarified that certain telecommuting expenses reimbursed by employers can be excluded from employees' taxable income. This includes costs related to internet services, office supplies, and a portion of utility bills, provided they are directly attributable to the employee's work from home. The regulation aims to streamline the deduction process and provide clearer guidance on what constitutes a deductible expense.

Employers should note that to qualify for these deductions, expenses must be substantiated by detailed records. This means that employees need to maintain logs or receipts that clearly separate personal use from business use. Failure to do so may result in the expenses being deemed taxable income.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Reimbursement Policies**: Employers should review and potentially revise their reimbursement policies to align with the new IRS guidelines. This includes defining what expenses are eligible for reimbursement and ensuring that employees understand the documentation requirements.

2. **Update Employee Agreements**: Amend telecommuting agreements to reflect these changes, specifying the types of expenses that will be reimbursed and the documentation required.

3. **Implement Record-Keeping Systems**: Establish or update systems for tracking and documenting reimbursable expenses. This might involve new software solutions or updated procedures to ensure compliance with IRS requirements.

4. **Communicate Changes**: Clearly communicate these changes to employees, particularly those who telecommute regularly. Providing training sessions or informational materials can help ensure that employees understand how to document their expenses properly.

## Conclusion

The IRS's revision of telecommuting expense deductions provides an opportunity for employers to refine their reimbursement practices and potentially reduce taxable income for employees. By taking proactive steps to align with these new regulations, employers can enhance compliance and support their telecommuting workforce effectively.
