## DOL Finalizes Prevailing Wage Rule for Federal Contractors

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on May 30, 2026, updating the prevailing wage requirements under the Davis-Bacon Act for federal construction projects. This rule impacts contractors and subcontractors working on federally funded or assisted construction projects, revising the methodology for determining prevailing wages and expanding coverage to more projects.

The Davis-Bacon Act (40 U.S.C. § 3141 et seq.) mandates that contractors pay laborers and mechanics no less than the locally prevailing wages and fringe benefits for corresponding work on similar projects in the area. The new rule modifies how these wage determinations are calculated, aiming to ensure workers on federal projects receive fair compensation reflective of current local standards.

### Key Changes in Wage Determination

The rule introduces a new methodology for calculating prevailing wages, incorporating data from a broader range of sources, including collective bargaining agreements and wage surveys from similar private projects. This shift is intended to provide a more accurate reflection of the local wage landscape, potentially increasing wages for workers on federal projects.

Additionally, the rule expands the scope of projects covered by the Davis-Bacon Act. It now includes certain federally assisted projects that were previously exempt, thereby increasing the number of workers who benefit from the prevailing wage protections.

### Compliance Timeline and Employer Action Items

The rule takes effect on July 15, 2026. Contractors and subcontractors must review and adjust their payroll practices to comply with the updated wage determinations. Employers should audit their current projects to identify those affected by the expanded coverage and ensure payroll systems are updated to reflect the new wage rates.

Contractors are advised to consult the updated wage determinations published by the DOL and adjust their bids on upcoming federal projects accordingly. Failure to comply with the new wage requirements can result in penalties, including debarment from future federal contracts.

### Implications for Federal Contractors

This rule is part of the DOL's ongoing efforts to ensure fair wages for workers on federal projects. While it may increase labor costs for contractors, it aims to improve the quality of work and reduce turnover by ensuring competitive wages.

Contractors should engage with legal counsel or HR professionals to navigate these changes effectively. Understanding the new wage determination process and its implications is crucial for maintaining compliance and competitive advantage in bidding for federal contracts.

In summary, the DOL's final rule on prevailing wages under the Davis-Bacon Act represents a significant update for federal contractors. Employers must act promptly to align their practices with the new requirements before the July 15, 2026, effective date.
