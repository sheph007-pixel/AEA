## IRS Adjusts Mileage Reimbursement Rates for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has announced an increase in the standard mileage reimbursement rates for business travel, effective January 1, 2026. This adjustment reflects the rising costs associated with operating a vehicle, including fuel prices and maintenance expenses. The new rate for business use will be 65.5 cents per mile, up from 62.5 cents in 2025.

The IRS sets these rates annually to provide a guideline for employers reimbursing employees for business-related travel expenses. For many businesses, this rate serves as a benchmark for calculating reimbursements, ensuring that employees are fairly compensated for the use of their personal vehicles.

### Impact on Employers

Employers should update their travel reimbursement policies to align with the new IRS rate. This adjustment is crucial for maintaining compliance and ensuring that employees do not incur out-of-pocket expenses beyond the standard rate. Companies that use a mileage reimbursement system based on the IRS rate should revise their internal systems and communicate the changes to employees promptly.

### Action Items

1. **Update Reimbursement Policies:** Employers should amend their travel reimbursement policies to reflect the new rate of 65.5 cents per mile. This ensures compliance with IRS guidelines and fairness to employees.

2. **Communicate Changes:** Inform employees of the new reimbursement rate and effective date. Clear communication helps prevent confusion and ensures smooth implementation.

3. **Adjust Payroll Systems:** Ensure that payroll and accounting systems are updated to accommodate the new rate. This includes any software used for tracking and processing mileage reimbursements.

### Considerations

While the IRS rate is a guideline, employers are not legally required to reimburse at this rate. However, reimbursing below the IRS rate could result in taxable income for employees. Employers should weigh the administrative burden of setting a different rate against the simplicity and tax advantages of adhering to the IRS standard.

Employers who opt to reimburse at a rate higher than the IRS standard should be aware that the excess amount may be subject to taxation unless the arrangement qualifies as a non-taxable accountable plan under IRS rules.

By adjusting to the new mileage rates, businesses can maintain equitable compensation practices and uphold employee satisfaction. As operational costs continue to fluctuate, staying aligned with IRS guidelines can help mitigate potential disputes and tax complications.
