## New Federal Guidelines

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has issued new guidelines on retirement plan fee disclosures, set to take effect on January 1, 2026. This update aims to enhance transparency for participants in employer-sponsored retirement plans, such as 401(k)s. The guidelines, under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA), require plan administrators to provide clearer information about fees and expenses associated with retirement plans.

## Key Changes

The updated guidelines emphasize the need for more detailed and comprehensible disclosure of fees. Plan administrators must now break down administrative, investment, and individual service fees more explicitly. This change is intended to help participants better understand how fees impact their retirement savings. Additionally, the guidelines mandate that disclosures be presented in a standardized format, making it easier for participants to compare different plans.

## Implications for Employers

Employers sponsoring retirement plans must ensure compliance with these new guidelines. This involves reviewing current disclosure practices and making necessary adjustments to align with the new requirements. Employers should collaborate with plan administrators and service providers to update fee disclosure documents and ensure they meet the new standards.

## Action Items

1. **Review Current Disclosures**: Employers should audit existing retirement plan fee disclosures to identify areas needing improvement. Ensure that all fee categories are clearly defined and presented in the required format.

2. **Coordinate with Plan Administrators**: Work closely with plan administrators to update and distribute the new fee disclosure documents. Ensure that all parties understand the new requirements and timelines.

3. **Educate Employees**: Consider hosting informational sessions or providing resources to help employees understand the changes in fee disclosures and how these fees affect their retirement savings.

4. **Monitor Compliance**: Establish a system to regularly review and update fee disclosures as needed, ensuring ongoing compliance with DOL guidelines.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new guidelines on retirement plan fee disclosures represent a significant change in how fees are communicated to plan participants. Employers must take proactive steps to ensure compliance by the January 1, 2026 deadline. By improving transparency, these changes aim to empower employees to make more informed decisions about their retirement savings.
