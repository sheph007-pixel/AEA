## NLRB Expands Union Access Rights

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a new rule on June 15, 2026, expanding union access rights on employer property. This change impacts how employers manage union organizers' access to non-work areas, such as break rooms and parking lots, during non-working hours. The rule modifies the interpretation of Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), which protects employees' rights to engage in concerted activities for mutual aid or protection.

## Key Changes and Implications

The revised rule allows union organizers to access non-work areas of employer property more freely, provided their activities do not disrupt operations. This marks a significant shift from previous guidance, which allowed employers more leeway to restrict such access. The rule emphasizes that employers must demonstrate substantial business justification to deny access, such as safety concerns or interference with operations.

For businesses, particularly those with limited experience in unionized environments, this change necessitates a review of current property access policies. Employers should ensure that any restrictions on access to non-work areas are clearly justified and documented to withstand potential legal scrutiny.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should take the following steps in response to the new NLRB rule:

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Audit existing policies regarding access to non-work areas. Amend them to align with the new NLRB requirements, ensuring that any restrictions are legally defensible.

2. **Training and Communication**: Educate management and security personnel about the new rule to prevent unauthorized denial of access. Clear communication will help avoid potential unfair labor practice charges.

3. **Document Justifications**: Maintain detailed records of any instances where access is restricted, documenting the business rationale behind these decisions. This documentation will be crucial if challenged by unions or the NLRB.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with labor law experts to ensure compliance with the new rule and to prepare for any potential union activities on your property.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's expansion of union access rights represents a significant shift in labor relations. Employers must adapt quickly to these changes to avoid legal pitfalls and maintain smooth operations. By updating policies, training staff, and consulting legal experts, businesses can navigate these new requirements effectively.
