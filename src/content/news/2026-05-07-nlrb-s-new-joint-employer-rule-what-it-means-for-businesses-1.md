## NLRB's New Rule Redefines Joint Employer Status

On April 26, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a final rule redefining the criteria for joint employer status under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This rule, effective June 1, 2026, broadens the definition of what constitutes a joint employer, affecting businesses in franchise, staffing, and subcontracting arrangements.

## Expanded Criteria for Joint Employer Status

Under the new rule, a company can be considered a joint employer if it has indirect control over essential terms and conditions of employment, such as wages, hours, and working conditions, or if it has the potential to exercise such control. This marks a departure from the previous standard, which required direct and immediate control over employment terms.

## Implications for Businesses

The expanded definition increases the liability and bargaining obligations for businesses deemed joint employers. Companies may now face increased exposure to unfair labor practice charges and collective bargaining responsibilities. This change could significantly impact industries reliant on contracted labor, such as hospitality, construction, and logistics.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Contracts:** Businesses should review existing contracts and agreements with staffing agencies, subcontractors, and franchisees to assess potential joint employer risks.
   
2. **Assess Control Practices:** Evaluate the level of control exercised over contracted workers. Consider minimizing direct involvement in day-to-day management to reduce joint employer liability.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Engage with legal counsel to understand the implications of the new rule on your business operations and to develop strategies for compliance.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's new joint employer rule signals a shift towards broader interpretations of employment relationships. Businesses should take proactive steps to understand the rule's implications and adjust their practices accordingly to mitigate potential risks.
