## Expanded Family Definitions in FMLA

On July 1, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) issued final regulations modifying the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) to broaden the definition of "family member." This change impacts employers with 50 or more employees, who must comply with the FMLA's requirements for unpaid leave. The updated regulation, found in 29 C.F.R. § 825.102, now includes "chosen family" and "close friend" as eligible family members for whom employees can take leave.

The FMLA traditionally allowed up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for specific family and medical reasons, including caring for a spouse, child, or parent with a serious health condition. The new rule expands eligibility, acknowledging that familial relationships extend beyond biological or legal ties. This change reflects evolving family structures and aims to provide greater flexibility for employees in caregiving roles.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must update their FMLA policies and employee handbooks to reflect these new definitions. HR professionals should prepare to handle an increased volume of leave requests and verify the legitimacy of "chosen family" claims. The DOL recommends maintaining documentation that supports the existence of a "close friend" relationship, similar to existing practices for verifying family relationships.

Employers should also consider training HR staff on the nuances of these changes to ensure compliance and prevent potential discrimination claims. The expansion of eligible family members could lead to more employees qualifying for FMLA leave, impacting workforce planning and staffing needs.

## Action Items

1. **Revise Policies**: Update FMLA policies to include "chosen family" and "close friend" in the definition of family members. Ensure that this information is communicated clearly in employee handbooks and onboarding materials.

2. **Training**: Conduct training sessions for HR staff and managers to familiarize them with the new definitions and documentation requirements. Emphasize the importance of non-discriminatory practices when evaluating leave requests.

3. **Documentation**: Develop a process for employees to substantiate claims of "chosen family" or "close friend" relationships. This might include affidavits or other forms of proof that demonstrate the nature of the relationship.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the potential for legal challenges, consult with legal counsel to ensure that your FMLA policies align with the new regulations and that you are prepared to handle any disputes that arise.

The updated FMLA regulations underscore the importance of adapting to modern family dynamics. Employers should act promptly to integrate these changes into their practices to support employees while maintaining compliance with federal law.
