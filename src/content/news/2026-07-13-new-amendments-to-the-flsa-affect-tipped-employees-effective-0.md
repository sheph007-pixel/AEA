## FLSA Amendments Impact Tipped Employees

The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) has been amended to include changes affecting tipped employees, effective January 1, 2026. The amendments revise tip pooling arrangements and adjust the minimum wage requirements for tipped workers, posing significant compliance challenges for employers in the hospitality and service industries.

### Key Changes to Tip Pooling

The FLSA amendments clarify and expand the rules around tip pooling. Under the new provisions, employers can now mandate tip pooling among employees who customarily and regularly receive tips, such as servers and bartenders. However, the amendments prohibit management and supervisors from participating in these pools, aligning with previous Department of Labor (DOL) guidance.

Additionally, the amendments introduce stricter recordkeeping requirements. Employers must now maintain detailed records of tip pooling arrangements, including the names of employees involved and the amounts distributed. Failure to comply with these requirements could result in penalties under 29 U.S.C. § 216(b).

### Minimum Wage Adjustments

The amendments also address the minimum wage for tipped employees. While the federal minimum wage remains at $7.25 per hour, the amendments increase the minimum cash wage for tipped employees to $5.00 per hour, up from the previous $2.13. Employers must ensure that the combination of the cash wage and tips meets or exceeds the federal minimum wage.

Employers should review their current pay practices to ensure compliance with these new wage requirements. Any discrepancies must be corrected before the January 1, 2026, deadline to avoid potential back pay liabilities and penalties.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Tip Pooling Practices:** Audit current tip pooling arrangements to ensure compliance with the new rules. Remove any managers or supervisors from the pools and update records accordingly.

2. **Update Payroll Systems:** Adjust payroll systems to reflect the new minimum cash wage for tipped employees. Ensure that all tipped employees receive at least $5.00 per hour in cash wages.

3. **Enhance Recordkeeping:** Implement or update systems to maintain detailed records of tip pooling arrangements and distributions. Ensure these records are easily accessible for DOL inspections.

4. **Train Staff:** Conduct training sessions for managers and payroll staff to familiarize them with the new requirements and compliance procedures.

By addressing these action items, employers can mitigate the risk of non-compliance and ensure a smooth transition to the new FLSA requirements for tipped employees.
