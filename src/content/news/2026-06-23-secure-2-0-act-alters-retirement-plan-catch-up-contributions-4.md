## New IRS Guidance on Catch-Up Contributions

The IRS released guidance on June 15, 2026, regarding changes to catch-up contributions under the SECURE 2.0 Act, affecting high earners starting in 2026. This guidance targets employees making over $145,000 annually, as defined in Internal Revenue Code § 414(v), who will now have to direct catch-up contributions to Roth accounts instead of traditional pre-tax accounts.

## Key Changes and Impact

Under the SECURE 2.0 Act, employees aged 50 and older are allowed to make additional catch-up contributions to their retirement plans. However, beginning January 1, 2026, those earning more than $145,000 in the previous year must allocate these contributions to designated Roth accounts. This change is significant as it alters the tax treatment of these contributions, which will now be made with after-tax dollars, allowing for tax-free withdrawals in retirement.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers need to update their retirement plan documentation and payroll systems to accommodate these changes. Specifically, they should:

1. **Amend Plan Documents:** Review and amend retirement plan documents to allow for Roth catch-up contributions for eligible employees.
   
2. **Payroll Adjustments:** Ensure payroll systems can differentiate between pre-tax and Roth contributions, especially for employees who exceed the $145,000 income threshold.

3. **Employee Communication:** Clearly communicate these changes to employees, particularly those nearing retirement age, to help them understand the implications for their retirement planning.

## Compliance and Deadlines

Employers must ensure compliance with these new rules by the end of 2025 to avoid potential penalties. The IRS has indicated that failure to implement these changes could result in disqualification of the retirement plan, emphasizing the need for timely updates.

## Conclusion

The IRS guidance on the SECURE 2.0 Act's catch-up contributions underscores the importance of proactive planning for both employers and employees. By updating plan documents, adjusting payroll systems, and effectively communicating with employees, employers can ensure compliance and help employees maximize their retirement savings under the new rules.
