## New Guidance on 401(k) Plan Auto-Enrollment

The Department of Labor (DOL) has issued new guidance on 401(k) plan auto-enrollment, effective January 1, 2025. This guidance clarifies the requirements under the SECURE 2.0 Act, aiming to increase retirement savings among employees. Companies with 401(k) plans must review their enrollment processes to ensure compliance with these updated guidelines.

The SECURE 2.0 Act mandates that all new 401(k) and 403(b) plans automatically enroll employees at a minimum contribution rate of 3%, escalating annually by 1% until reaching at least 10%, but not more than 15%. This requirement applies to plans established after December 31, 2024. Existing plans are exempt unless they make significant changes that would classify them as new under the Act.

### Key Points for Employers

1. **Automatic Enrollment**: New plans must include an automatic enrollment feature. Employers need to establish procedures to automatically enroll eligible employees and escalate their contributions annually.

2. **Contribution Escalation**: The auto-escalation feature must be clearly communicated to employees. Employers should ensure that their payroll systems can handle these automatic increases without errors.

3. **Exemptions and Exceptions**: Businesses with ten or fewer employees, new businesses (operating for less than three years), and church or governmental plans are exempt from these requirements.

4. **Employee Opt-Out**: Employees must be given the option to opt out of auto-enrollment or choose a different contribution level. Employers should provide clear instructions and deadlines for opting out.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Review and Update Plan Documents**: Ensure that plan documents are updated to reflect the new auto-enrollment and escalation requirements. Consult with plan administrators to make necessary adjustments.

- **Communicate Changes**: Develop a communication strategy to inform employees about the new auto-enrollment features, including how they can opt out or adjust their contribution levels.

- **System Adjustments**: Work with payroll and benefits providers to modify systems and processes to accommodate automatic enrollment and escalation.

- **Training and Support**: Train HR staff on the new requirements and ensure they can assist employees with questions or concerns about the changes.

The DOL's guidance aims to increase participation in retirement savings plans, thereby enhancing financial security for employees. Employers must act swiftly to align their 401(k) plans with these new regulations to avoid penalties and ensure a smooth transition for their workforce.
