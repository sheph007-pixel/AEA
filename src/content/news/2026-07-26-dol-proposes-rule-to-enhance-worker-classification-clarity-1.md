## DOL's New Proposal on Worker Classification

On July 20, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule intended to clarify the criteria used to distinguish employees from independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The proposal seeks to refine the "economic realities" test, which is crucial for determining worker classification and impacts wage, overtime, and other employment rights.

## Key Aspects of the Proposal

The proposed rule revisits the multifactor test previously used to assess the economic dependence of a worker on the hiring entity. It emphasizes two core factors: the nature and degree of control over the work and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss based on initiative or investment. The DOL aims to provide clearer guidance by detailing how these factors should be weighed, potentially simplifying the classification process for employers.

The proposal also suggests additional factors to consider, such as the permanency of the relationship, the worker's skill and initiative, and the extent to which the work performed is an integral part of the employer's business. By refining these criteria, the DOL hopes to reduce misclassification, which affects workers' access to benefits and protections.

## Implications for Employers

If finalized, the rule could lead to significant shifts in how businesses classify their workforce. Employers should assess their current independent contractor agreements and evaluate whether they align with the proposed criteria. Misclassification can result in substantial penalties, including back wages and fines.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Contracts**: Examine contracts with independent contractors to ensure they reflect the degree of control and independence outlined in the proposal.
   
2. **Audit Worker Roles**: Conduct an internal audit of roles currently classified as independent contractors to determine if reclassification is necessary.

3. **Update Policies**: Prepare to update company policies and practices to align with the final rule once it is enacted.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to navigate potential changes and mitigate risks associated with misclassification.

The DOL is currently accepting public comments on the proposal until September 18, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the comment process to voice their perspectives and concerns. The final rule, expected to be issued by early 2027, will provide further guidance on ensuring compliance with FLSA requirements.
