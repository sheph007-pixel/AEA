## DOL's New Proposal on Wellness Program Incentives

The Department of Labor (DOL) has proposed a new rule that aims to regulate the use of incentives in employer-sponsored wellness programs. Announced on July 21, 2026, the proposal seeks to amend existing guidelines under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and the Affordable Care Act (ACA), specifically targeting how financial and non-financial incentives are offered to employees.

## Key Changes and Implications

Under the proposed rule, the DOL intends to cap the incentives that employers can offer to employees who participate in wellness programs. Currently, employers can provide incentives up to 30% of the total cost of employee-only coverage under a group health plan. The new rule proposes reducing this cap to 15%, citing concerns over affordability and potential discrimination against employees who cannot or choose not to participate.

The DOL's proposal also emphasizes the need for wellness programs to be "reasonably designed" to promote health or prevent disease. This means programs should not be overly burdensome, a guise for collecting private health information, or designed mainly to shift costs to employees based on their health status.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should begin reviewing their current wellness programs to ensure compliance with the proposed changes. Key steps include:

- **Evaluate Incentive Structures:** Assess the financial and non-financial incentives currently offered and determine potential adjustments to align with the proposed 15% cap.
- **Review Program Design:** Ensure that wellness programs meet the "reasonably designed" criteria. Programs should have a clear health-related objective and be accessible to all eligible employees.
- **Prepare for Compliance:** Although the rule is still in the proposal stage, employers should be ready to make necessary adjustments once it is finalized. This includes updating policy documents and communicating changes to employees.

## Next Steps

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until September 22, 2026. Employers and HR professionals are encouraged to participate in the comment process to express concerns or support for the changes. The final rule is expected to be published by early 2027, with a compliance deadline likely set for later that year.

Staying ahead of these regulatory changes is crucial for maintaining compliant and effective wellness programs. Employers should monitor further developments and consider consulting legal counsel to navigate potential impacts on their wellness initiatives.
