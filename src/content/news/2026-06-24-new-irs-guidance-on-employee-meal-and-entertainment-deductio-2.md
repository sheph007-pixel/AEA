## IRS Clarifies Meal and Entertainment Deduction Rules

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released new guidance on June 15, 2026, clarifying the rules for deducting meal and entertainment expenses under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA). This guidance impacts employers who provide meals and entertainment to employees and clients, addressing ambiguities that have persisted since the TCJA's enactment in 2017.

## Key Changes in Deduction Eligibility

The TCJA significantly altered the landscape for meal and entertainment deductions, limiting the deductibility of most entertainment expenses while allowing a 50% deduction for meals. However, confusion remained over what constituted a deductible meal versus a non-deductible entertainment expense. The new IRS guidance specifies that meals provided during entertainment events are deductible if billed separately from the entertainment.

This clarification is crucial for businesses that host client dinners or employee events. If the meal portion of an event is not itemized separately, it risks being lumped into non-deductible entertainment costs. The IRS emphasizes the importance of keeping detailed records and obtaining itemized invoices to ensure compliance.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review their current accounting practices to ensure meal expenses are properly documented and separated from entertainment costs. This involves:

1. **Updating Accounting Systems:** Ensure that your accounting software can adequately separate meal expenses from entertainment expenses.
2. **Training Staff:** Educate staff involved in expense reporting on the importance of obtaining itemized invoices for meals during entertainment events.
3. **Policy Revision:** Amend corporate policies to align with the new guidance, clearly distinguishing between meal and entertainment expenses.
4. **Audit Past Records:** Conduct an audit of past expense reports to identify any potential misclassifications that could affect tax filings.

## Implications for Tax Filings

The IRS guidance, while clarifying, also imposes stricter documentation requirements. Businesses must now be meticulous in how they document and report meal expenses. Failure to comply could lead to disallowed deductions, impacting a company's bottom line.

Employers should consult with their tax advisors to ensure full compliance with the new guidelines and to explore any potential for amending past returns if necessary. The IRS guidance underscores the importance of transparency and accuracy in financial reporting, particularly in areas prone to misinterpretation.

The updated rules take effect immediately, influencing tax filings for the 2026 fiscal year. By taking proactive steps now, employers can avoid complications during tax season and maximize allowable deductions under the TCJA.
