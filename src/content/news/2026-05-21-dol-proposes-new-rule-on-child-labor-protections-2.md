## DOL Proposes New Rule on Child Labor Protections

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed new regulations on May 20, 2026, to enhance protections for minors in the workforce. The proposed changes aim to update the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regulations concerning hazardous occupations and permissible work hours for minors under 18.

The proposed rule seeks to amend 29 C.F.R. Part 570, which outlines the hazardous occupations prohibited for minors. The DOL intends to expand the list of prohibited occupations to include contemporary industries and technologies not previously covered. This includes certain roles in the gig economy and positions involving advanced machinery, reflecting the evolving nature of work environments.

### Key Changes

1. **Expanded Hazardous Occupations**: The proposal adds new categories to the list of hazardous occupations, such as certain roles in renewable energy sectors and technology-driven logistics environments. This update aims to protect minors from exposure to potentially dangerous equipment and situations that were not prevalent when the regulations were last revised.

2. **Work Hour Restrictions**: The DOL proposes stricter limits on the number of hours minors can work during school days and weeks. The rule suggests reducing the permissible work hours for 14- and 15-year-olds from 18 to 15 hours per week during the school year, aligning with educational priorities.

3. **Increased Penalties**: To ensure compliance, the DOL plans to increase penalties for violations of child labor laws. The proposed rule would raise the maximum civil monetary penalty for willful or repeated violations from $13,650 to $15,000 per violation.

### Implications for Employers

Employers with minor employees must prepare for potential changes by reviewing their current practices and ensuring compliance with the updated regulations once finalized. Key actions include:

- **Audit Current Roles**: Evaluate positions held by minor employees to determine if they fall under the proposed expanded hazardous occupations list. Adjust roles or reassign tasks as necessary to comply with the new guidelines.

- **Review Work Schedules**: Assess and adjust work schedules for minors to ensure they do not exceed the proposed limits on work hours during school weeks.

- **Training and Awareness**: Implement training programs for managers and HR personnel to understand and enforce the new regulations. This includes recognizing prohibited tasks and adhering to work hour restrictions.

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until July 20, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the regulatory process by submitting feedback or concerns regarding the potential impact of these changes on their operations.

These proposed updates underscore the DOL's commitment to safeguarding young workers in an ever-changing employment landscape. Employers should stay informed and proactive in adapting to these regulatory shifts to ensure both compliance and the well-being of their minor employees.
