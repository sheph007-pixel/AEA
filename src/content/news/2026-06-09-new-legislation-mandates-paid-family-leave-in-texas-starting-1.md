## Texas Enacts Paid Family Leave Law

On June 8, 2026, Texas Governor Greg Abbott signed into law a new mandate requiring employers to provide paid family leave to eligible employees, effective January 1, 2027. This landmark legislation marks a significant shift in Texas labor policy, aligning the state with others that have implemented similar requirements.

## Key Provisions of the Law

The law mandates that all employers with 10 or more employees must offer at least six weeks of paid family leave per year. This leave can be used for the birth or adoption of a child, or to care for a seriously ill family member. The leave will be compensated at a minimum of 60% of the employee's regular wages, capped at $1,000 per week.

The legislation, codified as Texas Labor Code Chapter 83, specifies that employees must have worked for the employer for at least 12 months and logged at least 1,250 hours during the preceding year to qualify for the leave.

## Employer Obligations

Employers are required to notify employees of their rights under this new law by December 1, 2026. This includes updating employee handbooks and posting notices in common areas. Employers should also prepare to adjust payroll systems to accommodate the new leave payments and ensure compliance with the wage replacement requirements.

## Potential Impact on Businesses

While the law aims to provide critical support for workers balancing family and employment responsibilities, it presents new challenges for employers. Small to mid-sized businesses, in particular, may need to assess their financial and operational capabilities to manage the costs associated with paid leave.

Businesses should consider conducting a financial impact analysis and exploring options such as temporary staffing solutions to mitigate potential disruptions. Additionally, organizations may need to review and possibly amend existing leave policies to ensure they comply with the new state requirements.

## Looking Ahead

Texas joins a growing number of states enacting paid family leave laws, reflecting a nationwide trend towards increased employee benefits. As the January 1, 2027, implementation date approaches, employers should stay informed of any additional guidance or amendments that may arise.

HR professionals and business owners are advised to consult with legal counsel or labor law experts to fully understand the implications of this legislation and to ensure their policies and practices are compliant with the new requirements.
