## DOL Clarifies Remote Worker Classification

On July 1, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule addressing the classification of remote workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The rule, effective August 15, 2026, aims to provide clarity on whether remote workers should be classified as exempt or non-exempt employees, impacting overtime eligibility.

## Key Provisions

The new rule specifies criteria under 29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1) to determine the exempt status of remote workers. It emphasizes the "primary duty" test, focusing on the actual work performed rather than job titles or remote status. The rule also reaffirms the salary basis test, maintaining the current threshold of $684 per week ($35,568 annually).

## Implications for Employers

Employers must evaluate remote workers' duties to ensure compliance with the new rule. The DOL's guidance highlights the importance of documenting job duties and responsibilities, emphasizing the need for accurate job descriptions reflecting actual work performed.

## Action Items

- **Audit Job Descriptions:** Employers should review and update job descriptions for remote workers to align with the new criteria.
- **Reassess Employee Classifications:** Conduct a thorough assessment of current classifications to ensure they meet the "primary duty" test.
- **Training for HR and Managers:** Implement training programs to educate HR personnel and managers on the new classification criteria and the importance of compliance.

## Conclusion

The DOL's rule on remote worker classification under the FLSA provides necessary guidance for employers navigating the complexities of remote work arrangements. By taking proactive steps to align with the new criteria, employers can mitigate potential legal risks and ensure compliance.
