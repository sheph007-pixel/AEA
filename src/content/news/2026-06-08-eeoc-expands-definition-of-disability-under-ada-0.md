## EEOC Updates ADA Guidance

On June 6, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) issued updated guidance expanding the definition of disability under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) to explicitly include long COVID. This change impacts how employers must handle accommodation requests related to long COVID, a condition affecting individuals after a COVID-19 infection.

## Implications for Employers

Under the ADA (42 U.S.C. § 12102), a disability is defined as a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities. The EEOC's latest guidance clarifies that long COVID can meet this definition, depending on the severity and impact on the individual.

Employers must now consider long COVID symptoms such as fatigue, difficulty concentrating, and respiratory issues when evaluating accommodation requests. This may require adjustments to work schedules, remote work options, or other job modifications.

## Action Items for HR and Employers

1. **Review Accommodation Policies**: Ensure current policies reflect the inclusion of long COVID as a potential disability. Update procedures for handling accommodation requests to account for this change.

2. **Train HR and Management**: Conduct training sessions for HR staff and managers to recognize long COVID symptoms and understand the updated ADA obligations.

3. **Assess Job Descriptions**: Re-evaluate job descriptions to determine essential functions and identify potential accommodations for employees with long COVID.

4. **Document Interactions**: Maintain thorough documentation of all accommodation requests and employer responses to ensure compliance and protect against potential legal challenges.

## Compliance and Risks

Failure to accommodate employees with long COVID could result in legal action under the ADA. Employers should proactively address requests and engage in the interactive process with affected employees. The EEOC's guidance emphasizes the importance of flexibility and communication in identifying reasonable accommodations.

By adapting policies and practices to include long COVID, employers can not only comply with the EEOC's updated guidance but also support employees dealing with this challenging condition.
