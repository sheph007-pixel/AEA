## FMLA Now Covers Mental Health Days

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has expanded the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) to include mental health days, effective January 1, 2026. This expansion allows eligible employees to take leave for mental health reasons under the same conditions as physical health issues. The change comes as part of the DOL's ongoing efforts to address mental health in the workplace and ensure employees have the necessary support.

## What Employers Need to Know

Under the revised FMLA, employees can take up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for mental health conditions that require treatment or cause significant impairment. The DOL has clarified that mental health conditions must be diagnosed by a healthcare provider, similar to physical health conditions. Employers should update their FMLA policies to reflect this change and ensure that HR teams are trained to handle mental health leave requests.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Update FMLA Policies:** Employers should revise their leave policies to include mental health days as a valid reason for FMLA leave. This includes updating employee handbooks and training materials.

2. **Train HR Staff:** Ensure that HR personnel are trained to recognize and process mental health leave requests. This includes understanding the documentation required and maintaining confidentiality.

3. **Communicate Changes:** Inform employees about the updated FMLA provisions and how they can request leave for mental health reasons. Clear communication can help reduce stigma and encourage employees to seek help when needed.

4. **Review Compliance:** Verify that your organization complies with the new FMLA requirements. This may involve consulting with legal counsel or HR consultants to ensure all changes are implemented correctly.

## Legal and Regulatory Context

The FMLA, originally enacted in 1993, provides eligible employees with up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for certain family and medical reasons. The inclusion of mental health days aligns with the DOL's broader initiative to address mental health in the workplace, as outlined in the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA). Employers should be aware of the interplay between these laws to ensure compliance and support employee well-being.

## Conclusion

The expansion of FMLA to include mental health days marks a significant step in recognizing the importance of mental health in the workplace. By updating policies and training staff, employers can support their employees' mental health needs and foster a more inclusive work environment. As the effective date approaches, timely action will be crucial to ensure compliance and support employee well-being.
