## EEOC Clarifies Religious Accommodation Obligations

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on June 15, 2026, regarding religious accommodations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. This update clarifies employer obligations in accommodating employees' religious practices, emphasizing a balanced approach between workplace policies and employees' religious needs.

## Key Points of the Guidance

The EEOC's new guidance focuses on the requirement for employers to provide reasonable accommodations for employees' religious beliefs and practices unless doing so would cause undue hardship on the conduct of business. This aligns with the Supreme Court's decision in Groff v. DeJoy, which refined the definition of "undue hardship" to mean a significant difficulty or expense, moving away from the previous minimal burden standard.

Employers are now encouraged to engage in a more interactive process with employees requesting accommodations. The guidance suggests considering factors such as the nature of the business, the employee's role, and the potential impact on operations and other employees.

## Practical Implications for Employers

Employers should review and possibly revise their accommodation policies to ensure compliance with the updated guidance. This includes training HR personnel and managers on the importance of engaging in a good-faith dialogue with employees seeking religious accommodations. Documenting these interactions and the rationale for any decisions made will be crucial in defending against potential claims of discrimination.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Policies**: Audit current religious accommodation policies and procedures to ensure alignment with the new EEOC guidance and the Supreme Court's interpretation of "undue hardship."

2. **Train Staff**: Conduct training sessions for HR and management teams on handling religious accommodation requests, focusing on the interactive process and documentation.

3. **Document Interactions**: Implement a system to meticulously document all requests for religious accommodations and the steps taken to address them, including any accommodations offered or denied.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Consider consulting with legal counsel to navigate complex requests and ensure compliance with both federal and state laws regarding religious accommodations.

By taking these steps, employers can better navigate the complexities of religious accommodations, fostering an inclusive workplace while minimizing legal risks.
