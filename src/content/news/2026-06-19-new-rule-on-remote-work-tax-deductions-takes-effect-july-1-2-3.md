## New IRS Rule on Remote Work Tax Deductions

The IRS issued a final rule on June 1, 2026, clarifying eligibility for remote work-related tax deductions, effective July 1, 2026. This rule impacts employers and employees who maintain home offices, providing specific guidelines under 26 U.S.C. § 280A.

The rule specifies that to qualify for deductions, a home office must be used exclusively and regularly for business purposes. The IRS has tightened the definition of "exclusive use," requiring that the space not be used for any personal activities during business hours. This change aims to address ambiguities that arose during the pandemic when many employees used shared spaces for work.

## Implications for Employers

Employers should review their remote work policies and communicate these changes to employees. It is crucial to ensure that employees understand the requirements for claiming deductions, particularly the exclusive use provision. Employers might consider offering guidance or resources to help employees set up compliant home offices.

Additionally, companies should revisit any existing reimbursement policies for home office expenses. While the IRS rule does not directly affect employer reimbursements, aligning company policies with IRS guidelines could prevent discrepancies during tax filing.

## Action Items

1. **Update Remote Work Policies**: Ensure that your company's remote work policies reflect the new IRS requirements. Clearly communicate these changes to employees, emphasizing the importance of maintaining a dedicated workspace.

2. **Educate Employees**: Provide resources or workshops to educate employees on how to set up a home office that meets the IRS's exclusive use criteria. Consider offering a checklist or guide to help employees self-assess their workspace.

3. **Review Reimbursement Practices**: Align reimbursement policies with IRS guidelines to avoid potential conflicts. Consider whether additional support or stipends for home office setups might be necessary to ensure compliance.

4. **Consult Tax Professionals**: Employers should consult with tax advisors to understand the broader implications of these changes on their operations and to ensure that both the company and its employees are prepared for the new tax season.

The IRS's clarification aims to streamline the tax deduction process for remote work, providing clearer guidelines for both employers and employees. By taking proactive steps, companies can ensure compliance and support their remote workforce effectively.
