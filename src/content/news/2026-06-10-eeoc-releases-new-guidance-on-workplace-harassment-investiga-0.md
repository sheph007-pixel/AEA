## New EEOC Guidance

On June 5, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on conducting workplace harassment investigations. This guidance aims to clarify employer obligations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and other relevant statutes. The EEOC's focus is on ensuring that investigations are prompt, thorough, and impartial, a critical step in preventing and addressing harassment claims.

## Key Points for Employers

The guidance outlines specific actions employers must take when harassment claims arise. Employers are required to conduct investigations that are not only timely but also comprehensive, ensuring that all relevant evidence is considered. The EEOC emphasizes the importance of maintaining confidentiality to the extent possible and protecting employees from retaliation under 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-3(a).

## Action Items

Employers should review and, if necessary, revise their current investigation procedures to comply with the new guidance. Key steps include:

1. **Train HR Staff**: Ensure that HR personnel and managers are trained on the updated guidelines to handle investigations appropriately.
   
2. **Update Policies**: Amend workplace harassment policies to reflect the requirement for prompt and impartial investigations. Clearly outline the steps employees should take to report harassment.
   
3. **Document Everything**: Keep detailed records of all investigations, including interviews, findings, and actions taken. This documentation is crucial if the EEOC requests evidence of compliance.

4. **Regular Reviews**: Conduct regular reviews of your harassment policies and investigation procedures to ensure ongoing compliance with EEOC standards.

## Implications for Employers

Failure to adhere to the EEOC's updated guidance could result in increased liability for employers. Non-compliance might be viewed as a failure to address workplace harassment adequately, potentially leading to costly litigation. Employers should take immediate steps to align their practices with the EEOC's expectations to mitigate these risks.

By implementing these measures, employers can create a safer workplace environment and reduce the likelihood of harassment claims escalating to legal action. The EEOC's guidance serves as a reminder of the importance of proactive and effective harassment prevention strategies in maintaining a compliant and respectful workplace.
