## Background

The Federal Trade Commission's (FTC) rule banning most noncompete agreements, originally issued in April 2024, faced immediate legal challenges that paused its enforcement. After a series of district court decisions reached conflicting conclusions, the matter moved to the federal appellate courts. In early 2026, the Fifth Circuit issued a decision largely upholding the FTC's authority to regulate noncompete agreements, while narrowing certain provisions. The FTC has since announced it will resume enforcement activities in the second quarter of 2026.

## What the Rule Requires

Under the FTC's rule as currently enforceable:

- **Existing noncompetes with most workers are void**: Employers may not enforce noncompete clauses with employees, independent contractors, or other workers, with limited exceptions
- **Senior executives are partially exempt**: Existing noncompete agreements with "senior executives" (defined as individuals earning more than $151,164 annually who hold policy-making positions) may remain in effect, but employers may not enter into new noncompete agreements with anyone
- **Notice is required**: Employers must provide written notice to affected workers that their noncompete agreements will not be enforced
- **Alternative protections remain available**: Employers may still use non-disclosure agreements (NDAs), non-solicitation agreements, and other restrictive covenants that do not prevent a worker from seeking employment

## State Law Considerations

Several states, including California, Minnesota, Oklahoma, and North Dakota, already prohibit or significantly restrict noncompete agreements. Other states have enacted their own reforms since the FTC's initial rule was proposed. Employers operating in multiple states should verify which restrictions apply in each jurisdiction.

## What Employers Should Do Now

- **Review all existing noncompete agreements** and identify which ones are affected by the federal rule
- **Issue required notices** to workers whose noncompete clauses are no longer enforceable
- **Strengthen alternative protections** such as NDAs, trade secret policies, and non-solicitation agreements
- **Update employment agreement templates** to remove noncompete provisions for new hires
- **Consult legal counsel** to navigate the intersection of federal and state noncompete laws applicable to your workforce
