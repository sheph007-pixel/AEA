## DOL's Proposed Overtime Rule

On July 7, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a rule that would significantly expand overtime pay eligibility by increasing the salary threshold for exempt workers. The proposal suggests raising the current threshold from $684 per week ($35,568 annually) to $1,059 per week ($55,068 annually). This change, if finalized, could extend overtime protections to an estimated 3.6 million additional workers.

The proposed rule aims to update the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) overtime regulations under 29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(1), which determine which employees are exempt from overtime pay. The DOL is also proposing automatic updates to the salary threshold every three years to account for wage growth, a move intended to prevent the threshold from becoming outdated.

## Impact on Employers

Employers with workforces that include salaried employees earning between the current and proposed thresholds will need to assess the financial impact of this potential change. Businesses may face increased payroll costs due to the need to either raise salaries above the new threshold or pay overtime to newly eligible employees.

The DOL estimates that the rule could result in approximately $1.2 billion in additional wages paid to workers in the first year alone. Companies should begin evaluating their payroll structures and employee classifications to prepare for the potential financial implications.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Employee Classifications:** Review current employee classifications to determine which employees may be affected by the proposed changes. Consider whether to reclassify employees as non-exempt or adjust salaries to maintain exempt status.

2. **Budget for Increased Costs:** Analyze the potential financial impact of increased overtime payments or salary adjustments. Prepare to adjust budgets accordingly to accommodate these changes.

3. **Engage in the Comment Period:** The DOL has opened a 60-day public comment period on the proposed rule. Employers should consider submitting feedback or concerns during this period to influence the final rule.

4. **Update Payroll Systems:** Ensure payroll systems are capable of handling changes in employee classifications and overtime calculations. This may require software updates or additional training for payroll staff.

5. **Communicate with Employees:** Prepare to communicate with employees about potential changes to their pay and classification. Transparency will help manage expectations and maintain employee morale.

The proposed rule is part of the Biden administration's broader effort to address income inequality and improve worker protections. Employers should stay informed about developments in this area and be ready to adapt to regulatory changes. The final rule could take effect as early as 2027, depending on the outcome of the public comment period and any legal challenges.
