## DOL's Minimum Wage Proposal for Federal Contractors

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has proposed a rule to increase the minimum wage for federal contractors to $15 per hour, effective January 1, 2027. This proposal, announced on June 20, 2026, aims to align wages for contractors with broader federal wage policies and improve the standard of living for workers on federal contracts.

The proposed rule would amend the regulations under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and the Service Contract Act (SCA), impacting thousands of businesses with federal contracts. Currently, the minimum wage for federal contractors is set at $12.15 per hour. The increase to $15 per hour represents a significant adjustment, potentially affecting contract pricing and payroll budgets for small to mid-sized enterprises engaged in federal contracting.

### Key Provisions

The proposed rule mandates that all federal contractors, including subcontractors, pay workers at least $15 per hour. This wage floor applies to new contracts, renewals, and extensions issued after the effective date. The rule also includes provisions for annual adjustments based on inflation, ensuring that the minimum wage keeps pace with the cost of living.

Additionally, the proposal includes a clause for tipped employees, requiring that their cash wage be at least $10 per hour, with tips making up the difference to the $15 minimum if necessary. This adjustment reflects changes in the FLSA's tip credit regulations and aims to ensure fair compensation for tipped workers under federal contracts.

### Implications for Employers

Employers with federal contracts should evaluate their current wage structures and prepare for potential adjustments. The proposed increase necessitates a review of payroll practices to ensure compliance by the effective date. Companies should also consider the impact on contract bids and pricing strategies, as labor costs will rise with the new minimum wage requirement.

It is crucial for employers to stay informed about the progress of this proposal. The DOL has opened a public comment period until August 31, 2026, allowing stakeholders to provide feedback. Engaging in this process could influence the final rule's provisions and implementation timeline.

### Action Items

1. **Review Current Wage Rates**: Assess current pay rates for employees on federal contracts to determine compliance with the proposed $15 minimum wage.

2. **Budget for Increased Labor Costs**: Adjust financial forecasts and contract bids to account for higher wages and potential inflation adjustments.

3. **Participate in Public Comment**: Submit feedback during the DOL's comment period to voice concerns or support for the proposed changes.

4. **Prepare for Implementation**: Develop a compliance plan to ensure all contracts and payroll systems align with the new wage requirements by January 1, 2027.

The DOL's proposal signifies a substantial shift in federal contractor wage policies, aiming to enhance worker compensation and align with national wage trends. Employers must act promptly to adapt to these changes and mitigate potential impacts on their operations and contract obligations.
