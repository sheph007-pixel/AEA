## New Overtime Regulations Could Impact Employee Compensation Structures

In late 2023, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) announced updates to the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) regarding overtime pay, which are set to take effect in 2024. These changes may be significant for many employers subject to the FLSA, as they could require adjustments to payroll practices and employee classifications, though the impact may vary by industry and employer size.

Under the new regulations, the salary threshold for exempt employees—those who are not entitled to overtime pay—has been proposed to increase from the current level of $684 per week (as of 2020) to $1,059 per week. This adjustment is designed to ensure that more workers may be entitled to overtime pay, reflecting current wage trends and cost of living increases. Employers should monitor official announcements for any finalized threshold, as this figure is subject to change based on future regulations.

### What Employers Need to Know

The DOL’s revision aims to broaden the scope of who qualifies for overtime pay, meaning that many employees who previously fell under the exempt category may now be eligible for overtime compensation. This shift is expected to affect a wide range of industries, particularly those that employ a significant number of administrative, executive, and professional staff.

Employers should be aware that while discussions may be ongoing regarding additional changes that could impact employee classification, including potential modifications to the duties test used to determine exemption eligibility, no definitive changes to the duties test have been proposed or confirmed. It is essential for HR professionals and business owners to stay informed and prepared for potential adjustments.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Employee Classifications**: Conduct an audit of your current employee classifications to identify those who may be affected by the new salary threshold. This review should include evaluating job duties and responsibilities to ensure compliance with the updated criteria.

2. **Adjust Compensation Structures**: Employers may need to adjust salaries for certain employees to maintain their exempt status. Alternatively, for those who will be classified as non-exempt, prepare to implement systems for tracking hours worked and calculating overtime pay.

3. **Communicate Changes to Employees**: Transparency is key. Once changes are determined, communicate clearly with affected employees about how their status and compensation may change. This will help manage expectations and maintain morale.

4. **Update Payroll Systems**: Ensure your payroll systems are capable of handling the new requirements. This may involve training for HR staff or investing in new software solutions that accommodate overtime calculations.

5. **Stay Informed on Future Changes**: The DOL may continue to refine these regulations, so it is crucial to stay updated through official announcements and guidance. Joining professional associations, attending webinars, or consulting legal experts can provide valuable insights.

As the effective date approaches, employers should proactively address these changes to minimize disruptions to their operations and ensure compliance with federal regulations. The new overtime rule is a reminder of the dynamic nature of labor laws and the importance of staying ahead in the ever-evolving landscape of employee compensation.
