## EEOC Clarifies Religious Accommodations Requirements

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on May 5, 2026, regarding religious accommodations in the workplace. This clarification affects all employers with 15 or more employees under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. It aims to resolve ambiguities about how religious accommodations should be handled, following recent court rulings that have shifted interpretations.

## Key Changes in the Guidance

The new guidance emphasizes the need for employers to genuinely consider and, where possible, accommodate employees' religious practices unless doing so would cause undue hardship. The EEOC has redefined "undue hardship" to require a more substantial burden on the operation of the business than previously understood, aligning with the Supreme Court's decision in *Groff v. DeJoy* (2025). Employers can no longer claim minimal costs or inconveniences as undue hardships.

The guidance also specifies that employers must engage in an interactive process with employees who request religious accommodations. This process should explore all possible options that could accommodate the employee's religious practices without imposing significant difficulty or expense on the business.

## Employer Action Items

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers should review their current policies on religious accommodations to ensure compliance with the new EEOC guidance. This includes updating employee handbooks and training materials to reflect the revised understanding of undue hardship.

2. **Train HR and Management**: Conduct training sessions for HR professionals and managers to ensure they understand the new requirements and can effectively engage in the interactive process with employees requesting accommodations.

3. **Document Accommodation Requests**: Implement a system to document all requests for religious accommodations and the steps taken to address them. This documentation will be crucial if the employer's decision is later challenged.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the potential complexities involved in religious accommodation cases, employers should consult with legal counsel to navigate specific situations and ensure that their policies are legally sound.

## Implications for Employers

The EEOC's updated guidance could lead to an increase in requests for religious accommodations, as employees become more aware of their rights. Employers must be prepared to handle these requests carefully to avoid potential litigation. By proactively updating policies and training staff, employers can mitigate risks and foster a more inclusive workplace environment.

This development underscores the importance of staying informed about changes in employment law and adapting practices accordingly. Employers who fail to comply with the new guidance may face increased scrutiny from the EEOC and potential legal challenges from employees.
