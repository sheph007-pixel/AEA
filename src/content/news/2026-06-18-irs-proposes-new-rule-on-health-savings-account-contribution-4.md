## IRS Proposes HSA Contribution Limit Changes

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has proposed a new rule adjusting the contribution limits for Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) for the tax year 2027. The proposal, announced on June 15, 2026, would increase the maximum annual contribution for individuals and families, reflecting inflation and healthcare cost trends. If finalized, this change could significantly impact tax planning and benefits strategies for employers and employees alike.

## Proposed Contribution Limit Increases

Under the proposed rule, the individual contribution limit for HSAs would rise to $4,200, up from the current $3,850. For families, the limit would increase to $8,400, a substantial jump from the existing $7,750. These adjustments align with the IRS's statutory mandate under 26 U.S.C. § 223(g) to periodically review and modify these limits based on cost-of-living adjustments.

## Implications for Employers

Employers offering HSAs as part of their benefits packages will need to prepare for these changes. The proposed increases may require updates to payroll systems and benefits administration platforms to accommodate higher employee contributions. Additionally, employers should consider communicating these changes to employees, emphasizing the potential tax advantages of maximizing HSA contributions.

## Action Items for HR Professionals

1. **Review Benefits Administration Systems**: Ensure that payroll and benefits systems can handle the updated contribution limits if the proposal is finalized.
   
2. **Update Employee Communication**: Prepare informational materials to educate employees about the new limits and the benefits of HSA contributions.

3. **Coordinate with Benefits Providers**: Engage with third-party benefits administrators to ensure seamless implementation of the new limits.

## Next Steps

The IRS is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until August 31, 2026. Employers and HR professionals are encouraged to review the full proposal and consider submitting feedback. The IRS aims to finalize the rule by the end of 2026, allowing employers adequate time to adjust their benefits offerings for the 2027 tax year.

By staying informed and proactive, employers can ensure compliance and optimize their benefits packages to attract and retain top talent.
