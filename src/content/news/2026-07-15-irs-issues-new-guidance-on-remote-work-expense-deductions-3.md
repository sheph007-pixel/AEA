## IRS Clarifies Deductible Remote Work Expenses

On July 10, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released new guidance on deductible expenses related to remote work arrangements. This clarification under IRS Notice 2026-45 aims to address uncertainties surrounding what employers can deduct concerning remote work costs incurred by employees.

### Key Details

The IRS guidance specifies which expenses employers can deduct under the Internal Revenue Code § 162(a), which covers ordinary and necessary business expenses. Notably, the guidance distinguishes between reimbursable and non-reimbursable expenses for tax purposes.

Eligible deductions include:

- **Office Supplies:** Employers may deduct costs for office supplies such as paper, pens, and other consumables provided to employees working remotely.
- **Technology Equipment:** Deductible expenses now explicitly include computers, monitors, and other necessary technology if they are primarily used for business purposes.
- **Internet and Communication Costs:** Employers can deduct a portion of internet and communication expenses if they are directly related to business operations.

### Non-Deductible Expenses

The IRS guidance also clarifies which expenses are not deductible. These include:

- **Home Office Renovations:** Costs associated with renovating or upgrading an employee's home office space are not deductible unless the space is exclusively used for business.
- **Personal Expenses:** Any expenses that are personal in nature, such as utilities and rent for the employee's home, remain non-deductible.

### Employer Action Items

Employers should review their current remote work expense policies to ensure compliance with the new IRS guidelines. Key steps include:

1. **Policy Updates:** Amend company policies to reflect the IRS's clear distinctions between deductible and non-deductible expenses.
2. **Documentation:** Maintain thorough records of all reimbursed expenses to substantiate deductions during tax filings.
3. **Employee Communication:** Inform employees about which expenses are eligible for reimbursement and the required documentation for claims.

### Implications for Employers

This guidance provides much-needed clarity for businesses supporting remote work arrangements. By understanding and implementing these directives, employers can optimize their tax positions while maintaining compliance. It is crucial for businesses, especially those with a significant remote workforce, to align their reimbursement strategies with the IRS requirements to avoid potential audits and penalties.

Employers are advised to consult with tax professionals to ensure full compliance and to leverage the guidance effectively in their tax planning strategies. This proactive approach will help mitigate risks and capitalize on available tax benefits associated with supporting remote work infrastructure.
