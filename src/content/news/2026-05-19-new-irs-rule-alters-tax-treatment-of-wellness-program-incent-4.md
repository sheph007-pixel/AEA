## IRS Alters Tax Treatment of Wellness Program Incentives

The IRS issued new guidance on May 15, 2026, altering the tax treatment of incentives provided under employer-sponsored wellness programs. This change affects how employers should account for these incentives under the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) § 105 and § 106, with implications for both payroll processing and employee tax reporting.

Previously, many wellness program incentives, such as gym memberships, cash rewards, and gift cards, were considered non-taxable benefits if they promoted health. However, under the new guidance, the IRS clarified that only incentives directly tied to medical care, as defined by IRC § 213(d), will remain non-taxable. Other incentives, particularly those not directly linked to medical care, must now be treated as taxable income.

## Impact on Employers

Employers offering wellness programs must reassess the tax implications of their incentives. The change requires that taxable incentives be included in employees' gross income, affecting payroll calculations and withholding taxes. Failure to comply could result in penalties and back taxes.

Employers should review their wellness programs to determine which incentives qualify as medical care under IRC § 213(d). For instance, incentives like smoking cessation and weight loss programs, if medically supervised, may remain non-taxable. Conversely, gym memberships and general wellness rewards are likely to be taxable unless directly prescribed for a medical condition.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Wellness Programs**: Employers should conduct an audit of their current wellness programs to identify which incentives fall under the new taxable category. This includes reviewing agreements with third-party wellness vendors to ensure compliance.

2. **Update Payroll Systems**: Adjust payroll systems to account for the taxable nature of certain wellness incentives. This includes updating W-2 forms to reflect the additional taxable income for employees.

3. **Employee Communication**: Inform employees about the changes in tax treatment for wellness incentives. Transparency is crucial to avoid confusion during tax season.

4. **Consult Tax Professionals**: Engage with tax advisors to navigate the complexities of the new guidance and ensure all aspects of the wellness program comply with the updated rules.

The IRS's updated guidance reflects a broader trend towards stricter scrutiny of employer-provided benefits. Employers need to remain proactive in adapting their benefits strategies to align with regulatory changes.
