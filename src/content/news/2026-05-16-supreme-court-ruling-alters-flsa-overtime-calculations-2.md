## Supreme Court Decision Alters Overtime Calculations

On May 10, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court issued a ruling in *Smith v. Jones Manufacturing* that significantly impacts how overtime pay is calculated under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The decision mandates that employers include non-discretionary bonuses and incentive payments in the regular rate of pay when calculating overtime for non-exempt employees. This ruling is effective immediately and requires employers to reassess their payroll practices to ensure compliance.

## Implications for Employers

The Supreme Court's decision clarifies that non-discretionary bonuses, such as performance-based incentives and attendance bonuses, must be included in the regular rate of pay. This inclusion affects the calculation of overtime pay, which is typically one and a half times the regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek. Employers who previously excluded these bonuses from the regular rate of pay need to adjust their payroll systems to avoid potential violations of the FLSA.

According to the FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 207(e)), the regular rate of pay includes all remuneration for employment, barring specific exceptions. The Court emphasized that bonuses tied to productivity, quality, or other measurable performance criteria are not excludable from the regular rate.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers must take immediate steps to comply with the Supreme Court's ruling. Key actions include:

1. **Review Payroll Practices:** Audit current payroll systems to ensure that all non-discretionary bonuses are included in the regular rate of pay. This may require recalculating overtime for affected employees.

2. **Adjust Bonus Structures:** Evaluate existing bonus and incentive programs to determine if they are classified correctly as discretionary or non-discretionary. Consider restructuring bonuses to align with the new requirements.

3. **Employee Communication:** Inform employees about changes in how their overtime pay is calculated. Clear communication can help manage expectations and reduce confusion.

4. **Training for HR and Payroll Staff:** Provide training to HR and payroll personnel to ensure they understand the new requirements and can accurately process payroll under the revised guidelines.

5. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Seek advice from legal professionals to ensure compliance and address any potential legal challenges arising from the ruling.

## Conclusion

The Supreme Court's decision in *Smith v. Jones Manufacturing* underscores the importance of accurately calculating the regular rate of pay under the FLSA. Employers must act swiftly to integrate non-discretionary bonuses and incentive payments into overtime calculations to remain compliant. Failure to adjust payroll practices could result in costly penalties and litigation.
