## IRS Updates on ESPP Regulations

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released new regulations on June 26, 2026, affecting Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs). These changes, under Section 423 of the Internal Revenue Code, aim to clarify tax reporting requirements and eligibility criteria for both employers and employees participating in ESPPs.

The updated rules address ambiguities in the previous guidelines, particularly concerning the tax treatment of stock options granted under ESPPs. Employers must now ensure that their plans comply with the new criteria to maintain favorable tax treatment for both the company and its employees. The IRS has emphasized the importance of accurate reporting of stock purchase discounts and the timing of income recognition.

### Key Changes

1. **Eligibility Criteria**: The new regulations specify that employees must have been employed for at least three months before participating in an ESPP. This change aims to prevent short-term hires from taking advantage of stock discounts without a substantial employment commitment.

2. **Discount Limitations**: The IRS has set a cap on the discount employers can offer on stock purchases through ESPPs at 15% of the stock's fair market value. This aligns with existing regulations but reinforces the need for strict adherence to prevent tax penalties.

3. **Income Recognition**: Employers must now report income from ESPP stock purchases in the year the stock is sold, rather than when it is purchased. This change could affect the timing of tax liabilities for both employers and employees.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Review and Amend ESPP Documents**: Employers should immediately review their ESPP documents to ensure compliance with the new eligibility and discount rules. Amendments may be necessary to align with the updated regulations.

- **Update Payroll and Tax Reporting Systems**: Adjust payroll systems to reflect the new income recognition timing. Ensure that all relevant transactions are reported accurately to avoid penalties.

- **Employee Communication**: Inform employees about the changes to ESPP participation and the potential impact on their tax obligations. Providing clear guidance can help mitigate confusion and ensure compliance.

- **Consult with Tax Professionals**: Given the complexity of the new rules, consulting with tax professionals can help employers navigate the changes and implement necessary adjustments effectively.

These regulatory updates are crucial for employers offering ESPPs, as non-compliance can lead to significant tax liabilities and penalties. By taking proactive steps, businesses can ensure their stock purchase plans remain a valuable tool for employee engagement and retention.
