## IRS Updates HSA Contribution Limits for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has announced new guidelines for Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) effective January 1, 2026. The updates include revised contribution limits and adjustments to high-deductible health plans (HDHPs) requirements under 26 U.S.C. § 223. Employers offering HSAs as part of their benefits package need to be aware of these changes to ensure compliance and optimize employee benefits.

## Increased Contribution Limits

For 2026, the IRS has increased the annual contribution limits for HSAs. Individuals can now contribute up to $4,150, while the limit for families is set at $8,300. This marks an increase from the 2025 limits of $4,050 for individuals and $8,100 for families. The catch-up contribution for account holders aged 55 and older remains unchanged at $1,000.

Employers should update payroll systems to accommodate these new limits and communicate the changes to employees. This adjustment provides an opportunity to remind employees of the tax advantages of maximizing their HSA contributions.

## Adjustments to HDHP Minimums

The IRS has also revised the minimum deductible and out-of-pocket maximums for HDHPs. For 2026, the minimum deductible is set at $1,600 for self-only coverage and $3,200 for family coverage. The maximum out-of-pocket expenses are capped at $8,050 for individuals and $16,100 for families.

Employers must review their health plan offerings to ensure they meet these new minimum requirements. Plans that fail to comply may disqualify the associated HSAs from favorable tax treatment, impacting both employer and employee benefits.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Update Payroll Systems:** Adjust systems to reflect the new HSA contribution limits for 2026.
   
2. **Review Health Plans:** Ensure HDHPs meet the revised deductible and out-of-pocket maximum requirements.

3. **Communicate Changes:** Inform employees about the updated limits and encourage them to review their contributions.

4. **Plan for Open Enrollment:** Incorporate these changes into open enrollment materials and sessions to educate employees on maximizing their HSA benefits.

By staying ahead of these regulatory changes, employers can maintain compliance and enhance the value of their benefits offerings.
