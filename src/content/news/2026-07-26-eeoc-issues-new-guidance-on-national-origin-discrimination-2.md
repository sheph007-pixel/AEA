## New EEOC Guidance on National Origin Discrimination

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on national origin discrimination on July 20, 2026. This guidance, effective August 1, 2026, clarifies employer obligations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2). Employers must ensure compliance to avoid potential legal pitfalls.

The updated guidance emphasizes that national origin discrimination includes treating applicants or employees unfavorably because they are from a particular country or part of the world, have a particular ethnicity or accent, or appear to be of a certain ethnic background. The guidance also highlights that national origin discrimination can occur even if the victim and the perpetrator are of the same national origin.

## Key Points for Employers

The EEOC's guidance stresses that employment decisions must not be based on stereotypes or assumptions about abilities, traits, or the performance of individuals of certain national origins. The guidance provides examples of unlawful practices, such as requiring employees to speak only English at all times unless justified by business necessity.

Furthermore, the guidance clarifies that harassment based on national origin is unlawful when it is so frequent or severe that it creates a hostile or offensive work environment or when it results in an adverse employment decision. Employers are encouraged to establish clear anti-harassment policies and procedures for reporting and addressing complaints.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers should audit current policies to ensure they prohibit national origin discrimination and harassment. Policies should clearly define prohibited conduct and outline procedures for reporting and addressing complaints.

2. **Train Employees and Managers**: Conduct training sessions to educate employees and managers about national origin discrimination and the importance of a diverse and inclusive workplace. Training should cover cultural sensitivity and the impact of stereotypes.

3. **Evaluate Language Requirements**: Assess any existing English-only rules to ensure they are justified by business necessity. Document the rationale for such rules and be prepared to modify or eliminate them if they cannot be justified.

4. **Implement Reporting Mechanisms**: Establish or reinforce mechanisms for employees to report discrimination or harassment without fear of retaliation. Ensure that complaints are investigated promptly and thoroughly.

5. **Monitor Workplace Practices**: Regularly monitor workplace practices and decisions to ensure they are free from national origin bias. This includes recruitment, hiring, promotions, and terminations.

By taking these steps, employers can better align with the EEOC's updated guidance and foster a workplace environment that respects and values diversity. Failure to comply with these guidelines could result in significant legal and financial consequences.
