## DOL Revises Tip Credit Regulations

On April 15, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) announced significant changes to the regulations governing the tip credit under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). The final rule, set to take effect on June 1, 2026, alters how employers can apply the tip credit towards the federal minimum wage for tipped employees. This change impacts restaurants, hotels, and other businesses employing tipped staff.

## Key Changes to Tip Credit Application

The new rule modifies the "80/20" rule, which previously allowed employers to claim a tip credit only if a tipped employee spent no more than 20% of their time on non-tipped duties. The revision introduces a more flexible standard, permitting employers to apply the tip credit as long as non-tipped duties are performed concurrently with tipped duties or within a reasonable time immediately before or after tipped duties.

## What Employers Need to Do

Employers must review and potentially adjust their payroll practices to comply with the new rule. Key actions include:

1. **Audit Current Practices**: Evaluate current wage calculations for tipped employees to ensure compliance with the revised rule. Employers should assess whether non-tipped duties fall within the new guidelines.

2. **Training for Managers**: Provide training for managers and payroll staff on the new standards to ensure proper application of the tip credit.

3. **Documentation**: Maintain detailed records of employees' duties and time allocation to defend against potential wage and hour claims.

4. **Policy Updates**: Amend employee handbooks and wage policies to reflect the changes in tip credit application.

## Legal and Financial Implications

Non-compliance with the new DOL rule could result in wage and hour claims, leading to costly litigation and potential back pay liabilities. Employers must ensure that their policies align with the revised regulations to mitigate legal risks.

## Conclusion

The DOL's updated tip credit regulations require immediate attention from businesses employing tipped workers. By auditing practices, training staff, and updating policies, employers can ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties. The June 1, 2026 effective date provides a short window for necessary adjustments, making prompt action essential.
