## DOL Clarifies FMLA Eligibility for Part-Time Employees

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued new guidance on June 15, 2026, clarifying how employers should calculate Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) eligibility for part-time employees. This addresses ambiguities in the existing regulations under 29 U.S.C. § 2611 and affects employers with 50 or more employees.

The FMLA entitles eligible employees to take unpaid, job-protected leave for specified family and medical reasons. Traditionally, to qualify, employees must have worked at least 1,250 hours over the past 12 months. However, the calculation of these hours for part-time workers has been a point of confusion for employers.

### Key Changes in Guidance

The new guidance specifies that employers must include all hours worked, including overtime, in the calculation for part-time employees. This clarification ensures that part-time workers are not disadvantaged in meeting the 1,250-hour threshold. Employers previously uncertain about counting overtime hours now have clear instructions to include them.

Additionally, the guidance addresses the treatment of employees with fluctuating work schedules. Employers are advised to use the average number of hours worked per week over the 12-month period to determine eligibility, rather than relying solely on scheduled hours.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers should immediately review their FMLA policies and procedures to ensure compliance with the new guidance. Key steps include:

1. **Audit Employee Records:** Check the calculation of hours for part-time employees over the past 12 months to verify FMLA eligibility.

2. **Update HR Systems:** Ensure that payroll and HR systems correctly track and calculate all hours worked, including overtime, for part-time employees.

3. **Train HR Staff:** Conduct training sessions for HR personnel to familiarize them with the updated guidance and its implications for FMLA eligibility assessments.

4. **Communicate with Employees:** Inform part-time employees about their potential eligibility for FMLA leave under the clarified rules.

### Implications for Employers

This clarification may increase the number of part-time employees eligible for FMLA leave, potentially impacting workforce planning and staffing. Employers should prepare for an uptick in leave requests and consider the operational adjustments necessary to accommodate these changes.

By taking proactive steps to align with the new DOL guidance, employers can avoid potential compliance issues and ensure that all eligible employees receive their entitled benefits under FMLA.
