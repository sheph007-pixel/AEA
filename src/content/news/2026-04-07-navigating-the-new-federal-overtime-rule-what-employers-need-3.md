## What Changed

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) has finalized its 2026 adjustment to the salary threshold for overtime-exempt employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). Effective July 1, 2026, the minimum salary for the executive, administrative, and professional (EAP) exemptions will increase to $1,128 per week ($58,656 annually), up from the current $1,059 per week threshold.

This adjustment reflects the DOL's commitment to periodic threshold updates based on wage data, a mechanism established in its 2024 rulemaking. Employers should begin preparing now to ensure compliance before the effective date.

## Who Is Affected

The updated threshold applies to all employers covered by the FLSA. Employees who currently earn between $1,059 and $1,128 per week and are classified as exempt will need to be reclassified as non-exempt — making them eligible for overtime pay — unless their salaries are increased to meet the new minimum.

Industries with a high proportion of salaried employees near the threshold, such as retail management, hospitality, and nonprofit organizations, may be disproportionately affected.

## Highly Compensated Employee Threshold

The DOL has also adjusted the total annual compensation requirement for the highly compensated employee (HCE) exemption to $151,164, up from $143,988. Employers relying on the HCE exemption should verify that affected employees meet the new threshold.

## What Employers Should Do

- **Audit current classifications**: Identify all employees currently classified as exempt who earn below the new $1,128 per week threshold
- **Decide on a compliance approach**: For each affected employee, determine whether to raise their salary to maintain exempt status or reclassify them as non-exempt with overtime eligibility
- **Update payroll systems**: Ensure timekeeping and payroll systems are ready to track hours for any newly non-exempt employees before July 1
- **Communicate changes early**: Notify affected employees about any changes to their classification or compensation structure well in advance of the effective date
- **Review state requirements**: Several states, including California, New York, and Washington, maintain their own salary thresholds that may exceed the federal level — employers should comply with whichever standard is higher

## Looking Ahead

The DOL has indicated that it intends to continue updating the salary threshold on a regular schedule. Employers should build periodic compensation reviews into their compliance planning to stay ahead of future adjustments.
