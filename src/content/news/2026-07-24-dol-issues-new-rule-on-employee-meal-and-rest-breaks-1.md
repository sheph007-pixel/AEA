## DOL Clarifies Meal and Rest Break Requirements

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on July 20, 2026, clarifying meal and rest break requirements under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This new rule aims to standardize break periods across industries, addressing inconsistencies that have led to confusion and litigation. The rule will take effect on January 1, 2027, and applies to all employers covered by the FLSA.

Under the new regulation, employees must receive a minimum 30-minute unpaid meal break for every six hours worked. Additionally, employees are entitled to two paid 10-minute rest breaks during an eight-hour shift. These breaks must be scheduled in a manner that provides reasonable rest periods.

## Compliance Requirements

Employers must update their break policies to comply with the new rule by the effective date. This includes revising employee handbooks, training managers on the new requirements, and ensuring that timekeeping systems accurately track break periods.

Failure to comply with these requirements could result in penalties under the FLSA. Employers should conduct an audit of their current break policies and practices to identify any necessary changes. It is also advisable to communicate these changes clearly to employees to avoid potential disputes.

## Impact on Employers

The DOL's clarification aims to reduce litigation related to meal and rest breaks, a common issue in wage and hour disputes. By standardizing break periods, the rule provides a clearer framework for both employers and employees, potentially reducing the risk of costly lawsuits.

Employers should also be aware of state laws that may impose additional or different requirements. The new federal rule does not preempt state laws that provide greater benefits to employees. Therefore, it is crucial for employers to review state-specific regulations in conjunction with the new federal requirements.

## Action Items

- **Review and update break policies:** Ensure compliance with the new minimum break requirements.
- **Train management staff:** Educate managers on the new rule to ensure consistent application.
- **Audit timekeeping systems:** Verify that systems can accurately track required break periods.
- **Communicate with employees:** Clearly explain the new break policy to all staff members.

By taking these steps, employers can align with the DOL's new rule, minimizing the risk of non-compliance and potential litigation.
