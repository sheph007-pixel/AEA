## IRS Clarifies Employee Retention Credit Claims

On June 1, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) released updated guidance on the Employee Retention Credit (ERC), providing clarity on eligibility criteria and documentation requirements. This move aims to assist employers who are navigating the complexities of claiming this significant tax credit, especially following numerous changes to the credit's provisions over the past years.

The ERC, originally established under the CARES Act, was designed to encourage businesses to keep employees on their payroll during the COVID-19 pandemic. The credit has undergone several modifications, including extensions and changes in eligibility criteria, leading to confusion among employers.

### Key Highlights of the New Guidance

The latest IRS guidance addresses several critical areas:

1. **Eligibility Criteria**: The IRS has reiterated that businesses must demonstrate a significant decline in gross receipts or a full or partial suspension of operations due to governmental orders related to COVID-19. The new guidance provides detailed examples to help employers determine if they meet these conditions.

2. **Documentation Requirements**: Employers are required to maintain comprehensive records to substantiate their claims. The IRS emphasizes the need for clear documentation of qualifying wages, evidence of a decline in gross receipts, and any relevant governmental orders that affected business operations.

3. **Interaction with Other Relief Programs**: The guidance clarifies the interaction between the ERC and other COVID-19 relief programs, such as the Paycheck Protection Program (PPP). Employers must ensure that wages used to claim the ERC are not also used to justify PPP loan forgiveness.

### Action Items for Employers

Employers looking to claim the ERC should take the following steps:

- **Review Eligibility**: Carefully assess whether your business meets the updated eligibility criteria. This includes analyzing gross receipts and understanding the impact of any governmental orders on your operations.

- **Gather Documentation**: Ensure all necessary documentation is in place. This includes payroll records, financial statements, and any governmental orders that led to operational suspensions.

- **Consult with Tax Professionals**: Given the complexity of the ERC and its interaction with other relief programs, consulting with a tax professional can help navigate the nuances and ensure compliance with IRS requirements.

- **File Amendments if Necessary**: If your business previously claimed the ERC without meeting the clarified criteria, consider filing amended returns to rectify any discrepancies.

The IRS's latest guidance aims to streamline the ERC claims process, reducing the risk of errors and ensuring that eligible businesses can benefit from this valuable tax credit. Employers should act promptly to align their documentation and claims with the updated requirements to avoid potential audits or penalties.
