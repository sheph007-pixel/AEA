## New Requirements for Job Postings in New York

Starting September 1, 2026, New York employers with at least four employees must include salary ranges in all job postings, as per the New York State Pay Transparency Law (N.Y. Lab. Law § 194-b). This legislation aims to promote pay equity by requiring transparency in compensation offers.

## Who is Affected?

The law applies to all employers with four or more employees, including employment agencies and businesses with employees working remotely in New York. It mandates that job advertisements include the minimum and maximum salary or hourly wage for the role. This requirement covers both internal and external job postings.

## Compliance Steps for Employers

Employers should immediately review and update their job posting procedures to ensure compliance by the September deadline. Key actions include:

1. **Audit Current Job Descriptions**: Evaluate existing job descriptions to determine appropriate salary ranges. Consider market rates, internal pay equity, and company compensation policies.

2. **Train HR and Recruiting Staff**: Ensure that those responsible for drafting job postings understand the new requirements and can accurately represent the company's pay practices.

3. **Update Job Posting Platforms**: Modify templates on job boards, company websites, and other recruiting platforms to include salary ranges.

4. **Document Pay Decisions**: Maintain clear records of how salary ranges are determined to defend against potential claims of pay discrimination.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the new law could result in penalties, including fines and potential legal action. The New York State Department of Labor (NYSDOL) is tasked with enforcement, and employees may file complaints if they believe their employer is not adhering to the law.

## Broader Implications

This move by New York follows similar legislation in other states, such as California and Colorado, reflecting a broader trend towards pay transparency in the United States. Employers operating in multiple jurisdictions should be aware of varying state requirements to ensure comprehensive compliance.

## Conclusion

New York's salary range disclosure requirement represents a significant shift in employment practices aimed at fostering pay equity. Employers must act swiftly to align their job posting processes with the new law, ensuring transparency and avoiding potential penalties.
