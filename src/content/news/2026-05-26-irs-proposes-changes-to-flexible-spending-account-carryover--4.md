## IRS Proposes Changes to FSA Carryover Limits

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) proposed a change to the carryover limits for Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs) on May 15, 2026. This proposal aims to increase the maximum amount employees can carry over to the next plan year, enhancing flexibility in managing healthcare expenses. The proposed rule would amend the current limit under 26 U.S.C. § 125(i) from $610 to $750, effective for plan years beginning in 2027.

FSAs allow employees to set aside pre-tax dollars for qualified medical expenses, offering significant tax savings. The current carryover limit, set at $610, has been criticized for not keeping pace with rising healthcare costs. The proposed increase to $750 intends to address this issue, providing employees with greater financial flexibility and reducing the risk of forfeiting unused funds.

## Implications for Employers

Employers offering FSAs should prepare for potential changes in plan administration if the proposal is finalized. The increased carryover limit would require updates to plan documents and employee communications. Employers may need to work with benefits administrators to ensure systems and processes are ready to accommodate the new limit. 

Additionally, HR professionals should consider how these changes might affect employee participation and satisfaction. An increased carryover limit could encourage more employees to enroll in FSAs, knowing they have more leeway to manage their healthcare spending without the fear of losing unused funds.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Plan Documents**: Employers should review and update FSA plan documents to reflect the proposed changes. This includes adjusting carryover provisions and ensuring compliance with IRS regulations.

2. **Communicate Changes**: Clear communication with employees is essential. HR should prepare to inform employees about the potential new carryover limits and how it impacts their benefits.

3. **Coordinate with Administrators**: Work with benefits administrators to update systems and processes. Ensure that carryover calculations and fund management align with the new limits.

4. **Monitor IRS Announcements**: Stay informed about the finalization of this proposal. The IRS will likely provide further guidance and a timeline for implementation.

If the IRS finalizes the proposal, these changes could significantly impact both employers and employees by enhancing the flexibility and attractiveness of FSAs. Employers should act proactively to ensure a smooth transition and maximize the benefits for their workforce.
