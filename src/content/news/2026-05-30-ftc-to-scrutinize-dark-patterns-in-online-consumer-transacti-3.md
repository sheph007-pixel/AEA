## FTC Targets Dark Patterns in Online Transactions

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) announced new guidelines on May 25, 2026, targeting "dark patterns" in online consumer transactions. These guidelines, effective July 1, 2026, aim to curb deceptive practices that manipulate consumer behavior in digital interfaces. The FTC's move follows growing concerns about the ethical implications of such practices, which can lead to uninformed consumer decisions.

Dark patterns refer to design choices in online interfaces that trick users into actions they might not otherwise take, such as unwittingly subscribing to services or sharing personal data. The FTC's guidelines clarify that these practices may violate Section 5 of the FTC Act, which prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in commerce.

## Key Provisions and Implications

The new guidelines outline several specific practices that could be deemed deceptive, including:

1. **Sneak into Basket**: Automatically adding products to a consumer's shopping cart without explicit consent.
2. **Roach Motel**: Making it easy for consumers to sign up for a service but difficult to cancel.
3. **Bait and Switch**: Advertising one service or product but delivering another.
4. **Confirmshaming**: Using guilt-inducing language to discourage users from opting out of services.

The FTC emphasizes the importance of transparency and clarity in online transactions. Businesses must ensure that all terms and conditions are clearly presented and that consumers have a straightforward path to consent or decline any service or product.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers, particularly those in e-commerce or digital services, should take immediate steps to review their online transaction processes. Key actions include:

- **Audit User Interfaces**: Conduct a thorough audit of all digital interfaces to identify and eliminate any elements that could be considered dark patterns.
- **Training and Compliance**: Train web development and marketing teams on the new guidelines to ensure compliance. Regular compliance checks should be scheduled.
- **Update Terms and Conditions**: Revise terms and conditions to enhance clarity and ensure they are prominently displayed and easy to understand.
- **Consumer Feedback Channels**: Establish clear and accessible channels for consumer feedback and complaints regarding online transactions.

Non-compliance with the FTC's guidelines could result in significant penalties, including fines and mandatory corrective measures. Businesses should prioritize aligning their practices with these guidelines to avoid potential legal and reputational risks.

## Looking Ahead

The FTC's focus on dark patterns reflects a broader regulatory trend towards protecting consumer rights in the digital economy. As digital transactions continue to grow, businesses must remain vigilant and proactive in ensuring their practices meet evolving legal standards. The FTC's guidelines serve as a critical reminder of the importance of ethical design practices in maintaining consumer trust and avoiding regulatory scrutiny.
