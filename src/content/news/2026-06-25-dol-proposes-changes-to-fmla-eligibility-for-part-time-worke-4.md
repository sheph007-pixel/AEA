## Proposed Rule Expands FMLA Access

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 15, 2026, aimed at expanding Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) eligibility to part-time employees. Currently, FMLA eligibility requires employees to work at least 1,250 hours in the previous 12 months. The proposed rule seeks to reduce this threshold to 750 hours, significantly increasing the number of part-time workers who could qualify for unpaid leave.

## Implications for Employers

If implemented, the rule would require employers to adjust their policies and track hours worked by part-time employees more diligently. The change could impact staffing and scheduling, as more employees may take advantage of FMLA leave. Employers with 50 or more employees, the threshold for FMLA applicability under 29 U.S.C. § 2611(4)(A)(i), should prepare for potential increases in FMLA leave requests.

## Action Items

Employers should begin evaluating their current workforce to determine how many part-time employees might become eligible under the new rule. HR departments should consider updating their leave policies and training managers on the implications of expanded FMLA eligibility. Additionally, employers might need to invest in systems to better track part-time hours to ensure compliance.

## Next Steps

The DOL has invited public comments on the proposed rule until September 15, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the comment process to express any concerns or support regarding the impact of the proposed changes. Finalization of the rule could occur by the end of the year, with potential implementation in 2027.

This proposal represents a significant shift in FMLA policy, reflecting growing recognition of the needs of part-time workers. Employers should stay alert to developments and prepare for changes that may affect their workforce management and benefits administration.
