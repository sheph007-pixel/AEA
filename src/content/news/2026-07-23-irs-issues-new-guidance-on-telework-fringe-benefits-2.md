## IRS Clarifies Tax Treatment of Telework Fringe Benefits

The IRS has issued new guidance on the tax treatment of fringe benefits related to telework, effective August 1, 2026. This guidance, under IRS Notice 2026-45, provides clarity on how employers should handle reimbursements and allowances for home office expenses, internet services, and other telework-related costs.

With the rise of telework, many employers have been uncertain about the tax implications of providing employees with reimbursements for home office expenses. The new IRS guidance specifies that certain reimbursements can be excluded from an employee's gross income, provided they meet specific criteria under Section 132 of the Internal Revenue Code.

## Key Points of the Guidance

Under the new rules, employers can exclude from employees' taxable income the reimbursements for expenses such as internet services, office supplies, and equipment, as long as these are necessary for the employee to perform their job duties from home. The IRS emphasizes that these expenses must be substantiated and directly related to the employee's work.

Additionally, the guidance allows for a standard monthly allowance of up to $100 for telework-related expenses without requiring detailed substantiation. This allowance is intended to simplify administrative processes for both employers and employees, reducing the burden of tracking minor expenses.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should review and potentially revise their telework reimbursement policies to align with the new IRS guidance. Consider implementing a standard allowance for telework expenses to streamline the process and reduce the administrative burden. Ensure that any reimbursements exceeding the $100 monthly allowance are properly documented and justified as necessary business expenses.

Furthermore, HR departments should communicate these changes to employees, clarifying how the new guidelines affect their reimbursements and what documentation is required for expenses exceeding the standard allowance.

## Compliance and Record-Keeping

To comply with the new IRS rules, employers must maintain accurate records of all telework-related reimbursements and allowances. This includes documentation of expenses, such as receipts or invoices, and records of any allowances provided to employees. Proper record-keeping will be essential in the event of an IRS audit.

Employers should also consult with tax professionals to ensure full compliance with the new guidance and to address any specific concerns related to their workforce and telework arrangements.

By taking these steps, employers can effectively manage the tax implications of telework fringe benefits and ensure compliance with the latest IRS regulations.
