## Federal Appeals Court Ruling Impacts FMLA Leave Calculations

On June 30, 2026, the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals issued a ruling in the case of Smith v. XYZ Corp., clarifying how employers must calculate Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) leave for employees with fluctuating work schedules. The decision addresses ambiguities in the application of 29 U.S.C. § 2612(a)(1) concerning leave entitlement and could impact employers across the United States, particularly those with operations in the Ninth Circuit's jurisdiction.

The court's decision emphasizes that employers must calculate FMLA leave based on the average number of hours worked by the employee in the 12 months preceding the start of the leave. This ruling clarifies that employers cannot default to a standard 40-hour workweek calculation for employees with variable schedules, potentially increasing the amount of leave available to such employees.

### Implications for Employers

The ruling mandates that employers reassess their current FMLA leave calculation methods, especially for employees whose work hours vary significantly. Employers must ensure that their HR systems accurately track and compute the average weekly hours worked over the past 12 months to determine the correct FMLA leave entitlement.

Failure to comply with this interpretation may lead to legal challenges and potential penalties under the FMLA. Employers should review their leave policies and procedures to align with this ruling and avoid potential litigation.

### Action Items

1. **Audit Leave Calculations**: Employers should immediately audit their current FMLA leave calculation methods. Ensure that HR personnel are trained to compute leave based on the average hours worked over the prior 12 months for employees with fluctuating schedules.

2. **Update HR Systems**: Modify HR systems to automatically calculate FMLA leave entitlement based on the new ruling. This may involve integrating payroll data to accurately reflect average hours worked.

3. **Revise Employee Handbooks**: Update employee handbooks and leave policies to reflect the new calculation method. Clearly communicate these changes to all employees, particularly those with variable work schedules.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal counsel to review the implications of the ruling and ensure compliance with FMLA requirements. This is particularly crucial for employers operating in multiple jurisdictions.

By taking these steps, employers can ensure compliance with the Ninth Circuit's ruling and mitigate the risk of legal challenges related to FMLA leave calculations. This decision underscores the importance of precise recordkeeping and compliance with federal employment laws.
