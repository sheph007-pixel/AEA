## IRS Clarifies Tax Treatment of Employee Stock Purchase Plans

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new guidance on May 15, 2026, regarding the tax treatment of Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs) under Section 423 of the Internal Revenue Code. This guidance impacts how employers administer these plans and report related income, affecting compliance and payroll processes.

Employee Stock Purchase Plans allow employees to purchase company stock at a discount, often through payroll deductions. The new IRS guidance clarifies the tax implications of these plans, specifically addressing the timing and nature of income recognition for employees participating in ESPPs.

### Key Points of the Guidance

The IRS guidance specifies that the discount on the stock purchase is not taxable at the time of purchase if the plan meets Section 423 requirements. Instead, taxation occurs when the employee sells the stock. If the stock is held for more than two years from the offering date and one year from the purchase date, any gain is treated as capital gain, and the discount portion is taxed as ordinary income. If these holding periods are not met, the entire gain is taxed as ordinary income.

Additionally, the guidance provides clarity on reporting requirements for employers. Employers must report the ordinary income portion of the discount on Form W-2 in the year the employee sells the stock, not at the time of purchase. This ensures that employees and the IRS are accurately informed about taxable income related to ESPPs.

### Employer Action Items

Employers offering ESPPs need to review their plan documents to ensure compliance with Section 423 requirements. This includes verifying the plan’s offering and purchase periods, discount rates, and eligibility criteria. Employers should also update their payroll systems to accurately track and report income related to ESPPs on Form W-2, ensuring that employees are informed about their tax obligations.

Training HR and payroll staff on these new reporting requirements is crucial to avoid potential compliance issues and penalties. Employers may also consider communicating these changes to employees participating in ESPPs, helping them understand the tax implications of their stock transactions.

### Implications for HR and Payroll

This IRS guidance provides much-needed clarity for employers managing ESPPs. By understanding the tax treatment and reporting requirements, employers can ensure compliance and minimize the risk of audits or penalties. Accurate reporting also benefits employees by preventing unexpected tax liabilities, fostering better financial planning.

Employers should consult with tax advisors or legal counsel to ensure their ESPPs are structured in compliance with the new IRS guidance. This proactive approach will help maintain the attractiveness of ESPPs as a valuable employee benefit while ensuring adherence to tax regulations.
