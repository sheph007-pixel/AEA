## IRS Clarifies ESPP Regulations

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has issued new regulations regarding Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs), effective January 1, 2026. These changes aim to clarify the tax treatment and eligibility criteria for ESPPs under Internal Revenue Code Section 423. Employers offering ESPPs must review their plans to ensure compliance or risk penalties.

## Key Changes and Implications

The new regulations address several key areas:

1. **Eligibility Criteria**: The IRS has clarified that employees must be employed for at least three months before participating in an ESPP. This change is intended to standardize eligibility and prevent rapid turnover from affecting plan integrity.

2. **Discount Limits**: The allowable discount on stock purchases remains at 15%, but the new rules specify that the discount must be applied to the lesser of the stock's market value at the beginning or end of the offering period. This ensures a consistent approach to calculating discounts.

3. **Tax Reporting Requirements**: Employers must report ESPP transactions on Form W-2, detailing the discount received by employees. This requirement aims to improve transparency and accuracy in tax reporting associated with ESPPs.

4. **Lookback Provision**: The regulations reaffirm the use of a lookback provision, allowing employees to purchase stock at the lower of the market price at the start or end of the offering period. This provision remains a critical feature for attracting and retaining employees.

## Employer Action Items

Employers need to take several steps to comply with the new IRS regulations:

- **Review Plan Documents**: Ensure that ESPP documents reflect the new eligibility criteria and discount calculation method. Amendments may be necessary to align with the updated rules.

- **Update Payroll Systems**: Modify payroll systems to accommodate the new tax reporting requirements. Ensure that Form W-2 accurately reflects ESPP transactions for each participating employee.

- **Employee Communication**: Inform employees about changes to the ESPP, including eligibility updates and how these may affect their participation. Clear communication will help maintain employee engagement and satisfaction.

- **Consult with Legal and Tax Advisors**: Engage with legal and tax professionals to review ESPP compliance and address any potential issues arising from the new regulations.

## Conclusion

These IRS updates to ESPP regulations require immediate attention from employers to ensure compliance by the January 1, 2026 deadline. By taking proactive measures, companies can continue to offer attractive stock purchase plans while avoiding potential penalties.
