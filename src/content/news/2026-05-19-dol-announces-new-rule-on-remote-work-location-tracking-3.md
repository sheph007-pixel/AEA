## DOL Mandates Remote Work Location Tracking

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) announced a new rule on May 18, 2026, requiring employers to track remote work locations to ensure compliance with wage and hour laws. This rule, effective January 1, 2027, aims to address potential discrepancies in the application of state labor laws due to remote work arrangements.

The rule, issued under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), necessitates that employers document the primary work location of each remote employee. This measure is intended to clarify which state’s labor laws apply, particularly regarding minimum wage, overtime pay, and rest breaks.

### Compliance Requirements

Employers must update their systems to record the primary work location for remote employees and ensure this information is accurately reflected in payroll systems. The DOL has specified that the primary work location is where the employee performs the majority of their work duties. This location must be reviewed and confirmed quarterly.

Employers should also be aware of potential tax implications that may arise from this rule. Different states have varying tax obligations, and tracking remote work locations can help ensure compliance with state tax laws.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Audit Current Systems**: Evaluate existing HR and payroll systems to determine their capability to track and report employee work locations.

2. **Update Employee Agreements**: Amend remote work agreements to include clauses about location reporting and compliance with state-specific labor laws.

3. **Quarterly Reviews**: Implement a process for quarterly verification of remote work locations to maintain compliance and adjust payroll and tax obligations accordingly.

4. **Training**: Educate HR personnel and managers on the new requirements and the importance of accurate location tracking.

5. **Consult Legal Advisors**: Engage with legal counsel to understand the implications of the new rule on your specific business operations and to ensure all remote work policies are compliant with state and federal laws.

### Implications

The new rule underscores the importance of location in determining applicable labor laws, a growing concern as remote work becomes more prevalent. By mandating the tracking of work locations, the DOL seeks to prevent potential violations of wage and hour laws that could arise from employees working in states with different labor regulations.

Employers need to be proactive in adapting to these requirements to avoid penalties and ensure that their remote work policies are compliant with both federal and state laws. The upcoming changes also highlight the need for businesses to maintain flexible and responsive HR systems capable of adapting to regulatory shifts in the remote work landscape.
