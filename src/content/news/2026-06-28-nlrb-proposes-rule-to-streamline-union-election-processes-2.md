## NLRB Proposes Rule to Expedite Union Elections

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) proposed a new rule on June 27, 2026, designed to streamline the union election process. This proposal seeks to shorten the time frame between the filing of a petition and the election date, potentially reducing the current average of 23 days to as few as 14 days. The rule aims to eliminate procedural delays and enhance the efficiency of the election process under the National Labor Relations Act (29 U.S.C. §§ 151-169).

## Key Changes and Implications

The proposed rule includes several key changes to the current election procedures. It mandates that pre-election hearings be scheduled within seven days of a petition filing, and that employers must submit their statements of position within five days. Additionally, the rule limits the scope of pre-election hearings to issues directly related to the question of representation, excluding issues such as voter eligibility, which would be resolved post-election if necessary.

Employers should be aware that these changes could significantly limit the time available to communicate with employees about unionization efforts. The accelerated timeline may also reduce the opportunity for employers to address employee concerns or misconceptions about union representation.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should prepare for the potential impact of this rule by reviewing and updating their labor relations strategies. Key steps include:

- **Training Management:** Ensure that supervisors and managers are trained to recognize and respond appropriately to union organizing activities.
- **Communication Plans:** Develop clear communication strategies to inform employees about the company's stance on unionization and the implications of joining a union.
- **Legal Review:** Consult with labor law experts to understand the implications of the proposed rule and to ensure compliance with all NLRB regulations.

## Next Steps and Public Comment

The NLRB has opened a public comment period on the proposed rule, allowing stakeholders to submit feedback until August 15, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in this process to express concerns or support for the proposed changes. The NLRB will review the comments before finalizing the rule, which could take effect as early as late 2026.

By staying informed and proactive, employers can better navigate the potential changes and maintain compliance with labor laws. As the landscape of labor relations continues to evolve, understanding these regulatory shifts is crucial for effective workforce management.
