## NLRB Guidance on Surveillance

On July 5, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) released new guidance regarding employer surveillance practices. This guidance clarifies how certain monitoring activities can infringe on employees' rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). The NLRB's memo specifically addresses the balance between legitimate business interests and employees' rights to engage in protected concerted activities.

## Key Takeaways

The guidance emphasizes that surveillance practices should not interfere with employees' ability to organize or engage in other protected activities. Employers using technologies such as video monitoring, GPS tracking, or software that monitors computer usage must ensure these practices do not discourage or intimidate employees from exercising their rights under Section 7 of the NLRA.

The NLRB's memo calls for employers to assess their surveillance practices, particularly those that could be perceived as overly intrusive or aimed at monitoring union activities. The Board highlighted that even indirect surveillance or the mere threat of surveillance can be considered unlawful if it deters employees from engaging in protected activities.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Surveillance Policies**: Employers should conduct a thorough review of their current surveillance and monitoring policies. This review should focus on identifying any practices that could potentially infringe on employees' rights under the NLRA.

2. **Conduct Impact Assessments**: Consider conducting assessments to determine the impact of surveillance technologies on employee rights. This includes evaluating whether the surveillance is necessary for business purposes and if less intrusive alternatives are available.

3. **Train Management**: Ensure that management and HR personnel are trained on the implications of the NLRB's guidance. Training should cover the legal boundaries of surveillance and the importance of respecting employees' rights.

4. **Communicate with Employees**: Clearly communicate the purpose and scope of any surveillance practices to employees. Transparency can help alleviate concerns and demonstrate compliance with the NLRB's guidance.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's recent guidance on employee surveillance practices underscores the need for employers to carefully navigate the intersection of technology and employee rights. By reviewing and adjusting current practices, employers can ensure compliance with the NLRA while maintaining effective workplace monitoring.
