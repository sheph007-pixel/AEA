## DOL Proposes Rule to Simplify FMLA Eligibility

On July 20, 2026, the Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule aimed at simplifying the eligibility criteria for the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). This proposal seeks to address longstanding concerns about the complexity and administrative burden associated with determining employee eligibility for FMLA leave.

The proposed rule intends to clarify the definition of "eligible employee" under the FMLA, which currently requires employees to have worked for their employer for at least 12 months and to have clocked at least 1,250 hours during the previous year. The proposed changes would introduce a more straightforward calculation method, potentially reducing the administrative load on HR departments.

### Key Changes

The proposed rule suggests a shift from the current hours-based eligibility requirement to a more simplified tenure-based system. Under the new proposal, employees would qualify for FMLA leave after completing a continuous 12-month period of employment, regardless of the number of hours worked. This change aims to eliminate the need for employers to track hours worked meticulously, thus easing compliance burdens.

Additionally, the rule proposes clearer guidelines on what constitutes a "serious health condition," which is a common point of confusion for both employers and employees. By providing more precise definitions, the DOL hopes to reduce disputes and streamline the process for approving FMLA leave requests.

### Employer Action Items

If the rule is finalized, employers will need to revise their FMLA policies and procedures to align with the new eligibility criteria. This includes updating employee handbooks, training HR personnel on the new guidelines, and ensuring that payroll systems can accommodate the revised tenure-based eligibility calculation.

Employers should also prepare to communicate these changes to their workforce, emphasizing how the new rule may impact their eligibility for FMLA leave. Clear communication will be essential to minimize confusion and ensure a smooth transition.

### Next Steps

The DOL will accept public comments on the proposed rule until September 18, 2026. Employers and HR professionals are encouraged to review the proposal in detail and submit any feedback or concerns during this period. The final rule, if adopted, is expected to take effect in early 2027.

The proposed changes to FMLA eligibility criteria represent a significant shift in how employers manage leave requests. By simplifying the process, the DOL aims to make it easier for employees to access their entitled benefits while reducing the administrative burden on employers. As this proposal moves through the rulemaking process, staying informed and prepared will be crucial for all stakeholders involved.
