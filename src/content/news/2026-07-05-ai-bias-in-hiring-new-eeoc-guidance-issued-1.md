## EEOC Issues New Guidance on AI Bias in Hiring

On June 30, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance addressing the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in hiring processes. The guidance focuses on preventing discrimination and ensuring compliance with Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. This move highlights the agency's growing concern over AI tools potentially perpetuating bias in employment decisions.

The EEOC's guidance outlines how employers using AI in recruitment and selection processes must ensure these tools do not result in discriminatory practices. It emphasizes that employers remain liable for any discriminatory outcomes, even if they arise from third-party AI systems. The guidance underscores the necessity for employers to regularly audit their AI tools for bias and to implement corrective measures if disparities are found.

### Key Points for Employers

1. **Regular Audits**: Employers must conduct regular audits of AI tools used in hiring to identify and mitigate any discriminatory impacts. This includes analyzing data to ensure that AI decisions do not disproportionately affect protected groups.

2. **Vendor Accountability**: When using third-party AI systems, employers should ensure vendors provide transparency about how their algorithms function. Employers should seek assurances that these tools comply with EEOC regulations.

3. **Training and Documentation**: Employers should train HR personnel involved in the hiring process on the potential biases of AI systems. Additionally, maintaining comprehensive documentation of AI tool evaluations and adjustments is crucial for demonstrating compliance with EEOC guidelines.

4. **Transparency with Candidates**: The guidance encourages employers to inform job applicants about the use of AI in the hiring process and to provide a means for candidates to request reconsideration of decisions made by AI tools.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Audit AI Tools**: Conduct immediate audits of AI systems to identify potential biases. Focus on how these tools impact different demographic groups.
- **Engage with Vendors**: Review contracts with AI vendors to ensure compliance with EEOC standards. Require transparency about AI algorithms and their decision-making processes.
- **Update Policies**: Amend hiring policies to include provisions for regular AI audits and corrective actions. Ensure policies are aligned with the latest EEOC guidance.
- **Educate Staff**: Implement training programs for HR staff on AI bias and discrimination prevention. Emphasize the importance of human oversight in AI-driven decisions.
- **Candidate Communication**: Develop clear communication strategies to inform candidates about AI use in hiring and their rights under EEOC guidelines.

The EEOC's latest guidance serves as a reminder of the legal responsibilities employers have when integrating AI into their hiring processes. By taking proactive steps to audit and adjust AI tools, employers can mitigate risks and foster fairer employment practices.
