## SEC's Proposed Rule on Human Capital Disclosures

On July 15, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed a new rule that would require public companies to provide more detailed disclosures about their human capital resources. This proposal aims to enhance transparency and provide investors with better insights into how companies manage and value their workforce.

The proposed rule builds on the SEC's 2020 amendments to Regulation S-K, which first introduced human capital as a disclosure topic but left the specifics largely undefined. Under the new rule, companies would need to disclose metrics related to workforce composition, turnover, skills development, and compensation. This move reflects the SEC's recognition of human capital as a critical factor in evaluating a company's long-term performance and sustainability.

## Key Requirements and Implications

The SEC's proposal outlines several key disclosure requirements:

1. **Workforce Composition**: Companies must provide detailed information on the diversity of their workforce, including gender, race, and ethnicity data.
   
2. **Turnover Rates**: Disclosure of voluntary and involuntary turnover rates will be required, offering insights into workforce stability and potential risks.

3. **Skills and Development**: Companies need to report on employee training and development programs, including expenditures and participation rates.

4. **Compensation Practices**: Detailed information on pay equity and compensation structures must be disclosed, highlighting efforts to ensure fair and equitable pay practices.

These requirements are designed to standardize human capital disclosures across industries, allowing investors to make more informed decisions. The SEC believes that consistent and comparable data will enable stakeholders to better assess how companies manage their workforce as an asset.

## Action Items for Employers

While the proposed rule specifically targets public companies, its implications may extend to private companies seeking to align with emerging best practices. Employers should consider the following steps:

- **Review Current Reporting Practices**: Assess existing human capital reporting mechanisms to identify gaps and areas for improvement.

- **Enhance Data Collection**: Develop robust systems for collecting and analyzing workforce data, ensuring accuracy and compliance with potential future requirements.

- **Focus on Diversity and Inclusion**: Strengthen diversity and inclusion initiatives, as these will be a focal point of the new disclosures.

- **Prepare for Implementation**: Although the rule is still in the proposal stage, public companies should start preparing for potential implementation by reviewing their human capital strategies and reporting capabilities.

The SEC is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until September 15, 2026. Employers and stakeholders are encouraged to provide feedback, which will be considered in the final rulemaking process.
