## IRS Amends Dependent Care Assistance Program Rules

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) announced amendments to the Dependent Care Assistance Program (DCAP) rules, effective January 1, 2027. The changes, detailed in IRS Notice 2026-45, aim to streamline the administration of DCAPs and enhance compliance with existing tax laws. Employers offering these benefits must prepare for the adjustments to ensure continued compliance.

## Key Changes and Implications

The amendments primarily focus on the annual contribution limits and reporting requirements for DCAPs. The maximum annual contribution limit will increase from $5,000 to $6,000 per household. This change aligns with inflation adjustments and aims to provide employees with greater flexibility in managing dependent care expenses.

Additionally, employers must now report DCAP contributions on employees' Form W-2, Box 10, with greater specificity. This involves breaking down the total contributions by source, distinguishing between employer contributions and employee salary reductions. The IRS intends this change to improve transparency and accuracy in tax reporting.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should take the following steps to comply with the new DCAP rules:

1. **Review and Update Contribution Limits**: Adjust payroll systems to accommodate the new $6,000 contribution limit. Ensure that all relevant payroll and HR systems reflect this change by the January 1, 2027, effective date.

2. **Update Reporting Procedures**: Revise internal reporting processes to ensure accurate breakdowns of DCAP contributions on Form W-2. Coordinate with payroll providers to implement necessary changes to reporting software.

3. **Communicate Changes to Employees**: Inform employees about the new contribution limits and reporting changes. Provide clear guidance on how these changes may affect their dependent care contributions and tax filings.

4. **Consult with Tax Advisors**: Engage with tax professionals to understand the full implications of the new rules and ensure compliance with all IRS regulations.

## Preparing for Compliance

Employers should act promptly to integrate these changes into their business operations. By updating systems and processes now, companies can avoid potential compliance issues and ensure a smooth transition to the new rules. As the effective date approaches, maintaining clear communication with employees and tax advisors will be crucial in managing the impact of these regulatory updates.
