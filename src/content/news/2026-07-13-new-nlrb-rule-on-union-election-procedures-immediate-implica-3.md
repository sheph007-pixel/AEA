## NLRB Revises Union Election Procedures

On July 10, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a final rule revising procedures for union representation elections. This rule, effective immediately, modifies the timeline and conditions under which union elections are conducted, potentially affecting how employers respond to unionization efforts.

The rule reinstates the "quickie" or "ambush" election procedures initially adopted in 2014 but later rolled back in 2017. These procedures shorten the time between the filing of a petition and the election date to as few as 10 days. Additionally, the rule limits pre-election hearings and restricts the scope of issues employers can raise before an election.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must act swiftly under the new timeline. The condensed period between petition filing and election reduces the time available for employers to communicate with employees about unionization. This change necessitates a proactive approach to labor relations.

Under the revised rule, employers have less opportunity to challenge voter eligibility and other pre-election issues. These challenges are now deferred until after the election, which could lead to more post-election litigation.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Labor Relations Strategies:** Employers should review and potentially update their labor relations strategies to ensure they are prepared to communicate effectively with employees about unionization within the shortened timeframe.

2. **Train Management:** Train managers and supervisors on lawful communication about unionization. Understanding what can and cannot be said is crucial to avoid unfair labor practice charges.

3. **Audit Employee Relations Policies:** Conduct an audit of current employee relations policies and practices to ensure they support a positive work environment, reducing the likelihood of unionization efforts.

4. **Monitor Union Activity:** Stay informed about union activity within the industry and locality. Being aware of broader trends can help anticipate and respond to potential organizing efforts.

5. **Prepare for Post-Election Challenges:** Given the new rule's emphasis on post-election challenges, employers should be prepared for potential litigation following union elections.

The NLRB's rule change represents a significant shift in union election procedures, necessitating immediate attention from employers to adapt their strategies and ensure compliance. Failure to adjust to these changes could result in increased unionization and associated legal challenges.
