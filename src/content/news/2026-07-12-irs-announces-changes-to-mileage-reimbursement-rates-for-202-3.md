## IRS Adjusts Mileage Rates for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has announced updated standard mileage reimbursement rates for 2026, effective January 1, 2026. This change affects employers who reimburse employees for business travel using personal vehicles. The new rate is set at 62 cents per mile, an increase from the 2025 rate of 60.5 cents per mile.

### Impact on Employers

Employers should update their expense policies to reflect the new mileage rate. This adjustment is critical to ensure compliance with IRS guidelines and to maintain accurate financial records. Failure to adhere to the new rate could result in discrepancies during audits and potential tax liabilities.

The mileage rate is used to calculate the deductible costs of operating an automobile for business, charitable, medical, or moving purposes. While the business rate has increased, the rate for moving and medical purposes remains unchanged at 22 cents per mile.

### Action Items for HR and Finance Teams

1. **Update Reimbursement Policies**: Revise employee handbooks and internal policies to reflect the new 62 cents per mile rate. Ensure that all relevant departments are aware of this change.

2. **Communicate with Employees**: Inform employees of the updated rate and how it impacts their mileage reimbursements. Clear communication will help prevent confusion and ensure smooth transitions.

3. **Adjust Accounting Systems**: Ensure that accounting software and expense tracking tools are updated to automatically apply the new rate for any travel occurring after January 1, 2026.

4. **Review Budget Allocations**: With the increase in the reimbursement rate, review and adjust budget allocations for travel expenses to accommodate potential increases in costs.

### Compliance and Recordkeeping

Employers must maintain accurate records of mileage reimbursements to comply with IRS requirements. This includes documenting the date, purpose, and distance of each trip. Proper documentation will support claims during IRS audits and help avoid penalties.

### Conclusion

The IRS's adjustment to the mileage reimbursement rate underscores the need for employers to remain vigilant about regulatory changes. By promptly updating policies and systems, businesses can ensure compliance and manage travel expenses effectively.
