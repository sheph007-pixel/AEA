## DOL Proposes New Rule on Workplace Injury Reporting

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a rule on June 1, 2026, aimed at enhancing workplace injury and illness reporting requirements under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (OSHA). This proposal seeks to improve the accuracy and accessibility of data related to workplace incidents, impacting employers across various industries.

Under the proposed rule, companies with 100 or more employees would be required to electronically submit detailed injury and illness records annually. This marks a significant change from the current threshold, which mandates electronic submission only for establishments with 250 or more employees or those in certain high-risk industries with 20 or more employees. The proposed rule also mandates that employers report all incidents resulting in an employee's hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye within 24 hours, aligning with existing requirements for fatalities.

The DOL emphasizes that these changes aim to enhance transparency and improve workplace safety by providing more comprehensive data for analysis and intervention. The proposal is open for public comment until August 15, 2026, allowing stakeholders to provide feedback before the rule is finalized.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should prepare for potential changes by reviewing their current injury and illness reporting procedures. Companies with 100 or more employees should assess their capacity to comply with electronic submission requirements and consider investing in systems that streamline data collection and reporting. Additionally, all employers should ensure they have robust processes to report serious incidents within the proposed 24-hour timeframe.

HR professionals and business owners should monitor the progress of this rule and participate in the public comment period if they have concerns or suggestions. Engaging with industry associations can provide additional insights and support during this transition.

By proactively addressing these potential changes, employers can better manage compliance risks and contribute to safer workplaces.
