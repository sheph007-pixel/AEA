## New Provisions Take Effect

The Setting Every Community Up for Retirement Enhancement (SECURE) 2.0 Act, signed into law on December 29, 2022, includes several provisions that take effect in 2026, impacting employer-sponsored retirement plans. Employers with 401(k) plans or similar retirement offerings must prepare for these changes to ensure compliance and optimize benefits for employees.

## Expanded Automatic Enrollment

Beginning January 1, 2026, SECURE 2.0 mandates automatic enrollment in new 401(k) and 403(b) plans. Under the new rules, employees must be automatically enrolled at a minimum contribution rate of 3%, increasing annually by 1% until reaching at least 10%, but not exceeding 15%. This requirement applies to new plans established after December 29, 2022, exempting existing plans and small businesses with ten or fewer employees.

### Action Item

Employers launching new plans should update enrollment procedures to incorporate automatic enrollment and escalation features. Consider communicating these changes to employees well in advance to ensure understanding and engagement.

## Student Loan Matching Contributions

Another significant change is the introduction of student loan matching contributions. Starting in 2026, employers can treat employees’ student loan payments as elective deferrals for the purpose of matching contributions in retirement plans. This provision aims to help employees who prioritize student debt repayment over retirement savings.

### Action Item

Review plan documents and consult with plan administrators to integrate this matching feature. Employers should also develop a communication strategy to educate employees about this new benefit and its potential impact on their financial well-being.

## Catch-Up Contribution Adjustments

SECURE 2.0 adjusts the rules for catch-up contributions. From 2026, employees aged 60 to 63 can make catch-up contributions up to $10,000 annually, indexed for inflation. This is a significant increase from the current limit of $7,500 for those aged 50 and over.

### Action Item

Employers should update plan documents to reflect the new catch-up contribution limits and ensure payroll systems can accommodate these changes. Communicating these updates to eligible employees will help them maximize their retirement savings opportunities.

## Required Minimum Distribution Age Increase

The age for required minimum distributions (RMDs) from retirement accounts will increase to 75 in 2026. This change allows employees to defer distributions longer, potentially enhancing their retirement savings.

### Action Item

Employers should revise plan documents to align with the new RMD age and inform employees of the benefits and implications of this change. Financial planning resources may be beneficial to help employees understand how this adjustment affects their retirement strategy.

## Conclusion

The SECURE 2.0 Act introduces several changes that require attention from employers offering retirement plans. By understanding and implementing these provisions, employers can enhance their retirement offerings and support employees in achieving better financial outcomes. Coordination with plan administrators and clear communication with employees will be crucial for a smooth transition and compliance with the new requirements.
