## DOL Finalizes Rule on Auto-Enrollment in Retirement Plans

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on May 20, 2026, mandating automatic enrollment for eligible employees in employer-sponsored retirement plans. This rule applies to employers with 10 or more employees and takes effect on January 1, 2027. The rule aims to increase retirement savings participation and is part of the broader effort outlined in the Retirement Enhancement and Savings Act (RESA).

Under the new regulation, employers must automatically enroll eligible employees into their retirement plans at a default contribution rate of at least 3% of the employee's salary. Employees retain the option to opt out or adjust their contribution rate at any time. This requirement is expected to significantly boost participation rates among workers who might otherwise neglect to enroll.

## Compliance Requirements

Employers must update their retirement plan documents and payroll systems to comply with the new auto-enrollment requirements. The rule specifies that employers must provide written notice to employees at least 30 days before the auto-enrollment takes effect. This notice must include information about the default contribution rate, the employee's right to opt out, and how to change contribution levels.

Failure to comply with the new rule could result in penalties under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). Employers should consult with their plan administrators or legal counsel to ensure their systems and documentation meet the new standards.

## Impact on Small and Medium-Sized Businesses

Small and medium-sized businesses may face challenges in implementing the necessary changes to comply with the new rule. However, the DOL has provided resources and guidance to assist employers in transitioning to auto-enrollment. These resources include templates for employee notices and best practices for updating payroll systems.

The DOL estimates that the rule will lead to a 20% increase in retirement plan participation among eligible employees. This change is expected to provide long-term benefits for employees by encouraging greater retirement savings.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Plan Documents**: Ensure that your retirement plan documents reflect the new auto-enrollment requirements.
   
2. **Coordinate with Plan Administrators**: Work with your plan administrators to implement the necessary changes in payroll and enrollment systems.

3. **Prepare Employee Notices**: Develop and distribute the required notices to employees at least 30 days before the auto-enrollment date.

4. **Monitor Compliance**: Establish procedures to regularly review and ensure compliance with the new rule to avoid potential penalties.

Employers should act promptly to integrate these changes and support their workforce in building a more secure financial future.
