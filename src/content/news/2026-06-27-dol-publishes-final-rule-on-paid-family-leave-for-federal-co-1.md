## DOL Finalizes Paid Family Leave Rule

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on June 15, 2026, requiring federal contractors to provide paid family leave to their employees, effective January 1, 2027. The rule, published under 29 C.F.R. Part 13, mandates that federal contractors offer at least 12 weeks of paid family leave for qualifying family and medical reasons. This development aligns with Executive Order 14099, aiming to improve work-life balance for federal contractor employees.

## Key Provisions of the Rule

The rule applies to all federal contractors with contracts exceeding $150,000. Covered employees include those working on or in connection with federal contracts. The paid leave can be used for various reasons, including the birth or adoption of a child, caring for a family member with a serious health condition, or addressing issues arising from a family member's military service.

The DOL specifies that the leave must be paid at the employee’s regular rate of pay. Employers must maintain health benefits during the leave period and restore employees to their original or equivalent positions upon return.

## Employer Action Items

Federal contractors should take immediate steps to comply with the new rule:

1. **Review Contracts**: Determine if your contracts exceed the $150,000 threshold and identify covered employees.
2. **Amend Leave Policies**: Update leave policies to reflect the 12-week paid family leave requirement.
3. **Communicate Changes**: Inform employees about the new leave entitlements and procedures for requesting leave.
4. **Train HR Staff**: Ensure HR personnel understand the rule’s provisions and can assist employees with leave requests.
5. **Budget for Compliance**: Plan for the financial impact of providing paid leave, including potential adjustments to staffing and operations.

## Implications for Federal Contractors

This rule marks a significant shift in federal contractor obligations, aligning them more closely with the benefits offered in the private sector. Contractors who fail to comply risk penalties, including the potential loss of federal contracts. The DOL has indicated it will conduct audits to ensure compliance, emphasizing the importance of timely and accurate implementation.

Contractors should also consider the broader implications for employee retention and recruitment. Offering paid family leave can enhance an employer's reputation and competitiveness in the labor market. As the effective date approaches, contractors must prioritize compliance to avoid disruptions and maintain their standing as federal partners.
