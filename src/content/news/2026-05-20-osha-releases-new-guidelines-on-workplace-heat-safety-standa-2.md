## OSHA's Updated Heat Safety Standards

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has released updated guidelines on workplace heat safety standards, effective June 1, 2026. These guidelines aim to address the increasing risks associated with excessive heat exposure in the workplace, a concern heightened by recent climate trends. The guidelines apply to all industries but focus on sectors with outdoor work environments, such as construction, agriculture, and landscaping.

## Key Provisions

Under the new standards, employers are required to implement a comprehensive heat illness prevention plan. This plan must include provisions for access to water, shade, and rest breaks when temperatures exceed 80 degrees Fahrenheit. Employers must also provide training on heat illness prevention and response for all employees and supervisors.

OSHA has specified that employers must develop a written plan tailored to their specific workplace conditions, detailing how they will monitor the temperature, identify high-risk conditions, and implement protective measures. The guidelines also mandate the appointment of a designated heat safety officer responsible for overseeing the implementation of these measures.

## Compliance and Penalties

Employers failing to comply with the new standards may face significant penalties. OSHA has indicated that enforcement will be stringent, particularly during the summer months when heat-related incidents are most likely to occur. Violations could result in fines ranging from $13,653 to $136,532 per violation, depending on the severity and frequency of non-compliance.

## Action Items for Employers

To comply with the new OSHA heat safety standards, employers should take the following steps:

1. **Develop a Heat Illness Prevention Plan:** Create a written plan that includes procedures for monitoring temperatures, providing access to water and shade, and ensuring regular rest breaks.

2. **Train Employees and Supervisors:** Conduct training sessions to educate staff about the risks of heat illness and the importance of prevention measures.

3. **Appoint a Heat Safety Officer:** Designate an individual responsible for overseeing the implementation and adherence to the heat safety plan.

4. **Review and Update Policies:** Ensure existing safety policies align with the new OSHA guidelines and make necessary adjustments.

5. **Monitor Compliance:** Regularly audit workplace practices to ensure compliance with the new standards and avoid potential penalties.

By taking these proactive steps, employers can not only comply with OSHA's updated guidelines but also protect their employees from the dangers of heat-related illnesses.
