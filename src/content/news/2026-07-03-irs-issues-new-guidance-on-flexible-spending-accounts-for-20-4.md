## IRS Updates Flexible Spending Account Guidelines

The IRS has released new guidance on July 1, 2026, regarding Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs), affecting contribution limits and carryover amounts for the upcoming year. These changes are crucial for employers managing employee benefits, as they will need to adjust their FSA offerings to comply with the updated regulations.

### Key Changes

The new guidelines increase the maximum annual contribution limit for health FSAs to $3,150, up from the previous limit of $3,050. This adjustment reflects inflationary changes and aims to provide employees with more flexibility in managing their healthcare expenses. Additionally, the IRS has increased the maximum carryover amount to $610, allowing employees to roll over more unused funds into the next plan year.

### Impact on Employers

Employers must update their benefits administration systems to accommodate these new limits. This may involve revising plan documents, communicating changes to employees, and ensuring payroll systems reflect the updated contribution limits. Failure to comply with the new guidelines could result in penalties or disqualification of the FSA plan.

### Recommended Actions

1. **Review and Amend Plan Documents:** Employers should immediately review their FSA plan documents to ensure they align with the new IRS guidelines. Amendments should be made to reflect the increased contribution and carryover limits.

2. **Communicate Changes to Employees:** Clear communication with employees about the new limits and carryover amounts is essential. Employers should inform employees about the changes well before the open enrollment period to allow informed decision-making.

3. **Update Payroll and Benefits Systems:** Ensure that payroll and benefits administration systems are updated to handle the new contribution limits. This includes adjusting deduction limits and configuring systems to manage increased carryover amounts.

4. **Monitor Compliance:** Regularly review FSA plan operations to ensure ongoing compliance with IRS regulations. This may include conducting internal audits and seeking external advice if necessary.

### Conclusion

The IRS's updated guidance on FSAs reflects an ongoing effort to adapt employee benefits to current economic conditions. Employers must act promptly to integrate these changes into their benefits programs, ensuring compliance and maximizing the value of FSAs for their employees. By taking proactive steps now, employers can avoid potential issues and enhance their benefits offerings.
