## NLRB Revises Standard for Employee Handbook Policies

On May 10, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a decision in Stericycle Inc., 372 NLRB No. 113, which revises the standard for evaluating employee handbook policies. This decision impacts how employers must draft and enforce workplace rules to ensure compliance with the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA).

### Key Changes

The NLRB's decision reinstates a more stringent standard for determining whether a workplace rule unlawfully restricts employees' rights under Section 7 of the NLRA. The Board will now scrutinize whether a rule could be "reasonably construed" by employees to prohibit protected concerted activity, which includes discussions about wages, working conditions, and union organizing.

Previously, under the Boeing Co. standard, the NLRB balanced the rule's potential impact on Section 7 rights against the employer's legitimate justifications. The Stericycle decision shifts the focus back to the potential for employee misinterpretation, making it more challenging for employers to justify broadly worded policies.

### Implications for Employers

Employers must review and possibly revise their employee handbooks and workplace policies to comply with the new standard. Policies related to confidentiality, communication, and conduct should be carefully evaluated to ensure they do not inadvertently infringe on employees' rights to engage in protected activities.

### Action Items

1. **Audit Current Policies**: Conduct a thorough review of existing handbook policies, focusing on those that regulate employee speech and behavior. Pay particular attention to rules that could be seen as overly broad or vague.

2. **Revise Ambiguous Language**: Clarify any language that might be interpreted as restricting employees' rights under the NLRA. Consider adding disclaimers that clarify the intent of the policy and affirm employees' rights.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the complexity of the new standard, consulting with labor law experts is advisable. They can help ensure that policies align with the latest NLRB guidance and reduce the risk of legal challenges.

4. **Train Management**: Update training programs for supervisors and managers to ensure they understand the implications of the new standard and can enforce policies without infringing on employee rights.

The Stericycle decision represents a significant shift in how the NLRB evaluates workplace rules, emphasizing the importance of precise language in employee handbooks. Employers should act promptly to align their policies with this new standard to avoid potential violations and ensure a compliant workplace environment.
