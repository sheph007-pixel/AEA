## New Worker Classification Rule

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on June 20, 2026, clarifying worker classification criteria under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) for gig economy workers. The rule, effective January 1, 2027, aims to provide clearer guidance for determining whether a worker is classified as an independent contractor or an employee. This decision impacts businesses utilizing gig workers, such as ride-sharing companies and delivery services.

## Key Provisions

The rule emphasizes the "economic reality" test, focusing on two core factors: the nature and degree of control over the work and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss based on initiative and investment. The DOL's decision to focus on these factors aims to simplify the classification process, reducing ambiguity and potential litigation.

### Economic Reality Test

1. **Control Over Work**: Evaluates if the worker has autonomy over their schedule, tasks, and work methods. Greater control suggests independent contractor status.
   
2. **Opportunity for Profit or Loss**: Considers if the worker can influence earnings through managerial skill or personal investment, favoring independent contractor classification.

Additional factors, such as the permanency of the relationship and the degree of skill required, serve as supplementary criteria if the core factors do not clearly indicate a classification.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should review their current worker classifications against the new criteria. Immediate steps include:

- **Audit Existing Contracts**: Evaluate current agreements with gig workers to ensure alignment with the new rule.
- **Update Hiring Practices**: Adjust recruitment and onboarding processes to reflect the clarified classification standards.
- **Train HR and Legal Teams**: Ensure teams understand the new criteria and can apply them effectively.

## Implications for Businesses

The rule's emphasis on the economic reality test aims to provide a more predictable framework, potentially reducing litigation risks associated with misclassification. However, businesses may need to alter operational structures to comply with the clarified standards, possibly affecting cost structures and workforce strategies.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new rule on worker classification offers significant clarity for businesses operating within the gig economy. By focusing on control and profit opportunities, the rule seeks to streamline classification processes, though it requires businesses to carefully evaluate their current practices before the January 1, 2027, effective date.
