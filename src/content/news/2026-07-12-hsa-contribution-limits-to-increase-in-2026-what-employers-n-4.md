## IRS Announces Increased HSA Contribution Limits for 2026

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has announced an increase in Health Savings Account (HSA) contribution limits for the year 2026. Effective January 1, 2026, individuals can contribute up to $4,150, while families can contribute up to $8,300. These adjustments, outlined in Revenue Procedure 2026-24, reflect inflationary changes and provide an opportunity for employers to enhance their benefits offerings.

## Impact on Employer-Sponsored Health Plans

Employers offering high-deductible health plans (HDHPs) paired with HSAs should prepare for these changes. The new limits allow employees to save more pre-tax dollars for medical expenses, potentially increasing the attractiveness of HDHPs. Employers should communicate these changes to employees during open enrollment periods to ensure they are informed of the increased savings potential.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Update Benefits Materials**: Employers must revise their benefits communication materials to reflect the new HSA contribution limits. This includes updating employee handbooks, benefits websites, and any printed materials distributed to employees.

2. **Communicate Changes**: Plan a communication strategy to inform employees about the increased limits. Consider hosting informational sessions or webinars to explain how these changes can benefit employees' financial planning.

3. **Review Payroll Systems**: Ensure payroll systems are updated to accommodate the new contribution limits. This may involve coordinating with payroll providers to implement necessary changes by the start of the new year.

4. **Evaluate HDHP Offerings**: Employers should evaluate their current HDHP offerings to ensure they remain competitive and attractive in light of the increased contribution limits. This might include assessing plan deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums.

## Compliance and Strategic Considerations

Employers must ensure compliance with IRS regulations regarding HSA contributions. Contributions above the new limits could result in tax penalties for employees. Additionally, employers should consider the broader implications of these changes on their overall benefits strategy, potentially using the increased limits as a tool to attract and retain talent.

The increase in HSA contribution limits presents an opportunity for employers to enhance their benefits packages and support employees' financial health. By taking proactive steps to update materials, communicate effectively, and review plan offerings, employers can maximize the benefits of these regulatory changes.
