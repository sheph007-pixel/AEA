## DOL Finalizes Rule on Remote Work Overtime Calculations

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a new rule on July 15, 2026, clarifying how employers should calculate overtime for remote workers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule addresses ambiguities in calculating work hours for remote employees, ensuring compliance with the FLSA's overtime provisions.

The new rule, effective January 1, 2027, specifies that all hours worked remotely must be tracked accurately, including time spent on emails or calls outside regular business hours. It emphasizes the employer's responsibility to maintain precise records of hours worked by remote employees to ensure proper overtime compensation.

### Key Provisions

The rule requires employers to:

1. **Implement Reliable Tracking Systems**: Employers must use reliable systems to track remote work hours. This includes digital timekeeping systems that record start and end times for work performed outside the office.

2. **Notify Employees of Time Tracking**: Employers must inform remote workers about the necessity of logging all hours worked, including any work performed outside standard hours.

3. **Compensate for All Hours Worked**: All time worked beyond 40 hours in a workweek must be compensated at a rate of one and a half times the employee's regular pay rate, as stipulated under 29 U.S.C. § 207(a).

### Employer Action Items

To comply with the new rule, employers should:

- **Audit Current Timekeeping Practices**: Evaluate existing systems to ensure they accurately capture all hours worked by remote employees.
  
- **Train Managers and Employees**: Conduct training sessions for managers and employees on the importance of diligent time tracking and the implications of the new rule.

- **Update Employee Handbooks**: Amend policies in employee handbooks to reflect the new requirements for remote work hours tracking and overtime compensation.

The DOL's clarification aims to prevent wage theft and ensure fair compensation for remote workers, a growing segment of the workforce. Employers who fail to comply with these requirements may face penalties, including back pay for unpaid overtime.

This rule highlights the importance of adapting business operations to the evolving nature of work, particularly as remote work becomes more prevalent. Employers should take immediate steps to align their practices with the new regulations to avoid potential legal issues and ensure fair treatment of remote employees.
