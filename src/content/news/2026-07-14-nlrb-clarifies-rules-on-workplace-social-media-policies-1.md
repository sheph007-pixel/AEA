## NLRB Issues New Guidance on Social Media Policies

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) released new guidance on July 10, 2026, clarifying the enforcement of workplace social media policies. This guidance impacts how employers can regulate employee social media use concerning workplace issues. The guidance follows recent cases where the NLRB found certain employer restrictions overly broad and potentially infringing on employees' rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA).

### Key Points of the Guidance

The NLRB's guidance emphasizes that social media policies must not infringe on employees' rights to engage in "protected concerted activities" under Section 7 of the NLRA. This includes discussions about wages, working conditions, and other employment-related issues. The guidance indicates that policies prohibiting employees from discussing these topics, even on personal social media accounts, may be deemed unlawful.

The NLRB has highlighted several areas where employers often overstep:

- **Broad Confidentiality Clauses**: Policies that broadly prohibit employees from discussing "confidential" information without clear definitions may violate the NLRA.
- **Restrictions on Criticizing the Employer**: Rules that prevent employees from making negative comments about their employer or supervisors can be problematic if they restrict protected concerted activity.
- **Use of Company Logos and Trademarks**: While employers can restrict the use of company logos, overly broad restrictions that prevent any use without permission may be deemed unlawful if they impede protected activities.

### Employer Action Items

Employers should immediately review their social media policies to ensure compliance with the NLRB's latest guidance. Specific actions include:

1. **Audit Existing Policies**: Examine current social media policies to identify and amend any overly broad language that could be interpreted as restricting employees' Section 7 rights.
   
2. **Define Confidential Information Clearly**: Ensure that any confidentiality clauses are specific and do not inadvertently include information that employees are legally allowed to discuss.

3. **Train Management and HR Teams**: Conduct training sessions for management and HR personnel on the implications of the NLRB guidance to prevent unlawful enforcement of social media policies.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Consider consulting with legal experts specializing in labor law to align policies with the NLRB's guidance and avoid potential legal challenges.

### Implications for Employers

The NLRB's guidance underscores the need for employers to balance protecting their business interests with respecting employees' rights under the NLRA. Employers must ensure that their social media policies do not inadvertently suppress legitimate employee activities protected by law. Failure to comply could result in legal challenges and potential penalties.

By taking proactive steps to align social media policies with the NLRB's guidance, employers can mitigate risks and foster a compliant workplace environment.
