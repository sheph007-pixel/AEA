## OSHA Issues Updated Guidance

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) released updated guidance on June 28, 2026, aimed at preventing workplace violence in the healthcare and social services sectors. This update revises the 2015 guidelines, reflecting recent trends and data in workplace safety.

## Key Changes and Recommendations

The updated guidance expands on previous recommendations, emphasizing the need for comprehensive workplace violence prevention programs. It suggests employers conduct regular risk assessments, implement engineering controls such as secure rooms and alarm systems, and establish clear reporting procedures for incidents. The guidance also highlights the importance of training employees to recognize and de-escalate potentially violent situations.

## Who Is Affected

This guidance specifically targets employers in healthcare settings, including hospitals, nursing homes, and outpatient clinics, as well as social service providers. These sectors have historically seen higher rates of workplace violence incidents compared to other industries.

## Legal Context

While the guidance itself is not legally binding, it falls under the General Duty Clause of the Occupational Safety and Health Act (29 U.S.C. § 654), which requires employers to provide a workplace free from recognized hazards. Employers failing to comply with OSHA's guidance may face increased scrutiny and potential citations if workplace violence incidents occur.

## Action Items for Employers

Healthcare and social service employers should review and update their workplace violence prevention programs in line with OSHA's latest guidance. Key actions include:

- Conducting a comprehensive risk assessment to identify potential hazards.
- Installing security measures such as panic buttons and surveillance cameras.
- Developing clear procedures for reporting and responding to incidents.
- Providing training for employees on de-escalation techniques and recognizing warning signs.

Employers should document all preventive measures and training sessions to demonstrate compliance with OSHA's expectations.

## Conclusion

The updated OSHA guidance underscores the agency's ongoing commitment to reducing workplace violence in high-risk sectors. Employers in healthcare and social services should take proactive steps to align their safety programs with these recommendations, enhancing both employee safety and regulatory compliance.
