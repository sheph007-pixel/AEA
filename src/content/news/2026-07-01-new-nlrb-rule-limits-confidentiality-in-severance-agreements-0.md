## NLRB Restricts Confidentiality Clauses

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) has issued a new rule limiting the use of confidentiality clauses in severance agreements, effective July 1, 2026. This rule aims to uphold employees' rights under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) to discuss their employment terms and conditions openly.

## Key Details

The rule specifically targets confidentiality provisions that could be interpreted as restricting an employee's ability to discuss their severance terms with third parties, including former coworkers and union representatives. The NLRB's decision follows its ruling in the case of *McLaren Macomb*, which emphasized protecting employees' rights to engage in "concerted activities" under Section 7 of the NLRA.

## Impact on Employers

Employers with severance agreements containing broad confidentiality clauses need to reassess and potentially revise these agreements. The NLRB's new rule suggests that clauses infringing on employees' rights to discuss workplace conditions with others could be deemed unlawful. This change affects all private-sector employers under the NLRB's jurisdiction, excluding public sector employers, agricultural laborers, and independent contractors.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Existing Agreements**: Employers should audit current severance agreements to identify confidentiality provisions that may violate the new rule.

2. **Amend Severance Templates**: Update template agreements to ensure compliance, focusing on language that does not restrict employees' rights under the NLRA.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with labor and employment attorneys to ensure that severance agreements align with the latest NLRB guidance.

4. **Employee Communication**: Prepare to communicate changes to affected employees and train HR personnel on the implications of the new rule.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's rule change underscores the importance of aligning severance agreements with federal labor laws. Employers should act swiftly to ensure compliance and avoid potential legal challenges. By revisiting and revising confidentiality clauses, companies can protect themselves from liability while respecting employees' rights to free discussion about their employment terms.
