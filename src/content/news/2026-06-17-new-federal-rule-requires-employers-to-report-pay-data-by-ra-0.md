## New Reporting Requirements

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) announced on June 15, 2026, that employers with 100 or more employees must submit pay data categorized by race, ethnicity, and gender by September 30, 2026. This requirement, reinstated under the EEO-1 Component 2 filing, aims to address wage disparities across different demographics in the workplace.

The EEOC's mandate falls under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin. The agency believes that collecting this data will help identify and rectify pay inequities.

## Compliance Steps

Employers must prepare to comply with these new requirements promptly. The first step is to ensure that HR and payroll systems can categorize and report employee compensation data accurately by race, ethnicity, and gender. Employers should also conduct a preliminary internal audit to identify any significant pay gaps and consider corrective actions if disparities exist.

Next, businesses should familiarize themselves with the EEOC's filing process. The EEOC will provide a web-based portal for data submission, and employers should ensure that responsible personnel are trained to use this system effectively.

## Legal Implications

Failure to comply with the new reporting requirements could result in significant penalties. Under 29 C.F.R. § 1602.7, non-compliance may lead to investigations and potential lawsuits for discriminatory practices. Employers need to document their data collection and reporting processes meticulously to defend against any claims of non-compliance or discrimination.

## Action Items

Employers should take the following actions:

1. **Audit Pay Structures:** Conduct an internal audit of pay structures to identify and address any disparities before the September deadline.
   
2. **Update Systems:** Ensure that HR and payroll systems are updated to collect and report the necessary data accurately.
   
3. **Train Staff:** Provide training for staff responsible for data collection and reporting to ensure compliance with EEOC guidelines.
   
4. **Consult Legal Counsel:** Engage with legal counsel to review compliance strategies and address any potential legal risks associated with the new requirements.

By taking these steps, employers can ensure compliance with the EEOC's new pay data reporting requirements and mitigate the risk of legal challenges related to pay discrimination.
