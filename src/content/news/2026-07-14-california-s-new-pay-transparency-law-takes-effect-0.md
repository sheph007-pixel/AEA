## California's Pay Transparency Law

California's pay transparency law, SB 1162, took effect on July 1, 2026. This law requires employers with 15 or more employees to include salary ranges in all job postings. The law aims to address pay disparities and promote equitable compensation practices.

Employers must disclose the minimum and maximum salary or hourly wage for each position advertised. This requirement applies to both internal and external job postings, including those shared on third-party platforms. Employers must also provide current employees with the pay scale for their position upon request.

## Compliance and Penalties

Noncompliance with SB 1162 can result in significant penalties. The California Labor Commissioner's Office is authorized to investigate complaints and impose fines ranging from $100 to $10,000 per violation. The severity of the penalty depends on factors such as the employer's size and the number of violations.

Employers should ensure that their job postings are updated to reflect this new requirement. This may involve coordinating with hiring managers and external recruiters to ensure compliance across all platforms where job listings appear.

## Action Items for Employers

To comply with SB 1162, employers should take the following steps:

1. **Review and Update Job Postings:** Ensure all job advertisements include the required salary range information. This applies to new postings and existing ones that remain active.

2. **Train HR and Recruitment Staff:** Educate those involved in the hiring process about the new requirements. Ensure they understand how to communicate salary ranges effectively and accurately.

3. **Coordinate with External Recruiters:** If using third-party recruiters, verify they are aware of the law and are including salary ranges in postings on your behalf.

4. **Prepare for Employee Inquiries:** Be ready to provide current employees with the pay scale for their positions upon request. Develop a process for handling these inquiries promptly and consistently.

5. **Monitor Compliance:** Regularly audit job postings and internal processes to ensure ongoing compliance with SB 1162.

## Broader Implications

California's pay transparency law reflects a growing trend toward greater transparency in compensation practices. Similar laws are being considered in other states, and employers operating in multiple jurisdictions should stay informed about potential changes.

Adopting transparent pay practices can enhance employer reputation and improve employee satisfaction. By proactively addressing pay disparities, employers can foster a more equitable workplace and reduce the risk of legal challenges related to compensation practices.
