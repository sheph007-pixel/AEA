## DOL Proposes New Rule on Employee Data Privacy

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 1, 2026, aimed at strengthening employee data privacy protections. This proposed rule would require employers to implement specific data protection measures to safeguard employee information, significantly impacting how businesses manage and store employee data.

### Key Provisions

The proposed rule mandates that employers establish comprehensive data protection policies. These policies must include regular risk assessments, employee training on data privacy, and the implementation of technical safeguards to prevent unauthorized access to employee data. Additionally, the rule would require immediate notification to employees and the DOL in the event of a data breach.

Under the proposed rule, employers must also limit data collection to only what is necessary for legitimate business purposes and ensure that any third-party vendors handling employee data comply with equivalent privacy standards.

### Implications for Employers

If enacted, this rule will necessitate substantial changes in current data management practices for many employers. Companies will need to audit their current data handling procedures and potentially invest in new technologies to comply with the enhanced security requirements. Employers should begin reviewing vendor contracts to ensure compliance with the new standards.

### Action Items

1. **Conduct a Data Audit:** Review current data collection and storage practices to identify potential vulnerabilities.
2. **Update Policies:** Develop or update data privacy policies to align with the proposed requirements.
3. **Vendor Compliance:** Ensure that third-party vendors adhere to the same data protection standards.
4. **Employee Training:** Implement regular training sessions for employees on data privacy and security best practices.

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until August 1, 2026. Employers and HR professionals should consider providing feedback, particularly on how the rule might impact small and medium-sized businesses.

While the proposed rule is not yet in effect, preparing for these changes can help mitigate potential disruptions and ensure compliance once finalized.
