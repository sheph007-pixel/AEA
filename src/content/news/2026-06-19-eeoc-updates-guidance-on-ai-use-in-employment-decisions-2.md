## EEOC Issues New AI Guidance

On June 15, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidance on the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in employment decisions. This guidance clarifies how employers should apply federal anti-discrimination laws when using AI tools in hiring, promotions, and other employment-related decisions.

The EEOC's guidance highlights the potential for AI systems to inadvertently result in biased outcomes, which could violate Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. The guidance emphasizes that employers are responsible for ensuring that their use of AI does not result in discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.

## Key Points for Employers

The EEOC's guidance outlines several key considerations for employers using AI in employment decisions:

1. **Algorithmic Fairness**: Employers must ensure that AI tools are designed and implemented in a way that does not disproportionately impact any protected class. This includes conducting regular audits and validations of AI systems to detect and mitigate bias.

2. **Transparency**: Employers should provide clear information to applicants and employees about how AI tools are used in decision-making processes. This transparency can help build trust and ensure compliance with legal obligations.

3. **Training and Oversight**: Employers need to train HR staff and decision-makers on the appropriate use of AI tools. Additionally, there should be oversight mechanisms in place to monitor AI-driven decisions and address any potential issues promptly.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should take the following steps to align with the EEOC's guidance:

- **Audit AI Systems**: Conduct a thorough review of all AI tools used in employment decisions to ensure they comply with anti-discrimination laws. This includes testing for disparate impact and making necessary adjustments.

- **Policy Updates**: Update internal policies to reflect the use of AI in employment decisions, including guidelines on transparency and accountability.

- **Employee Training**: Implement training programs for HR personnel and managers to understand the implications of using AI and how to address potential biases.

- **Vendor Assessment**: If using third-party AI tools, conduct due diligence to ensure vendors comply with federal anti-discrimination laws and provide documentation of their fairness assessments.

The EEOC's updated guidance serves as a critical reminder for employers to scrutinize their AI practices to prevent unintentional discrimination. By taking proactive steps, employers can leverage AI tools responsibly while maintaining compliance with federal laws.
