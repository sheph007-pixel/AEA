## DOL Clarifies Mental Health Parity Compliance

The Department of Labor (DOL) released new guidance on June 15, 2026, clarifying compliance requirements for the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA) of 2008. This guidance aims to ensure that employer-sponsored health plans do not impose more restrictive limitations on mental health and substance use disorder benefits than on medical/surgical benefits.

The MHPAEA, codified at 29 U.S.C. § 1185a, requires parity in financial requirements and treatment limitations. The new guidance emphasizes the need for employers to conduct comparative analyses of non-quantitative treatment limitations (NQTLs) applied to mental health and substance use disorder benefits.

## Key Compliance Requirements

The DOL's guidance highlights specific areas where employers must focus their compliance efforts:

1. **Comparative Analysis**: Employers must conduct and document a thorough comparative analysis of NQTLs to demonstrate compliance. This analysis should include how the NQTLs are designed and applied, with a focus on ensuring they are comparable to and applied no more stringently than those for medical/surgical benefits.

2. **Documentation**: Employers must maintain documentation that supports the comparative analysis. This includes the processes, strategies, evidentiary standards, and other factors used in designing and applying NQTLs.

3. **Transparency**: Employers are required to provide the comparative analysis and supporting documentation to the DOL upon request. Failure to provide adequate documentation could result in a determination of non-compliance.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers sponsoring health plans should take immediate steps to ensure compliance with the DOL's guidance:

- **Conduct a Comparative Analysis**: Review all NQTLs applied to mental health and substance use disorder benefits. Ensure they are comparable to those applied to medical/surgical benefits.

- **Update Documentation**: Ensure all processes and criteria used in the design and application of NQTLs are well-documented. This documentation should be readily available for review by the DOL.

- **Engage with Plan Administrators**: Work closely with third-party administrators and insurers to ensure they are aware of and compliant with the MHPAEA requirements.

- **Prepare for DOL Requests**: Be prepared to provide the DOL with the comparative analysis and supporting documentation promptly if requested.

## Conclusion

The DOL's recent guidance underscores the importance of compliance with mental health parity requirements. Employers need to ensure their health plans do not impose unfair limitations on mental health and substance use disorder benefits. By conducting thorough comparative analyses and maintaining comprehensive documentation, employers can demonstrate compliance and avoid potential penalties.
