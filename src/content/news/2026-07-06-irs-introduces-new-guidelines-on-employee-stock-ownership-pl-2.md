## IRS Updates ESOP Guidelines

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) has issued updated guidelines for Employee Stock Ownership Plans (ESOPs), effective July 1, 2026. These updates clarify tax compliance and reporting requirements under the Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 409(p), impacting companies with ESOPs that exceed a 10% ownership threshold.

The new guidelines aim to address ambiguities in the application of anti-abuse rules intended to prevent disqualified persons from receiving disproportionate benefits. The IRS has outlined specific criteria to determine when an ESOP fails to meet the non-allocation rules, which could lead to significant tax consequences for the plan sponsor and participants.

## Key Changes

The IRS's updated guidelines provide a clearer framework for determining compliance with Section 409(p). Key changes include:

1. **Clarification of Disqualified Persons**: The IRS has refined the definition of "disqualified persons" to include individuals who hold more than 10% of the company's stock, directly or indirectly, through family or related entities.

2. **Non-Allocation Year Criteria**: The guidelines specify conditions under which an ESOP may trigger a non-allocation year, emphasizing the need for companies to monitor stock allocations closely to prevent violations.

3. **Increased Reporting Requirements**: Companies must now provide more detailed information in their annual Form 5500 filings, including the identification of disqualified persons and verification of compliance with Section 409(p) requirements.

4. **Penalties for Non-Compliance**: The IRS has reiterated the penalties for failing to comply with Section 409(p), which include excise taxes and potential disqualification of the ESOP's tax-deferred status.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers with ESOPs should immediately review their plans in light of the new IRS guidelines. Key actions include:

- **Conducting a Compliance Audit**: Employers should conduct a thorough audit of their ESOPs to ensure compliance with the updated Section 409(p) requirements. This includes verifying the ownership percentages of all participants and identifying any potential disqualified persons.

- **Updating Reporting Practices**: Amend Form 5500 reporting practices to include the newly required information. This may necessitate coordination with third-party administrators or legal counsel to ensure accurate and timely submissions.

- **Training and Policy Updates**: Provide training for HR and benefits staff on the new guidelines and update internal policies to reflect changes in compliance and reporting requirements.

By taking these steps, employers can mitigate the risk of non-compliance and avoid potential penalties. Companies should also consider consulting with tax advisors or legal experts specializing in ESOPs to navigate the complexities of the updated guidelines effectively.
