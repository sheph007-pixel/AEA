## DOL Proposes New Rule on Independent Contractor Classification

The Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 20, 2026, aimed at clarifying the criteria for classifying workers as independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposal seeks to replace the existing guidance issued in 2023, which has been criticized for creating uncertainty among employers.

Under the proposed rule, the DOL emphasizes a "totality of the circumstances" approach, focusing on six primary factors to determine whether a worker is economically dependent on the employer or in business for themselves. These factors include the nature and degree of control over the work, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, and the permanency of the relationship.

## Key Changes and Implications

The proposed rule moves away from the narrower focus of the 2023 rule, which heavily emphasized two core factors: control and opportunity for profit or loss. By broadening the analysis to six factors, the DOL aims to provide a more comprehensive framework that accounts for the varied nature of modern work arrangements.

For employers, this change could mean reevaluating current worker classifications to ensure compliance with the new criteria. Misclassification can lead to significant penalties, including back wages and tax liabilities.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Current Classifications:** Employers should conduct an audit of their current workforce classifications. Pay particular attention to roles that may be on the borderline between employee and independent contractor status.

2. **Update Contracts and Policies:** Ensure that contracts with independent contractors clearly outline the nature of the relationship and align with the proposed criteria. This includes detailing the level of control and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss.

3. **Training for HR and Legal Teams:** Provide training for HR and legal teams on the new criteria to ensure they understand the implications and can apply them consistently.

4. **Prepare for Potential Changes:** Although the rule is currently in the proposal stage, employers should prepare for its potential adoption. This includes considering the impact on business operations and budgeting for possible reclassification costs.

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until August 19, 2026. Employers and other stakeholders are encouraged to participate in the comment process to express support, concerns, or suggestions for modifications.

Understanding and adapting to this proposed rule will be crucial for employers to maintain compliance and avoid costly misclassification issues. The final rule, once enacted, will likely have a significant impact on industries that heavily rely on independent contractors, such as gig economy platforms, construction, and freelance services.
