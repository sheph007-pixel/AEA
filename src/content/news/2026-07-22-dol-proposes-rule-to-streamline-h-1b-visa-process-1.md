## DOL Proposes Streamlined H-1B Visa Process

On July 20, 2026, the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule intended to streamline the H-1B visa application process. The proposal aims to reduce administrative burdens and expedite the hiring of foreign workers in specialty occupations. This development is significant for HR professionals and employers who rely on H-1B visas to fill critical roles.

The proposed rule would amend existing regulations under 20 C.F.R. § 655, focusing on reducing paperwork and processing times. Key changes include the introduction of electronic filing options for Labor Condition Applications (LCAs) and a streamlined attestation process for employers. The DOL anticipates that these changes will decrease processing times by up to 30%.

## Impact on Employers

Employers who utilize H-1B visas should prepare for potential adjustments in their application procedures. The proposed rule, if finalized, will require employers to transition to electronic submissions for LCAs, which may necessitate updates to internal HR systems and processes. Additionally, the streamlined attestation process will require employers to ensure that their attestations are accurate and complete, as the DOL plans to implement stricter penalties for non-compliance.

The proposal also seeks to clarify the definition of "specialty occupation," aligning it more closely with industry standards. This clarification is expected to reduce the number of RFEs (Requests for Evidence) issued by the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), further expediting the visa approval process.

## Next Steps for Employers

Employers should begin reviewing their current H-1B application processes and identify areas where electronic submissions can be integrated. HR departments may need to collaborate with IT teams to ensure systems are ready for the transition. Additionally, training sessions for HR staff on the new attestation requirements and the revised definition of specialty occupations may be necessary to ensure compliance.

The DOL is accepting public comments on the proposed rule until September 18, 2026. Employers are encouraged to participate in the comment process to express support or concerns regarding the proposed changes. This feedback will be instrumental in shaping the final rule.

In summary, the DOL's proposed rule to streamline the H-1B visa process represents a significant shift in how employers will manage their foreign workforce. By preparing now, employers can ensure a smooth transition and continue to benefit from the diverse talent pool that H-1B visas provide.
