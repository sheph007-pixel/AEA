## New York Expands Salary History Ban

On July 1, 2026, New York State expanded its salary history ban, affecting employers across the state. Under the amended New York Labor Law § 194-a, employers are prohibited from requesting or relying on an applicant's salary history during the hiring process. This expansion aims to close the gender pay gap and promote fair compensation practices.

The original law, effective since January 6, 2020, restricted employers from inquiring about or relying on salary history information to determine whether to offer employment or set compensation. The recent amendments now extend these prohibitions to internal transfers and promotions, ensuring that current employees are also protected from salary history inquiries.

## What Employers Need to Know

The expanded law applies to all employers in New York, regardless of size, and covers both public and private sectors. It prohibits any form of questioning, whether verbal or written, about an applicant’s past compensation. Employers are also barred from conducting searches of publicly available records to obtain salary history information.

However, if an applicant voluntarily discloses their salary history without prompting, the employer may consider this information in determining salary. The onus is on the employer to ensure that any disclosure is truly voluntary and not coerced.

## Compliance and Action Items

Employers must update their hiring and promotion practices to comply with the expanded law. Here are key steps to take:

1. **Review and Amend Policies:** Audit current hiring and promotion policies to eliminate any language that requests salary history. Ensure job application forms and interview scripts are compliant.

2. **Train HR and Hiring Managers:** Conduct training sessions for HR personnel and hiring managers on the new requirements to prevent inadvertent violations.

3. **Update Internal Systems:** Modify HR systems and databases to prevent the storage or use of salary history information in decision-making processes.

4. **Monitor Voluntary Disclosures:** Establish procedures to document and verify that any salary history disclosed by candidates is voluntary.

Failure to comply with the expanded salary history ban can result in significant penalties. Employers may face civil fines and be required to pay damages to affected individuals. The New York State Department of Labor is responsible for enforcing the law and can investigate complaints and impose sanctions for non-compliance.

## Looking Ahead

The expansion of New York’s salary history ban reflects a broader trend across the United States toward greater pay transparency and equity. Employers should anticipate similar legislative changes in other jurisdictions and proactively adjust their practices to align with evolving legal standards.

By adhering to these requirements, employers not only avoid legal repercussions but also foster a more equitable workplace.
