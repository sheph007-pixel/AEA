## California Privacy Rights Act Expands to Employees

The California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) will extend its protections to employee data starting January 1, 2026. This expansion affects all businesses with employees in California and annual gross revenues over $25 million, or those that buy, receive, or sell the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households, or derive 50% or more of their annual revenues from selling consumers' personal information.

## Key Provisions

The CPRA, initially focusing on consumer data, now includes employee data under its purview. Employers must comply with new requirements for handling personal information, including providing notice at collection, honoring requests to access and delete personal data, and implementing reasonable security measures (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.).

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Update Privacy Notices**: Employers must revise privacy notices to include employee data collection and processing details. Notices should clearly outline the categories of personal information collected and the purposes for which it is used.

2. **Review Data Handling Practices**: Conduct a thorough audit of current data handling practices. Ensure that all employee data is collected, stored, and processed in compliance with the CPRA's requirements.

3. **Implement Data Access and Deletion Procedures**: Establish procedures to address employee requests for access to their personal data and requests for deletion. These procedures must be operational by January 1, 2026.

4. **Enhance Data Security Measures**: Evaluate and strengthen data security measures to prevent unauthorized access to employee information. This includes updating cybersecurity protocols and conducting regular security audits.

5. **Train HR and IT Staff**: Provide training for HR, IT, and any other staff involved in data processing to ensure they understand the new legal obligations and how to comply with them.

## Compliance Challenges

The CPRA's expansion to include employee data presents several compliance challenges. Businesses must navigate the complexities of integrating employee data into existing privacy frameworks designed primarily for consumer data. Additionally, the requirement to honor access and deletion requests may necessitate significant changes to data management systems.

## Conclusion

The CPRA's inclusion of employee data marks a significant shift in privacy law compliance for businesses operating in California. By taking proactive steps to update privacy notices, review data handling practices, and enhance security measures, employers can ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties. As the January 1, 2026 deadline approaches, prioritizing these actions will be crucial for maintaining legal compliance and safeguarding employee privacy rights.
