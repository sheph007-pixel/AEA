## New NLRB Rule on Union Representation Elections

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a new rule on June 30, 2026, revising the procedures for union representation elections. This rule, effective immediately, shortens the timeline for elections and modifies the process for challenging voter eligibility, impacting how employers must prepare for potential unionization efforts.

### Key Changes and Implications

The new rule accelerates the election process by reducing the time between the filing of a petition and the election date. Previously, employers had approximately 23 days to prepare; this is now reduced to 14 days. This change requires employers to quickly assess and respond to unionization efforts.

Additionally, the rule alters procedures for challenging voter eligibility. Challenges must now be resolved post-election, rather than before the vote. This means employers may face elections with unresolved eligibility disputes, potentially complicating the election outcome.

### Action Items for Employers

1. **Rapid Response Preparedness**: Employers should establish a rapid response team to address union petitions swiftly. This team should include HR, legal counsel, and management representatives trained to handle unionization efforts within the new 14-day window.

2. **Employee Communication**: Develop clear communication strategies to inform employees about the company’s stance on unionization. Ensure that all communications comply with the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) to avoid unfair labor practice charges.

3. **Training and Education**: Conduct training sessions for supervisors and managers on recognizing union organizing activities and understanding the legal boundaries of their responses.

4. **Review and Update Policies**: Regularly review employee handbooks and policies to ensure compliance with labor laws. Amend any ambiguous language that could be interpreted as infringing on employees' rights to organize.

5. **Legal Consultation**: Engage labor law experts to navigate the complexities of the new rule and provide guidance on best practices for compliance and employee relations.

### Conclusion

The NLRB's new rule on union representation elections demands that employers act swiftly and strategically. By preparing in advance and understanding the procedural changes, employers can better manage the challenges posed by accelerated election timelines and unresolved voter eligibility issues.
