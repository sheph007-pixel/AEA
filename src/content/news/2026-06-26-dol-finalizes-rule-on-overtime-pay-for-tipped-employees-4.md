## DOL Finalizes Rule on Overtime Pay for Tipped Employees

The Department of Labor (DOL) finalized a rule on June 25, 2026, altering the calculation of overtime pay for tipped employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule, effective August 1, 2026, impacts how employers determine the overtime rate for workers who receive tips, addressing longstanding ambiguities in wage calculations.

Under the new rule, employers must calculate overtime pay for tipped employees based on the full minimum wage, not the lower cash wage that employers are allowed to pay tipped workers. Previously, employers could calculate overtime based on the cash wage, which is as low as $2.13 per hour under federal law, provided tips make up the difference to the $7.25 federal minimum wage. This change aligns with the DOL's ongoing efforts to ensure fair compensation practices for tipped employees.

### Key Changes and Implications

The rule mandates that when calculating overtime, employers must use the standard minimum wage as the baseline. For example, if a tipped employee works more than 40 hours in a week, their overtime pay rate should be at least 1.5 times the federal minimum wage of $7.25, rather than 1.5 times the cash wage.

This adjustment means that tipped employees will receive higher overtime pay, potentially increasing labor costs for businesses relying heavily on tipped staff, such as restaurants and hospitality services. Employers must ensure compliance to avoid potential penalties and back pay liabilities.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Review Payroll Systems**: Employers should immediately review and, if necessary, update their payroll systems to ensure the correct calculation of overtime pay for tipped employees.
  
- **Train HR and Payroll Staff**: It is crucial to train HR and payroll personnel on the new calculation method to prevent errors in payroll processing.

- **Communicate with Employees**: Clear communication with employees regarding the changes in overtime pay calculations can help manage expectations and reduce confusion.

- **Audit Past Overtime Payments**: Conduct an audit of past overtime payments to identify and rectify any discrepancies under the previous calculation method.

### Compliance and Enforcement

The DOL has indicated it will begin enforcing this rule starting August 1, 2026. Employers found in violation may face penalties, including the requirement to pay back wages. The DOL encourages employers to reach out for guidance if there are uncertainties regarding compliance.

By acting promptly, employers can ensure they meet the new requirements and avoid costly penalties. The finalized rule reflects the DOL's commitment to protecting workers' rights and ensuring fair compensation practices across industries.
