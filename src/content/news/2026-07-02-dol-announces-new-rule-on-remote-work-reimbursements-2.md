## New DOL Rule on Remote Work Reimbursements

The Department of Labor (DOL) has issued a new rule requiring employers to reimburse employees for certain expenses incurred while working remotely. This rule, effective January 1, 2027, aims to address the growing trend of remote work and the associated costs employees bear. The rule is part of the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and mandates that employers compensate employees for necessary expenses directly related to their job functions.

## Key Provisions

Under the new rule, employers must reimburse employees for expenses such as internet usage, office supplies, and equipment necessary for performing their job duties from home. The rule specifies that only expenses deemed "necessary and reasonable" for the execution of work duties are eligible for reimbursement. Employers are not required to cover expenses that are primarily for personal use.

The rule applies to all employers covered by the FLSA, which includes businesses with annual sales of $500,000 or more and those involved in interstate commerce. Employers must ensure that reimbursements do not reduce an employee's earnings below the federal minimum wage of $7.25 per hour (29 U.S.C. § 206) or affect overtime pay calculations.

## Employer Action Items

Employers should take the following steps to comply with the new rule:

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers need to update their expense reimbursement policies to align with the new DOL requirements. Clearly define what constitutes necessary and reasonable expenses.

2. **Implement a Reimbursement Process**: Establish a clear and efficient process for employees to submit reimbursement claims. This should include guidelines for documentation and approval.

3. **Communicate with Employees**: Inform remote workers about the new reimbursement policy and process. Ensure they understand what expenses are covered and how to submit claims.

4. **Budget for Reimbursements**: Adjust budgets to accommodate the new reimbursement obligations. Consider the potential impact on operating costs and plan accordingly.

5. **Train HR and Payroll Staff**: Ensure HR and payroll staff are trained on the new rule and the updated reimbursement process to avoid compliance issues.

## Compliance and Enforcement

The DOL will enforce compliance with the new rule through audits and investigations. Employers found in violation may face penalties, including back pay for unreimbursed expenses and fines. It is crucial for employers to maintain accurate records of all reimbursement claims and payments to demonstrate compliance.

This new rule reflects the evolving nature of work and the need for regulatory frameworks to adapt to modern work environments. Employers should act promptly to ensure compliance and support their remote workforce effectively.
