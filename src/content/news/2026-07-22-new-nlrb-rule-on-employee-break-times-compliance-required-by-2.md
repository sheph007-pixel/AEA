## NLRB's New Break Time Rule

The National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) has issued a new rule requiring employers to provide specific break times to employees, effective January 1, 2025. This rule mandates that non-exempt employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) must receive a minimum of two 15-minute breaks during an eight-hour workday. The rule aims to standardize break times across industries and ensure fair working conditions.

## Details of the Rule

Under the new rule, employers must provide two paid 15-minute breaks for every eight hours worked. These breaks are in addition to any meal periods required by state law. The NLRB's decision aligns with its goal to enhance worker welfare and address disparities in break time policies across states. This rule applies to all industries, affecting a broad range of employers, particularly those in retail, manufacturing, and service sectors.

Employers must document these breaks as part of their timekeeping records. Failure to comply could result in penalties, including fines and potential back pay for missed breaks. The NLRB has stated that employers cannot require employees to perform any work-related tasks during these break periods.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should review and amend their current break time policies to ensure compliance with the new rule by the January 1, 2025 deadline. This includes updating employee handbooks, training managers on the new requirements, and adjusting timekeeping systems to accurately record break times.

Additionally, employers should communicate these changes to their workforce, emphasizing the importance of taking designated breaks. Companies may also consider implementing automated reminders for employees to take their breaks, ensuring adherence to the new regulations.

## Legal Implications

The rule stems from Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA), which protects employees' rights to organize and engage in concerted activities for mutual aid and protection. By standardizing break times, the NLRB seeks to prevent potential unfair labor practices related to inconsistent break policies.

Employers should consult with legal counsel to understand the full implications of the rule and ensure all aspects of compliance are addressed. This includes assessing any state-specific break time laws that may impose additional requirements beyond the federal mandate.

## Conclusion

The NLRB's new break time rule represents a significant shift in workplace policy, emphasizing the importance of employee well-being. Employers must act promptly to align with these regulations, avoiding penalties and fostering a compliant workplace environment. As the effective date approaches, staying informed and prepared will be crucial for seamless implementation.
