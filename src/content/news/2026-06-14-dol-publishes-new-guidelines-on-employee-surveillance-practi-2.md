## DOL's New Employee Surveillance Guidelines

The Department of Labor (DOL) released new guidelines on June 10, 2026, concerning employee surveillance practices, effective August 1, 2026. These guidelines aim to balance employer monitoring needs with employee privacy rights. The guidelines apply to all employers under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and provide clarity on permissible surveillance methods.

## Key Provisions

The guidelines specify that employers must disclose any form of electronic monitoring to employees, including video, audio, and digital communications tracking. Employers are required to provide a clear notice detailing the type of surveillance, its purpose, and duration. This notice must be issued at the time of hiring and whenever there are changes to surveillance practices.

The guidelines impose restrictions on the use of surveillance data. Employers are prohibited from using collected data for purposes unrelated to employment, such as personal matters or non-work-related activities. Additionally, data retention is limited to a maximum of two years unless otherwise required by law.

## Employer Action Items

Employers must review and potentially revise their current surveillance policies to comply with the new guidelines. This includes updating employee handbooks and issuing written notices to all current employees by the effective date. Employers should also conduct training sessions to educate management on the new requirements and ensure consistent application across the organization.

Employers using third-party services for surveillance must ensure that these providers comply with the guidelines. Contracts with such providers should be reviewed and amended if necessary to align with the new standards.

## Legal Implications

Non-compliance with the DOL's guidelines could result in penalties under the FLSA. The DOL has indicated that it will conduct audits and investigations to ensure adherence. Employers found in violation may face fines and be required to implement corrective measures.

## Conclusion

The DOL's new guidelines on employee surveillance practices underscore the importance of transparency and respect for employee privacy. Employers should take immediate steps to align their policies with these guidelines before the August 1, 2026, deadline to avoid potential legal repercussions.
