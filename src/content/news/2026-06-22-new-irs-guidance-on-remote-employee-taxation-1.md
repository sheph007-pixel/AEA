## IRS Clarifies Tax Obligations for Remote Workers Across State Lines

On June 15, 2026, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued new guidance clarifying the tax obligations of remote employees who work across state lines. This guidance, outlined in Notice 2026-45, addresses the complexities faced by employers in withholding and reporting taxes for remote workers amidst the rise of telecommuting.

The IRS guidance clarifies that employers must withhold state income tax based on the employee's physical work location, not the location of the employer's office. This ruling affects businesses with employees working remotely in states different from where the company is headquartered. The guidance also emphasizes the importance of maintaining accurate records of employee work locations to ensure compliance.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers must take immediate steps to comply with these new requirements. First, audit current payroll systems to ensure they accurately track the work locations of remote employees. This may involve implementing software that logs the IP addresses or physical locations from which employees are working.

Second, update employee agreements to reflect the tax implications of remote work. Clearly outline the responsibility of employees to report their work location to ensure accurate tax withholding.

Finally, consult with tax professionals to understand the specific tax obligations in each state where employees reside. This is crucial for businesses with employees working in multiple states, as tax laws can vary significantly.

## Impact on Small and Medium-Sized Businesses

This guidance is particularly relevant for small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) that may not have robust systems in place for tracking remote work locations. The IRS's clarification aims to simplify compliance but could present challenges for businesses without the necessary infrastructure.

SMBs should consider investing in payroll software that automates tax withholding based on employee locations. Additionally, training HR and payroll staff on the new requirements will be essential to avoid penalties for non-compliance.

## Conclusion

The IRS's new guidance on remote employee taxation underscores the need for businesses to adapt to the evolving landscape of remote work. By taking proactive measures to comply with these requirements, employers can mitigate risks and ensure accurate tax reporting for their remote workforce.
