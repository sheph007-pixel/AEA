## EEOC Guidance on AI and Age Discrimination

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance on May 15, 2026, addressing age discrimination in AI-driven hiring tools. This guidance impacts employers using artificial intelligence in recruitment, emphasizing compliance with the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA) of 1967. The EEOC's focus is on preventing age bias that may arise from algorithmic decision-making processes.

## Key Points of the Guidance

The EEOC's guidance outlines how employers can ensure their AI tools do not inadvertently discriminate against older job applicants. It stresses the importance of auditing AI systems for potential age bias and adjusting algorithms that might favor younger candidates. Employers are reminded that the ADEA prohibits employment discrimination against individuals 40 years of age or older, and this extends to AI tools used in hiring.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers utilizing AI in their recruitment processes should take specific actions to comply with the EEOC's guidance:

1. **Audit AI Systems**: Conduct regular audits of AI-driven hiring tools to detect and correct any age biases. This involves reviewing the data sets used to train AI models and ensuring they are representative of all age groups.

2. **Training and Oversight**: Implement training for HR professionals and hiring managers on the implications of AI in recruitment and the importance of preventing age discrimination. Establish oversight mechanisms to monitor AI tool outputs for compliance with the ADEA.

3. **Documentation and Transparency**: Maintain thorough documentation of the AI systems and decision-making processes. Transparency in how AI tools evaluate candidates can help demonstrate compliance and address potential discrimination claims.

4. **Vendor Collaboration**: Work closely with AI vendors to ensure their products comply with anti-discrimination laws. Require vendors to provide detailed information on how their AI systems are developed and tested for bias.

## Implications for Employers

Failure to address age discrimination in AI hiring tools can result in legal challenges and reputational damage. The EEOC's guidance serves as a reminder that while AI can streamline recruitment, it must be implemented carefully to avoid violating age discrimination laws. Employers should prioritize compliance to mitigate risks and foster an inclusive hiring process.

By taking proactive steps, employers can leverage AI technology responsibly while ensuring fair treatment of all candidates, regardless of age.
