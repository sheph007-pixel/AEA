## IRS Clarifies Tax Treatment of Educational Assistance Programs

The IRS released new guidance on July 1, 2026, clarifying the tax treatment of educational assistance programs under Section 127 of the Internal Revenue Code. This guidance is crucial for employers offering educational benefits to their employees, as it impacts how these benefits are taxed and reported.

Section 127 allows employers to provide employees with up to $5,250 per year in educational assistance benefits without including it in the employee's gross income. The new guidance reiterates that this tax-free benefit applies to both undergraduate and graduate courses, provided the courses are not related to sports, games, or hobbies unless they are part of a degree program.

## Key Details of the Guidance

The IRS guidance emphasizes that educational assistance programs must be a separate written plan that meets specific requirements. Employers must ensure that the benefits are available to employees on a nondiscriminatory basis. The guidance also clarifies that the exclusion applies only to employees and does not extend to spouses or dependents.

Additionally, the IRS highlighted that educational assistance benefits must not be part of a cafeteria plan. Employers should review their current benefits offerings to ensure compliance with these stipulations, as improperly structured plans could result in taxable income for employees.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers currently offering or planning to offer educational assistance programs should take immediate steps to review their plans. Key actions include:

1. **Audit Current Plans**: Ensure that your educational assistance program is a separate written plan that complies with Section 127 requirements.
   
2. **Review Eligibility Criteria**: Confirm that the program is available to employees on a nondiscriminatory basis and does not include ineligible courses.

3. **Update Documentation**: Amend any documentation to clarify that the benefits are not part of a cafeteria plan, and ensure records are in place to substantiate compliance.

4. **Communicate with Employees**: Inform employees about the tax-free nature of these benefits and any changes to the program.

5. **Consult with Tax Advisors**: Work with tax professionals to ensure proper implementation and reporting of educational assistance benefits.

By aligning with the IRS guidance, employers can continue to offer valuable educational benefits while ensuring tax compliance. Failure to adhere to these guidelines could result in unintended tax liabilities for both employers and employees.
