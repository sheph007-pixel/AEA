## IRS Announces New Limits for Transportation Fringe Benefits

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) announced on June 9, 2026, an increase in the limits for qualified transportation fringe benefits under Section 132(f) of the Internal Revenue Code. Effective January 1, 2026, the monthly limit for commuter highway vehicle and transit passes will rise to $310, up from the previous $300. The monthly parking benefit limit will also increase to $310.

These changes are part of the IRS's annual cost-of-living adjustments that reflect inflation and economic shifts. Employers offering transportation benefits should prepare to adjust their benefit plans accordingly to take advantage of the increased limits.

## Impact on Employers and Employees

Employers who provide transportation fringe benefits can now offer employees higher pre-tax contributions towards commuting costs. This adjustment can enhance employee satisfaction by reducing their taxable income and increasing their take-home pay. The rise in limits also helps align benefits with the increasing costs of commuting in urban areas.

For employees, the increased limits mean greater savings on commuting expenses. By utilizing the full pre-tax benefit, employees can reduce their taxable income, which may result in lower federal income tax liabilities.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Benefit Plans**: Employers should review their current transportation benefit offerings and update them to reflect the new IRS limits. This may involve revising plan documents and communicating changes to employees.

2. **Adjust Payroll Systems**: Ensure that payroll systems are updated to accommodate the increased limits starting January 1, 2026. This includes adjusting any automated deductions for employee transportation benefits.

3. **Communicate Changes to Employees**: Employers should inform employees of the new limits and how they can maximize their transportation benefits. Consider holding informational sessions or distributing written materials to explain the benefits of increased pre-tax contributions.

4. **Coordinate with Benefit Providers**: If using third-party administrators for transportation benefits, coordinate with them to ensure a smooth transition to the new limits.

By taking these steps, employers can ensure compliance with IRS regulations while providing valuable benefits to their employees. The adjustment in transportation fringe benefit limits presents an opportunity to enhance employee compensation packages in a cost-effective manner.
