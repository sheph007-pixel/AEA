## EEOC's New Guidelines on Religious Accommodations

On July 20, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released updated guidelines on religious accommodations in the workplace. This revision impacts how employers handle requests for religious accommodations under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000e). The EEOC's new guidance clarifies what constitutes a reasonable accommodation and the extent of undue hardship on the conduct of the employer's business.

The update comes after a series of court rulings that necessitated a clearer framework for employers to follow. The EEOC emphasizes that employers must engage in a meaningful dialogue with employees requesting accommodations due to religious beliefs. The guidelines provide examples of accommodations, such as flexible scheduling, voluntary shift substitutions, and modifications to workplace policies or practices.

## Implications for Employers

The EEOC's updated guidance requires employers to reassess their current policies regarding religious accommodations. Employers must ensure that their HR teams are trained to recognize and appropriately respond to accommodation requests. The guidelines highlight the necessity of documenting the interactive process between employer and employee to demonstrate compliance with Title VII.

Employers should note that the EEOC's definition of undue hardship remains consistent with the precedent set by the Supreme Court in Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison. An accommodation may be denied if it imposes more than a de minimis cost or burden on the employer's operations. However, the EEOC's examples provide a nuanced understanding of what may or may not constitute undue hardship in various contexts.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review and Update Policies**: Employers should immediately review their existing policies on religious accommodations to ensure alignment with the new EEOC guidelines. This includes updating employee handbooks and training materials.

2. **Train HR Personnel**: Conduct training sessions for HR staff and managers on the updated guidelines to ensure they understand the requirements and can effectively manage accommodation requests.

3. **Document the Accommodation Process**: Implement a standardized process for documenting all accommodation requests and the interactive process that follows. This documentation is crucial in demonstrating compliance with the EEOC's requirements.

4. **Engage in Dialogue**: Encourage open communication between employees and management regarding religious accommodations. Employers should foster an environment where employees feel comfortable requesting accommodations without fear of retaliation.

By taking these steps, employers can better navigate the complexities of religious accommodations and ensure compliance with the EEOC's updated guidelines. Adhering to these practices not only minimizes legal risks but also promotes a more inclusive workplace.
