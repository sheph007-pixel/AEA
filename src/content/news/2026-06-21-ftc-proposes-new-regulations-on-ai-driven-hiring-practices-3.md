## FTC's Proposed AI Hiring Regulations

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) proposed new regulations on June 15, 2026, targeting the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in hiring practices. These regulations aim to address potential biases and discrimination that may arise from AI-driven hiring tools. The proposed rules would require companies using AI in recruitment to conduct regular bias audits and maintain transparency with applicants regarding AI's role in the hiring process.

## Key Provisions and Compliance Requirements

Under the proposed regulations, employers must ensure that AI tools used in hiring are free from discriminatory biases. This involves conducting annual bias audits and submitting the results to the FTC. The audits should evaluate the AI's decision-making processes and outcomes across various demographic groups to ensure compliance with anti-discrimination laws, such as Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Additionally, companies must disclose to job applicants when AI is used in evaluating their applications. This transparency requirement is designed to provide candidates with a clear understanding of how their application is being assessed and to whom they can direct concerns regarding potential biases.

## Impact on Employers

For businesses, particularly those with limited resources, these proposed regulations may necessitate significant adjustments to their hiring processes. Companies might need to invest in new technologies or services to perform the required bias audits. Moreover, the transparency mandate could require updates to recruitment communications and applicant tracking systems.

Employers should start by reviewing their current AI tools and consulting with legal and technical experts to assess compliance with the proposed rules. Organizations may also consider training HR personnel on the implications of AI in hiring to ensure informed decision-making and adherence to the new requirements.

## Next Steps and Deadlines

The FTC has opened a public comment period lasting 60 days, during which employers and other stakeholders can submit feedback on the proposed regulations. Following the comment period, the FTC will review the submissions and potentially adjust the regulations before finalizing them.

Employers should monitor developments closely and prepare for potential implementation by early 2027. This preparation includes budgeting for compliance measures and establishing internal protocols to meet the new regulatory demands.

In summary, the FTC's proposed regulations represent a significant shift in how AI-driven hiring practices are governed. By taking proactive steps now, employers can better navigate these changes and mitigate potential risks associated with AI bias in recruitment.
