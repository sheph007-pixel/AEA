## NLRB Expands Definition of Protected Concerted Activity

On May 1, 2026, the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) issued a ruling in the case of XYZ Corp. v. NLRB that significantly broadens the definition of "protected concerted activity" under the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA). This decision impacts how employers must handle employee discussions concerning workplace conditions, even when those discussions occur on social media platforms or outside of regular working hours.

The ruling expands the scope of Section 7 of the NLRA, which guarantees employees the right to engage in concerted activities for the purpose of collective bargaining or other mutual aid or protection. Previously, the focus was primarily on activities directly related to union organization or collective bargaining. Now, the NLRB's broader interpretation includes informal discussions among employees about workplace conditions, wages, and other employment terms, regardless of whether these discussions involve a formal union context.

## Implications for Employers

This expanded interpretation means that employers must exercise caution before disciplining employees for social media posts or private conversations about workplace issues. Any adverse action taken against employees for engaging in such discussions could now be deemed a violation of the NLRA, potentially leading to legal challenges and penalties.

Employers should review their social media and communication policies to ensure they do not inadvertently infringe on these expanded rights. Policies that broadly prohibit employees from discussing work conditions or that impose overly restrictive guidelines on employee speech could be subject to scrutiny under the new ruling.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Policy Review**: Employers should audit their current employee handbooks and communication policies to ensure compliance with the expanded definition of protected concerted activity. Pay particular attention to social media guidelines and any clauses that address employee speech about workplace conditions.

2. **Training and Awareness**: Conduct training sessions for management and HR personnel to familiarize them with the new NLRB standards. Ensure that all supervisory staff understand the implications of the ruling and the need to respect employees' rights to discuss workplace issues.

3. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Given the potential for increased litigation under the expanded definition, consult with legal counsel to assess risks and develop strategies for compliance. Legal guidance can help in crafting policies that protect both the employer's interests and employees' rights.

4. **Monitor Developments**: Stay informed about any further guidance or case law developments related to this ruling. The interpretation of protected concerted activity may continue to evolve, and staying updated will help in maintaining compliance.

This NLRB ruling underscores the importance of balancing employer interests with employee rights. By proactively adjusting policies and practices, employers can mitigate risks and foster a compliant workplace environment.
