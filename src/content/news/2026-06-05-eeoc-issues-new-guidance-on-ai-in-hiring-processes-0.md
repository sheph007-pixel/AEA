## EEOC Guidance on AI in Hiring

On June 1, 2026, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) released new guidance on the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in hiring processes. This guidance aims to ensure compliance with existing anti-discrimination laws, particularly Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. The EEOC's document highlights potential risks of bias and discrimination when using AI technologies for recruitment, selection, and other employment-related decisions.

Employers using AI tools must ensure these technologies do not disproportionately exclude or disadvantage protected groups. The EEOC emphasizes that the responsibility for compliance rests with employers, even if third-party vendors provide the AI tools. Employers should conduct regular audits of AI systems to identify and mitigate any discriminatory impacts.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should take immediate steps to comply with the EEOC's guidance. First, review all AI tools currently in use for hiring and assess them for potential bias. This may involve collaborating with technical experts to understand the algorithms and data sets used by these systems.

Second, implement a monitoring process to continuously evaluate the impact of AI on hiring decisions. This includes tracking demographic data and hiring outcomes to identify any patterns of discrimination.

Finally, ensure that all employees involved in the hiring process are trained on the implications of using AI and the necessity of maintaining compliance with anti-discrimination laws. Employers might consider engaging legal counsel to navigate the complexities of AI compliance and to develop robust policies that address these new challenges.

## Implications of Non-Compliance

Failure to adhere to the EEOC's guidance could result in significant legal and financial consequences. Employers found to be in violation of Title VII due to biased AI practices may face lawsuits, penalties, and reputational damage. The EEOC's guidance serves as a critical reminder of the need for diligence and proactive measures in the adoption of AI technologies in the workplace.

By staying informed and taking necessary precautions, employers can leverage AI tools effectively while upholding their commitment to equal employment opportunities.
