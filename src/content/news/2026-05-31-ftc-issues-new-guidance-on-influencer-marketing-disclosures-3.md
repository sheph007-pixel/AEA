## FTC Clarifies Influencer Marketing Disclosure Requirements

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has issued new guidance on influencer marketing disclosures, effective July 1, 2026. This update aims to ensure that sponsored content is clearly identified to consumers, addressing the growing influence of social media on purchasing decisions.

The guidance clarifies the requirements under the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 41-58), emphasizing that influencers and brands must disclose any material connections that could affect the credibility of endorsements. This includes payments, free products, or any other incentives provided to influencers.

## Key Requirements for Employers

Employers utilizing influencer marketing should note the FTC's emphasis on clear and conspicuous disclosures. The guidance specifies that disclosures must be placed in a location that consumers will not miss, using simple and understandable language. Phrases like "ad," "sponsored," or "paid partnership" are recommended for clarity.

The FTC also highlights that disclosures should be made in the same format as the content. For instance, if an endorsement is made in a video, the disclosure should be included both in the video and in the accompanying text description.

## Action Items for Compliance

To comply with the new guidance, employers should:

1. **Review Influencer Agreements**: Ensure contracts with influencers include explicit requirements for FTC-compliant disclosures.
   
2. **Audit Current Campaigns**: Examine ongoing influencer marketing campaigns to verify that disclosures meet the new standards.

3. **Train Marketing Teams**: Provide training to marketing staff and influencers on the updated disclosure requirements to prevent non-compliance.

4. **Monitor Influencer Content**: Implement a monitoring system to regularly check influencer content for proper disclosures.

Failure to comply with these guidelines could result in FTC enforcement actions, including fines and corrective measures. By taking proactive steps, employers can mitigate risks and maintain consumer trust in their brand.
