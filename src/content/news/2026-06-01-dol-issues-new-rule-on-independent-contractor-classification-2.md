## DOL's New Rule on Independent Contractor Status

The Department of Labor (DOL) issued a final rule on May 15, 2026, redefining the criteria for determining independent contractor status under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This rule, effective July 1, 2026, aims to clarify the distinction between employees and independent contractors, impacting businesses across various sectors.

The rule emphasizes an "economic reality" test, focusing on two core factors: the worker's opportunity for profit or loss based on initiative and investment, and the degree of control exercised by the worker over their work. These factors are intended to determine whether a worker is economically dependent on the employer or operates as a separate business.

## Implications for Employers

Employers must reassess their workforce classifications to ensure compliance with the new criteria. Misclassification can lead to significant penalties, including back wages and fines. The rule's clarification aims to reduce litigation and provide a clearer framework for businesses to follow.

The rule replaces the previous administration's guidance, which was vacated by the courts. This new regulation seeks to strike a balance by providing flexibility for businesses while protecting workers' rights.

## Action Items for HR and Legal Teams

1. **Audit Current Classifications**: Review and update the classification of current independent contractors. Ensure they meet the criteria outlined in the new rule.
   
2. **Training and Compliance**: Conduct training sessions for HR and management teams to understand the implications of the rule and how to apply the economic reality test.

3. **Legal Review**: Engage legal counsel to evaluate contracts and business practices to mitigate risks of misclassification.

4. **Documentation**: Maintain detailed records of the basis for classification decisions to defend against potential audits or legal challenges.

By taking these steps, employers can better navigate the complexities of worker classification and avoid costly legal disputes. The DOL's new rule provides a framework that requires careful consideration and proactive adjustments by businesses to remain compliant.
