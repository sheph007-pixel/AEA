## California Expands Bereavement Leave Rights

California Governor signed the Bereavement Leave Expansion Act into law on May 15, 2026. This law, effective July 1, 2026, mandates that employers with five or more employees must provide up to ten days of unpaid bereavement leave. This expansion amends the California Family Rights Act (CFRA) under Cal. Gov. Code § 12945.2, enhancing employee rights during family loss.

### Key Provisions

The new law requires employers to grant eligible employees leave for the death of a family member, defined as a spouse, child, parent, sibling, grandparent, grandchild, domestic partner, or parent-in-law. The leave must be completed within three months of the death and may be taken consecutively or intermittently.

Employers must maintain confidentiality regarding the employee’s bereavement leave and are prohibited from retaliating against employees who exercise their rights under this law. Documentation, such as a death certificate or obituary, may be requested by the employer but must be treated with confidentiality.

### Employer Action Items

1. **Update Employee Handbooks**: Reflect the new bereavement leave policy in employee handbooks and ensure that all HR personnel are trained on the updated procedures.
   
2. **Review Leave Policies**: Adjust existing leave policies to accommodate the new requirements, ensuring compliance with both state and federal laws.

3. **Communicate Changes**: Notify employees of their rights under the new law, including the process for requesting leave and any documentation requirements.

4. **Train Managers**: Educate managers on the new requirements to prevent potential retaliation claims and ensure proper handling of leave requests.

### Implications for Employers

This expansion aligns California with other states providing bereavement leave, such as Oregon and Illinois. Employers should review their leave policies to ensure compliance and avoid potential penalties. Non-compliance could lead to complaints filed with the California Department of Fair Employment and Housing (DFEH), which enforces the CFRA.

While the law mandates unpaid leave, employers may allow employees to use accrued paid time off during bereavement leave. This flexibility can help mitigate the financial impact on employees.

### Conclusion

The Bereavement Leave Expansion Act underscores the growing trend of states enhancing employee leave rights. Employers in California must act swiftly to update policies and educate staff to ensure compliance by the July 1, 2026, effective date. Failure to comply could result in penalties and damage to employer reputation.
