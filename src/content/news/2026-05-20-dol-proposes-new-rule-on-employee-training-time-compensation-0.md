## DOL Proposes New Rule on Employee Training Time Compensation

The Department of Labor (DOL) announced a proposed rule on May 18, 2026, that could change how employers compensate employees for training time. This proposal seeks to clarify when training time should be considered compensable under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA).

### What's in the Proposed Rule?

The proposed rule aims to address ambiguities in the current regulations concerning when employers must pay employees for time spent in training. Under the existing standard, training time is generally compensable unless it meets all four criteria: it occurs outside regular working hours, is voluntary, is not job-related, and no other work is concurrently performed (29 C.F.R. § 785.27).

The DOL's proposal suggests expanding the definition of compensable training to include mandatory sessions that enhance an employee's skills directly related to their current job responsibilities, even if conducted outside normal working hours. This shift could particularly impact industries where continuous skill development is essential, such as technology and healthcare.

### Implications for Employers

If adopted, the rule would require employers to reassess their training programs and compensation policies. Employers would need to ensure compliance by identifying which training sessions qualify as compensable under the new definition. This could lead to increased payroll costs, particularly for businesses that rely heavily on after-hours training to upskill their workforce.

### Action Items for Employers

- **Review Training Programs:** Employers should conduct an audit of all training programs to determine which sessions might become compensable under the new rule.
- **Update Payroll Systems:** Adjust payroll systems to account for potential changes in compensable hours, ensuring accurate tracking and compensation for training time.
- **Policy Revisions:** Amend employee handbooks and training policies to reflect any changes in compensation for training time.

### Next Steps

The proposed rule is open for public comment until July 18, 2026. Employers and other stakeholders are encouraged to submit feedback to the DOL. The final rule will be issued after the comment period, with potential implementation later this year.

Employers should keep an eye on developments regarding this proposal, as it could significantly impact training budgets and operational practices.
