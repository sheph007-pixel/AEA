## The New Guidance

The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) has issued its final guidance on the use of artificial intelligence and automated systems in employment decisions. The guidance, which builds on the agency's 2023 technical assistance documents, clarifies that employers bear full responsibility for discriminatory outcomes produced by AI tools — even when those tools are developed and operated by third-party vendors.

The guidance applies to AI and algorithmic tools used in hiring, promotions, performance evaluations, compensation decisions, and terminations.

## Key Requirements

1. **Employer liability is non-delegable**: If an AI tool produces a hiring or employment decision that results in disparate impact against a protected class, the employer — not the software vendor — is liable under Title VII, the ADA, and other federal anti-discrimination laws

2. **Bias audits are expected**: While the guidance stops short of mandating specific audit methodologies, the EEOC states that employers should conduct regular disparate impact analyses of AI tools and document the results

3. **Accommodation obligations apply**: Employers must ensure that AI-driven assessments do not screen out individuals with disabilities who could perform essential job functions with reasonable accommodation

4. **Transparency to applicants**: The EEOC recommends that employers inform applicants and employees when AI tools are being used in employment decisions, consistent with emerging state transparency laws in Illinois, Colorado, and New York City

5. **Record retention**: Employers should retain records related to AI tool selection, validation, bias testing, and employment outcomes for the same periods required under existing EEOC record retention rules

## State and Local Laws Are Moving Faster

Several jurisdictions have enacted their own AI employment laws that may impose additional requirements:

- **Illinois** requires notice and consent before using AI in video interviews
- **Colorado** enacted the AI Act requiring impact assessments for high-risk AI systems in employment
- **New York City** requires bias audits for automated employment decision tools

Employers should comply with the most restrictive applicable requirements across all jurisdictions where they operate.

## Action Items

- **Inventory your AI tools**: Catalog all AI and automated systems used in employment processes across your organization
- **Request vendor documentation**: Ask vendors to provide bias audit results, validation studies, and information about how their tools were tested for fairness
- **Conduct independent testing**: Do not rely solely on vendor-provided audit results — conduct your own disparate impact analysis using your applicant and employee data
- **Update applicant communications**: Add disclosures about AI tool usage to application processes and employee handbooks
- **Establish human review checkpoints**: Build in human oversight for significant employment decisions that rely on AI tool outputs
