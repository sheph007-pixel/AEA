## California's Pay Transparency Law

California has enacted a new pay transparency law requiring employers to include pay ranges in job postings, effective January 1, 2026. This law, Senate Bill 1162, applies to all employers with 15 or more employees. The legislation aims to promote pay equity and transparency in the hiring process.

Under the new law, employers must disclose the salary or hourly wage range that they reasonably expect to pay for a position. The range must be included in any job advertisement posted by the employer or a third party. This requirement extends to remote positions if the job can be performed in California.

## Compliance Requirements

Employers must take specific steps to comply with the new mandate. First, they should review current job postings and ensure that salary ranges are included for all applicable positions. Additionally, employers should audit their compensation structures to ensure that the disclosed pay ranges are accurate and reflect current market conditions.

Failure to comply with the pay transparency requirements could result in penalties. The California Labor Commissioner is authorized to investigate complaints and impose fines. Employers found in violation may face penalties of up to $10,000 per violation.

## Record-Keeping Obligations

The law also imposes record-keeping obligations on employers. Companies must maintain records of job titles and wage rate histories for each employee throughout their employment and for three years after their employment ends. These records must be made available to the Labor Commissioner upon request.

## Action Items for Employers

Employers should take immediate action to prepare for the law's implementation:

1. **Audit Job Postings:** Review and update all job postings to include salary ranges.
2. **Review Compensation Policies:** Ensure that pay ranges are consistent with internal compensation policies and market rates.
3. **Update Record-Keeping Practices:** Implement systems to maintain required records for job titles and wage rates.
4. **Train HR Personnel:** Educate HR staff on the new requirements to ensure compliance in recruitment and record-keeping processes.

## Conclusion

California's new pay transparency law represents a significant shift in employment practices, aiming to foster pay equity. Employers must act now to ensure compliance by the January 1, 2026 deadline. By taking proactive steps, companies can avoid penalties and contribute to a more transparent and equitable workplace.
