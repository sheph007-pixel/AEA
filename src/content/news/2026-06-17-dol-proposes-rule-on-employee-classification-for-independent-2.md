## New DOL Proposal on Independent Contractor Classification

The U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) proposed a new rule on June 15, 2026, to clarify the classification of independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). This proposal aims to address ambiguities faced by employers in distinguishing between employees and independent contractors, a critical distinction that affects wages, benefits, and tax liabilities.

The proposed rule introduces a revised economic reality test to determine a worker's status. This test focuses on the degree of control an employer has over the worker, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, the permanency of the relationship, and the worker's investment in equipment or materials. These factors are designed to provide clearer guidance than previous interpretations.

## Implications for Employers

If implemented, the rule could significantly impact how businesses classify their workforce. Misclassification can lead to costly penalties, including back wages and fines. Employers need to reassess their current worker classifications to ensure compliance with the proposed guidelines.

The DOL's proposal is open for public comment until August 14, 2026. Businesses are encouraged to review the proposed changes and consider submitting feedback. The DOL will review these comments before finalizing the rule, which could take several months.

## Action Items for Employers

1. **Review Worker Classifications**: Conduct an audit of current worker classifications. Focus on those classified as independent contractors to ensure they meet the criteria outlined in the proposed rule.

2. **Submit Feedback**: Consider submitting comments on the proposed rule by the August 14 deadline. Providing input can help shape the final rule to better reflect industry needs and realities.

3. **Prepare for Changes**: Begin planning for potential reclassification of workers. This includes budgeting for potential increases in payroll costs and adjusting contracts to align with the new criteria.

4. **Consult Legal Counsel**: Engage with legal experts to understand the implications of the proposed rule on your specific business operations and to ensure compliance with both federal and state laws.

The proposed rule is part of a broader effort by the DOL to ensure fair labor practices and protect workers' rights. Employers should stay informed about the developments of this proposal to mitigate risks associated with worker misclassification.
